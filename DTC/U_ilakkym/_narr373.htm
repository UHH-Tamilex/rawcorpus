  9. puṇarvatu kollō nāḷiyu namakkē.

{{Readable Translation}}

  • Will [there be] uniting, friend, also tomorrow for us,
  • when chasing away from the little millet with curved ears
  • the green groups, having mounted the broad bamboo platform
  • on the Vēṅki, the mouth of an astrologer, who opened [its] black(?)[FNote_1038] buds,
  • [when] playing in the waterfall on the fearful mountin-side
  • with the man from a land, where the creeper woman, who sang(?)[FNote_1039] of the mountin black with collyrium [clouds] of [her] father, is pounding the white paddy of the wild rice, while the low-headed she-monkey approaches the pulp
  • coming from the jacktree in the frontyard [and] chokes [it] up?

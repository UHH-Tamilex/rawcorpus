{{Metrical text}}

  1. aṇivari maruṅki ṉituvaḷarn tiṭṭa
  2. maṇiyēr tōṭṭa miyā rēṉa
  3. lirumpiṭit taṭakkiyiṟ ṭaṭiiya perumpuṇaṅ
  4. kāval kaṇṇiṉa māyi ṉāyiḻi
  5. nannili yiṭiterin tuṇarāṉ ṟaṉmali ((5))
  6. yāra nīviya vaṇikiḷa rākañ
  7. cāra ṉīḷiṭic cālavaṇ ṭarppac
  8. celvañ celluṅko ṟāṉē yuyarvarip
  9. peruṅkal viṭarakañ cilampa virumpuli
  10. kaḷiṟutolit turaṟuṅ kaṭiyiṭi maḻicettuc ((10))
  11. centiṉi yuṇaṅka ṟokukku
  12. miṉkaḷ yāṇarttam muṟivi ṉūrkkē.

{{Readable Translation}}

  • If our eyes [are] guarding, [o you of] choice jewels,
  • the big field, plump[FNote_986] as the broad trunk of the large she-elephant,
  • of millet, full of collyrium, with leaves resembling sapphires(?),
  • grown [and] planted wonderfully on the decorative mountin side,
  • will he be going[FNote_987] himself,
  • while the bees are humming very much, on the long way on the slope,
  • on [his] adorned lustrious breast smeared with sandal
  • from his mountin, he who does not perceive [and] distinguish our state,
  • - to the village of their(?) residence, fertile with sweet toddy,
  • where they gather the dried [corn] of the red millet,
  • thinking it speedily thundering rin, when the big tiger, having destroyed
  • the elephant bull, roars so that the inside of the cave of big stones resounds on the hight mountin. (?)

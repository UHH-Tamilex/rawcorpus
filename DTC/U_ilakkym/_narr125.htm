{{Readable Translation}}

  • If we beg [his] marrying [us and then] coming,
  • saying "we fear [your] coming at midnight,
  • when the broad-mouthed male of the bear searching for prey
  • bends to reach the anthill with curved stripes,
  • roars for the good snake to shiver [and] dig [it] out,
  • sighing inside as the bellows the smith blows on the furnace",
  • he will certinly go with us, friend, without it being long
  • that he meets [his] bride on a good day, on our good mountin(?),
  • - to his land of big stones, where the sleeping elephant bull awakes,
  • when[FNote_406] those wearing chaplets of Vēṅki in soft [shoots?][FNote_407] produce the long heaps of soft millet on the green rocks, inside(?), as if one sees the ground
  • of those on the threshing-floor throwing the bull.

eḻukūṟṟirukkai
"Eḻukūṟṟirukkai is one of the figures of speech depending for its effect on sound alone as opposed to poruḷ aṇi; it is a verse in the composition of which the numerals one to seven occur first in the ascending order and then in the descending order. Besides this one there are one eḻukūṟṟirukkai by tirumaṅkai āḻvār, one by nakkiratēvanāyaṉar in the caivaite eleventh book, one in the commentary of yāpparuṅkalam (p.410 [Oriental Manuscript Library edition, 1960]), one by aruṇakirinātār and one by the author of māṟaṉalaṅkāram." [VMS, p.2:423]
ēkapātam
"`ēkapātam āvatu ōraṭiyē nāṟkāl uccarittāl pāṭṭup poruḷ vēṟupaṭṭu muṭivatu' (yāpparuṅkala virutti, cū.96, P.404, 410; edition 1960, M.V. Venugopal Pillai). Ekapātam is that kind of verse in which the same line is repeated four times, but the meaning for each line is different (yāpparuṅkala virutti, cūtram 96; page 404, Oriental Manuscript Library edition, 1960)" [VMS, p.2:413]
ēṇ cīr viruttam
"tāṇṭakam is what is called in later literature as eṇcīr viruttam. There are also two taṇṭakams in Vaishnava hymnal literature by tirumaṅkai āḻvār: tirukkuṟuntāṇṭakam, tiruneṭuntāṇṭakam. As Tirunāvukkaracar specialised in tāṇṭakam, the sixth book of Tēvāram arranged according to paṇ contains tāṇṭakam. He is also called taṇṭakaccaturar, one who is capable of composing tāṇṭakam" [VMS, p.1:31]
(tiruk) kaṭaik kāppu
The last verse in Ñāṉacampantar's patikam is called tiruk kaṭaik kāppu in Saiva tradition; This mentions the benefits one would get if one recites the verses (Periya Purāṇam, Tiruñāṉacampantar Purāṇam, 80); [VMS1:1:11, Vol.2, p. 168]
Kattaḷaikkalittuṟai
"In Tēvāram, what is called as Kattaḷaikkalittuṟai is known as tiruviruttam. In vaishnava hymnal literature also there is one work by nammāḻvar which goes by the name of tiruviruttam" [VMS, p.1:23]

(tiruk)kōmūttiri
"kōmūttiri. iraṇṭu variyāka eḻuti mēlum kīḻum oṉṟu iṭaiviṭṭu vācittālum atuvē āvatu (yāpparuṅkala virutti, p.414, Govt. Oriental manuscripts Library edition, 1960)
kōmūttiri eṉpatu iraṇṭu iraṇṭu variyāka oru ceyyuḷai eḻuti mēlum kīḻum oṉṟu iṭaiyiṭṭu vācittalum ac ceyyuḷē yāvatu (taṇṭiyalaṅkāram, p.223; S.S.Kaḻakam edition, 1956)
naṭai peṟum cēviṉatu mūttira oḻukkam pōṉṟu naṭappiṉ atu kōmūttiri yām; aḵtāvatu oru ceyyuḷai iraṇṭu variyāka eḻuti mēlum kīḻum oṉṟu iṭaiyiṭṭu vācikkavum ac ceyyuḷāy naṭattal (maṟaṉalaṅkāram, p.492; tamiḻ caṅkam edition, 1929)
In rhetoric, verse of two lines composed in such a way that the first letter of the 1st, the 2nd of the 2nd, the 3rd of the 1st, the 4th of the 2nd, etc. when read together form the 1st line, and the 1st of the 2nd, the 2nd of the 1st, the 3rd of the 2nd, the 4th of the 1st, etc. form the 2nd line.
(This decade of verse are in antāti; the last letter, syllable, foot or line of the previous stanza begins the next stanza)" [VMS, p. 2:440]
cakkara māṟṟu
"Cakkaramāṟṟu. The occasion in which this was sung: when campantar went to the palace of neṭumāraṉ to cure his fever, [....] he enquired campantar about his native place. This was sung in reply to that. There are two decades of verse like this. The following is one of those. In this in the first verse all the twelve names of Kaḻumalam are given in an order. The second verse begins with the second name and so on." [VMS, p.2:430]
"In this decade of verse the first stanza mentions all the twelve names of Cīkāḻi in an order. The second stanza begins with the name mentionned last in the first stanza and so on." [VMS, p.2:434]
canta viruttam
"This decade is composed in what is called canta viruttam (canta viruttam) which means stanza in viruttam metre with a uniform rhythmic movement." [VMS, p.2:354]

(tiru)mālaimāṟṟu
"(Tirumālaimāṟṟu) prosody these [sic!] are called embellishment of words which is a kind of verse that remains identical when its letters are read in the reverse direction. Yāpparuṅkalam names them as `muṟaikkavi' i.e. `laboured poetry'. Such verses were composed by Ñāṉacampantar when he was staying in Cīkāḻi after being visited by Tirunāvukkaracu; cf. periya purāṇam, tiruñāṉacampantar purāṇam, 273-277; [cf.] miṟaikkavip pāṭṭē (yāpparuṅkalam, 94)" [VMS, p.2:349]

moḻimāṟṟu
"a mode of construing a verse in which the words have to be transposed for the ascertainment of its proper meaning; one of eight poruḷkōḷ. Only if the verse are transliterated [sic!] the spirit of the original can be preserved. I have given the meaning sacrificing the poruḷkōḷ." [VMS, p.2:408]
yamakam
"Repetition in a stanza with changes of meaning sometimes effected by changes in the division of words. It is also called maṭakku. In rhetoric, it is repetition of a word, foot or line of poetry, in a stanza, each time in a different sense (taṇṭiyalaṅkāram, 90)" [VMS, p.2:454]
vaḻimoḻittiruvirākam
"a kind of rhythmic verse in which there is agreement in rhyme of all the feet in every line of a stanza" [VMS, p.2:445]
[tiru]virākam:
"a kind of verse characterised by rapid movement as almost all the words in those verses will be of short letters (kuṟilkaḷ)" [VMS, p.2:296]

{7:9}__1+
{$} malaikkum makaḷ añca matakariyai urittīr
you flayed a frenzied elephant to make the dughter of the mountin to fear.
varumuppuraṅkaḷ erittīr
you burnt the three cities that came opposing you.
cilaikkum kolaiccē ukantu (1) ēṟṟu oḻiyīr
you would not leave riding with desire on a bull which is capable of killing, and is bellowing.
cil palikku ilkaḷtoṟum celavu oḻiyīr
you would not stop going to each and every house for a small quantity of alms.
kalaikkompum karimaruppum iṭaṟik kalavam mayil pīliyum kār akilum alaikkum puṉalcēr aricil teṉ karai aḻaku ār tirupputtūr aḻakaṉīrē
Beautiful god in Puttūr full of beauty, on the southern bank of the river Aricil whose water tosses the feathers of the peacock with a bushy til and black eagle-wood and washing the elephants tusk and the horns of the stag!
[[aḻakaṉīrē is the nominative of address of aḻakaṉār]]
[[malaikkum makaḷ, kalavam mayil the underlined letters are augmentations for rhythm]]
[[Variant reading: (1)ēṟu]]
{7:9}__2+
{$} arumalarōṉ ciram oṉṟu aṟuttīr
you cut off one head of Piramaṉ who is seated in a rare (lotus) flower.
aḻaṟcūlattil antakaṉaic ceṟuttīr
you killed an acuraṉ by name Antakaṉ by your trident which was spitting fire.
tirumakaḷkōṉ neṭumāl
the tall Māl, the husband of Tirumakaḷ.
pala nāḷ ciṟappu ākiya pūcaṉai ceypoḻutil
when he was performing eminent worship for many days.
orumalar āyirattiṉ kuṟaivā niṟaivāka ōr kaṇ malarcūṭṭalumē
as soon as he adorned your feet with an eye resembling flower when one flower was deficient to complete the thousand.
poruviṟal āḻipurintu aḷittīr
you gave with desire a discus of fighting strength.
poḻil ār tirupputtūrppuṉitaṇīrē
the pure Civaṉ in Tirupputtūr which is full of gardens!

{7:9}__3+
{$} tarikkum tarai nīr taḻal kāṟṟu antaram cantiraṉ cavitā iyamāṉaṉ āṉīr
you are the earth that bears everything, water, fire air, sky, moon, sun and the performer of sacrifices
[[this describes the light forms of Civaṉ; aṣṭamūrttam]]
carikkum palikkut talai aṅkai ēntit taiyalār peyyakkoḷvatu takkatu aṉṟu
it is not fit for your greatness when young ladies place alms, holding a skull in the palm to receive it, which is got by wandering from place to place.
murikkum taḷirc cantaṉattōṭu vēyum muḻaṅkum tiraikkaikaḷāl vāri mōti
striking aginst the banks taking in a swoop by the roaring waves which are like hands, bamboos and sandal woods which have leaves that break off.
arikkum puṉal cēr aricil teṉkarai aḻaku ār tirupputtūr aḻakaṉīrē
Beautiful Civaṉ in beautiful Tirupputtūr on the southern bank of the river Aricil, which has water which sifts all things.
{7:9}__4+
{$} koṭi uṭai mummatiḷ ventaḻiya
the three forts which had flags to be destroyed by being burnt.
(1) kuṉṟamvillā nāṇiyil kōl oṉṟiṉāl iṭipaṭa eytu erittīr, imaikkum aḷavil
you burnt them to produce a thunderous sound by a single arrow which was fixed in the bow-string having the mountin as the bow, in the twinkling of the eye.
emperumāṉ
my Lord!
umakku ār etir
who can equal you?
kaṭipaṭupūṅkaṉaiyāṉ karuppuccilaik kāmaṉai vēvakkaṭaikkaṇṇiṉāl poṭipaṭa nōkkiyatu eṉṉai (kol ō)
what is the reason for fixing your gaze of the outer corner of the eye to burn and reduce to ashes the cupid who has the bow of a sugar-cane and who has fragrant flowers as arrows.
poḻil ārtirupputtūrp puṉitaṉīrē
pure god in Tirupputtūr which is full of gardens.
[[kol ō: expletives]]
[[variant readings: (1)kuṉṟavillāṉāṇiyiṟ]]
{7:9}__5+
{$} vaṇaṅkit toḻuvār avar māl piramaṉ maṟṟum vāṉavar tāṉavar māmuṉivar
Māl, Piramaṉ and the celestials acurar and great sages pay obeisance and worship you with joined hands.
uṇaṅkal talaiyil palikoṇṭal eṉṉē
when that is your greatness what is the idea in receiving alms in a well-dried skull?
ulakaṅkaḷ ellām uṭaiyīr! uraiyīr
you have all the worlds as you possessions; please tell me.
iṇaṅkik kayal cēl iḷavāḷaipāya
when the kayal, cēl and young scabbard fish leap joining together and being friendly.
iṉakkeṇṭaituḷḷa
and when groups of keṇṭi (keṇṭai) jump up.
kaṇṭuirunta aṉṉam
the swans which were silently watching them.
aṇaṅkik kuṇamkoḷ aricil teṉkarai aḻaku ār tirupputtūr aḻakaṉīrē
the pure Civaṉ in beautiful Tirupputtūr on the southern bank of the Aricil, show their true nature by eating them!
[[koṇṭal-koḷḷal; vāṉ kāval koṇṭāṉ vaḻi niṉṟu vaikalum tāṉ kāval koṇṭal takum (puṟapporuḷ veṇpāmāli, 179); naṉaviṟkaṇṭa naṉṉutal mātaraik, kaṉaveṉak koṇṭaliṉ iṉiyōrkkum uraiyāṉ (peruṅkati, 4-7-132-133); this is a rare usage in this meaning; it is used in the meaning of cloud generally]]

{$} akattaṭimai ceyyum antaṇaṉ tāṉ
the brahmin who was doing services in the sanctuary.
aricil puṉalkoṇṭuvantu āṭṭukiṉṟāṉ
was bathing you bringing water from the river Aricil.
[[āṭṭukiṉṟāṉ historic present]]
mikattaḷarvu eytik kuṭattai num muṭimēl viḻuttiṭṭu naṭuṅkutalum
when he trembled cusing the water pot to fall on your head on account of excessive faintness (due to hunger)
avaṉukkuvakuttu (1) nittaṟpaṭiyum varum eṉṟu oru kāciṉai niṉṟa naṉṟip pukaḻttuṇai kaippukac ceytu ukantīr
you felt happy by putting into the hands of Pukaḻttuṇi who was firm in his good acts, having apportioned to him (and telling in his dream) you will receive as dily batta a gold coin (till the end of the famine)
[[This story is hinted by Campantar in the 7th verse of the decade on this shrine; this verse gives more detils about that; the name of the priest is also mentioned.]]

{7:9}__7+
{$} paḻikkum peruntakkaṉ eccam aḻiya
sacrifice of the great Takkaṉ who reviled you, to be destroyed.
pakalōṉ mutalāp pala tēvaraiyum teḻattiṭṭu avar aṅkam citaittaruḷum ceykai (1) eṉṉai (kolō)
what is the reason for injuring the limbs of many tevars beginning from the sun, having distressed them?
maikoḷ cemmiṭaṟīr
Civaṉ who has a black neck in a small patch, which is in all other places red!
viḻikkum taḻaippīliyoṭu ēlam unti viḷaṅkum maṇimuttoṭu poṉ varaṉṟi aḻikkum puṉalcēr aricil teṉkarai aḻakuār tirupputtūr aḻakaṉīrē
Beautiful god in beautiful Tirupputtūr on the southern bank of the Aricil whose water cuses damage to the banks, scraping gold, shining pearls, and pushing cardamom and feathers of peacock's til which has eyes that seem to be open.
[[Variant reading: (1)eṉṉaikolām]]

kuṟaḷ pāriṭaṅkaḷ paṟaitāmmuḻakka

piṟaikkoḷ caṭaitāḻap peyarntu naṭṭam peruṅkāṭu araṅkā niṉṟu āṭal eṉṉē
what is the reason for dancing having the big cremation ground as the stage, changing the steps of the feet when the caṭi which has the crescent, hangs low.
kaṟaikkoḷ maṇikaṇṭamum tiṇtōḷkaḷum karaṅkaḷ ciram taṉṉilum kaccumākap poṟikkoḷ (1) aravam palavum puṉaintīr
you adorned on your beautiful neck which has poison, strong shoulders, hands and head respectively as necklace, armlets on the shoulders, as bangles on the hands, as chaplet on the head and as belt in the waist, many cobras with spots on their hoods.
poḻil ār tiruppattūrp puṉitaṉīrē

{$} maḻaikkaṇ maṭavāḷai ōrpākam vaittīr
you kept on one half a young lady who has cool eyes.
vaḷarpuṉ caṭaik kaṅkaiyai vaittu ukantīr
you felt happy by placing Kaṅki on the growing golden caṭi.
muḻaikkoḷ aravōṭu eṉpu aṇikalaṉā
having worn as ornaments cobra which live in anthills, and bones
[[muḻaikkoḷaravu is a generic epithet]]
muḻunīṟumeypūcutal eṉṉai (kol ō)
what is the reason for smearing all over the body sacred ash?
kaḻaikkoḷ karumpum katalik kaṉiyum kaḻukiṉ paḻukkāyum kavarntu koṇṭiṭṭu
having stolen sugar-cane with stems, and plantin fruits, and ripe fruits of areca-palm.
aḻaikkum puṉalcēr aricil teṉkarai aḻakuār tiruppattūr aḻakaṉīrē
beautiful Civaṉ in beautiful Tirupputtūr on the southern bank of the river, Aricil, which brings floods making a loud sound;

{7:9}__10+
{$} kaṭikkum aravāl malaiyāl amarar kaṭalaik kaṭaiya(v) eḻukāḷa kūṭam oṭikkum ulakaṅkaḷai eṉṟu ataṉai umakkē amutā uṇṭīr
you drank for yourself the poison which rose when the immortals churned the ocean of milk using a biting serpent as a cord to turn the churning staff and a mountin as a churning staff, thinking that it would destroy all the worlds.
umiḻīr
you would not spit it out
iṭikkum maḻaivīḻttu iḻuttiṭṭu aruvi irupālum ōṭi iraikkum tiraikkai aṭikkum puṉalcēr aricil teṉkarai aḻaku ār tirupputtūr aḻakaṉīrē
beautiful Civaṉ in beautiful Tirupputtūr on the southern bank of the river, Aricil, which brings floods which beats with its hands of roaring waves, dragging the thundering cloud pulling it down, running on both sides as a stream and dashing at both the banks.
{7:9}__11+
{$} kār ūr maḻaipeytu poḻi aruvik kaḻaiyōṭu akil untiṭṭu irukaraiyum pōr ūr puṉalcēr aricil teṉkaraip poḻil ār tirupputtūrp puṉitar tammai
describing the spotless Civaṉ in Tirupputtūr full of gardens, situated on the southern bank of the Aricil which has floods that seem to be in battle with both the banks, pushing eagle-wood and bamboos found in the streams that fall from the mountin when the clouds rin excessively.
ārūraṉ aruntamiḻ aintiraṉoṭu aintu aḻakāl uraippārkaḷum kēṭpavarum
those who recite beautifully the ten rare tamiḻ verses composed by Ārūraṉ without committing mistakes in words and music, and those who listen to them.
cīr ūrtaru tēvarkaṇaṅkayoṭum iṇaṅkic civalōkam (atu) eytuvarē
will reach Civalōkam joining with the groups of eminent celestials.

{4:3}__1+
{$} mātarp piṟaik kaṇṇiyāṉai malaiyāṉ makaḷoṭum pāṭi
praising Civaṉ who has a chaplet of beautiful crescent, together with the daughter of the mountain.
pōtoṭu nīrcumantu ēttippukuvār avarpiṉ pukuvēṉ
I, who was entering the temple following people who entered into it carrying flowers and water.
yātum cuvaṭu paṭāmal aiyāṟu aṭaikiṉṟapōtu
when I reached aiyāṟu without the slightest indication of the body having become worn out.
kātal maṭap piṭiyōṭum kaḷiṟuvaruvaṉa kaṇṭēṉ
I saw male elephants coming along with their loving young female elephants.
avar tiruppātam kaṇṭēṉ
I saw his holy feet.
kaṇṭu aṟiyātaṉa kaṇṭēṉ
I saw what I did not see previously
[[cuvaṭupaṭāmal This word hints about nāvukkaracar's journey to mount Kailācam in which he wore out his legs, hands and chest. As ordered by Civaṉ he dived into a tank near Kailācam and appeared in a tank in tiruvaiyāṟu, his body restored to its former state. All living beings in tiruvaiyāṟu appeared to him as cakti and civam. This decade describes that. AT the exact spot, ulōkamātevi, one of the queens of Rājarāja I built a temple to commemorate this event and it is how known as ulōkamateviccuram. This festival is celebrated on the new moon day of the Tamiḻ month of āṭi]]

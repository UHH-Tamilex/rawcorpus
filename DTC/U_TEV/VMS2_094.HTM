{2:94}__1+
{$} cākai āyiram uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has thousand branches of the Vētam
[[āyiram does not denote exactly one thousand; but is used in the sense of countless]]
cāmamum ōtuvatu uṭaiyār
He chants Cāmavētam also
īkaiyār kaṭai nōkki irappatum pala pala uṭaiyār
he has the business begging very many times looking at the entrances of those who will give alms.
tōkai mā mayil aṉaiya tuṭiyiṭai pākamum uṭaiyār
he has as a half a lady whose waist is like the drum tapering at the middle, and who is as tender as the big peacock with a long til.
vākai nuṉ tuḷivīcum vāḻ koḷi puttūr uḷār
he is in vāḻkoḷiputtūr where the vāki tree (sirissa) scatters small drops (of honey) (vākai is the tree under which the Civaliṅkam was established and worshipped in this shrine)
{2:94}__2+
{$} eṇ il īramum uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has boundless love
ivar aṟaṅkaḷ ettaṉaiyōr
He has countless charitable deeds to his credit.
kaṇṇum āyiram uṭaiyār
he has thousand eyes.
kaiyum ōr āyiram uṭaiyār
he has thousand hands.
peṇṇum āyiram uṭaiyār
He has power which is of many kinds (peṇ is symbolic of prowess)
perumai ōr āyiram uṭaiyār
he has greatness of thousand kinds
vaṇṇam āyiram uṭaiyār
he has thousand colours
vāḻ koḷi puttūr uḷār
he is in vaḻkoḷiputtūr.

{$} noṭi ōr āyiram uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has thousand languages.
nuṇṇiyar ām avar nōkkum vaṭivum āyiram uṭaiyār
he who is very minute has thousands of forms on which his benign looks fall
vaṇṇamum āyiram uṭaiyār
he has thousand colours
muṭiyum āyiram uṭaiyār
He has thousand heads.
moy kuḻalāḷaiyum uṭaiyār
He has a lady who has thick tresses of hir.
vaṭivum āyiram uṭaiyār
He has thousand forms.

{$} pañci nuṇ tukil aṉṉa paiṅkaḻaṟ cēvaṭi uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has lotus red feet wearing anklets made of unalloyed gold, which are as soft as like the fine cloth woven with cotton.
[[PP_X: pañci - pañcu;
vil eṟipañcipōla
(Naṟṟiṇi, 299-7);
[[PP_X: arakku ārnta, pañci koṇṭu ūṭṭiṉum paiyeṉap paiyeṉaveṉṟu, añcip piṉvāṅkum aṭi
(Nālaṭiyār, 396)]]
[[PP_X: pañcitaṉ collā
(Naṉṉūl, 24)]]
kuñci mēkalai uṭaiyār
he has a small mekali (mekali - an ornament worn round the waist)
kontu aṇi vēl valaṉ uṭaiyār
he holds on his right side a terrifying and beautiful trident
(kontu = kottu it may mean also bunch of flowers; a trident on which is worn a bunch of flowers)
añcum veṉṟavarkku aṇiyār
He is within easy reach of those who have conquered the five senses.
āṉaiyiṉ īr uri uṭaiyār
has a skin of an elephant wet with blood.
vañcinuṇ iṭai uṭaiyār
has a lady whose small waist is like the creeper, vañci.
vāḻkoḷi puttūr uḷār
such a god resides in vāḻkoḷiputtūr
{2:94}__5+
{$} paravu vāraiyum uṭaiyār
God has devotees who prise him,
paḻittu ikaḻ vāraiyum uṭaiyār
has also people who despises and ridicule him
viravu vāraiyum uṭaiyār
has true devotees
veṇ talaip pali koḷvatu uṭaiyār
receives alms in the white skull
aravam pūṇpatum uṭaiyār
has cobras as his ornaments.
āyiram pēr mika uṭaiyār
has thousand names which are very popular
1. varamum āyiram uṭaiyār
has innumerable boons that he grants to his devotees
[[(āyiram is used in the sense of countless);
This is known as `anantavāci' by the commentator of tākkayākapparaṇi;
catameṉpatu anantavāci- that which denotes many]]
vāḻ koḷi puttūr uḷār
such a god is in vāḻkoḷiputtūr
[[Variant reading: 1. varavum]]
{2:94}__6+
{$} taṇṭum tāḷamum kuḻalum taṇṇumaik karuviyum puṟavil koṇṭa pūtamum uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has the muscial instruments of a lute, cymbals, flute and taṇṇumi and an army of pūtam got in the forest
[[taṇṭuvīṇai;
carikama pata nip pāṭal taṇṭu taivaru ceṅkaiyōṉ- (villipāratam, intirappirattaccarukkam, 42)]]
kōlamum pala pala uṭaiyār
has very many beautiful forms
kaṇṭu kōṭalum ariyār
it is very difficult to comprehend his true nature.
kāṭciyum ariyatu
It is difficult to have a vision of cicaṉ
karantai vaṇṭu vāḻ pati uṭaiyār
he has a place in which bees live in the flower, fragrant basil. (karantai - tirunīṟṟuppaccai or vipūtip paccai)
vāḻ koḷiputtūruḷār
such a god is in vāḻkoḷiputtūr.
{2:94}__7+
{$} māṉa vāḻkkaiyatu uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has a dignified life
malaintavar matiṟ paricu aṟuttār
He destroyed the nature of the forts of his enemies.
tāṉa vāḻkkaiyatu uṭaiyār
He leads a life of giving gifts in charity.
nām pukaḻntu ēttattavat toṭu ñāṉa vāḻkkaiyatu uṭaiyār
He leads a life of penance and divine knowledge; to be prised and worshipped by us, his devotees.
naḷḷiruḷ makaḷir niṉṟu ētta vāṉa vāḻkkaiyatu uṭaiyār
He lives in heaven to be prised by the divine damsels, at midnight.
vāḻ koḷi puttūr uḷār
such a god is in vālkoḷiputtūr
{2:94}__8+
{$} ēḻum mūṉṟum ōr talaikaḷ uṭaiyavaṉ iṭar paṭa aṭarttu
pressing down (Irāvaṇaṉ) who had ten heads to undergo suffering
vēḻvi virumpic ceṟṟatum uṭaiyār
Civaṉ destroyed the sacrifice (of takkaṉ) intentionally
avar pala pala viruppu uṭaiyār
he has very many desires.
veṇ piṟai aṉṉa kēḻal 1. keḻumaṇi miṭaṟu niṉṟu ilaṅka
as the hog`s tusk which is like the white crescent, to shine in the neck which resembles the sapphire.
vāḻi cāntamum uṭaiyār
he has sandal-paste besmeared on his person. (cāntam may also mean composure)
vāḻkoḷi puttūr uḷār
such a god is in vāḻkoḷiputtūr (vēḷvi has been changed into vēḻvi to suit etuki (rhyme); vāḻi : expletive)
[[Variant reading: 1. kēḻ]]
{2:94}__9+
{$} veṉṟi mā mala rōṉum virikaṭal tuyiṉṟavaṉ tāṉum eṉṟum ettukai uṭaiyār
Civaṉ is always prised by the victorious one who is seated in the big (lotus) flower and one who slept in the expansive ocean (of milk)
imaiyavar tuti ceya virumpi
Desiring the prises of the celestials who do not wink.
muṉṟil māmalar vācam, mutumati tavaḻ poḻil tillai maṉṟil āṭal (atu) uṭaiyār
he dances in the hall in tilli which has gardens where the full moon crawls and the fragrance of the flowers spread in the frontyard.
vāḻ koḷiputtūr uḷār
such a god is in vāḻkoḷiputtūr.

{$} maṇṭai koṇṭu uḻal tērar, mācuṭaimēṉi vaṉ camaṇar kuṇṭar pēciya pēccuk koḷḷaṉ miṉ
(People of this world!) Do not mind the speeches of the strong and low camaṇar (jins) whose bodies are dirty, and tērar who wander holding a begging bowl (maṇṭai an earthernware resembling in form the skull)
tikaḻ oḷi nalla tuṇṭa veṇpiṟai cūṭi
wearing on the head a good and white crescent of shining light.
cuṇṇaveṇ poṭi aṇintu
Having besmeared white holy ash as scented powder.
eṅkum vaṇṭu vāḻ poḻil cūḻnta vāḻ koḷi puttūr uḷār
such a god is in vāḻkoḷiputtūr which is surrounded everywhere by gardens in which bees live.

{$} valam koḷ veṇ maḻuvāḷaṉ vāḻ koḷi puttūr uḷāṉai ilaṅku veṇ piṟaiyāṉai
on the god in vāḻkoḷiputtūr who has a victorious white battle-axe, and shining white crescent.
nalam koḷ pūm poḻiṟ kāḻi naṟṟamiḻ ñāṉacampantaṉ ēttiya kamiḻ ivai vallār
those who are capable of receiting the tamiḻ verses composed to prise the god by ñāṉacampantaṉ who was well-versed in chaste tamiḻ and is a native of Kāḻi surrounded by flower-gardens which yield good products.
nalam koḷ cintaiyar āki naṉṉeṟi eytuvar
will attin spiritual wisdom which is the fruit of following the right path, their minds being filled with good intentions.

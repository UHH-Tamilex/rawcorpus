{1:64}__1+
{$} (1) muṟai ār muṭi cēr teṉṉar cērar cōḻarkaḷ tām vaṇaṅkum tiṟaiār oḷicēr cemmai ōṅkum teṉtiruppūvaṇam(ē)
Tiruppūvaṇam in the South which is eminent by the impartiality, has the renown of tributes paid by vassals and which is worshipped by Teṉṉār, Cērar and Cōḻar who wear crowns by the right of heredity.
aṟaiār puṉalum āmalarum āṭuaravum caṭaimēl kuṟaiār matiyum cūṭi mātu ōr kūṟu uṭaiyāṉ iṭam ām
is the shrine of Civaṉ who has a lady as one half and wears a crescent, water that produces sound by dashing waves, the flowers of āccā tree, a dancing cobra, all on the caṭai
[[ā-āccā;]]
[[PP_EXT: āvum āram ōṅkiṉa eṅkaṇum
(Cilappatikāram, 12, 2-2)]]
[[PP: ātaṅku paiṅkuḻalāḷ pākam koṇṭār (Nāvukkaracar, tiruppantaṇainallūr, 1).]]
[[Variant reading: (1) muṟaiyāḷ]]
{1:64}__2+
{$} maruvār matilmūṉṟu oṉṟa eytu
shooting an arrow simultaneously on the three forts of the enemies to destroy them.
māmalaiyāṉmaṭantai orupāl pākam ākac ceyta umparpirāṉ (avaṉ) ūr
the shrine of the master of the celestials, who has on one side as a half the daughter of the great mountain.
karuār cāli ālai malkik kaḻalmaṉṉar kāttu aḷitta tiruvāl malinta cēṭar vāḻum teṉtiruppūvaṇam(ē)
is tiruppūvaṇam in the south where great men are crowded possessing wealth that were donated by the kings wearing kaḻal and also protected from enemies and where the superior variety of paddy with its embryo, and sugar-cane are flourishing.
[[kāttu aḷitta-aḷittuk kātta]]
{1:64}__3+
{$} pōrār matamā urivai pōrttu
covering himself with the skin of an elephant which can fight.
poṭi aṇimēṉiyaṉāy
having a body adorned with sacred ash.
kārār kaṭaliṉ nañcam uṇṭa kaṇṇutal viṇṇavaṉ ūr
the shrine of the tēvaṉ who has a frontal eye and who consumed the poison that rose in the ocean from which clouds draw water.
pār ār vaikaip (1) puṉalvāy parappi
the river vaikai which is used by the people, spreading its water in the channels.
palmaṇipoṉ koḻittu
to waft on the banks many gems and gold.
cīrār vāri cēra niṉṟa teṉtirup pūvaṇam(ē)
is pūvaṇam in the south where the reputed water accumulates.
[[Variant reading: (1) puṉal parappip]]
{1:64}__4+
{$} kaṭiār alaṅkal koṉṟaicūṭi
wearing a garland of fragrant koṉṟai flowers.
kātil ōr vārkuḻaiyaṉ
wearing in the ear a long men's ear-ring.
koṭiār veḷḷai ēṟu ukanta kōvaṇavaṉ iṭam ām
the place of Civaṉ who wears a loin-cloth and feels elated by holding a flag on which a white bull is drawn.
paṭiyār kūṭi nīṭi ōṅkum palpukaḻāl parava
the people of this world gather and praise Civaṉ by his many famous deeds which have been for a long time on increase.
ceṭiār vaikai cūḻa niṉṟa teṉtiruppūvaṇam(ē)
is tiruppūvaṇam in the south surrounded by the river, vaikai which has many shrubs.
{1:64}__5+
{$} kūr ār vāḷi cilaiyil kōttu
fixing a sharp arrow in the bow.
koṭimatil kūṭṭu aḻitta (1) pōr ār villi
Civaṉ the archer who with a bow destroyed simultaneously the forts which had flags.
melliyalāḷ ōrpāl makiḻntāṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who felt happy in having on one side a lady of tender nature.
ārā aṉpiṉ teṉṉar cērar cōḻarkaḷ pōṟṟu icaippa
teṉṉar (pāṇtiyar) cērar and coḻar to sing the praises, with insatiate love.
tēr ār vīti māṭam nīṭum teṉtiruppūvaṇam (ē) ām
is tiruppūvaṇam in the south where the storeys are high in the streets where cars stay.
[[Variant readings : (1) pērāṇvilli, pārarvilli]]
{1:64}__6+
{$} naṉṟu tītu oṉṟu ilāta nāṉmaṟaiyōṉ kaḻalē ceṉṟu pēṇi ētta niṉṟa tēvarpirāṉ iṭam
the shrine of the master of the tēvar whose feet the devotees praise with love going to that place, and who revealed the four vētams and who has neither good nor bad things attached to him.
kuṉṟil oṉṟi
being united with the hill.
ōṅkamalku kuḷir poḻil cūḻ malarmēl teṉṟal oṉṟi
the southern balmy breeze being united with flowers in the cool gardens which flourish to grow high.
muṉṟil ārum teṉtiruppūvaṇam ām
is tiruppūvaṇam in the south where it fills the front-yard of the houses.
{1:64}__7+
{$} paivāy aravam araiyil cātti
having tied in the waist a cobra which has a hood.
pāriṭam pōṟṟu icaippa
the pūtams to sing his praises.
meyvāy mēṉinīṟu pūci
having smeared the sacred ash on the body, which is colourful
[[mēṉiniṟam ākupeyar;]]
[[PP: nīlamēṉi neṭiyōṉ kōyilum (cilappatikaram 5-172);
mēṉi eṉṟār, ākupeyarāṉ niṟattai (Do. aṭiyarkkunallār)]]
ēṟu ukantāṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who felt joy in riding on a bull.
kaivāḻ (1) vaṉaiyār maintarōṭum kalaviyiṉāl (2) neruṅkic ceyvār toḻiliṉ pāṭal ōvāt teṉ tiruppūvaṇam(ē) ām
is Pūvaṇam in the South where the songs are sung by the young women who wearing bangles on their hands, are close together with youths during coition.
[[Variant readings : (1) viṉaiyār (2) neruṅkac]]
{1:64}__8+
{$} māṭavīti maṉilaṅkai maṉṉaṉai māṇpu aḻittu
destroying the honour of the king of ilaṅkai where streets with storeys are permanent.
kūṭaveṉṟivāḷ koṭuttu āḷ koḷkaiyiṉārkku (1) iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who has the idea of admitting him as a protege giving a victorious sword in addition to his grace.
pāṭalōṭum āṭal ōṅkip palmaṇi poṉkoḻittu (2) ōṭi nīrāl vaikai cūḻum uyartiruppūvaṇam(ē) ām
is the exalted tiruppūvaṇam where the river, vaikai flows and surrounds by its water wafting on the banks many gems and gold, and which is eminent by music and dance.
[[Variant readings : (1) iṭamām (2) ōṭa]]
{1:64}__9+
{$} poyyā vēta nāviṉāṉum pūmakaḷ kātalaṉum
Piramaṉ who chants the vētams which never prove false, and Māl the husband of the goddess in the lotus flower.
kaiyāl toḻutu kaḻalkaḷ pōṟṟak kaṉalēri āṉavaṉ ūr
the place of Civaṉ who assumed the form of burning fire, worshipping him with joined hands and to praise feet.
mai ār poḻiliṉ vaṇṭu pāṭa
when the bees hum like music in the gardens where clouds rest.
vaikai maṇikoḻittu
as the river, vaikai wafts on the banks gems.
ceyār kamalam tēṉ arumpum teṉ tiruppūvaṇam (ē)
is tiruppuvaṇam in the south where the lotus growing in the fields gives out honey in drops.
{1:64}__10+
{$} alai ār puṉalai nīttavarum tērarum aṉpu ceyyā nilaiyāvaṇṇam māyam vaitta niṉmalaṉtaṉ iṭam
the place of the spotless god who kept ignorance of spiritual wisdom so that the doctrines of the amaṇar who do not bathe in water with waves and of tērar (buddhists) both of whom do not cherish him with love, are not permanent;
[[PP_EXT_(puṉalai nīttavar):
uṇṇāmaiyiṉ uyaṅkiya maruṅkiṉ
āṭāp paṭivattu āṉṟōr pōla,
viraiceṟi ciṟuneṟi niraipu uṭaṉ cellum,
kāṉa yāṉai
(Akam, 123, 1-4)]]
malaipōl tuṉṉi veṉṟi ōṅkum māḷikai cūḻntu
surrounded by mansions eminent by their victory and being close together like mountains.
cilaiār puricai paricu paṇṇum teṉtiruppūvaṇam ām
is Tiruppūvaṇam in the South where the walls of fortification built of stones add beauty to it.
{1:64}__11+
{$} tiṇār puricai māṭam ōṅkum teṉtiruppūvaṇattup peṇ ār mēṉi em iṟaiyai
on our master who has on his body a lady, in Pūvaṇam in the South where the storeys with strong walls of fortification rise high.
pēr iyal iṉtamiḻāl
by sweet tamiḻ verses which move about with fame.
naṇṇār uṭka
the enemies to be struck with awe
kāḻimalku ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉapaṇār pāṭal pattum vallār payilvatu vāṉiṭai (ē)
those who can recite all the ten verses full of music composed by Ñāṉacampantaṉ whose fame is increasing in Kāḻi, will always be in heaven.

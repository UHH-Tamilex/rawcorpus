This shrine is now known by the name of Paṉiyapuram

{2:53}__1+
{$} viṇ amarntaṉa mummatilkaḷai vīḻa veṅkaṇaiyāl eytāy
you discharged a cruel arrow to make the three forts which remined in the sky to fall down.
viripaṇ amarntu olicēr puṟavalār paṉaṅkāṭṭūrp peṇ amarntu orupākam ākiya piññakā
destroyer of all things who has a lady on one half desiring it, in Puṟavār Paṉankāṭṭūr where the sweet sound of spreading melody types gathers together.
piṟaicēr nutaliṭaikkaṇ amarntavaṉē
Civaṉ who has an eye in the forehead where the crescent stays!
kalantārkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to those who come near you.
{2:53}__2+
{$} nīṭal kōṭal alara
the long white species of malabar glory lily to blossom.
mullai nīr malar niraittātu aḷam ceya
the pollen of the rows of flowers of arabian jasmine resembling water heap like salt in salt pans.
pāṭal vaṇṭu aṟaiyum puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr
in Puṟavi Paṉankāṭṭūr where humming bees make a loud noise.
tōṭu ilaṅkiya kātu ayalmiṉ (1) tuḷaṅka
the light to shine brightly by the side of the ear where the women's ear-ring is shining.
veṇkuḻai tuḷḷa
the white men's ear-ring to jump up.
naḷḷiruḷ āṭum caṅkaraṉē
Civaṉ who bestow auspicious things on devotees and who dances at mid-night;
[[naḷḷiruḷil naṭṭam payiṉṟu āṭum nātaṉē (tiruvācakam, Civapurāṇam, 89)]]
aṭaintārkku aruḷāyē
[[see 1st verse]]
[[Variant reading: (1) tulaṅka]]
{2:53}__3+
{$} vāḷaiyum kayalum miḷir poykaivārpuṉaṟkarai aruku elām vayaṟ pāḷai oṇ kamukamum puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr
in Puṟavār Paṉankaṭṭūr, the colourful areca-palm which has spathes, growing by the side of the long bank of the natural tank full of water in which the scabbard and carp roll.
pūḷaiyum naṟuṅkoṉṟaiyum matamattamum puṉaivāy
Civaṉ who adorns himself with flowers of javanese wool plant, fragrant koṉṟi and datura flowers!
kaḻal iṇaittāḷaiyē paravum tavattārkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to those who possess the meritorious deed of prising your two feet which wear Kaḻal.
{2:53}__4+
{$} mēti
the buffaloes.
vaikaṟai mēyntu
grazing grass at daybreak.
cennel meṉkatir kavvi
and seizing by the mouth the soft ears of corn of the superior variety of paddy.
(1) mēl paṭukaliṉ pāynta taṇ paḻaṉap puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr
in Puṟavār Paṉankāṭṭūr in the fields it jumps on the land on the bank of a river fit for cultivation which is beyond it.
āynta nāṉmaṟaipāṭi āṭum aṭikaṉ eṉṟu eṉṟu araṟṟi
shouting with excitement many times as the god who dances singing the four Vētams which were investigated.
naṉmalar cāynta aṭi paravum tavattārkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to those who have meritorious deeds and who prise your feet in which fresh flowers thrown by devotees have fallen.
[[naṉmalar cāynta aṭi may also mean the feet which defeated the lotus by its beauty]]
[[Variant reading: (1) mēṟpaṭutaliṉ]]
{2:53}__5+
{$} ceṅkaya(l)loṭu cēl ceruc ceya
as the cēl (a fresh water fish) fights with the red coloured carp
[[kayalloṭu the underlined letter is augmentation for rhythm]]
cīṟiyāḻmural tēṉiṉattoṭu paṅkayam malarum puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr
in Puṟavār Paṉankāṭṭūr the lotus blossoms with the swarms of bees which hum like the music of the small yāḻ.
kaṅkaiyum matiyum kamaḻcaṭaik kēṇmaiyāḷoṭum kūṭi
united with a lady who has the relationship of wife, and a fragrant caṭi in which Kanki and a crescent are staying.
māṉmaṟi aṅkai āṭalaṉē
who has on his beautiful hand a young deer!
aṭiyārkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to your devotees.
{2:53}__6+
{$} (1) nīriṉ ārvarai kōli mālkaṭal nīṭiya poḻilcūḻntu
surrounded by long gardens which are like the great sea, fixing the boundary by the water;
[[PP_EXT: kaṭalpōl tōṉṟala kāṭu iṟantōrē
(Akam, 1-19)]]
vaikalum pāriṉār piriyāp puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr
in Puṟavār Paṉankāṭṭūr which the people of this world do not leave every day.
kāriṉ ārmalarkkoṉṟai tāṅku kaṭavuḷ eṉṟu kaikūppi
joining their hands in worship saying, the god who bears koṉṟi flowers which blossom profusely in winter.
nāḷtoṟum cīriṉāl vaṇaṅkum tiṟattārkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to those who pay homage to you dily in an excellent manner.
[[Variant reading: (1) nīriṉārkaraikōli]]
{2:53}__7+
{$} (1) kai arivaiyar melviral(l) avai kāṭṭi ammalark kāntaḷ amkuṟi pai arā viriyum puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr
in Puṟavār Paṉankāṭṭūr where the beautiful marks the red specie of malabar glory lily, exhibiting like the soft fingers of ladies, unfolds its petals like the beautiful hood of the cobra.
mey arivai ōr pākam ākavum mēviṉāy
you desired to have in your body as a half a lady.
kaḻal ētti nāḷtoṟum poy ilā aṭimaipurintārkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to those who did faithfully service dily prising your feet.
[[Variant reading: (1) kaiyarivvaiyar melvirallavai kāṭṭiyum]]
{2:53}__8+
{$} tūvi amciṟai melnaṭai a(ṉ)ṉam malki olkiya tūmalarp poykaip pālil
in the expanse of the natural tanks of fresh flowers where the swans of soft git and downs and internal feathers increase and become bent.
vaṇṭu aṟaiyum puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr mēvi
dwelling in Puṟavār Paṉaṅkāṭṭūr where the bees hum loudly.
annilai āy arakkaṉ tōḷ aṭarttu
pressing down the shoulders of the arakkaṉ who was in that position of being cught under the mountin.
avaṉ pāṭalkēṭṭu aruḷ ēviya perumāṉ eṉpavarkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to those who prise you as the god who ordered his grace to reach him on hearing him sing Cāmavētam.
{2:53}__9+
{$} amtaṇ mātavi, puṉṉai, nalla acōkamum, aravintam, mallikai paintaṇ nāḻalkaḷ cūḻ puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr
in Puṟavār Paṉankāṭṭūr surrounded by the beautiful and cool delight of the woods, mast wood trees, flourishing asoka trees, lotus, jasmine, and verdant, cool fetid cassia.
entu iḷam mukil vaṇṇaṉ nāṉmukaṉ eṉṟivarkku aritāy nimirntatu or cantam āyavaṉē
beautiful, Civaṉ who grew tall as a column of fire so as to be difficult for Māl who has the colour of the forming cloud rising high and Piramaṉ of four faces.
[[entu-ēntu: the initial letter shortened for the sake of rhyme?]]
tavattārkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to those who did penance to have a vision of your form.
{2:53}__10+
{$} nīṇam ār muruku uṇṭu vaṇṭiṉam nīlamāmalarkavvi
the swarm of bees biting the big blue nelumbo flowers drinking the honey found in abundance
[[nīṇam=nīḷam; cf. nīṇutal ceytu (campantar, tiruppiramapuram (1)-9)]]
nēricai (1) pāṇil yāḻ muralum puṟavār paṉaṅkāṭṭūr
in Paṉankāṭṭūr humming like the melody nērici among the melodies, like the music of the yāḻ
[[nēricai:
nēricaiyāka aṟupatam muraṉṟu (campantar, venkuri (1) 3)]]
nāṉ aḻintu uḻalvār camaṇarum (2) naṇpuil cākkiyarum naka
the camanar who wander naked without shame and cākkiyar who have no friendly feeling, to lugh derisively at you.
talai ūṇ uriyavaṉē
Civaṉ who has the right of eating in the skull!
ukappārkku aruḷāyē
grant your grace to those who desire you.
[[Variant reading: (1) pāṇiyāḻ (2) nampil]]
{2:53}__11+
{$} maiyiṉ ār maṇipōl miṭaṟṟaṉai
on Civaṉ who has a beautiful neck like the sable cloud.
mācu il veṇpoṭip pūcum mārpaṉai
who smears on his chest the white sacred ash which has no blemish.
paiya tēṉ poḻil cūḻ puṟavārpaṉaṅkāṭṭūr aiyaṉai
and the master who is in Puṟavār Paṉankaṭṭūr surrounded by gardens having fresh honey.
pukaḻ āṉa kāḻiyuḷ āynta nāṉmaṟai ñāṉacampantaṉ ceyyuḷ (1) pāṭavallār
those who are able to sing the poems of Ñāṉacampantaṉ who had knowledge of the four vētams which he investigated and who is a native of famous Kāḻi.
civalōkam cērvārē
will reach Civalōkam after death.

tōṟṟiṉāṉ eyiṟu kavvit toḻil uṭai arakkaṉ taṉṉait tēṟṟuvāṉ
to convince the arakkaṉ who committed evil acts and appeared biting the lips with the teeth (in anger)
cikkeṉat tavirum eṉṟu (1) ceṉṟu collavum
though he was told to give up the idea quickly going to him without heeding that advice
[[Nanti and Irāvaṇaṉ's ministers advised him to desist from lifting the mountin]]
vīṟṟiṉai uṭaiyaṉ āki
thinking only of his own greatness.
veṭuveṭuttu (2) eḻuntavaṉ taṉ āṟṟalai aḻikka vallār avaḷivaṇallūrārē
Civaṉ in Avaḷivaṇallūr has the strength of destroying the strength of that arakkaṉ who rose lughing boisterously

[[VAR_PIFI: (1) ceṟṟu (ceṟṟu)]]
[[VAR_PIFI: (2) eṭuttavaṉ (eṭuttavaṉ)]]

arakkar ellām mikac caḻakku āyiṟṟu, vempiṉār eṉṟu cempiṉāl eṭutta kōyil cikkeṉac citaiyum eṉṉa nampiṉār eṉṟu colli naṉmaiyāl mikku nōkki
looking at those who were suffering from Irāvaṇaṉ's cruel acts, with great compassion to grant them happiness and thinking that they firmly believed that the palace which resembled copper in colour will quickly meet with ruin and all the arakkar were distressed in mind that the acts of Irāvaṇaṉ were becoming directly opposite to the right path.
ampiṉāl aḻiya eytār avaḷivaṇallūrār (ē)
Civaṉ in Avaḷivaṇallūr cused it to be destroyed by Irāmaṉ shooting arrow in it.
[[cempiṉāl eṭutta : āl is expletive;
[[PP: cempuṟaḻ puricaic cemmal mūtūr: see puṟam, 37-11 and its parallel quotations.]]

kīrttimai uḷḷatu ākil kīḻpaṭak karutalāmō
can I think low as there is the fame of my enormous strength?
tōṭ peruvaliyiṉāl yāṉ malaiyait tolaippaṉ eṉṟu
thinking I shall remove the mountin (which is an obstacle) by the great strength of my shoulders.
vēṭpaṭa vaittavāṟē
as soon as he fixed his toe to share the fate of Kāmaṉ who was destroyed.
vitirvitirttu arakkaṉ vīḻntu āṭpaṭak karutip pukkār avaḷivaṇallūrār (ē)
Civaṉ in Avaḷivaṇallūr took it into his mind to admit Irāvanaṉ into his grace when he fell down trembling.
{4:59}__4+
{$}
nilaivalam vallaṉ allaṉ
Irāvaṇaṉ was not capable of victory which was permanent.
nērmaiyai niṉaiya māṭṭāṉ
would never think of right ways.
cilaivalam koṇṭa celvaṉ cīriya kayilai taṉṉait talaivalam karutippukkut tākkiṉāṉ taṉṉai aṉṟu alaikulai (2) ākkuvittār avaḷivaṇallūrār (ē)
Civaṉ in Avaḷivaṇallūr destroyed long ago Irāvaṇaṉ who had the victory gined by his archery and who attacked the eminent Kayili thinking of the strength of his heads.
[[Variant reading: (1) tāṅkiṉāṉ (2) ākki vaittār]]

tavvali oṉṟaṉ ākit taṉatu oru perumaiyālē (1) meyvaliyuṭaiyaṉ āki
having the strength of his body by his unequalled greatness and but really having only the decreasing strength as his help.
mikap perun tērai ūrntu
flying on a very big chariot.
cevvali kūr viḻiyāl (2) cirampattāl eṭukkuṟṟāṉai avvali tīrkka vallār avaḷivaṇallūrār (ē)
Civaṉ in Avaḷivaṇallur is capable of destroying that strength of the arakkaṉ who lifted by all his ten heads, looking at it with his red and very cruel eyes;
[[Variant reading: (1) melvali (2) ciramattāl]]
{4:59}__6+
{$}
naṉmaitāṉ aṟiyamāṭṭāṉ
without knowing what is good for him.
naṭu ilā arakkar kōmāṉ
the king of the arakkar who had no uprightness.
vaṉmaiyē karutic ceṉṟu
approaching the mountin thinking only of his strength.
valitaṉaic celuttal uṟṟu
pushing forward that strength.
kaṉmaiyāṉ malaiyai ōṭik karutittāṉ eṭuttu
when he lifted the mountin running towards it thinking that it was simply a stone.
vāyāl ammaiyō eṉṉa vaittār avaḷivaṇallūrār (ē)
Civaṉ in Avaḷivaṇallūr made him cry aloud out of pin - `amma'
[[ammaiyō an exclamation of astonishment or pity.]]
{4:59}__7+
{$}
katampaṭap pōtuvārkaḷ pōtum akkaruttiṉāl
with that intention when people who were apt to be angry went along with him.
citampaṭa niṉṟa nīrkaḷ cikkeṉat tavirum eṉṟu
admonishing them you who are here to parade your wisdom! leave me quickly.
matampaṭu maṉattaṉ āki
with an arrogant mind.
vaṉmaiyāṉ mikku (1) nōkka
when the strong Irāvaṇaṉ looked at the mountin very often (to uproot it )
(2) atam paḻattu uruvu ceytār avaḷivaṇallūrār (ē)
Civaṉ in Avaḷivaṇallūr made him by crushing his body to be a mass of flesh like an over-ripe fruit of the fig-tree.
[[Variant reading: (1) nōkki (2) atampaḻitturuvu]]
{4:59}__8+
{$}
nāṭu mikku uḻitarkiṉṟa naṭu ilā arakkaṉ taṉṉai ōṭu mikku eṉṟu colli
telling the arakkaṉ who had no uprightness and who was wandering in the countries often, `run quickly'.
ūṉṟiṉāṉ ukiriṉālē
pressed him with the nil of toe.
pāṭi mikku uyvaṉ eṉṟu paṇiya
when he pid homage, being unable to bear it, by saying I shall save myself by singing his prises much
nal tiṟaṅkaḷ kāṭṭi
showing his good graces such as long life, a sword.
āṭum aravam mikkup pūṇṭār avaḷivaṇallūrār (ē)
Civaṉ in avaḷivaṇallūr adorned himself profusely with cobras which dance spreading their hoods.
{4:59}__9+
{$}
ēṉamāy (1) iṭanta mālum eḻiltaru muḷariyāṉum
Māl who digged the earth taking the form of a pig and Piramaṉ who is in the beautiful lotus (transforming into a swan)
ñāṉam tāṉ uṭaiyarāki
possessing intelligence to know Civaṉ.
naṉmaiyai aṟiyamāṭṭār
could not know the good way to see him.
cēṉam (tāṉ) nilā arakkaṉ ceḻuvarai eṭukka ūṉṟi
fixing his toe firmly when the arakkan who was wandering always like the kite, lifted the fertile mountin.
āṉanta aruḷkaḷ ceytār avaḷivaṇallūrār (ē)
Civaṉ in Avaḷivaṇallūr granted his grace in many ways which bring happiness.

when the arakkan ran and shook the mountin

taṉṉai añca nōkkiṉār nōṉpu iṟa ūṉṟu colli
removing the toe which was fixed to destroy his penance and the pin he sustined by being crushed, looked at him with compassion when he was seized with fear,

{3:33}__1+
{$} nīriṭaittuyiṉṟavaṉ, tampi, nīḷcāmpuvāṉ, pōr uṭaiccukkirīvaṉ, aṉumāṉ, toḻa
to be worshipped by rāmaṉ who is an incarnation of Māl who slept in the ocean of milk, his brother, ilakkuvaṉ, cāmpuvaṉ who has a long span of life, cukkirivaṉ always engaged in battle, an danumāṉ.
kār uṭai nañcu uṇṭu kāttu aruḷ ceyta em cīruṭaiccēṭarvāḻ tiruvucāttāṉam (ē)
tiruvucāttāṉam is the place where the great god of fame, who bestowed his grace by saving the celestials by consuming the poison of black colour resides. (toḻac cēṭarvāḻ tiruvucāttāṉam is the syntactical link).
{3:33}__2+
{$} kollaiēṟu uṭaiyavaṉ
Civaṉ has a bull that lives in the forest region.
kōvaṇaāṭaiyaṉ
dresses himself in a men's loin-cloth.
pal (ai) ār paṭutalaippalikoḷum paramaṉār
the supreme being who receives alms on a dead skull which has its teeth intact.
mullai ār puṟavu aṇi mutupati naṟaikamaḻ tillaiyāṉ uṟaiviṭam tiruvucāttāṉam (ē)
tiruvucāttāṉam is the abode of the god in tilli where fragrance spreads, and the ancient place which is made beautiful by the forest region where the creepers, arabian jasmine are flourishing.
{3:33}__3+
{$} (1) tām alār pōlavē takkaṉārvēḷviyai ūmaṉār tamkaṉā ākkiṉāṉ
Civaṉ made the sacrifice performed by takkaṉ as the dream dreamt by a dumb person which he cannot narrate and must keep within himself, like an enemy
[[ūmaṉ kaṇṭakaṉā: tuñcu ūmaṉkaṇṭakaṉā (tirikaṭukam, 7); ūmaṉārkaṇṭakaṉavilum paḻutāy oḻintaṉa kaḻinta annāḷkaḷ (periyatirumoḻi, 1-1-3); takkaṉār; ūmaṉār here the ār is added to denigrate them]]
oru noṭikkāmaṉār uṭal keṭak kāynta em kaṇṇutal
Civaṉ who has a frontal eye with which he destroyed the body of Kāmaṉ in an instant which is the time-measure taken for snapping the thumb and the middle finger. (kāmaṉār here also the ār is used to denigrate him.)
cēmamā uṟaiviṭam tiruvucāttāṉam (ē)
tiruvucāttāṉam is the abode of Civaṉ where he dwells for the welfare of the world.
[[Variant reading: (1) tāmaṉār pōlavē]]
{3:33}__4+
{$} maṟitarukarattiṉāṉ
Civaṉ holds a young one of the deer in his hand.
mālviṭai ēṟiyāṉ
rides on a big bull.
kuṟitaru kōlam naṟkuṇattiṉār aṭitoḻa
the devotees who are distinguished by their form and good qualities to worship his feet.
neṟitaruvētiyar nittalum cey ceṟitarupoḻil aṇi tiruvucāttāṉam (ē)
tiruvucāttāṉam is the abode of Civaṉ which is beautiful by dense gardens where the brahmins who follow the right path, perform their dily routine of duties.

VMS7 / {3:33}__PIFI5+
{$} paṇṭu iraittu ayaṉum mālum palapattarkaḷ toṇṭu iraittum malar tūvittōttiram cola
from time immemorial Ayaṉ, Māl and many pious people sing prises loudly with joy, scattering flowers.
koṇṭa iraikkoṭiyoṭum kurukiṉil nalliṉam teṇtiraik (1) kaḻaṉicūḻ tiruvucāttāṉam (ē)
tiruvucāttāṉam is the abode of Civaṉ where the good flock of birds go round and round and fields which have clear waves, along with the crows which have cught their prey (koṇṭa+irai have become koṇṭirai)
[[Variant reading: (1) kaḻikaḷ cūḻ]]
VMS8 / {3:33}__PIFI6+
{$} maṭavaralpaṅkiṉaṉ malaitaṉai matiyātu
without any regard for the mountin belonging to Civaṉ who has a young lady as his half.
caṭacaṭa eṭuttavaṉ talai pattum neritara aṭartara ūṉṟi aṅkē avaṟku aruḷ ceytāṉ
Civaṉ bestowed his grace at that place itself, to Irāvaṇaṉ fixing firmly his toe to press and crush his ten heads who lifted it to make a sound like catacaṭa' (caṭacaṭa: double imitative word, onamatopoeia).
tiṭameṉa uṟaiviṭam tiruvucāttāṉam (ē)
is tiruvucāttāṉam is his abode where he dwells with certinly.
VMS9 / {3:33}__PIFI7+
{$} āṇ, alār, peṇ alār, ayaṉoṭu mālukkum kāṇoṇā vaṇṇattāṉ, karutuvār maṉattu uḷāṉ
Civaṉ is neither male nor female; (he has both the form in one body); has a nature not being seen by Ayaṉ and Māl (but at the same time) dwells in the hearts of those who meditate upon him.
pēṇuvār piṇiyoṭum piṟappu aṟuppāṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who completely destroys the birth and diseases of those who cherish him with love.
cēṇ uyar māḷikait tiruvucāttāṉam (ē)
is tiruvucāttāṉam which rise high into the sky.
VMS10 / {3:33}__PIFI8+
{$} kāṉam ār (1) vāḻkkaiyāṉ
Civaṉ dwells in the cremation ground.
kār amaṇtērar col ūṉamākkoṇṭu
disregarding the words of the black amaṇar and tēvar (buddhists) as faulty teachings.
uyyaeṉil
if you want to save yourselves.
vāṉam ār matil aṇi māḷikai, vaḷarpoḻil tēṉamā matiyam tōy tiruvucāttāṉam (ē) nīr uraimiṉ
speak about tiruvucāttāṉam where the mansions which are adorned by walls of fortification which seem to reach the sky and flourishing gardens seem to come into contact with the moon which contins sweet nectar.
[[Variant reading: (1) vāṭkaiyāṉ]]
VMS11 / {3:33}__PIFI9+
{$} varai tirintu iḻiyum nīr vaḷavayal pukalimaṉ
the chief of Pukali which has fertile fields which have water which has undergone changes according to the land in which it flows, descending from the mountin.
tiraitirintu eṟikaṭal tiruvucāttāṉarai
on the god in tiruvucāttāṉam which is on the shore of the sea which dashes aginst the shore by the waves which are wandering
urai terintu uṇarum campantaṉ oṇtamiḻ vallavar
those who are capable of reciting the excellent tamiḻ verses, composed by campantaṉ who realised Civaṉ knowing the proper way to realize him.
naraitirai iṉṟiyē naṉṉeṟi cērvar (ē)
will follow the path of wisdom without oldage whose signs are grey hirs and wrinkles in the skin.

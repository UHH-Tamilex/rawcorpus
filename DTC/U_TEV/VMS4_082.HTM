{4:82}__1+
{$} pār koṇṭu mūṭik kaṭal koṇṭa ñāṉṟu
on that day when the ocean covered the earth and engulfed it
niṉ pātam ellām nāl añcu puḷḷiṉam ēntiṉa eṉpar
people who are in the know of things say that a flock of twenty birds held high and supported all your feet.
naḷir matiyam kāl koṇṭa caṭai vaṇkai virittāṭum kaḻumalavarkku
to the Lord in Kaḻumalam who dances stretching his matted hirs on which the cool crescent is well established, and his munificent hands.
āḷ aṉṟi maṟṟum uṇṭō amataṇ āḻi akaliṭam
is there anything else that the people of the wide world girt by the beautiful and cool ocean, do except becoming his humble devotees?
{4:82}__2+
{$} uṭaitalai mālaiyum ukantucūṭi aruḷi
having adorned himself with a garland of broken skulls, desiring it.
viṭaitāṉ uṭaiya avvētiyaṉ kaṭai ār koṭi neṭumāṭaṅkaḷ eṅkum kalantu ilaṅka
that Vētiyaṉ who has a bull and dwells in Kaḻumalam where the tall storeys with flags hoisted on their entrances, are shining.
uṭaiyāṉ aṭaivār viṉaikaḷ (avai) eḷka nāḷ toṟum āṭuvar
the master of everything dances dily to make the sins of those who reach that place, to go away from them with fright.
{4:82}__3+
{$} tiraivāyp peruṅkaṭal muttam kuvippa
as the big ocean heaps pearls by its waves.
mukantu koṇṭu
taking up and lifting them in their hands
nuraivāy nuṉaicciyar ōṭi
the women of the fishermen caste who are foaming in their mouths, running towards it.
kaḻumalattuḷ aḻuntu viṇṇavaṉ
the Tēvaṉ who is inlid like a precious stone in Kaḻumalam
viraivāy naṟumalarcūṭiya viṇṇavaṉ
and who adorned himself with good flowers of fragrance.
taṉṉaṭikkē varaiyāp paricu ivai nāḷtoṟum namtamai āḷvaṉavē
and offering them as tribute to the feet of the Lord is that which admits us into his grace.
{4:82}__4+
{$} arumporuḷ uḷḷuvar kēṭkil
if people who desire knowing rare truths.
viḻumiyanūl uraikkil vētaṅkaḷ ōtum
the Lord will recite the Vētam which is pre-eminent
arumpatam virikkum
will expound the difficult words.
[[He will recite it in a manner called patam', if he is pleased to give an eminent work]]
ulakamuṟṟum irikkum paṟaiyoṭu pūtaṅkaḷ pāṭa
when the gobline sing with a drum whose sound frightens and makes everything in the world run in a disorderly manner.
kaḻumalavaṉ niruttam paḻampaṭi āṭum kaḻal nammai āḷvaṉa
the feet of the Lord in Kaḻumalam which dance as before, will admit us into his grace;
[[The meaning of the first two lines is not clear; the syntactical link of words is also a little bit difficult]]
{4:82}__5+
{$} maṉamē
my mind!
kaḻumalattai niṉaiyāmuṉ pantittaval viṉai tīrkka vallāṉai, pacupatiyai
the Lord who can remove the strong sins which bind us, even before we think of Kaḻumalam, and the master of all beings.
cantitta kālam aṟuttum eṉṟu eṇṇi iruntavarkku muntittoḻu
worship him before those people who think `we will completely cut off the sins at the time we visit the temple.'
cintittu eḻu
start thinking of that
kaḻal nāḷ toṟum nantamai āḷvaṉavē
those feet admit us into his grace dily.
{4:82}__6+
{$} nilaiyum perumaiyum nītiyum cāla aḻaku uṭaittāy
Being very beautiful by its permanence, greatness and nature.
alaiyum peru veḷḷattu aṉṟu mititta ittōṇipuram
in this Tōṇipuram which floated above the big water of the deluge which was wandering, in the distant past.
1. cilaiyiṟṟiripuram mūṉṟu erittār
one who burnt the three wandering cities with a bow.
nam kaḻumalar alarum kaḻal aṭi nāḷ toṟum namtammaiyāḷvaṉa
the feet of our Lord in Kaḻumalam which wears anklets and pervades everywhere will admit us into their grace, dily.
[[In the 3rd line tam may be scribal error for nam]]
[[Variant reading: 1. cilaiyiṉ tiripuram]]
{4:82}__7+
{$} cūḻ aravam teṟṟikkiṭantu
the cobras which circle, lying intertwining (in the matted hir)
veṅkoṉṟaḷamtuṉṟi
[[the meaning of this phrase is not clear.]]
veṇtiṅkaḷ cūṭum kaṟṟaic caṭaimuṭiyārkku iṭam āya kaḻumalam
Kaḻumalam which is the place of the one who has a collection of matted hirs on which he wears the white crescent.
muṟṟikkiṭantu munnīril mitantu uṭaṉ moyttu amarar cuṟṟik kiṭantu toḻappaṭukiṉṟatu
is worshipped by the immortals who crowd simultaneously, and surround it and which once upon a time floated above the ocean which besieged the world.
[[For veṅkoṉṟaḷantuṉṟi in the 3rd line there is no satisfactory meaning]]
{4:82}__8+
{$} uṭalum uyirum oruvaḻiccellum ulakattuḷḷē
in the world where the life within directs the body in one direction
aṭaiyum uṉaivantu aṭaintār amarar aṭi iṇaikkīḻ
the immortals came and took refuge in your feet, and reached you who is fit to be sought.
naṭaiyum viḻavum nāḷ toṟum malkum kaḻumalattuḷ viṭaiyaṉ taṉippatam
the peerless feet of the Lord who has a bull, in Kaḻumalam where dances and festivals are on the increase dily.
nam tamai āḷvaṉa
admit us into their grace.
{4:82}__9+
{$} paravaikkaṭal nañcam uṇṭatum illai
the god did not drink the poison which rose in the extensive ocean?
[[paravai: vast area]]
[[He neither spot it out. He stationed it in his neck]]
ippār muḻutum niravikkiṭantu toḻappaṭukiṉṟatu
it is adorned and worshipped, its fame having spread everywhere in this world.
kaḻal aṭi kāṇpataṟkē nīṇṭu iruvar ciramappaṭa vantu cārntār
The two people, Māl and Piramaṉ, went down and flew up to see the head and feet and returned exhusted.
aravakkaḻal aṭi nāḷtoṟum namtamai āḷvaṉa
those famous feet which wear as anklet a cobra admit us into their grace, dily.
{4:82}__10+
{$} karai ār kaṭalcūḻ ilaṅkaiyarkōṉ taṉ muṭicitaṟat tolaiyā malaraṭi ūṉṟalum
when god pressed with his feet resembling lotus which do not know defeat, to scatter the heads of the king of Ilanki surrounded by sea which is roaring.
uḷḷam vitirvitirttu
his mind having become tremulous.
talaiyāyk kiṭantu uyarntāṉtaṉ
when Irāvaṇaṉ was lying with his heads only to be seen clearly, the god who was shining, seated high on the mount Kailācam.
[[kiṭantu should be changed into kiṭakka]]
kaḻumalam kāṇpataṟkē 1. alaiyāpparicu ivai nāḷ toṟum nam tamai āḷvaṉa
those feet admit us into their good grace everyday, to visit Kaḻumalam of god without driving us hither and thither.

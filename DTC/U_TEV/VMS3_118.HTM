maṭal mali koṉṟai, tuṉṟu vāḷ erukkum, vaṉṉiyum, mattamum caṭai mēl
having on the matted hirs, indian laburnam of profuse petals, bright and dense yarkam flowers, leaves of indian mesquit and dhātura flowers.
oli paṭal tiraikaḷ mōtiya kaṅkait talaivaṉār tam iṭam pakaril
if we want to mention the place of the chief who has Kaṅki also, which has waves, dashes and makes a sound.
viṭal oli paranta veṇtirai muttam ippikaḷ koṇarntu
the oysters in the white waves whose sound spreads as they are wafted by the wind, bringing pearls
kaṭal oli ōtam mōta vantu alaikkum kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we may mention it is Kaḻumalam where the noisy waves of the sea dash and wander back and forth.

{$}
miṉṉiya aravum, veṟimalar palavum, virumpiya tiṅkaḷum taṅkum ceṉṉi (atu) uṭaiyāṉ
the god on whose bed are staying the crescent everyone wishes, many fragrant flowers and glittering cobra
tēvar tam perumāṉ
and the master of the tēvar
cēyiḻaiyoṭum uṟaivu iṭam ām
the place where he dwells with a lady wearing beautiful ornaments.
poṉ iyal maṇiyum, murikari maruppum, cantamum untu vaṉ tiraikaḷ kaṉṉiyar āṭak kaṭal oli maliyum kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we may say that it is the city of Kaḻumalam where as the young girls are bathing the strong waves which push up gold, flawless precious stones, curved tusks of elephants and sandal-wood, and produce a sound as big as the roar of the sea.

cīr uṟu toṇṭar ceḻumāl puṉaloṭu tūpam koṇṭu aṭi pōṟṟa
when the great devotees worship the feet of the god with pure water, fertile flowers, and articles of incense.
tār uṟu koṉṟai tam muṭi vaitta caivaṉār taṅku iṭam
the place where the civaṉār who placed on his head many garlands of indian laburnam
ūr uṟu patikaḷ ulakuṭaṉ poṅki (1) oli puṉal koḷa
when the water which is making a noise, foams and rages covering the many cities which are the centre for many places, and the world
uṭaṉ mitanta (kaḻumalam)
Kaḻumalam which at the same time floated above the water.
kār uṟu cemmai naṉmaiyāl mikka kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we can say that it is Kaḻumalam which is favoured with seasonal rins, it neither being excessive nor deficient.
[[Variant reading: (1) olikaṭal]]

maṇṇiṉār ētta vāṉ uḷār paraca antarattu amararkaḷ pōṟṟa ellām paṇṇiṉār
God who created all things, to be prised by people of this world, by the inhabitants of heaven and by those who dwell between heaven and earth.
[[antaram the place between earth and heaven;
māka vicumpu (paripāṭal, 1-50, parimēlaḻakar)]]
pala pala vēṭam uṭaiyavar payilvu iṭam
the place where he who has innumerable forms, resides always.
eṅkum eṇṇiṉāl mikkār, iyalpiṉāl niṟaintār ēntu iḻaiyavaroṭu maintar kaṇṇiṉāl iṉpam kaṇṭu
the youths are everywhere and are superior by their thoughts and are full of good nature to enjoy pleasure with their look in the company of ladies who wear superior ornaments
oḷi parakkum kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we may say that it is the city of Kaḻumalam which spreads brightness.

curutiyāṉ talaiyum nāmakaḷ mūkkum cuṭaravaṉ karamum iṟuttu
Having cut off the head of Piramaṉ who is the source of the vētam, the nose of the goddess of learning whose seat is the tongue, and the arm of the god of fire.
muṉ iyaṅkum parutiyāṉ pallum iṟuttu
and having knocked out the teeth of the sun who is moving in the sky, before our eyes.
[[veyyavaṉ aṅki viḻuṅkat tiraṭṭiya kaiyait taritta vāṟu untīpaṟa, kalaṅkiṟṟu vēḷvi yeṉṟu untīpaṟa;
nāmakaḷ nāci ciram piramaṉpaṭac, cōmaṉ mukaṉ nerittu untī paṟa; tollai viṉai viṭa untī paṟa;
cūriyaṉār toṇṭai vāyiṉiṟ paṟkaḷai, vāri nerittavāṟu, untī paṟa, mayaṅkiṟṟu vēḷvi eṉṟu untī paṟa;
nalmalariṉ mēl nāṉ mukaṉār talai, ollai arinta teṉṟu untī paṟa, ukirāl arintateṉṟu untī paṟa (tiruvācakam, tiruvuntiyār 7, 13,15,18);
kāmaṉ uṭal, uyir kālaṉ, palkāy katirōṉ, nāmakaḷ nāci, ciram piramaṉ, karam eriyaic, cōmaṉ kalai talai takkaṉaiyum eccaṉaiyum; tūymaikaḷ ceytavā tōṇōkkam āṭāmō (Do. tōṇōkkam, 11)]]
avarkku aruḷum paramaṉār payiṉṟu iṉitu irukkai
the place of the supreme being who bestowed his grace on them and who stays always happily
virutiṉ nāṉ maṟaiyum aṅkam ōr āṟum
brahmins who are well-versed in all the four vētam-s and six aṅkam-s and won victory.
vēḷviyum vēṭṭavar ñāṉam karutiṉār
who performed sacrifice and fixed their thoughts only on spiritual wisdom
ulakiṟ karuttuṭaiyār cēr kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we may say that Kaḻumalam is the city where people who have a purpose of life in this world, gather together.

puṟṟil vāḷ aravum āmaiyum pūṇṭa puṉitaṉār
the holy god who adorned himself with a cruel cobra that lives in the anthill, and the shell of a tortoise.
paṉi malark koṉṟai paṟṟi, vāl matiyam caṭaiyiṭai vaitta paṭiṟaṉār
the deceitful god who placed on his matted hir the white crescent and the cool flowers of indian laburnam
payiṉṟu iṉitu irukkai
the place where he dwells always happily
ceṟṟu vaṉ tiraikaḷ oṉṟoṭu oṉṟu ōṭic ceyirttu
the strong waves which gather in crowds running one after another and being angry.
vaṇ caṅkoṭu vaṅkam kal tuṟai varaikaḷ karaikku vantu uraikkum kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we can say that it is the city of Kaḻumalam where the ships and boulders which have ghats of stone and big conches come near the shore and rub aginst it.
{3:118}__7+
{$}
alai puṉal kaṅkai taṅkiya caṭaiyār
one who has a matted hir on which the Kaṅki of roaming water stays.
aṭal neṭu matil oru mūṉṟum kolaiyiṭaic centī ventu aṟakkaṇṭa kuḻakaṉār kōyil atu eṉpar
people say that the temple of the youth who saw to it that the three strong and high fortresses were completely burnt in the red fire which has destruction as its nature.
malaiyiṉ mikku uyarnta marakkalam carakku (maṟṟu maṟṟu) iṭai iṭai eṅkum kalai kaḷittu ēṟik kāṉalil vāḻum kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we may say that it is the city of Kaḻumalam where the stags climb joyfully upon the merchandise brought by ships which are taller than mountins, and live in the sea-shore garden.

orukka muṉ niṉaiyāt takkaṉ taṉ vēḷvi uṭaitara uḻaṟiya paṭaiyār
God disturbed the sacrifice of tākkaṉ who did not always think of god previously, to disperse in several ways by an army of pūtam.
[[orukka always;]]
[[PP: kāmam orukka oru taṉmai niṟkumō (paripāṭal 6-72)]]
arakkaṉai varaiyāl āṟṟal aṉṟu aḻitta aḻakaṉār amarntu uṟai kōyil
the temple where the beautiful God who destroyed on that day the strength of the arakkaṉ by a mountin, resides with desire.
parakkum vaṇ pukaḻār paḻiyavai pārttup (1) pala pala aṟaṅkaḷē payiṟṟi
the people who have a fame that spreads far and wide by their munificence, perform many charitable acts, being equations lest any blame should attach to them.
karakkum māṟu aṟiyā vaṇmaiyāl vāḻum kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we may say that it is the city of Kaḻumalam where those people lead a life with munificence and do not know to conceal what they can give
[[Variant reading: (1) pala paṭa]]

aruvarai poṟutta āṟṟaliṉāṉum aṇikiḷar tāmaraiyāṉum iruvarum ētta
to be prised by both Māl who lifted and held a mountin contining precious things, and Piramaṉ who is seated in a beautiful lotus flower.
eri uru āṉa iṟaivaṉār uṟaivu iṭam viṉavil
if one asks, what is the residence of the god who assumed the form of fire?
oruvar ivvulakil vāḻkilā vaṇṇam oli puṉal veḷḷam muṉ parappa
when in the distant past the noisy flood during deluge spread so that no one could be able to live in this world.
karuvarai cūḻnta kaṭaliṭai mitakkum kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we may say that it is the city of Kaḻumalam which floated above the sea which is surrounded by waves resembling black mountins.
{3:118}__10+

uyar uruvil urintu uṭai tavirntārum
the amaṇar who are without dress having stripped off their clothes worm on their tall bodies.
attukil pōrttu uḻalvārum
and the buddhists who cover their bodies with a beautiful cloth.
terintu puṉ moḻikaḷ ceppiṉa kēḷāc cemmaiyār naṉmaiyāl uṟaivu ām
the god who is spotless and resides with excellences and does not pay heed to the viles words spoken having chosen them.
kuruntu uyar kōṅku koṭi viṭu mullai mallikai ceṇpakam vēṅkai, karuntaṭaṅkaṇṇiṉ maṅkaimār koyyum kaḻumala nakar eṉalām
we may say that it is the city of Kaḻumalam where girls who have black and big eyes pluck flowers from the east indian kino-tree, jasmine and arabian jasmine creapers, tall common cung and wild lime-tree.

ñāṉacampantaṉ naṟṟamiḻ mālai
the garland of good tamiḻ verses composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ
kāṉal am kaḻaṉi ōtam vantu ulavum kaḻumala nakar uṟaivār mēl
on the god who dwells in the city of Kaḻumalam where the tides wander in the fields situated in the sea-side grove
naṉmaiyāl urai ceytu navilvār
those who recite often these verses with good intention.
ūṉa campantattu uṟu piṇi nīṅki
as the many diseases which are connected with the physical body are removed
uḷḷamum oruvaḻik koṇṭu
concentrating their minds.
vāṉiṭai vāḻvar; maṇmicaip piṟavār
I will live in the world of Civaṉ; will not be born on this earth.
(maṟṟu) itaṟku āṇaiyum namatē
for this our word is the authority.

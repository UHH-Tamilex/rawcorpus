{2:80}__1+
{$} variya maṟaiyār
Civaṉ who revealed the vētams which have melody of music.
piṟaiyār
bears the crescent on his head.
malai ōr cilaiyā vaṇaṅki
bending the mountin into a peerlesss bow.
eriya matilkaḷ eytār
shot an arrow on the fortification to burn them.
eṟiyum mucalam uṭaiyār
has a pestle-like weapon of war that can be thrown on enemies (mucalam may also mean a club)
kariya miṭaṟum uṭaiyār
has also a black neck.
kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
loved to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam.
periya viṭaimēl varuvār
comes riding on a big bull.
avar emperumāṉ aṭikaḷ
he is our divine being.
{2:80}__2+
{$} maṅkai maṇanta mārpar
Civaṉ has united in his chest a lady;
[[cf. (vilniṟa vāḷnutal vēl niṟakkaṇ melliyalai mallal taṉniṟam oṉṟil irutti niṉṟōṉ (Kōviyār, 58), though pērāciriyar has given the meaning for niṟam as body; niṟam also means chest.]]
maḻuvāḷ oṉṟuvalaṉ ēnti
holding the weapon of a battleaxe in the right hand.
kaṅkai caṭaiyiṟ karantār
concealed the Kaṅki in his caṭi.
kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
loved to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam.
ceṅkaṇ veḷḷēṟu ēṟic celvam ceyyā varuvār
comes granting the wealth of his audience to his devotees, riding on a white bull with red eyes.
aṅkai ēṟiya maṟiyār
has a young dear which he holds in his beautiful hand (aṅkai: am+kai not akam+kai which means palm).
avar em perumāṉ aṭikaḷ
see 1st verse.
{2:80}__3+
{$} īṭual iṭapam icaiya ēṟi
riding on a bull which has nothing comparable to it, fitting his greatness.
maḻuoṉṟu ēnti
holding a battle-axe.
kāṭu (atu) iṭamā uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has the cremation ground as his place.
kaṭavūrmayāṉam amarntār
wished to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam.
pāṭal icaikoḷkaruvi paṭupatam palavum payilvār
practises vocal music, musical instruments and several kinds of dance.
paṭitam
is also a variant form of this word;
[[paṭitam pala pāṭa (campantar, tirumīyaccūr, 4); kaṭuvaṉ ... paṭitam payiṟṟum eṉpa, maṭiyāk kolaivil eṉṉaiyar malaiyē (verse quoted in the commentary of yāppuñkalakkāriki p.148, Dr.V.Swaminatha Ayyar Library edition, 1948).]]
āṭal aravam uṭaiyār
has a dancing cobra.
avar emperumāṉ aṭikaḷ
see 1st verse.
{2:80}__4+
{$} iṟai niṉṟu ilaṅku vaḷaiyāḷa, iḷaiyāḷ orupāl uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has a young lady on whose forearm bangles are shining.
maṟai niṉṟu ilaṅku moḻiyār
his words are the eminent vētams.
malaiyār maṉattiṉ micaiyār
is in the minds of those who are not confused or confounded.
kaṟai niṉṟu ilaṅku poḻilcūḻ kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
wished to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam surrounded by gardens where darkness reigns supreme.
piṟainiṉṟu ilaṅku caṭaiyār
has a caṭi on which the crescent is shining without leaving it.
avar emperumāṉ aṭikaḷ
see 1st verse.
{2:80}__5+
{$} veḷḷai eruttiṉ micaiyār
Civaṉ is seated on a white bull.
viritōṭu oru kātu ilaṅkattuḷḷum iḷamāṉ maṟiyār
has a very young deer which leaps in his hand, when the womens' ear-ring which emits rays is shining in one ear.
cuṭar poṟcaṭaikaḷ tuḷaṅkak kaḷḷam naku veṇtalaiyār
has a lughing and guileful white skull, when the flittering golden cati is bright.
kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
wished to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam.
piḷḷai matiyam uṭaiyār
has a crescent (on his head)
avar emperumāṉ aṭikaḷ
see 1st verse.
{2:80}__6+
{$} poṉ tātu utirum maṇamkoḷ puṉai pūṅkoṉṟai puṉaintār
Civaṉ adorned himself with beautiful and lovely koṉṟi of fragrance, from which golden pollen drops off
oṉṟāveḷḷēṟu uyarntatu uṭaiyār
has a white bull, hoisted on his flag, which has no equal
kaṉṟu āviṉam cūḻpuṟavil kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
wished to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam which has forest tracts where calves and herds of cows go round.
piṉtāḻ caṭaiyar oruvar
has a caṭi that hangs low on the back; has no equal.
avar emperumāṉ aṭikaḷ
see 1st verse.
{2:80}__7+
{$} pācam āṉa kaḷaivār
Civaṉ will weed out the five impurities of the soul (the five impurities are (1) āṇavam (2) māyi (3) kaṉmam (4) māyēyam (5) tirōtāṉam)
[[malaṅkaḷ aintāl cuḻalvaṉ (tiruvacakam 193).]]
parivārkku amutam aṇaiyār
is like the nectar to those who are affectionate towards him.
ācaitīrak koṭuppār
will give so that the wish to acquire a desired object is fulfilled.
alaṅkal viṭaimēl varuvār
will come riding on a bull wearing garlands.
kācai malarpōl miṭaṟṟār
has a neck resembling in colour the flowers of iron-wood tree;
[[kācaimalar-kāyāmpū the flower of the iron-wood tree which is blue in colour; kācaicēr kuḻalāḷ kācai cey kuḻalumai (campantar, tiruppūṇtarāi, 1-8; do. paṇ-Kāntāram, 8); meṉkācaiyaṉṉa māmiṭaṟṟārkkum (inscription in taṉkāci temple)]]
kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
wished to dwell in Kaṭāvūr mayāṉam.
pēcavaruvār
if we prise him he will come near us.
oruvar
is the unequalled one.
avar emperumāṉ aṭikaḷ
see 1st verse.

the god who pressed the hill with stones by his tender toe of the shining lotus red feet to cuse the angry arakkaṉ to roar.
kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
wished to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam.
maṟṟu oṉṟu iṇaiilvaliya mātuil veḷḷimalaipōl peṟṟu oṉṟu ēṟivaruvār

{$} varum mākariyiṉ uriyār
Civaṉ has a skin of an elephant that came to kill him.
vaḷarpuṉcaṭaiyār, viṭaiyār
has a red caṭi that grows, has a bull.
karumāṉ uritōl uṭaiyār
has a skin of a black antelope flayed from it; (uritōl: viṉaittokai)
kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
wished to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam
tirumāloṭu nāṉmukaṉum tērntum kāṇamuṉ oṇṇāpperumāṉ eṉavum varuvār
he is prised as the god who was unable to be found out by tirumāl and Piramaṉ on four faces though they searched for his feet and head in the past.
avar emperumāṉ aṭikaḷē
see 1st verse.
{2:80}__10+
{$} tūyaviṭaimēl varuvār
Civaṉ rides on a spotless white bull.
tuṉṉāruṭaiya matilkaḷkāya vēvacceṟṟār
destroyed the cities of the enemies to be burnt by hot fire.
kaṭavūr mayāṉam amarntār
wished to dwell in Kaṭavūr mayāṉam.
tīyakarumam collum ciṟupuṉtērarcamaṇar, pēy pēy eṉṉa varuvār
comes to be abused by the low and mean tēvar and camaṇar who talk about bad acts, as pēy, pēy.
avar emperumāṉ aṭikaḷē
see 1st verse.

{$} maravam poḻilcūḻ kaṭavūr maṉṉum mayāṉam amarnta aravam acaitta perumāṉ akalam aṟiyal (1) āka
to understand the pervasiveness of Civaṉ who tied a cobra and who wished to stay in the temple mayāṉam which is permanent in Kaṭavūr surrounded by gardens of common catampatree.
paravum muṟaiyē payilum pantaṉ ceñcolmālai iravum pakalum paravi niṉaivār viṉaikaḷ ilar
those who prise god with the garland of verses contining words in their primary significance and meditation of prising, will be free from Karmams (Pantaṉ a part of the full name ñāṉacampantaṉ)

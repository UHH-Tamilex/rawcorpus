kālai eḻuntu
waking up early in the morning
kaṭimalar tūyaṉatām koṇarntu
having brought pure and fragrant flowers.
mēlai amarar virumpum iṭam
the shrine which is desired by the immortals in the upper world for worship.
viraiyāl malinta cōlai maṇam kamaḻ
(Ōṟṟuttuṟi) where the gardens spread their fragrance as fragrant flowers are abundant.
cōṟṟuttuṟai uṟaivār caṭaimēl mālai matiyam aṉṟō empirāṉukku aḻakiyatē
Is not the crescent that shines in the evening, beautiful on the caṭi of our god who dwells in Ōṟṟuttuṟi.
{4:85}__2+
{$}
vaṇṭu aṇai koṉṟaiyum vaṉṉiyum mattamum vāraivum koṇṭu aṇaintu ēṟum muṭi uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has a holy head on which koṉṟi flowers which are approached by bees, leaves of indian mesquit, dhatura flowers and a killing cobra come near and thrive, as the place fit for them.
kuraicēr kaḻaṟkē toṇṭu aṇaintu āṭiya cōṟṟuttuṟai uṟaivār caṭaimēl veṇtalai mālai aṉṟō empirāṉukku aḻakiyatē
is not the garland of white skulls worn on the long caṭi of Civaṉ who dwells in Ōṟṟuttuṟi where the devotees reach and dance, performing service and approaching the jingling armour of his feet.
{4:85}__3+
{$}
aḷakkum neṟiyiṉaṉ
Civaṉ has a means by which he measures the piety of his devotees.
aṉparkaḷ tam maṉattu āyntu koḷvāṉ
he will investigate into the minds of his devotees.
viḷakkum aṭiyavarmēl viṉai tīrttiṭum viṇṇavar kōṉ
is the chief of the celestials residing in heaven and completely destroys the accumulated karmams of previous birth and karmams accumulating in this birth yet to bear their fruit, of the devotees who explin the greatness of the feet of Civaṉ and themselves.
tuḷakkum kuḻai aṇi cōṟṟuttuṟai uṟaivār caṭaimēl tiḷaikkum matiyam aṉṟō empirāṉukku aḻakiyatē
Is not the crescent that is always on the caṭi of Civaṉ without leaving it, who dwells in Ōṟṟuttuṟi and wears a brilliant ear-ring, beautiful!
{4:85}__4+
{$}
āynta kaivāḷ aravattoṭu mālviṭaiēṟi eṅkum pērntakai māṉnaṭam āṭuvar
Civaṉ will dance with a deer held in the hand which swings everywhere, riding on a big bull with a lean and killing cobra.
piṉṉu caṭaiyiṭaiyē cērnta kaimmāmalar tuṉṉiya cōṟṟuttuṟai uṟaivār ēntu kaic cūlammaḻu empirāṉukku aḻakiyatē
to Civaṉ on whose intertwining caṭi the flowers that were scattered by the hands of devotees get close together, and who dwells in Ōṟṟuttuṟi, the trident and battle-axe the Lord holds in his hands, are beautiful.
{4:85}__5+
{$}
kūṟṟaik kaṭantatum kōḷ aravu ārttatum kōḷ (1) uḻuvai nīṟṟil tutaintu tiriyum paricatum nām aṟiyōm
we do not know the manner in which the skin of the tiger capable of killing, being close to the holy ash and rolling on the body, destroying the god of death and tying a killing cobra.
āṟṟil kiṭantu aṅku alaippa alaippuṇṭu acaintatu okkum cōṟṟut tuṟai uṟaivār caṭaimēlatu ōr tūmatiyē
the spotless crescent which is on the caṭi of the Lord who dwells in Ōṟṟuttuṟi appears like moving here and there, being tossed as the waves in the river (Kaṅki) in which it is lying, toss it.
[[Variant reading: (1) uruvai]]
{4:85}__6+
{$}
vallāṭi niṉṟu valipēcuvār kōḷar val acurar
the strong acurar will play rude pranks and speak arrogant words and are capable of killing.
kollāṭi niṉṟu kumaikkilum
though they commit murders and give annoyance.
vāṉavar vantu iṟaiñcac collāṭi niṉṟu payilkiṉṟa cōṟṟuvattuṟai uṟaivār villāṭi niṉṟa nilai empirāṉukku aḻakiyatu
to our Lord, who dwells in Ōṟṟuttuṟi, who freely converses with the celestials when they descend from heaven and worship him; the posture of his standing having made use of the bow, is beautiful.
{4:85}__7+
{$}
āyam uṭaiyatu nām aṟiyōm
we do not know his owning a big army;
[[veṟpar āyam ōṭi (periya purāṇam. kaṇṇappa nāyaṉār.77)]]
araṇattavaraik kāyakkaṇai cilai vāṅkiyum eytum tuyakku aṟuttāṉ
Civaṉ put an end to the distraction of the acurar who had three forts by bending the bow and discharging an arrow from it, to destroy them.
tūyaveṇṇīṟṟiṉaṉ
who besmears with pure white holy ash.
cōṟṟuttuṟai uṟaivār caṭaimēl pāyum veṇṇīrt (1) tiraik kaṅkai emmāṉukku aḻakiyatu
the Kaṅki of waves and white coloured water rushing down on the caṭi of our master who dwells in Ōṟṟuttuṟi is beautiful.
[[Variant reading: (1) tiraṭ]]

aṇṭar amarar kaṭaintu eḻuntu ōṭiya (1) nañcu ataṉai uṇṭum
consuming the poison which ran rising from the ocean when the enemies of the immortals (acurar) and the immortals churned the ocean.
[[aṇṭar=pakaivar (enemies);
This is a corrupt form of the word aṇṭār in which the long vowels has been shortened to suit the rhyme;
kālvalpuravi aṇṭar ōṭṭi (Patiṟṟuppattu, 88-9);
uyarnilai umpar oṉṉala ruṭaṉē, iṭaiyar aṇṭar eṉṉalākum (piṅkalam, 3055);
ōṭiya nañcu:
muṉṉum kaṭuviṭam-parantu cellum kaṭiyaviṭam Kōviyār; 236);
``The poison which made those who churned the ocean to flee by its severity'' is another [possible] meaning]]
ataṉai oṭukkavallāṉ
Civaṉ who was capable of subduing its severity.
mikka umparkaḷ kōṉ toṇṭu payilkiṉṟa cōṟṟuttuṟai uṟaivār caṭaimēl iṇṭai matiyam aṉaṟō empirāṉukku aḻakiyatē
Are not the circlet of flowers and the crescent which are on the caṭi of our Lord who is in Ōṟṟuttuṟi where (Intiraṉ) the king of the countless celestials beings practises service to god.
[[iṇṭai matiyam the crescent which resembles the circlet of flowers is another meaning;
intiraṉ vaṉattu mallikai malariṉ iṇṭai cāttiyateṉa niṟainta cantiraṉ muṭimēl vantuṟum cōṉa cailaṉē kailai nāyakaṉē (oṇacilamāli, 57);
iṇṭai matiyam : may be taken either as an elliptical compound in which the conjuction part um is understood (iṇṭaiyum, matiyamum) or as an elliptical compound in which the sign of comparison is understood (iṇṭai pōṉṟa matiyam)]]
[[Variant reading: 1. nañcu taṉai]]

kaṭal maṇi vaṇṇaṉ karutiya nāṉ mukaṉ tāṉ 1. aṟiyāviṭam aṇikaṇṭam uṭaiyavaṉ
Civaṉ who has a neck made beautiful by the poison and who could not be found by Māl whose colour is that of the sea and sapphire, and by Piramaṉ who thought that he could easily find out the head.
[[cf. tirumaṇi, tiraipāṭu avinta munnīr, varumaḻai iruñcūl mūṉṟum puraiyum māmey (paripātal, 4-6-7)]]
eṉai āḷuṭaiyāṉ
who accepted me as his servant.
cuṭar aṇintu āṭiya cōṟṟuttuṟai uṟai vār caṭaimēl paṭam aṇi nākam aṉṟō empirāṉukku aḻakiyatu
Does not the cobra which is beautiful by its hood and which stays in the caṭi of Civaṉ who dwells in ōṟṟuttuṟi where the suns rays fall on the linkam, and our master, appear beautiful?
[[Variant reading: 1. aṟiyāṉ]]

ilaṅkaikku iṟaivaṉ irupatutōḷum muṭi(um) neriyak kalaṅka
the twenty shoulders and ten heads of the king of ilaṅki to be crushed and he himself to become confused in mind.
viraliṉul ūṉṟi avaṉaikkaruttu oḻitta tulaṅkal maḻuviṉaṉ
ivaṉ with a glittering battle-axe who destroyed his pride by fixing firmly the toe.
cōṟṟuttuṟaivār caṭaimēl ilaṅku matiyam aṉṟō empirāṉuṉakku aḻakiyatē
Does not the bright crescent on the caṭi of the Civaṉ who dwells in ōṟṟuttuṟi and who is our Lord, appear beautiful?

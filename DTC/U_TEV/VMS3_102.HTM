kāmpiṉai veṉṟa meṉ tōḷi pākam kalantāṉ
Civaṉ who combined in himself a half of the lady of soft shoulders which are superior to bamboo.
nalam tāṅku, tēm puṉal cūḻ tikaḻ mā maṭuviṉ, tirunāraiyūr mēya (caṭaiyāṉ)
who is in Tirunāriyūr which has big and prominent pools surrounded by sweet water and which has many good properties.
pūm puṉal cēr puri puṉ caṭaiyāṉ
who has golden twisted caṭi on which the beautiful water (of Kaṅki) stays
puliyiṉ uri tōl mēl pāmpiṉai vīkkiya paṇṭaraṅkaṉ pātam paṇivōm
we shall bow before the feet of paṇṭaraṅkaṉ who tied tightly a cobra on the skin of a tiger which was flayed.
{3:102}__2+
{$}
tīviṉaiyāyiṉa tīrkka niṉṟāṉ
Civaṉ was steadfast in completely removing the evil actions.
tirunāraiyūr mēyāṉ
who dwells in Tirunāriyūr
pūviṉai mēvum caṭaimuṭiyāṉ
who has matted hirs coiled into a crown on which there are flowers.
pala pūtam puṭai cūḻa āviṉil aintum koṇaṭu āṭṭu ukantāṉ
who derived pleasure by being bathed with all the five products got from the cow, and who is surrounded by many pūtam
aṭaṅkār matil mūṉṟum ēviṉai eytu aḻittāṉ kaḻalē paravā eḻuvōm
we shall rise high in doing service by prising the feet of Civaṉ who destroyed all the three forts of the enemies by discharging an arrow.
{3:102}__3+
{$} māyavaṉ
Civaṉ is black in colour.
cēyavaṉ
He is red in colour
veḷḷiyavaṉ
He is white in colour
[[These refer to the three faces namely akōram, vāmatēuam and cattiyōcatam of the five faces of the Catacivamūrti; according to grammar, implying something which has not been made explicit by expressing another thing associated or connected with it, we must add the remaning two faces, taṟpuruṭam, and īcāṉam which are like gold and marble in colour.]]
viṭam cērum mai miṭaṟṟaṉ āyavaṉ
He has a neck as black as the cloud, in which the poison stays.
ōr antaramum avaṉ eṉṟu āki
being himself each and every difference, like this.
varai ākam tīyavaṉ
His body which is like the mountin, is fire.
nīravaṉ
He is the water
pūmiyavaṉ
He is the earth
tirunāraiyūr taṉṉil mēyavaṉait toḻuvār avar mēl viṉai āyiṉa vīṭum
the Karmams of those who worship Civaṉ who is in Tirunāriyūr will perish.
{3:102}__4+
{$}
tuñcu iruḷ āṭuvar
Civaṉ will dance in that darkness when everything is absorbed in the great infinite.
tū muṟuval tuḷaṅkum uṭam piṉarāy am cuṭar ār eri āṭuvar
he will dance in the bright and unbearable fire with a body moving from side to side, with an innocent smile
viḻikkaṇ ār aḻalār
he has in his frontal eye fire of unbearable heat.
nañcu umiḻ nākam araikku acaippar
he will tie round his waist a cobra that spits poison.
nalaṉ ōṅku nāraiyūr em civaṉārkku aṭimaip paṭuvārkku iṉi ētam illai
for those who become the slaves of our Civaṉār in nāriyūr whose prosperity is always increasing, there will be no sufferings here-after.
{3:102}__5+
{$}
poṅku iḷam koṉṟaiyiṉār
Civaṉ wears fresh and verdent flowers of koṉṟi
kaṭalil viṭam uṇṭu imaiyōrkaḷ taṅkaḷai ār iṭar tīra niṉṟa talaivar
is the chief who stood as a help to remove the inescapable suffering of the immortals who do not wink, by drinking the poison that rose in the ocean.
caṭai mēl ōr tiṅkaḷai vaittu aṉal āṭaliṉār
Having placed on the caṭi a crescent he dances in the fire.
tirunāraiyūr mēya veṅkaṉal veṇṇīṟu aṇiyavallār avarē viḻumiyar
He who smears on his body white holy ash well burnt in the hot fire and who is in Tirunāriyūr is the most exalted person.
{3:102}__6+
{$}
pār uṟum vāymaiyiṉār paravum paramēṭṭi
Civaṉ who is in the most exalted place is worshipped by truthful people whose fame has spread throughout the world.
paiṅkoṉṟait tār uṟum mārpu uṭaiyāṉ
He wears on his chest fertile garlands of koṉṟi flowers
malaiyiṉ talaivaṉ
he is the chief of the mountin (Kayili);
[[Though malai is a common name, it denotes here Kayili, a proper name.]]
malaimakaḷaiccīr uṟum
He will embrace the dughter of the mountin with great esteem
māmaṟukil ciṟai vaṇṭu aṟaiyum tirunāraiyūr uṟai em iṟaivarkku ivai oṉṟoṭu oṉṟu ovvāvē
these things are at variance with one another for the Lord who dwells in Tirunāriyūr where in the big streets bees with wings hum.
{3:102}__7+
{$} kaḷḷi iṭutalai ēntu kaiyar
Civaṉ holds in his hand a skull left in the cremation ground where spruge grows.
kari kāṭar
he lives in the scorched cremation ground
kaṇṇutalar
he has a frontal eye.
veḷḷiya kōvaṇa āṭai taṉmēl miḷir āṭu aravu ārttu
tying a shining and dancing cobra on the dress which is a white loin-cloth.
naḷḷiruḷ naṭṭam (atu) āṭuvar
he will dance in the dense darkness.
nal nalaṉ ōṅku nāraiyūr uḷḷiya pōḻtil emmēl varu valviṉai āyiṉa ōṭum
the irresistible Karmams which are to approach us will flee when we think of Nāṟiyūr where prosperity is always on the increase.

nāmam (1) eṉaippalavum uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has ever so many names
[[eṉaippalavum: eṉai will denote plural, whereas ēṉai will denote only singular; This is the correct reading.]]
nalaṉ ōṅku nāraiyūr tām mom meṉap paṟai yāḻ kuḻal tāḷ ār kaḻal payila
when the sound of the anklets, flute and lute blend with the sound of the drum which makes a sound like `mom' in Nāriyūr of increasing beauty
[[PP_(mom meṉa):
mum meṉṟicai muralvaṇṭukaḷ
(Campantar, tiruvīḻimiḻi (1) 3)={1:11}__3]
īma viḷakku eri cūḻ cuṭalaiyiyampum iṭukāṭṭil
in the burial-ground, where the light of the firebrand burns with a sound, lighting the dance-arena.
cāmam uraikka niṉṟu āṭuvāṉum taḻal āya caṅkaraṉē
it is Caṅkaraṉ who is himself the form of fire and dances, when the noise in the cremation ground resembles Cāmavētam
[[Variant reading: (1) eṉap palavum]]
{3:102}__9+
{$}
ūṉ uṭai veṇ talai koṇṭu uḻalvāṉ
Civaṉ wanders holding a white skull which has flesh in it.
oḷir puṉ caṭai mēl ōr vāṉiṭai veṇmati vaittu ukantāṉ
He was pleased to place a white crescent that rises in the sky, on the shining golden caṭi
vari vaṇṭu yāḻ murala
when the bees with lines on their bodies hum like the music of the lute.
tēṉ uṭai māmalar aṉṉam vaikum tirunāraiyūr mēya āṉiṭai aintu ukantāṉ aṭiyē paravā aṭaivōm
we will reach prising the feet of Civaṉ who was pleased to bathe in the five products of the cow and who is in Nāṟiyūr where the swans are sitting on the lotus flowers which have honey.
{3:102}__10+
{$}
tūcu puṉai tuvar āṭai mēvum toḻilār
Buddhists who desire [to have] robes soaked in myrtle-dye as their dress
uṭam piṉiluḷ mācu puṉaintu uṭai nīttavarkaḷ mayal nīrmai kēḷātē
without paying heed to the words of confusing nature of the jins who have discarded dress but have accumulated dirt on their bodies.
tēcu uṭaiyīrkaḷ
people who have brilliant knowledge!
tirunāraiyūr taṉṉil pūcu poṭit talaivar aṭiyār aṭiyē poruttamē
the feet of the devotees of the chief who smears himself with holy ash and who is in Tirunāriyūr, is the proper place for our refuge.
teḷintu aṭaimiṉ
reach them clarifying your mind

oṇ mati cēr caṭaiyāṉ tirunāraiyūr taṉ mēl
on Tirunāriyūr where Civaṉ who wears a bright crescent on his caṭi, dwells.
taṇ mati tāḻ poḻil cūḻ pukalit tamiḻ ñāṉacampantaṉ
Ñāṉacampantaṉ, who is well-versed in Tamiḻ and is a native of Pukali which is surrounded by garden on which the cool moon stays.
paṇ matiyāl coṉṉa pāṭal pattum payiṉṟār viṉaipōki
those who always recite the ten verses with paṇ which were composed by his superior knowledge, being rid of their karmam-s
maṇ matiyātu
and without minding as of any worth the pleasures of this world.
pōy vāṉōr etir koḷa vāṉ pukuvar
will enter into heaven, being received by the inhabitants of heaven when they go there.

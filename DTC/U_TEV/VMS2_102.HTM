{2:102}__1+
{$} aṉṉam meṉṉaṭai arivaiyoṭu iṉitu uṟai amarartam perumāṉār
the chief of the immortals who gladly resides with a lady having a graceful git like that of the swan.
miṉṉu ceñcaṭai veḷ erukka (m) malar vaittavar
he placed on the shining red caṭi flowers of white yarcum.
vētam tām paṉṉu nuṇ poruḷ payantavar
He gave the abstruse meaning which the vētam-s contin, extensively.
parumatiṟ cirapurattār
the god who is cirapuram having thich wall of enclosure.
cīr ār poṉṉiṉ māmalar aṭi toḻum aṭiyavar viṉaiyōṭum poruntār
the devotees who worship the feet of that god, which are like the eminent big flower made of gold, will not come into contact with actions, good and bad
[[These ten verses have the theme that the actions of the devotees will be removed if they recite the verses dily.]]
{2:102}__2+
{$} kōlam mā kari urittavar
the god who flayed the skin of a big and beautiful elephant.
aravoṭum; ēṉak kompu, iḷa āmai, cālap pūṇṭu taṇ mati (atu) cūṭiya caṅkaraṉār
caṅkaraṉ who profusely adorned himself with the shell of a young tortoise, the hog`s tusk along with cobra, and wore on his head a cool crescent.
tammaip pōlat tam aṭiyārkkum iṉpu aḷippavar
He grants happiness to his devotees also, like him who is the embodiment of happiness. (God is the embodiment of happiness; he grants that form to devotees who approach his feet; iṉpu - iṉpam; am is dropped)
porukaṭal viṭam uṇṭa nīlattār miṭaṟṟu aṇṇalār cirapuram toḻa viṉai nillā
the actions will not remin, if one worships cirapuram belonging to the god who has a neck resembling blue nelumbo flower, which got its colour as he devoured the poison which rose in the ocean which is always dashing aginst the shore.
[[(porukaṭal : the underlined epithet is a generic one;
nīlattār has been split as nīlam + tār; tār flower;
koṇṭalum nīlamum purai tirumiṭaṟar (campantar, Kaḻumalam (2) 2);
kuvaḷaik kaḷattu ampalavaṉ;
kāvi niṉṟu ērtaru kaṇṭar;
ampalattuk, kāviyai vellum miṭaṟṟōṉ(Kōviyār, 33, 41, 349)]]
{2:102}__3+
{$} māṉat tiṇpuya vari cilaip pārttaṉait tavamkeṭa matittu
Having considered to destroy the penance of pārttaṉ who had a bow fastened with stnaps kept on the strong shoulders having dignified appearance.
aṉṟu kāṉakattē tiri vēṭaṉāy amar ceyak kaṇṭu aruḷ purintār
granted boons to him having assumed the guise of a hunter wandering in the forest, and seeing him fight with him (god) on that day.
pūntēṉait tērntu cēr vaṇṭukaḷ tiritarum cirapurattu uṟai eṅkaḷ, kōṉaikkumpiṭum aṭiyaraik koṭuviṉai kuṟṟaṅkaḷ kuṟukā
the cruel sins and moral blemish will not come near the devotees who worship with their joined hands our king who resides in cirapuram where swarming bees wander seeking honey in flowers.
{2:102}__4+
{$} māṇitaṉ uyir matittu uṇavanta akkālaṉai utai ceytār
the god kicked that kālaṉ who came thinking to take away the life of the bachelor (here mārkaṇtēyaṉ)
pēṇi uḷkum mey aṭiyavar peruntuyarp piṇakku aṟuttu aruḷ ceyvār
He bestows his grace after removing the disagreements which bring suffering for the true devotees who think of him with great regard.
vēṇi veṇ piṟai uṭaiyavar
has a white crescent moon on his caṭi.
viyaṉ pukaḻc cirapurattu amarkiṉṟa āṇip poṉṉiṉai aṭi toḻum aṭiyavarkku aruviṉai aṭaiyā
the irresistable actions will not reach the devotees who worship the feet of the god who is like the gold of the finest quality without impurities, and who resides gladly in cirapuram which has a fame that has spread far and wide.

{$} pārum nīroṭu palkatir iraviyum paṉi mati ākācam, orum vāyuvum oṇkaṉal vēḷviyil talaivaṉumāy niṉṟāṉ
the god who has the following forms; the earth, water, sun of many rays, cool moon, sky, air which is felt, and the performer of sacrifice.
[[These eight are called aṣṭa mūrttam eight forms,
aṭṭa mūrtti aṇṇāmalai;
irunilaṉāyt tiyāki, nīrum āki iyamāṉaṉāy eriyum kāṟṟum āki, aru nilaiya tiṅkaḷāy ñāyiṟāki ākācamāy aṭṭa mūrtti āki (appar, tiruvaṇnamali, tirukkuṟuntoki (2) 1); niṉṟa tiruttāṅtakam, 1)]]
cērum cantaṉam ākiloṭu vantiḻi ceḻumpuṉal kōṭṭāṟu, vārum taṇ puṉal cūḻ cirapuram toḻum aṭiyavar varuntār
the devotees who worship cirapuram which is surrounded by the cool water flowing in Kōttāṟu which enters along with its fertile water carrying sandal wood and eagle-wood, will not suffer.
kōṭṭāṟu
a chhannel that flows through cīkaḻi;

{2:102}__6+
{$} ūḻi antattil oli kaṭal 1. ōṭṭantu ivvulakaṅkaḷ avai mūṭa
when the roaring oceans came overflowing the shores and covered the earth at the end of aeon.
āḻi entai' eṉṟu amararkaḷ caraṇ puka
when the immortals sought refuge in Civaṉ saying, our father who can be compared to the ocean in point of grace!
antarattu uyarntār
the Lord rose high into the heaven.
yāḻiṉ nēr moḻi ēḻaiyōṭu iṉitu uṟai iṉpaṉ
Dear husband who resides gladly with the lady who speaks words as sweet as the music of yāḻ.
em perumāṉ ūr
the place of our great person.
vāḻi mānakarc cirapuram toḻutu eḻa valviṉai aṭaiyā
the strong actions will not come near those who walk up from sleep worshipping the big city of cirapuram (vāḻi : expletive)

{2:102}__7+
{$} vēy koḷ tōḷi (tāṉ) veḷkiṭa mā nāṭakam āṭum vittakaṉār
the skilful god who danced to make Kaḷi whose shoulders resemble bamboo, feel ashamed.
pēykaḷ pāṭap pūtaṅkaḷ tuti ceyap piṇam iṭu cuṭukāṭṭil
in the cremation ground where corpses are buried; when the demons sing and the goblins prise him
oṇ cāykaḷ (tāṉ) mika uṭaiya taṇ maṟaiyavar taku cirapurattār (tām)
the god who is in cirapuram where gentle brahmins who have brilliant fames of many kinds are suited to the place.
[[cāy - pukaḻ; intiraṉ taṉ cāyāp peruñcāy keṭattām pukaḷāl taṭantōḷ pōy ārttavaṉ (Kamparāmāyaṇam 6 - nākapācappaṭalam 21)]]
pal uyirkkum tāykaḷāyiṉār tamait tomumavar taḷarār
those who worship the god who is the mother for the many living beings will not be enfeebled (the plural tāykaḷ is used to denote that god is the mother to each and every living being; he is like as many mothers as there are as many living beings)
{2:102}__8+
{$} ilaṅku pūṇ varai mārpuṭai irāvaṇaṉ eḻil koḷ veṟpu eṭuttu aṉṟu, kalaṅkac, ceytalum
when Irāvaṇaṉ who has a chest adorned with bright ornaments and strong like the mountin made (Umi) to fear by lifting up the beautiful mountin on that day.
kaṇṭu tam kaḻal aṭi neriya vaittu aruḷ ceytār
God on seeing that pressed him down with his feet wearing anklets and later bestowed boons on him.
1. pulaṅkaḷ ceṅkaḻunīr malart teṉṟal maṉṟu ataṉiṭai pukuntu ārum cirapuram
cirapuram where the balmy southern breeze which carries with it the fragrance of the purple indian water-lily growing in the fields enters into the hall of assembly and fills it (malar : stands for its fragrance).
kulam koḷ mā maṟaiyavar cirapuram toḻutu eḻaviṉai kuṟukā
sins will not come near one if he worships cirapuram where there are great brahmins of noble lineage, and wakes up from sleep.
[[Variant reading: 1. pulaṅkoḷ]]
{2:102}__9+
{$} vaṇṭu ceṉṟu aṇai malar micai nāṉ mukaṉ māyaṉ eṉṟa ivar
these two persons who are māyaṉ, and the four-faced one who is seated on a lotus to which bees go (in search of honey) and stay there. (eṉṟivar eṉṟa ivar)
aṉṟu kaṇṭu koḷḷa ōr ēṉamōṭu aṉṉamāyk kiḷaṟiyum paṟantum tām paṇṭu kaṇṭatu kāṇavē nīṇṭa em pacupati
Civaṉ, Lord of souls, who grew long when those two on that day were determined to find out the head and feet by transforming themselves into a pig and a swan and went digging the earth and flying in th sky, to make them see only what they had seen before.
paramēṭṭi
the supreme being in the most exalted place.
koṇṭa celvattuc cirapuram toḻutu eḻaviṉai (avai) kūṭā
sins will not attach to those who worship cirapuram of great wealth which the Lord wished as his place.
{2:102}__10+
{$} paṟitta puṉtalaikkuṇṭikaic camaṇarum; pārmicait tuvar tōynta, ceṟitta cīvarattērarum tērkilāt tēvarkaḷ perumāṉār
the chief of the tevar who could not be understood by camaṇar who carry an ascetics pitcher and have a small head from which hirs are plucked, and buddhists who don the cīvaram which has been soaked in the myrthe dye, who move in this world.
mētikaḷ karumpu muṟittu, tiṉṟu āviyiṉ mūḻkiṭa
when the buffaloes eat the sugar-cane by breaking them and plunge into the tanks.
iḷa vāḷai veṟittuppāy vayal cirapuram toḻa viṉai mika viṭṭiṭum
the sins will mostly leave those who worship cirapuram which has fields in which the scabbard fish jump being frightened. (The description in the last two lines is appropriate to the agricultural tract in which cikāḻi is situated)
{2:102}__11+
{$} paracu pāṇiyai
on the god who has a battle-axe on his hand.
pattarkaḷ attaṉai
the father of the true devotees.
pai aravōṭu akku niraicey pūṇ tirumārpuṭai nimalaṉai
the spotless god who has on his chest a ornament made of the rows of bones, and a cobra with a hood.
nittilap perun tottai
God who is as white as a big cluster of pearls.
virai cey pūm poḻil cirapurattu aṇṇalai
the eminent one in cirapuram which has flower parks of fragrance.
viṇṇavar perumāṉai
the Lord of the immortals in heaven.
paravu campantaṉ centamiḻ vallavar paramaṉaip paṇivār
those who are able to recite the chaste tamiḻ songs composed by Campantaṉ who prises god, will always worship the supreme being.

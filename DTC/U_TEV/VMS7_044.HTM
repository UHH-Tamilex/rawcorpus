muṭippatu kaṅkaiyum tiṅkaḷum
Civaṉ bears on his head Kaṅki and the crescent
ceṟṟatu mū eyil
what he destroyed was the three forts.
noṭippatu māttirai nīṟu eḻak kaṇai nūṟiṉār
he destroyed them for the ashes to rise in a such a short time taken to snap the fingers, with an arrow.
vaṉ tirukkaikaḷāl piṭippatu pāmpu
cobra is the thing held in his strong holy hands.
kaṭippatum ēṟum eṉṟu añcuvaṉ
I fear that the poison will spread as soon as the cobra bites.
aṉṟi, illaiyō empirāṉukkē
is there no other thing for my master?
[[This decade derives its name from the first two words of the first verse]]

empirāṉukkē
for my master.
tūṟu aṉṟi āṭu araṅku illaiyō
is there no other stage except the cremation ground.
cuṭalaip poṭinīṟu aṉṟic cāntam maṟṟu illaiyō
is there no other sandal-paste except the fine powder of sacred ash found on the cremation-ground?
imavāṉmakaḷ kūṟu aṉṟik (1) kūṟuvatu illaiyō
is there no other half to be prised by the devotees except the half of the dughter of imavāṉ.
collaic cillai veḷ ēṟu aṉṟi ēṟuvatu illaiyō
is there no other vehicle to ride upon except the white unruly bull living in the forest tract?

taṭumāṟṟattai
unsteady mind.
mānilattu ōṭṭu eṉum ōṭṭu eṉum uyirkōṟalait taṭṭu ēṉam taṭṭu eṉum toṇṭarka
devotees who declare that acts of killing living beings which have this great world as their standing place, are obstacles to the pursuit of righteous path.
ciṭṭaṉ
the superior god.
tiripuram cuṭṭa tēvarkaḷ tēvaṉai
the god above gods, who burnt the three cities.
toṇṭarkāḷ
devotees!
empirāṉai(ē) veṭṭeṉap pēcaṉmiṉ
do not speak disparaging our master (you will be damned if you talk in that manner).

{$} toṇṭarkāḷ
devotees!
naritalai kavva niṉṟu ōri kūppiṭa
when the foxes drag the skulls of dead people, seizing by the mouth and the old jackals hawl.
naḷiruḷ eritalaip pēy puṭaicūḻa
surrounded by peys which have hirs looking like burning fire, on all sides, at midnight.
āriruḷ kāṭṭiṭai
in the cremation ground which has darkness which cannot be pierced by light.
piritalaip pēcaṉmiṉ
do not speak words to indicate leaving.
ciritalaimālai caṭaikku aṇinta em celvaṉai, empirāṉai (ē)
our master who adorned his caṭi with a garland of lughing skulls (Do not despise him by saying these things; these are not his real nature).

vēy aṉa tōḷi malaimakaḷai virumpiya māyam ilmāmalai nāṭaṉ ākiya māṇpaṉai
Civaṉ who has the eminence of dwelling in the big mountinous country, who loved the dughter of the mountin whose shoulders are like the bamboo and who has no deception.
āyaṉa colli niṉṟārkaḷ allal aṟukkiṉum
even if he cuts at the root of sufferings of those who prise him according to their ability.
empirāṉaiyē pēyaṉē, pittaṉē eṉpar (āl)
some ignorant people call our master, he dances with the peys; he is mad.

emperumāṉai iṟaivaṉ eṉṟu ēttappōy

tuṟaioṉṟi

{7:44}__7+
{$} empirāṉukku (ē)
for our master.
tārum taṇkoṉṟaiyum kūviḷam taṉimattamum
Civaṉ's garlands are the cool koṉṟi, bel leaves and very low datura flowers.
ātiyum antamum ārum aḷavu aṟiyāta
his begining and end are such that no one can measure those things.
ukappār toḻa ūrum oṉṟu ilai; ulaku elām
there is no fixed place for the devotees who cherish him with love; the whole world is his shrine.
pērum ōr āyiram eṉpar (āl)
he has one thousand names has no single name to recite.

ariyoṭu pūmicaiyāṉum ātiyum aṟikilār
ari (tirumāl) and Piramaṉ seated in a lotus flower were unable to have a vision of his head and feet.
varitaru pāmpoṭu vaṉṉi tiṅkaḷum mattamum puritaru puṉcaṭai vaitta em puṉitaṟku, empirāṉukku
for our pure god and our master who placed on his twisted and murky caṭi a cobra with lines on its body, leaves of indian mesquit, a crescent and datura flowers.
iṉi eri aṉṟi aṅkaikku oṉṟu illaiyō
is there nothing except fire to hold in his palm?

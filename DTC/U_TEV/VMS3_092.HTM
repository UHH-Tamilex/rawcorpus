{3:92}__1+
{$} naṉṉeñcamē
my good mind!
maruntu avai mantiram
Civaṉ's name which is pañcakaram like medicine.
maṟumai naṉṉeṟiyavai
it is the right path to the next world;
[[cf. maruntu vēṇṭil (l) ivai mantiraṅkaḷ (ḷ) ivai (campantar, tiruntutēvaṉ kuti, 1)]]
maṟṟum ellām
and iit is all other things.
aruntuyar keṭum
sufferings that cannot be removed by other means will perish.
avar nāmamē cintai cey
meditate upon his names only.
poruntu taṇ puṟaviṉil
in the cool forest in which all things are found.
koṉṟai poṉ coritara
koṉṟai trees shed flowers like gold.
tuṉṟu (1) paimpūñ cerunti cempoṉ malar tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
the Lord who dwells in Tirunelvēli where the dense flourishing and beautiful panicled golden blossomed pear tree unfolds flowers like gold;
[[vide notes on cāykkātu by Campantar (verse 9)]]
[[Variant reading: (1) taṇpūñ]]
{3:92}__2+
{$} tuṉṟu paimpoḻil nuḻaintu eḻuviya kētakaippōtu aṇaintu
creeping through a narrow passage into the verdant garden in which trees are crowded, mingling with the flowers of the fragrant screw-pine.
teṉṟal vantu ulaviya tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
the god who dwells in Tirunelvēli where the southern balmy breeze is moving.
eṉṟum ōr iyalpiṉar eṉa niṉaivu ariyavar
cannot be thought as having the same nature always.
ēṟu (atu) ēṟi
riding on a bull.
ceṭicciyar maṉaitoṟum ceṉṟu
going to each and every house of bad women;
[[ceṭicciyar; ceṭiccikaḷ maṉṟiliṭaip palitērap pōvatu vāḻkkaiyē (cuntarar, tirumutukuṉṟam, 7)]]
palikoḷum iyalpu (atu, ē)
it is his nature to receive alms.
{3:92}__3+
{$} poṟi (1) kiḷar aravamum pōḻ iḷamatiyamum kaṅkai eṉṉum neṟipaṭu kuḻaliyaic caṭaimicaic culavi
the cobra which has a shining spot, a crescent and a lady of curly tresses of hair, called Kaṅkai to move round the caṭai.
veṇṇīṟu pūci
smearing white sacred ash.
kiṟipaṭa naṭantu
walking with playful mischief for alms.
naṟkiḷimoḻiyavar maṉam kavarvar (pōlum)
will captivate the minds of ladies who speak sweet words like a beautiful parrot.
ceṟipoḻil taḻuviya tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
the god who dwells in tirunelvēli surrounded by dense gardens.
[[Variant reading: (1) vaḷar]]
{3:92}__4+
{$} kāṇtaku malaimakaḷ katir nilā muṟuvalceytaruḷavēyum
though the beautiful daughter of the mountain smiles showing her teeth which are like moonlight shedding rays, (not conducting himself according to that)
pūṇṭanākam
with the cobras as ornaments.
puṟaṅkāṭu araṅkā naṭam āṭal pēṇi
desirous of performing dance in the cremation ground using it as a stage.
īṇṭu māmāṭaṅkaḷ māḷikai mītu eḻu koṭi matiyam tīṇṭi vantu ulaviya tirunelvēli uṟai celvar(tām, ē)
is the god who dwells in tirunelvēli where the flags that rise on the mansions which have big storeys close to each other, come into contact with the moon, and flutter.
{3:92}__5+
{$} tēṉil vaṇṭu amarpoḻil tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
the Lord who dwells in tirunelvēli which has gardens in which the bees settle upon the honey.
ēṉaveṇkompoṭum eḻil tikaḻmattamum iḷa aravum kūṉalveṇpiṟai(um) tavaḻcaṭaiyiṉar
has a caṭai in which white tusk of the hog, datura flowers which are beautiful, a young cobra and white curved crescent crawl (eḻiltikaḻ is ironical)
(1) kolpulit tōl uṭaiyār
dresses himself with the skin of a tigher capable of killing.
(2) āṉil nal aintu ukantu āṭuvar arumaṟaikaḷ pāṭuvar
will bathe with pleasure in the five good products of the cow; will chant the abstruse vētams.
[[variant readings : (1) kōṭpulit (2) āṉiṉilaintu]]
{3:92}__6+
{$} ceṭipaṭu poḻil aṇi tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
the Lord who dwells in tirunelvēli adorned by gardens which have bushes.
veṭitaru talaiyiṉar
has a skull full of crevices.
vēṉal veḷ ēṟṟiṉar
has an angry white bull. (vēṉal denotes anger)
viricaṭaiyar
has a loosened caṭai.
poṭiaṇi mārpiṉar
smears his chest with sacred ash.
puliataṉ āṭaiyar
has a dress made of the tiger's skin.
poṅku aravar
has ornaments of angry cobras.
vaṭivuuṭai (ya) maṅkai ōr paṅkiṉar
has a beautiful lady of distinction on one half (the name of the goddess in this shrine is vaṭivammai).
mātarai maiyal ceyvār
he will make ladies infatuated with love.
{3:92}__7+
{$} varitaru vaṇṭu paṇmuralum cōlait tikku elām (1) pukaḻuṟum tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
the Lord who dwells in tirunelvēli which is praised by the people from all directions and which has gardens in which the bees with lines on their bodies hum melody-types.
aṅku (2) ulām araiyiṉar
has chank beads which move in his waist.
tirai (3) ulām muṭiyiṉar
has on his head water which is moving (tirai: ākupeyar)
aṭikaḷ aṉṟu takkaṉār vēḷviyaic cāṭiya caturaṉār
the able person who destroyed the sacrifice performed by takkaṉ long ago (takkaṉār: ār is added to denigrate him)
katir koḷ cemmai pukkatu ōr puriviṉar
has a twisted caṭai which has the red colour of the rising sun.
[[variant readings : (1) pukaḻvuṟum (2) ulāvaraiyiṉar (3) kulā]]
{3:92}__8+
{$} munti mā vilaṅkal aṉṟu eṭuttavaṉ muṭikaḷ tōḷ neritaravē unti māmalaraṭi oruviral ukir nutiyāl aṭarttār
Civaṉ pressed down with the tip of the nail of a single toe in the foot resembling a great flower, thrusting it forward to crush the heads and shoulders of Irāvaṇaṉ who lifted the great mountain on that day, marching forward.
kantam ārtaru poḻil mantikaḷ pāytara matuttivalai cintupūntuṟai kamaḻ tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
is the Lord who dwells in tirunelvēli where the drops of honey spill in the ghat as the female monkeys jump in the gardens filled with fragrance, and spread everywhere.
{3:92}__9+
{$} paiṅkaṇ vāḷ aravuaṇaiyāṉoṭu paṉimalarōṉum (1) kāṇā! aṅkaṇā! aruḷ' eṉa avar avar muṟaimuṟai iṟaiñca niṉṟār
Civaṉ remained to be worshipped by Māl who reclines in a serpent-bed whose eyes are fuming with anger, and Piramaṉ who is in a cool (lotus) flower, in turns praying gracious eyed Civaṉ, grant us your grace as they could not see his head and feet.
caṅkam nāṉmaṟaiyavar niṟaitara ariyavaiyar āṭal pēṇat tiṅkaḷ nāḷ viḻa malku tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
is the Lord who dwells in tirunelvēli where monthly and daily festivals are on the increase, where crowds of brahmins who have learnt the four vētams fill the place, and ladies dance.
[[variant readings : (1) kāṇātaṅkaṇā]]
{3:92}__10+
{$} tuvar uṟuviritukil āṭaiyar vēṭam il camaṇar eṉṉumavar uṟu ciṟucolai avam eṉa niṉaiyum em aṇṇalār (tām)
our deity who considers the words of contempt spoken by buddhists who cover their bodies with a cloth soaked in red ochre and camaṇar who do not wear any dress as useless words.
kavar uṟu koṭi malku māḷikaic cūḷikai mayil ālattivar uṟu matitavaḻ tirunelvēli uṟai celvar (tām, ē)
is the Lord who dwells in tirunelvēli where the moon which moves in the heaven, crawls in the mansions where in the open terrace the flags which attract the attention of people are increasing, and the peacocks dance (cūḷikai: māṭamali cūḷikaiyilēṟi (campantar, tiruppaḻuvur 2))
{3:92}__11+
{$} peruntaṇmāmalarmicai ayaṉ (avaṉ) aṉaiyavar
brahmins who are equal to Ayaṉ (Piramaṉ) who is seated in a big great and cool flower.
pēṇu kalvit tiruntu mā maṟaiyavar tirunelvēli uṟai celvar tammai
on the Lord who dwells in tirunelvēli where great brahmins who have erudition which is appreciated by all, are living.
nīr poruntu taṭamalku pukaliyuḷ ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa aruntamiḻmālaikaḷ pāṭi āṭak keṭum aruviṉai (ē)
the acts which are difficult to be removed will perish if one sings the precious garlands of verses composed by Ñāṉacampantaṉ of Pukali which has many tanks filled with water, and dance out of joy.

{1:1}__1+
{$} tōṭu uṭaiya ceviyaṉ
the Lord has an ear on which a lady's ear-jewel is worn.
viṭai ēṟi
He rides on a bull.
ōr tūveṇmaticūṭi
having worn a spotlessly pure white crescent moon of a single phase.
kāṭu uṭaiya cuṭalaip poṭi pūci
He smeared himself with the ash in the cremation ground which has the nature of a forest.
eṉ uḷḷam kavar kaḷvaṉ
the thief who has captivated my mind
[[Variant reading: eṉatuḷḷam kavar]]
ēṭu uṭaiya malarāṉ muṉai nāṭpaṇintu ētta aruḷ ceyta pīṭu uṭaiya piramāpuram mēviya pemmāṉ ivaṉ aṉṟē
this person is really the great one who resides gladly in Piramapuram possessing greatness, where the Lord bestowed his grace on Piramaṉ who is seated in a (lotus) flower having petals, who bowed to him and worshipped him, in the distant past.
[[Cīkāli has twelve name of which Piramapuram is one; as Piramaṉ worshipped the Lord, with his consort, the goddess of learning it got the name of Piramapuram.]]
[[Piramāpuram: the long letter is used to suit the metre. The fact that Piramaṉ worshipped the Lord in Cīkāḻi is mentioned in other decades of Ñāṉacampantar;
ōma moṭuyar maṟai piṟaviya vakaitaṉō ṭoḷikeḻu, pūmakaṉ alaroṭu puṉal koṭu vaḻipaṭu puṟavamē (Tiruppuṟavam (3) 5;)
nā iyalum maṅkaiyoṭu nāṉmukaṉtāṉ vaḻipaṭṭa nalaṅkoḷ kōyil (Tirukkaḻumalam (4) 1);
curuticira vuraiyiṉāl piramaṉ uyaṉāl, piramaṉ uyar araṉ eḻil koḷ caraṇa iṇai paravavaḷar piramapuramē (vaḻimoḻit tiruvirākam, 1)]]
[[Many scholars have written elaborate commentaries on this one stanza. The circumstances in which this patikam was sung is described in detil in Periya Purāṇam, Tiruñāṉacampantar Purāṇam, verses 53-80]]

{$} muṟṟal āmai iḷa nākamoṭu ēṉa muṉaikkompu avai pūṇṭu
having adorned himself with the mature shell of an aged tortoise, a young cobra and the horn of full growth of a pig.
vaṟṟal ōṭu kalaṉāppali tērntu eṉatu uḷḷam kavar kaḷvaṉ
the thief who captivated my mind, having collected alms in a bowl of skull from which the flesh has completely disappeared.
kaṟṟal kēṭṭal uṭaiyār periyār kaḻal kaiyāl toḻutu ētta
The great people who have learning and knowledge acquired by listening to words of wisdom worship with folded hands and prise him.
peṟṟam ūrnta piramāpuram mēviya pemmāṉ ivaṉ aṉaṟē
This person is the Lord who rides on a bull and gladly resides in Piramāpuram
[[ūrnta pemmāṉ]]
{1:1}__3+
{$} nīr paranta nimir puṉcaṭai mēl ōr nilā veṇmati cūṭi
having adorned a white crescent moon that gives light, on the golden matted locks of hir standing upright on which the water has spread.
ērparanta iṉa veḷvaḷai cōra eṉ uḷḷam kavar kaḷvaṉ
the thief who captivated my mind, to make the group of white and beautiful bangles, to slip off my hand.
ūr paranta ulakil mutalākiya ōr ūr itu eṉṉappēr paranta piramāpuram mēviya pemmāṉ ivaṉ aṉṟē
this person is truly the Lord residing gladly in Piramāpuram which has a fame spread far and wide as a place which is like a seed in the world which has many holy places.

{$} viṇ makiḻnta matil eytatum aṉṟi
in addition to having discharged an arrow on the forts which delighted in flying in the sky.
[[The three forts were made of gold, silver and iron; amarar oṉṉār, irumpuṟu māmatil, poṉ iñci, veḷḷip puricai aṉṟu ōr, turumpuṟac ceṟṟa koṟṟattu empirāṉ (Kōviyar 167)]]
viḷaṅkutalai ōṭṭil uṇmakiḻntu pali tēriya vantu eṉatu uḷḷamkavar kaḷvaṉ
the thief who captivated my mind, by coming to me in order to collect alms with gladness to heart, in the bright skull.
maṇ makiḻnta aravam malark koṉṟai malintavarai mārpil
in the chest comparable to the mountin where there are abundant koṉṟi flowers and cobras which live in ant-hills desirous of them.
[[maṇ makiḻnta (aravam) is an attribute common to the species]]

This person is truly the Lord who resides gladly in Piramāpuram and rejoices in having a lady (in the chest)

{$} aruneṟiya maṟaivalla muṉi alar mēya akaṉ poykai peruneṟiya, piramāpuram mēviya, pemmāṉ ivaṉ taṉṉai
about this person who gladly resides in Piramāpuram who can lead one in the path of obtining eternal bliss, that Piramāpuram which has a natural tank of lotus flowers deriving its name from Piramaṉ who is well versed in the Maṟi (Vētam) which has lid down many difficult ways of reaching god.

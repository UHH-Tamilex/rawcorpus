umaiyāḷ olka urakkaḷiṟṟai urittavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ flayed a strong male elephant to make Umiyāḷ tremble with fear.
ōṅkārattu oruvaṉ
is the unequalled one who is the meaning of ōmkāram.
uṇar meyññāṉam virittavaṉ (kāṇ)
expounded the objects which can be known only by true knowledge.
viritta nāl vētattāṉ (kāṇ)
has the four vētam-s with which he expounded those things (he is the chief of the vētams)
viyaṉ ulakil palluyirai vitiyiṉālē terittavaṉ (kāṇ)
created many living beings in the wide world according to their predestined acts.
cil uruvāyt tōṉṟi eṅkum tiraṇṭavaṉ (kāṇ)
accumulated himself as a big thing, having come into existence as small forms.
tiripurattai vēva villāl erittavaṉ (kāṇ)
burnt with a bow the three cities, the fire to blaze.
eḻil ..... eṇṇattāṉē
(see 1st verse in the previous decade)
Is the the Lord in Ēkampam of Kacci which has many gardens full of beauty; he is always in my thoughts;
[[All the kāṇ in this verse and the following verses are to be treated as expletives.]]
[[eḻil-eṇṇattāṉē
the finishing part of the verses in this as well as the previous decade are in the form of a refrin]]

nēcarkku nēcaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ loves those who cherish him with love.
nēcam taṉpāl illāta neñcattu nīcar tammaik kūcaṉ (kāṇ)
feels shy to approach the low people who have no love towards him in their hearts.
kūcātār neñcu tañcē kuṭi koṇṭa kuḻakaṉ (kāṇ)
is the youth who occupies the hearts of those who do not feel shy to worship him, easily.
[[tañcu-tañcam easy of approach;
tañcak kiḷavi eṇmaip poruṭṭē Tolkappiyam, iṭi iyal, 18, Rajan edition 1960]]
aḻaku ār koṉṟai vācaṉ (kāṇ)
wears a beautiful and fragrant garland of koṉṟi.
malai maṅkai paṅkaṉ (tāṉ-kāṇ)
has as a half the dughter of the mountin.
vāṉavarkaḷ eppoḻutum vaṇaṅki ēttum īcaṉ (kāṇ)
- is the Lord of the universe to whom the celestials pay homage always and prise him.

poṟaiyavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ is the embodiment of patience.
pūmi ēḻ tāṅki ōṅkum puṇṇiyaṉ (kāṇ)
is the personification of all virtuous acts and rises in esteem by supporting the seven worlds.
puṇṭarīkap pōtil naṇṇiya maṟaiyavaṉ (kāṇ)
is the brahmin (Piramaṉ) who is seated in the lotus flower.
maṟaiyavaṉaip payantōṉ (tāṉ-kāṇ)
is Māl who begot the brahmin (Piramaṉ)
vār caṭai mācuṇam aṇintu vaḷarum piḷḷaip piṟaiyavaṉ kāṇ
has on his long caṭi a cobra and a waxing crescent
piṟai tikaḻum eyiṟṟup pēḻvāyp pēyōṭu aṅku iṭukāṭṭil elli āṭum iṟaiyavaṉ (kāṇ)
is the leader among gods who dances at night in the burial ground along with pēys which have large mouths adn teeth which are like the crescent in shape and colour.
eḻil ārum ..... eṇṇattāṉē
see 1st verse.

vicumpu avaṉ (kāṇ)

paṉi varaikaḷ iraviṉoṭu pakalāy niṉṟa cīravaṉ (kāṇ)

ticaiyavaṉ (kāṇ)

ticaikaḷ eṭṭum ceṟintavaṉ (kāṇ)
he pervades all the eight directions.
ciṟanta aṭiyār cintai ceyyum pēravaṉ (kāṇ)

pēr āyiraṅkaḷ ēttum periyavaṉ (kāṇ)
is the great god who has thousand names with which the devotees prise him.
ariyavaṉ (kāṇ)
is difficult to approach those who do not love him.
peṟṟam ūrnta ēravaṉ (kāṇ)

peruntavattu em piññakaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ has the form for performing penance and an adornment of a peacock's feather on his head.
piṟai cūṭi (kāṇ)
wears a crescent.
pētaiyēṉ vātaiyuṟu piṇiyait tīrkkum maruntu avaṉ (kāṇ)
is the cure which removes the diseases which inflict sufferings on me who is ignorant.
mantiraṅkaḷ āyiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is the many mantirams.
vāṉavarkaḷ tām vaṇaṅkum mātēvaṉ (kāṇ)
is the great god to whom the celestials pay homage.
aruntavattāḷ āyiḻaiyāḷ umaiyāḷ pākam amarntavaṉ (kāṇ)
desired the half of Umiyāl adorned with choice ornaments, who performed severe penance.
amararkaḷ tām arccittu ētta iruntāṉ (kāṇ)
remined to be worshipped and prised by the immortals.

mūvariṉum mutal āṉāṉ (kāṇ)

mū ilai vēl mūrtti (kāṇ)
the god of visible form who has a trident of three blades.

Civaṉ is our holy god who has a twisted caṭi which resembles gold;
[[cf. poṉṉum maṇiyum pavaḷamum pōlap polintilaṅki miṉṉum caṭaiyōṉ (Kōviyar, 189)]]
pūtakaṇa nātaṉ (kāṇ)
is the master of the groups of pūtams.
pulittōl āṭai taṉ icaiya vaitta eḻil araviṉāṉ (kāṇ)
has a beautiful cobra which he placed fit for the dress of a tiger's skin.
caṅka veṇkuḻaik kātiṉ 1. caturaṉ (tāṉ-kāṇ)
is the skilled god who wears in his ear a men's ear-ring made of white conch.
miṉ icaiyum veḷḷeyiṟṟōṉ vekuṇṭu veṟpai eṭukka, aṭi aṭarppa mīṇṭu avaṉ taṉ vāyil iṉṉicai kēṭṭu ilaṅku oḷivāḷ īntōṉ
gave a sword glittering with brightness after hearing sweet music as aginst pressing him down with his feet when the arakkaṉ of white teeth resembling lightning lifted the mountin Kayili out of anger.

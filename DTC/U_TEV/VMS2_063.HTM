This shrine is known by the name of Aḻakār Puttūr and Tirupputtūr

{2:63}__1+
{$} miṉṉum caṭaimēl iḷa veṇ tiṅkaḷ viḷaṅkavē
when the white crescent is shining on the glittering caṭi.
tuṉṉum kaṭal nañcu iruḷ tōy kaṇṭar toṉmūtūr
the very ancient place of Civaṉ who has a neck in which the black colour of the poison which rose in the ocean where many valuable things were crowded.
aṉṉam paṭiyum puṉal ār aricil āṉai koṇṭu
the river aricil in whose water the swans bathe, with the help of its waves.
peṉṉum maṇiyum poru teṉkarai mēṟ puttūr
is Puttūr on the southern bank on which gold and precious stones are dashed
{2:63}__2+
{$} mēvā acurar mēvu eyil vēva malaivillāl, ēvu ār eri veṅkaṇaiyāl eytāṉ eytum ūr
the place where Civaṉ who shot a cruel arrow of fire from the bow of a mountin to burn the forts of the acurar who did not live Civaṉ, dwells.
nāvāl nātaṉ nāmam ōti nāḷ tōṟum pūvāl nīrāl pūcurar pōṟṟum puttūrē
is puttūr where the brahmins who are considered as the tevar living on the earth, worship Civaṉ with flowers and water, uttering the Lord`s names in their mouths.
{2:63}__3+
{$} pal ār talai cēr mālai cūṭi
wearing on the head a garland of skulls with all the teeth intact.
pāmpum pūṇṭu
having adorned himself with cobras also.
ellā iṭamum veṇṇīṟu aṇintu
having smeared all over the body sacred white ash.
ōr ēṟu ēṟi
-and riding on a bull.
kal ār maṅkai pākarēṉum
though he has on one half a dughter of the mountin.
kāṇuṅkāl
if he think about him.
pollār allar
he is not ugly to look at.
aḻakiyar puttūrp puṉitarē
the pure Civaṉ in puttūr is beautiful.
{2:63}__4+
{$} vari ēr vaḷaiyāḷ arivai añca varukiṉṟa kari ēr urivai pōrtta kaṭavuḷ karutum ūr
the place which the god, Civaṉ who covered himself with the beautiful skin of an elephant which came aginst him, to make the lady wearing bangles with beautiful lines, tremble with fear, desires to dwell.
ari ēr kaḻaṉip paḻaṉam cūḻntu aṅku aḻakāya pori ēr puṉku coriyum cōlaip puttūrē
-is puttūr where in the gardens the indian beech tree sheds its beautiful flowers resembling tried paddy and which is surrounded by tanks and fields where corn stalks are heeped.
{2:63}__5+
{$} eṉpōṭu aravam ēṉattu eyiṟōṭu eḻil āmai, miṉpōṟpuri nūl viravippūṇṭa (1) maṇimārpar
Civaṉ who adorns his chest with bones, a cobra, a hog's tusk, the beautiful shell of a tortoise and a sacred thread of three strands glittering like lightning, all of them combining together.
aṉpōṭu urukum aṭiyārkku aṉpar amarum ūr
the place which is desired by him who loves his devotees whose hearts melt out of live.
poṉpōtu alar kōṅku ōṅku cōlaip puttūr
is Puttūr where in the gardens common cung which has flowers like gold in colour, grow high.
[[Variant reading: (1) varaimārpar]]
{2:63}__6+
{$} vaḷḷi mulai tōy kumaraṉ tātai
the father of Kumaraṉ (Murukaṉ) who embraces the breasts of Vaḷḷi;
[[kuṟavi tōḷ maṇanta celvakkumaravēḷ tātai- (nāvukkaracar, peruvēḷūr 3);
nam centil mēya vaḷḷi maṇāḷaṟkuttātaikaṇṭāy- (Do. maṟikkāṭu, taṇṭakam, 4)]]
vāṉtōyum veḷḷimalaipōl viṭai oṉṟu uṭaiyāṉ mēvum ūr
the ple which Civaṉ who has a bull resembling the silver mountin (Kayili) which seems to touch the sky, desires.
[[veḷḷi malai aṉṉa māl viṭaiyōṉ- (Koviyār, 128)]]
teḷḷi varu nīr aricil teṉpāl ciṟaivaṇṭum puḷḷum malipūm poykai cūḻnta puttūr
is puttūr surrounded by natural tank which abound in flowers, birds, and winged bees and which is situated on the southern side of the river, aricil which brings clear water.
{2:63}__7+
{$} ūr
the place.
nilam taṇīrōṭu aṉal kāl vicumpiṉ nīrmaiyāṉ
of Civaṉ who has the nature of the earth, fire, air and sky.
cilanti 1. ceṅkaṭ cōḻaṉākac ceytāṉ
and who made the spider as the red-eyed colaṉ (ceṅkaṭcolaṉ).
alanta aṭiyāṉ aṟṟaikku aṉṟu ōr kācu eyti
the devotee who suffered privation having received, from day to day, a coin.
pularnta kālai mālai pōṟṟum puttūr
is puttūr where he worshipped Civaṉ in the morning when day breaks and in the evening.
[[The incident mentioned by campantar is mentioned with more detils by cuntarar in his verse on the same shrine;
akattaṭimai ceyyum antaṇaṉ tāṉ ari ciṟ puṉal koṇṭu vantu āṭṭu kiṉṟāṉ, mikattaḷarvu eytik kuṭattai num muṭimēl viḻuttiṭṭu naṭuṅkutalum, vakuttu avaṉukku nittaṟ paṭiyum varum eṉṟu oru kāciṉai niṉṟa niṉṟip, pukaḻttuṇai kaippukac ceytu ukantīr, poḻil ār tirupputtūrp puṉitarē (cuntarar, 6)]]
[[Variant reading: 1. ceṅkōṟ]]
{2:63}__8+
{$} 'ittēr 1. ēka immalai pōrppaṉ' eṉṟu ēntum pattu ōrvāyāṉ varaikkīḻ alaṟa
(the arakkaṉ) who had ten mouths and who lifted Kayili saying `I shall remove this mountin for my chariot to fly without any obstacle', to roar under that mountin.
pātam tāṉ vaittu ār aruḷ cey varataṉ maruvum ūr āṉa puttūr kāṉappukuvār viṉaikaḷ pōkumē
the Karmams of those who go to visit puttūr where the god who grants boon, granted his valuable grace, after fixing his leg firmly (to crush him), will go away from them.
[[Variant reading: 1. pōka]]
{2:63}__9+
{$} muḷ ār kamalattu ayaṉ māl muṭiyōṭu aṭitēṭa
Ayaṉ who is in a lotus which has on its stem thorns, and Māl to search for the head and feet.
oḷ ār eriyāy uṇartaṟku ariyāṉ ūr (pōlum)
the place of Civaṉ who could not be known by them as he rose as a column of bright fire.
kaḷ ār neytal kaḻunīr āmpal kamalaṅkaḷ puḷ ār poykaip pūppala tōṉṟum puttūr
is puttūr which has in its natural ponds birds and many flowers like the white indian water lily, purple indian water lily, red indian water lily and lotus, blossoming.
{2:63}__10+
{$} collu
the words of.
kai ār cōṟu kavar kuṇṭarkaḷum
the low people, camanar who eat with avidity the food keeping it in their hands.
tuvar uṇṭa pōrvai meyyār maṇṭaiyar mey alla
and the buddhists who have an earthern vessel resembling in shape skull and who cover their bodies with a blanket soaked in myrtle-dye, do not contin any truth.
poyyā moḻiyāl antaṇar pōṟṟum puttūril aiyā eṉpārkku aiyuṟavu iṉṟi aḻaku ām
brilliance of the body will come without doubt to those who say, `the father in puttur where the brahmins worship you with the words of the vētams which never become false'.
{2:63}__11+
{$} poṟi koḷ aravam pūṇṭāṉ āṇṭa puttūr mēl
on puttur in which Civaṉ who adorned himself with cobras having spots, ruled.
naṟavam kamaḻ pūṅkāḻi ñāṉacampantaṉ ceṟivaṇ tamiḻ ceymālai ceppavallārkaḷ
those who can recite the garland of fruitful verses contining many meanings composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ of beautiful Kāḻi where the fragrance of honey spreads.
aṟavaṉ kaḻal cērntu aṉpōṭu iṉpam aṭaivār
will reach the feet of Civaṉ who is the embodiment of all virtues, and derive love and happiness.

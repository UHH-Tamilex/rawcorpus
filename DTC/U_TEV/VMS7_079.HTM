māṉum marai iṉamum mayil iṉamum kalantu eṅkum tāmē mika mēyntu
the deer, herd of indian elk and flocks of peacocks miring together everywhere they themselves having eaten their food sumptuously according to their desire.
taṭañcuṉai nīrkaḷaip paruki
drinking the water in several big mountin springs.
pū māmaram uriñci
rubbing their bodies aginst the big blossomed trees.
poḻilūṭē ceṉṟu
going through the denseness of those trees.
pukku
entering.
tēmām poḻil nīḻal tuyil cīparppatamalaiyē
the mountin called Cīparppatam is the mountin of Civaṉ, where in the shade of the sweet mango-groves, they sleep.

malaiccāralum poḻiṟcāralum puṟamē varum iṉaṅkaḷ malaippāṟ koṇarntu
catching the herds of elephants that come from beyond the sides of the mountins and the gardens.
iṭittu ūṭṭiṭa
feed them reproving them sharply.
mayaṅkit tam (1) kaḷiṟṟai aḻaittu ōṭiyum
the female elephants being perturbed in mind run in search of their males uttering a loud cry.
piḷiṟī alamantu vantu eyttu
roar, get confounded return back exhusted (piḷiṟī-piḷiṟi the lengthening of the letter is for rhythm)
tikaittu ōṭi
run not knowing what to do.
tampiṭi (1) tēṭiṭum cīparppatamalaiyē
cīparpparpatamali is the place of Civaṉ where the female elephants search for their male elephants.
[[Variant reading: (1) taṉakaḷiṟṟai (2) piṭittēṭiṭum]]

puṉam kāval maṭamoḻiyāḷ maṉṉip puṉam kākka
the girls who speak words of simplicity whose duty is to keep over the millet-farm to do that duty without a break.
kaṉṉik kiḷi vantu kavaik kōlik katirkoyya
when the young parrots come and take away the ears of corn which sprout from the forked stem.
eṉṉaik kiḷi matiyātu eṉṟu kavaṇ eṭuttu olippa
when she makes a sound with the sling, saying these parrots do not pay heed to my words.
(1) teḷ naṟkiḷi tirintu ēṟiya cīparppata malaiyē
cīparppatamali where the beautiful and good parrots change their minds and fly from the farm, is the place of our Civaṉ.
[[Variant reading: (1) teṉṉakkiḷi]]

mai ār taṭaṅkaṇṇāḷ maṭamoḻiyāḷ puṉaṅkākka
when the girls of beautiful words and big eyes coated with collyriyam guard the millet farm.
cevvē tirintu
wandering well all over the farm.
āyō eṉap (1) pōkāviṭa viḷintu
getting angry as they did not leave the farm by uttering the word, ayō (āyō): exclamatory expression intended to scare away birds from preying on the ripe corn and used by the women folk of the hilly tracts who usually guard millet fields on the mountin sides,
[[veñcoṟ pēcum vēṭā maṭavār itaṇam atu ēṟi, añcoṟ kiḷikaḷ āyō eṉṉum aṇṇāmalaiyārē (campantar, tiruvaṇṇāmali (2) 3)]]
kai paraviya kavaṇāl maṇi eṟiya irintu ōṭic cevvāyaṉa kiḷipāṭiṭum cīparppatamalaiyē
cīparppatamali where the parrots of red beak repeat that word fleeing in all directions when those girls throw gems from the slings which are held in the hands.
[[Variant reading: (1) pōkāviṭam viḷintu]]

āṉaikkulam
the herd of elephants.
taṉpiṭi
and the herd of their female elephants
cūḻalil tiriya
when they wandered in many places the slopes.
irintu ōṭi
and run helter skelter.
tāṉappiṭi cevi tāḻttiṭa
the female elephants droop their ears to listen attentively, as they could not see the herd of male elephants.
ataṟku mika iraṅki
taking much pity for that state.
māṉak kuṟa aṭal vēṭarkaḷ ilaiyāl kalai kōli
the hunters who belong to the strong and heroic Kuṟavar tribe make plates of leaves seen together like a cup.
tēṉaip piḻintu iṉitu ūṭṭiṭum cīparppata malaiyē
cīrparppatamali where the feed them with pleasure, with honey squeezed from the honey-combs and pouring it into them, is the place of Civaṉ.

matavēḻam
a male elephant of flowing must.
kai eṭuttu
rising its trunk.
māṟṟuk kaḷiṟu aṭaintāy eṉṟu
saying to its female elephants you had union with a different male elephant.
taḻal umiḻntum
scattering from its eyes sparks of five denoting anger.
(1) mūṟṟi matam poḻintum
pouring the kinds of fluid of must
[[mūṟṟi is derived from mūṉṟu
nūṟṟup pattu nuvaṉṟa tōlmukaṉ mūṟṟaik kaiyiṉaṉ (Kantapurāṇam, Kayamukaṉ vatippaṭalam, 2);
mukam cuḻiya
to contract its face in disgust.
(2) tūṟṟat tarikkillēṉ eṉṟu colli
the female elephant telling its male I could not brook this injust accusation.
ayal aṟiyat tēṟṟi
clarifying its fidelity to be known to others who are near at hand.
piṭi kuḷaṟum cīparppatamalaiyē
Cīparppatamali where the female elephants swears and join its male, is the place of Civaṉ

appōtu vantu uṇṭīrkaḷukku aḻaiyātu muṉ iruntēṉ
I was inactive without uttering a loud cry to scare away you who came before and ate the ears of corn'
eppōtum vantu uṇṭāl emai emarkaḷ (1) cuḻiyārō
will not our relatives be angry with us if you come always and eat the ears of corn?
ippōtu umakku (2) ituvē toḻil eṉṟu ōṭi
running saying now this is the thing that I should do.
akkiḷiyaic ceppu ēntu iḷamulaiyāḷ ēṟi cīparppatamalaiyē
cīparppatamali where the young girls with rised and young breasts hits with stones hurled from the sling those parrots, is the place of Civaṉ.
[[Variant readings: (1) cuḷiyārō (2) ituvō]]

tiriyum puram nīṟu ākkiya celvaṉ taṉ kaḻalai ariya tirumālōṭu ayaṉ tāṉum (m) avar aṟiyār
tirumāl who is difficult to be approached by others and Ayaṉ (Piramaṉ) do not know the feet (and head) of the god who reduced to ashes the wandering cities.
kariyiṉ iṉamōṭum piṭi tēṉ uṇṭavai kaḷittut tiritantivai tikaḻvāl poli cīparppatamalaiyē
cīparppatamali is the place of Civaṉ where the female elephants drinking the honey along with the herd of male elephants wander with joy; it shines with the brilliance of those elephants.

ēṉattiraḷ kiḷaikka eripōla maṇi citaṟa
as the wild hog roots up, the gems scatter like fire.
ēṉal(l) avai malaiccāralil tiriyum karaṭīyum māṉum marai iṉamum mayil maṟṟum pala ellām
the bears which flee from the slopers of the mountin where millet is grown, deer, herd of indian elk, peacock and all other living beings (karaṭī-karaṭi the lengthening of the letter is for rhythm).
tēṉ uṇ poḻil cōlai miku cīparppatamalaiyē
cīparppatamali where they drink the honey in flower gardens and other gardens which are in plenty, is his hill.
[[Variant reading: (1) tīyeṉṟavai]]

nallār avar pāl vāḻtaru vayal nāval ūraṉ
nampirārūraṉ, a native of nāvalūr which has fertile fields and where many good people live.
cellal uṟal ariya cīparppata malaiyai
about the hill, cīparppatam which is difficult to be reached.
allal (avai) tīrac coṉa tamiḻ mālaikaḷ vallār
those who are able to recite the garlands of tamiḻ which he composed to remove the sufferings of devotees.
ollai cela uyar vāṉakam āṇṭu aṅku iruppārē
in a short time, they will rule over the heaven and dwell there.

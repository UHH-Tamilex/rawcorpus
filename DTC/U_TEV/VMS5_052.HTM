tirunākēccaravar
Civaṉ in Tirunākēccaram
nallar
is good god.
nallatu ōr nākam koṇṭu āṭṭuvar
will cuse the good cobra to dance, holding it in his hand.
valviṉai tīrkkum maruntukaḷ vallavar
is capable of administering remedies that can remove the irresistible Karmams.
pal il ōṭu kai ēnti
holding a teethless skull
pali tiri celvar (pōl)
is a person having wealth of grace, to wander for alms.
[[pōl: expletive; this applies to the following verses.]]

tirunākēccaravar
Civaṉ in Tirunākēccaram
nāval am peruntīviṉil vāḻpavar mēvi vantu vaṇaṅki
when the inhabitants of (India), the island that got its name from the jumoon plum tree which grows here, come with love and bow to him
viṉaiyoṭu pāvam āyiṉa paṟṟu aṟuvittiṭum tēvar (pōl)
is the tēvar who cuses by his grace to have the bondage of karmam-s and sins cut at their root completely

ōtam ār kaṭaliṉ viṭam uṇṭavar

cantira(ṉ)ṉoṭu cūriyar tām uṭaṉ vantu cīr vaḻi pāṭukaḷ ceyta piṉ
after the moon and the twelve suns came here and performed their famous worship
aintalai araviṉ paṇi koṇṭaruḷ maintar (pōl)
is the strong person who accepted the service and worship of the cobra with five heads

māṉai ēntiya kaiyiṉar
holds in his hand a deer

kuḷir nākēccaravar
Civaṉ in cool nākēccaram
kaḻal koḷ kāliṉar
wears on his legs an armour of heores.
kālaṉaik kāyantavar
destroyed the god of death.
taḻal koḷ mēṉiyar
has a form like fire
cānta veṇṇīṟu aṇi aḻakar
is a beautiful person who smears himself with the holy white ash as sandal paste
āl niḻaṟ kīḻ aṟam ōtiya kuḻakar (pōl)
is the youth who gave religious instruction under the shade of the banyan tree.

Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram
vaṭṭam māmatil mūṉṟuṭaṉ vallaraṇ cuṭṭa ceykaiyar ākilum
though he burnt the strong fortifications including the great circular walls of enclosure of the three cities.

is the noble person who makes the minds of people who meditate on him, free from diseases like leprosy and other irresistible Karmams, which are its effect, and consoles them

Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram
tūrttaṉ tōḷ muṭi tāḷum tolaiyac cērttiṉār tiruppātattu oruviral
fixed one toe to destroy the heads and shoulders and feet of the debuchee (iravaṇaṉ)

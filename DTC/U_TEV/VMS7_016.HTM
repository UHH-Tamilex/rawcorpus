{7:16}__1+
{$} kurumpai mulai malarkkuḻalikoṇṭatavam kaṇṭu
knowing the penance undertaken by Umi who had breasts like immature cocoanuts, and tresses of hir adorned with flowers.
kuṟippiṉoṭum ceṉṟu
going to that place of penance with the intention of marrying her.
avaḷ taṉ kuṇattiṉai naṉku aṟintu
understanding her nature well after testing her.
virumpum varam koṭuttu
granting the boons she desired.
avaḷai vēṭṭaruḷicceyta viṇṇavarkōṉ
the chief of the celestials who married her.
kaṇṇutalōṉ mēviya ūrviṉavil
if one asks about the place where Civaṉ with a frontal eye, dwells.
arumpu arukēcurumpu aruva
as the kind of bee, curupu, gently afflict the mature buds.
[[aivar eṉṉuḷpukuntu oḻiyātu aruvittiṉṟiṭa, ūṉpotinaramputōlkurampaiyuḷpukku aruvinōyceytu (periya tirumoḻi, 7-7-1, 9-7-6)]]
aṟupatam paṇpāṭa
the beetle with six legs sings melody-types.
aṇimayilkaḷ naṭamāṭum aṇipoḻil cūḻayaliṉ
by the side of the gardens that lend beauty where lovely peacocks dance using them as the stage.
karumpu arukē karuṅkuvaḷai kaṇvaḷarum kaḻaṉikkamalaṅkaḷ mukam malarum kalaya nallūrkāṇ
is Kalayanallūr where lotus blossoms like faces in the fields where the blue nelumbo by the side of sugar-canes close their petals as if they are in sleep (kāṇ expletive in all the verses).
{7:16}__2+
{$} cerumēvucalantaraṉaippiḷantacuṭar āḻiceṅkaṇ malarpaṅkayamāc ciṟantāṉukku aruḷi
granting the brilliant discus which cut calantaraṉ into two who desired battle, to Māl who lid his red eye which is like lotus, at the feet of Civaṉ.
iruḷ mēvum antakaṉmēl tiricūlampāycci
thrusting the trident of three blades into the body of antakācuraṉ who was like darkness.
intiraṉaittōḷmuritta iṟaiyavaṉ ūrviṉavil
if one asks about the place of the Lord who broke off the shoulders of intiraṉ
perumētai maṟai oliyum
the sound of vētams which grants supreme intelligence.
pērimuḻavuoliyum
the sound of pēri and muḻavu.
piḷḷai iṉam tuḷḷiviḷaiyāṭṭu oliyum peruka
and the sound of groups of children who jump and play, are increasing
karumētipuṉalmaṇṭa
as the black buffalo dives into the water.
kayalmaṇṭa
the Kayal fish fall on the lotus in a group.
kamalam kaḷivaṇṭiṉ kaṇam iriyum kalaya nallūr kāṇ
is Kalayanallūr where the swarm of intoxicated bees scatter in all directions from the lotus flowers.
{7:16}__3+
{$} iṇṭaimalarkoṇṭu
with a circlet of flowers and loose flowers.
maṇal iliṅkam atu iyaṟṟi
improvising an iliṅkam from the sand of the river maṇṇi.
iṉattu āviṉpāl āṭṭa
bathing with milk got from the herd of cows.
iṭaṟiya tātaiyaittāḷ tuṇṭam iṭu caṇṭi aṭi aṇṭar toḻutu ētta
the tēvar to worship and prise the feet of caṇṭi who cut into two the feet of his father who kicked the milk-pots.
toṭarntu avaṉaip paṇikoṇṭa viṭaṅkaṉatu ūrviṉavil
if one asks about the place of the beautiful god who admitted caṇṭi into his grace.
maṇṭapamum kōpuramum māḷikai cūḷikaiyum
in the halls public tower-gates, mansions and open terraces intended for enjoying moonlight
maṟaioliyum viḻavu oliyum maṟuku niṟaivu eyti
the sound of chanting vetams, and the bustle of festivals filling the streets.
kaṇṭavarkaḷ maṉam kavarum puṇṭarikap poykaik kārikaiyār kuṭaintu āṭum kalaya nallūr kāṇ
is Kalayanallūr where beautiful ladies bathe diving into the natural ponds where lotus flowers captivate the minds of people who look at them.
{7:16}__4+
{$} malaimaṭantai viḷaiyāṭi vaḷai āṭu karattāl (1) makiḻntavaḷ kaṇputaittalumē
as soon as the dughter of the mountin playing with Civaṉ close out of joy the eyes in the face by her hands wearing bangles.
valliruḷāy ellā ulakuṭaṉ tāṉmūṭa
when impervious darkness enveloped all the worlds.
iruḷ ōṭumvakai neṟṟioṟṟaikkaṇ paṭaittuukanta uttamaṉ ūrviṉavil
if one asks the about the place of the supreme god who became eminent by creating a frontal eye in order to cuse the darkness of flee away.
alaiaṭainta puṉalperuki
swollen with floods having waves
yāṉai maruppu iṭaṟi
stumbling upon the tusk of elephants.
akiloṭu cantu untivarum ariciliṉ teṉkaraimēl
on the southern bank of the river, aricil which pushes eagle wood with sandalwood.
kalai aṭaintu kalikaṭi antaṇar ōmappukaiyāl
by the smoke issuing from the sacrifices performed by brahmins who drove away poverty by being well-versed in arts.
kaṇamukil pōṉṟa aṇikiḷarum kalayanallūr kāṇ
is Kalayanallūr where the mass of smoke shoot up like the beauty of a collection of clouds (pōṉṟaṇi=pōṉṟa aṇi)
[[Variant reading: (1) makiḻntavaṉ]]

{$} niṟpāṉum kamalattil iruppāṉum mutalā amarar niṟaintu kuṟaintu irappa niṉaintaruḷi
thinking of their sufferings in his mind when the immortals beginning from Māl who is in a standing posture and Piramaṉ who is seated in a lotus, petitioned reduced in their greatness to redness their grievance, filling his presence to its utmost capacity
[[Māl has standing, sitting and reclining postures.]]
avarkkāy
for their sake.
veṟpu ārvil, aravu nāṇ, eri ampāl
with a bow of a mountin contining the serpent as the bow-string and the fire as the arrow.
viravār puram mūṉṟum erivittavikirtaṉ ūr viṉavil
if one asks about the place of Civaṉ who is different from the world, and had all the three cities of the enemies burnt.
coṟpāla, poruṭpāla curuti orunāṉkum tōttiramum pala colli
saying prayers and the four vētams contining different kinds of words and different kinds of meanings.
tutittu
prising god.
iṟaitaṉ tiṟattē kaṟpārum kēṭpārumāy eṅkum naṉku ārkalaipayil antaṇarvāḻum kalayanallūrkāṇ
it is Kalayanallur where brahmins who study arts which contin good matter live and where people who read books on god and listen to talks on him are full everywhere.
{7:16}__6+
{$} peṟṟimai oṉṟu aṟiyāta takkaṉatu vēḷvi
in the sacrifice performed by takkaṉ who knew little about the greatness of Civaṉ.
peruntēvar ciram tōḷ palkaramkaṇpīṭuaḻiyacceṟṟu
having punished the eminent tēvar to lose the greatness of their head, shoulders, hands and eyes.
[[The god of sacrifice lost his head; intiraṉ, his shoulders; the sun, his teeth, the god of fire, his hands, the sun by name pakaṉ his eyes.]]
(1) matikkalai citaiyattiruviralāl tēyvittu
and cusing the phases of the moon to be reduced and destroyed by the holy toe.
aruḷperuku civaperumāṉ cērtarum ūrviṉavil
if one asks about the place where Civaṉ of increasing grace, dwells.
teṟṟu koṭimullaiyoṭu mallikai ceṇpakamum tiraiporutu varupuṉalcēr ariciliṉteṉ karaimēl
on the shouthern bank of the river, aricil which brings floods whose waves dash and carry away the flowers of the intertwining creeper of arabian jasmine, jasmine and champak trees.
kaṟṟiṉam
the herd of calves.
karumpiṉ muḷaikaṟikaṟka
to learn to nibble the shoot of the sugar-cane.
kaṟavai kamaḻkaḻunīrkavar kaḻaṉikkalaya nallūrkāṇ
is Kalayanallūr where in the fields the cows graze with avidity the fragrant purple indian lily flowers.
[[Variant reading: (1) matikalai]]

{$} ilaṅkaiyarkōṉ cirampattōṭu irupatu tiṇtōḷum iṟṟu alaṟa oṟṟaiviral veṟpataṉmēlūṉṟi
fixing firmly a single toe on the mountin to make Irāvaṇaṉ, the King of the people of ilaṅki to roar as his ten heads and twenty shoulders were crushed.
nilam, kiḷar, nīr, neruppoṭu, kāṟṟu, ākācamāki
being the earth, rising water, fire, air, and the space.
niṟpaṉavum naṭappaṉavum ām niṉmalaṉ ūr viṉavil
if one asks about the place of immaculate Civaṉ who is himself the immovable and movable things.
palaṅkaḷpalatirai unti
pushing many fruits by its waves.
parumaṇipoṉkoḻittu
sifting big precious stones and gold.
pātiricantu akiliṉoṭu kētakaiyum (1) paruṅki
tearing off yellow-coloured trumpet flower trees, sandal wood, eagle wood and fragrant screw-pine trees.
[[paruṅki:palpala iruṅkaṉi, paruṅki mika uṇṭavai neruṅki iṉamāyk, kal atira niṉṟu karumanti viḷaiyāṭu kāḷattimalaiyē (campantar, tirukkālatti, tiruvirākam, 3.)]]
kalaṅkupuṉal alampi varum ariciliṉ teṉkaraimēl kayal ukaḷum vayalpuṭai cūḻkalayanallūr kāṇ
is Kalayanallūr surrounded by fields where carps frisk and which is on the southern bank of the river, sricil in which turbid water flows with a great noise.

{$} māl ayaṉum kāṇpu ariya māleriyāy nimirntōṉ
Civaṉ who rose high as a big column of fire which could not be seen by Māl and Ayaṉ.
vaṉṉi maticeṉṉimicai vaittavaṉ
placed leaves of indian mesquit and the crescent on his head.
moyttu eḻunta vēlaiviṭam uṇṭa maṇikaṇṭaṉ
the god with a blue neck who consumed the poison which rose in the ocean spreading its head everywhere.
viṭaiūrumvimalaṉ
Civaṉ who rides on a bull.
umaiyavaḷōṭum mēviya ūrviṉavil
if one asks about the place of Civaṉ who is united with Umayavaḷ.
cōlaimalikuyilkūva
when the cuckoos which are in large numbers sing in the gardens.
kōlam mayil āla
the beautiful peacocks dance.
curumpoṭu vaṇṭuicaimurala
bees along with curumpu (a Superior variety of bee) hum like music.
pacuṅkiḷicol tutikka
and the green parrots prise god as they heard men prising him.
kālaiyilum mālaiyilum kaṭavuḷ aṭipaṇintu
falling at the feet of god both in the morning and evening.
kacintamaṉattavar payilumkalayanallūr; kāṇ
is Kalayanallūr where devotees of tender hearts live in large numbers.
{7:16}__9+
{$} porumpalam atuuṭai acuraṉ tārakaṉaipporutu
fighting with tārakaṉ, the acuraṉ, who was strong enough to give battle.
poṉṟu vitta (1) poruḷiṉai muṉ paṭaittu ukanta puṉitaṉ
the pure Civaṉ who became eminent begetting the son who cused him to die.
karumpuviliṉ malarvāḷik kāmaṉ uṭalvēvak kaṉalviḻitta kaṇṇutalōṉ karutum ūrviṉavil
if one asks about the place of Civaṉ who opened the frontal eye to issue fire to burn the body of Kāmaṉ who has a bow of sugar-cane and arrows of flowers, which Civaṉ desires warmly.
irumpuṉal veṇtirai perukiēlam ilavaṅkam irukaraiyum porutu alaikkum ariciliṉ teṉkaraimēl
on the southern bank of the river, aricil, which cuses to wander back and forth, eroding both the banks, by its swollen floods and increasing white waves, cardamon and cloves.
[[Variant reading: (1) poṉṉiṉai]]
karumpu(ṉ)ṉai veṇ muttu arumpi poṉ malarntu pavaḷakkaviṉ kāṭṭum kaṭipoḻil cūḻkalayanallūr kāṇ
is Kalayanallūr surrounded by fragrant gardens where the black mast-wood trees put forth buds like white pearls, blossom like gold and exhibit the beauty of gold.
{7:16}__10+
{$} taṇkamalappoykai puṭaicūḻntu aḻakuār talattil
in the proper and beautiful place surrounded by natural ponds having cool lotus flowers.
taṭaṅkoḷ peruṅkōyiltaṉil takkavakaiyālē, vaṇkamalattu ayaṉ muṉṉāḷ vaḻipāṭuceyya makiḻntaruḷi iruntaparaṉ maruviyaūr viṉavil
if one asks about the place where the supreme god who dwelt being pleased with the worship done in the past by Ayaṉ in the fertile lotus, according to rules, in the big temple with the an extensive area.
veṇkavari karumpīli vēṅkaiyoṭu kōṅkiṉ viraimalarumviravu puṉal ariciliṉ teṉkaraimēl
on the southern bank of the river, aricil which has water in which the fragrant flowers, east indian kino tree and common cung mix with white yak-tils and blue feathers of the peacock.
kaṇ kamukiṉ pūmpāḷai matuvācam kalanta kamaḻteṉṟal pukuntu ulavum kalayanallūrkāṇ
is Kalayanallūr where the fragrant balmy breeze of the south mixed with the smell of the honey found in the beautiful spathe of the areca-palm with joints, enters into the streets and moves here and there.
{7:16}__11+
{$} taṇpuṉalum veṇmatiyum tāṅkiya ceñcaṭaiyaṉ
Civaṉ who bears on his red caṭi cool water and a white crescent.
tāmaraiyōṉ talaikalaṉāk kāmaram muṉpāṭi
singing the melody musical mode, cīkāmaram, having the skull of Piramaṉ sitting in the lotus, as the bowl.
uṇpalikoṇṭu uḻal paramaṉuṟaiyum ūr
the place where the supreme god who wanders receiving alms for eating.
niṟainīr oḻukupuṉal ariciliṉ teṉkalayanallūr ataṉai
on Kalayanallūr which is on the southern bank of the river, aricil where plenty of water flows.
naṇpu uṭaiya naṉcaṭaiyaṉ icaiñāṉi ciṟuvaṉ
the son of catiyaṉ of good qualities who is friendly towards all and iciñāṉi
[[Catiyaṉ is the father's name; iciñāṉi is the mother's name]]
nāvalarkōṉ ārūraṉ nāviṉnayantu uraiceypaṇpayilum pattum ivai (1) patticeytu pāṭavallavarkaḷ
those who are capable of singing all the ten verses which can be set to music, with piety, and composed by the chief of the people of nāvalūr and who has the name of ārūraṉ, with love.
allaloṭu pāvam ilar (tām, ē)
will not have suffering and sins.

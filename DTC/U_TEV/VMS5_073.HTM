tam (0) kalappiya takkaṉ peru vēḷvi (1) aṅku alak kaḻittu ār aruḷ ceytavaṉ
Civaṉ who bestowed his rare grace on Takkaṉ who was connected in relationship as father-in-law, after laying waste the big sacrifice performed by him
koṅku alark kuḻal kompu aṉaiyāḷoṭu maṅkalakkuṭimēya maṇāḷaṉ
is the bridegroom who is dwelling with pleasure in Maṅkalakkuṭi with a lady as tender as a branch and who has tresses of hir on which are tucked fragrant flowers.
[[In verses 1, 3, and 10 the name of the shrine, Maṅkalakkuṭi has been placed in yetuki.]]
[[Variant reading: (1) aṅkalat taḻittu]]
[[Variants (TVG vs VMS): (0) alappiya vs. kalappiya]]
{5:73}__2+
{$}
kāviriyiṉ vaṭakaraik kāṇ taku māviriyum poḻil maṅkalakkuṭi
in maṅkalakkuṭi which has large mango-groves which are pleasing to look at, and situated on the north bank of the Kāviri
tēvu ariyum piramaṉum tēṭa oṇāt tū eric cuṭarc cōtiyuḷ cōti
Civaṉ who is the inner light in the soul, with a flame that emits pure light which could not be known by Māl, of divinity and Piramaṉ, though they searched for him, (is seated majestically)
[[From this we learn that maṅkalakkuṭi is a shrine on the north bank of the Kāviri.]]

{$}
vār kaṭal cūḻ maṅkalakkuṭi mañcaṉ
the god of great strength in maṅkalakkuṭi surrounded by extensive tanks
nañcam ār amutāka nayantu koṇṭu
drinking the poison with pleasure as the rare nectar
añcum āṭal amarntu
pleased with being bathed in the five products of the cow
aṭiyēṉuṭai neñcam ālayamākak koṇṭu niṉṟatē
what a wonder tha he had as temple his slaves mind and stayed there permanently
[[mañcaṉ : maintaṉ syllable or letter substituted for another letter or syllable; kaṭal ; here means only extensive tanks as it is an inland shrine]]

celvam malku tirumaṅkalakkuṭi
tirumaṅkalakkuṭi of increasing wealth.
celvamalku civa niyamattarāy
possessing the wealth of grace and the conduct lid down in civite works.
celvam malku ceḻu maṟaiyōr toḻa
to be worshipped by properous brahmins of increasing wealth
celvaṉ tēviyoṭum tikaḻ kōyilē
is the temple where Civaṉ is prominent with his consort.

{$}
maṇṉucīr maṅkalakkuṭi maṉṉiya, piṉṉuvārcaṭaip piññakaṉ taṉ peyar, uṉṉuvārum uraikkavallārkaḷum
those who are able to fixe their thoughts on, and repeat the names of, Civaṉ who has the function of destruction, who has a long and intertwining caṭi and who is dwelling permanently in maṅkalakkuṭi possessing everlasting fame
toṭarvu eyta naṉṉeṟi tuṉṉuvār
will reach the righteous path as they become familiar with knowledge of Civaṉ.

mātarār maruvum maṅkalakkuṭi āti nāyakaṉ
Civaṉ the origin of everything, who is in maṅkalakkuṭi in which beautiful ladies live.
aṇṭarkaḷ nāyakaṉ
the chief of the tēvar
vēta nāyakaṉ
the chief who is spoken often in vētam
vētiyar nāyakaṉ
the chief of the brahmins
pūta nāyakaṉ
and the master of all living beings
puṇṇiya mūrtti
has a form which is the embodiment of all virtues
{5:73}__8+
{$}
viṇṭa tātaiyait tāḷ aṟa vīciya, caṇṭa nāyakaṉukku aruḷ ceytavaṉ
God who bestowed his grace on caṇṭanāyakaṉ who cut the feet of his father who was opposed to his acts.
vaṇṭu cēr poḻil maṅkalakkuṭi, tuṇṭamāmati cūṭiya cōtiyē
is the light that wore on its head a big crescent in maṅkalakkuṭi which has gardens where bees swarm. As maṅkalakkuṭi is very near to ceyñalūr, the birth place of canṭekar, he is mentioned in this verse.

kūcuvār alar, kuṇṭar, kuṇam ilar, nēcam ētum ilātavar nīcarkaḷ, mācar pāl
from the association of jins who had faults and bodily dirty, low people, who did not have the slightest love, who had no good qualities, mean men and who were not ashamed of performing evil acts
maṅkalakkuṭi mēviya īcaṉ vēṟu paṭukka uyntēṉ
I saved myself when Civaṉ who is in maṅkalakkuṭi separated me from them. (This is an autobiographical incident)

maṅkalakkuṭiyāṉ kayilaim malai(aṅku) alaittu eṭukkuṟṟa arakkar kōṉ taṉ karattoṭu tāḷ talai tōḷ takarntu
as the shoulders, head, feet and hands of the King of arrakar who shook and lifted the mountin, Kayili, belonging to the god in maṅkalakkuṭi, were broken to pieces.
(aṅku) alaittu aḻutu naṉṟu uyntaṉaṉ
saved himself greatly by beating himself for his folly and by weeping.

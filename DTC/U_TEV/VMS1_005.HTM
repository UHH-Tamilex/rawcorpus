[[There are two shrines having the name Kāṭṭuppaḷḷi; so this is known as Kīḻitirukkāṭṭupaḷḷi; the other one is known as Mēlikkāṭṭuppaḷḷi.]]

{1:5}__1+
{$} cey arukē puṉal pāya
the water flows near the fields.
ōṅkic ceṅkayal pāya
the red carp fish jumps rising high.
kaiyarukē vāḻai kaṉi īṉṟu
the plintan trees yield fruits within reach of the hand.
cila malarttēṉ kāṉal ellām kamaḻkāṭṭuppaḷḷi
in Kāṭṭupaḷḷi where the honey flowing from some flowers spread its fragrance throughout the sea-shore gardens.
pai arukē aḻal vāya aivāyp pāmpu aṇai yāṉ paṇaittōḷipākam mey arukē uṭaiyāṉai uḷki
meditating upon the Lord who has on one half of his form a lady with shoulders like bamboo and Māl who lies on the bed of serpent of five hoods in which there is poison near the hood.
[[That Civaṉ has Māl as well as Umi on his form can be known from the following quotations;
maiyarikkaṇṇiṉāḷum mālum ōr pākamāki (appar, Kōyil, tirunērici (1) 4);
maṟṟu vaittāy aṅkor māl orupākam makiḻntuṭaṉē, uṟṟu vaittāy umaiyāḷoṭum kūṭum pariceṉavē (Do. tiruppaḻaṉam, 7)]]
viṇṭavar mēl ulaku ēṟuvar
those who have left their attachments will ascend into the upper world.

VMS3 / {1:5}__PIFI2+
{$} tiraikaḷ ellā malarum cumantu ceḻumaṇi muttoṭu poṉ varaṉṟik karaikaḷ ellām aṇicērntu uriñcik kāvirikāl poru kāṭṭuppaḷḷi:
in Kāṭṭuppaḷḷi where Kāviri and its channels and all their waves carry all kinds of flowers, scrape big precious stones along with pearls and gold, make the banks beautiful, rubbing with one another and dashing aginst the banks.
uraikaḷ ellām uṇarvu eyti nalla uttamarāy ulakil uyarntār
Having learnt all that has to be learnt, those who are good enabled persons in the world.
aravam ellām arai ārtta celvarkku āṭceyya allal aṟukkalām
can completely destroy their sufferings if they pay homage to the Lord who has tied all cobras round his waist.
VMS4 / {1:5}__PIFI3+
{$} tōl uṭaiyāṉ
the Lord wears a skin on his waist.
vaṇṇappōrvaiyiṉāṉ
has a shining blanket of the same.
cuṇṇa veṇṇīṟu tutaintu ilaṅku nūl uṭaiyāṉ
has a bright sacred thread on the white powder of sacred ash, which has a thick layer.
imaiyōr perumāṉ
the Lord of the celestials who do not wink.
nuṇṇaṟivāl vaḻipāṭuceyyum kāluṭaiyāṉ
has feet which are worshipped by devotees with acute intellect.
karitu āya kaṇṭaṉ
has a black neck.
imaiyāta mukkaṇ mēluṭaiyāṉ
Has the third eye which is not ordinarily opened, on the forehead
[[mukkaṇ is used in the sense of mūṉṟām kaṇ; the third eye; it is used in the ordinal sense.]]
miṉ iṭaiyāḷoṭum kātalikkappaṭum kāṭṭuppaḷḷi vēṇṭiṉāṉ
He delighted in staying in Kāṭṭuppaḷḷi which he longs for, with a lady who has a waist as minute as lightning.
VMS5 / {1:5}__PIFI4+
{$} calacala cantu akilōṭum unti
pushing out sandal-wood and eagle-wood trees with the sound of calacala
[[calacala is onomatopoeia.]]
cantaṉamē karai cārtti
depositing the sandal-wood on the banks.
eṅkum palapala vāyttalai ārttu maṇṭi
moving swiftly roaring everywhere in very many head-sluices of channels.
pāyntu iḻikāvirippāṅkariṉvāy
by the side of the river Kāviri which spreads and descends into the sea.
kalakala niṉṟu atirum kaḻalāṉ kātalikkappaṭum kāṭṭuppaḷḷi
in Kāṭṭupaḷḷi which is desired by the Lord wearing tinkling anklets which make a sound resembling kala kala
[[kala kala : onomatopoeia]]
cola vala toṇṭarkaḷ ētta niṉṟa cūlam vallāṉ kaḻalcolluvōm
we shall prise the feet of the Lord who can wild the trident which stand permanently to be prised by the devotees capable of prising him.
[[kaḻal: here it is ākupeyar; it stands for the feet on which it is worn;
niṉṟa: qualifies kaḻal]]
VMS6 / {1:5}__PIFI5+
{$} taḷai aviḻ taṇniṟa nīlam neytal tāmarai ceṅkaḻu nīrum ellām kaḷai aviḻum kuḻalār kaḷaiya
the peasant women who have loosened locks of hir remove as weed all the caṅkaḻunīr (purple indian water lily) lotus, white indian water lily and blue nelumbo flowers which blossom (in the fields).
[[kaḷai kaḷaiya is the syntactical order]]
kātalikkappaṭum kāṭṭuppaḷḷi
in Kāṭṭuppaḷḷi which is desired by the Lord.
tuḷaipayilum kuḻal yāḻ murala
when the yāḻ and flute having holes are played to produce a soft music.
tuṉṉiya iṉṉicaiyāl tutaintu (celvar)
the Lord being crowded the intense sweet music.
[[Variant reading: tutaintu vaḷaipayil]]
aḷaipayil pāmpu arai ārtta celvarkku āṭceyya
if we do humble service to the Lord who ties cobras which live in ant-hills.
[[aḷaipayil pāmpu: the epithet underlined is given as a general epithet pertining to the species. The cobras on the form of the Lord were born in the sacrificial fire of the sages of tārukāvaṉam;
puṟṟila vāḷaravu eṉpataṟku vēḷvit tīyil piṟantu tirumēṉik kaṇ vāḻtalāl puṟṟaiyuṭaiya allāt araveṉṟu uraippiṉum amaiyum Kōviyār 97, pērāciriyar).]]
allal aṟukkalām
one can cut at the root of all sufferings.

karitu āya kaṇṭaṉ
has a neck which became black after drinking the poison.
poṭi aṇimēṉiṉāṉai uḷki
meditating upon the Lord who adorns his form with ash.
pōtoṭu nīr cumantu ētti
and prising him carrying flowers and water.
muṉniṉṟu aṭikaiyiṉāl toḻa valla toṇṭar aruviṉaiyait turantu āṭ ceyvār
the devotees who can stand in his presence and worship his feet with their hands, will drive away actions which are very difficult to drive without his grace, will do services to him.

periyōrkaḷ pemmāṉ

peykaḻal nāḷtoṟum pēṇi ētta maṟaiyuṭaiyāṉ

[[As this decade mentions the marks of distinction of Civaṉ this tāṇṭakam got its name of atiyāḷattiruttāṇtakam!]]

{6:4}__1+
{$} cantiraṉai mākaṅkai tiraiyāl mōtac caṭā makuṭattu iruntumē
Civaṉ fixes permanently in the caṭi coiled into a crown the crescent to be beaten by the waves of the big Kaṅki.
(1) cāmavēta kantaruvam virumpumē
will desire the music of the cāma vētam.
kapālam ēntum kaiyaṉē
holds a skull in the hand.
kaṉakamēṉi meyyaṉē
has a body as colourful as gold.
pantu aṇavu melviralāḷ pākaṉ āmē
has on one half a lady with soft fingers in which she holds a ball. (2)
pacu ēṟumē
will ride on a bull. (3)
paramayōkiyāmē
is the supreme yōki.
aintalaiya mācuṇam koṇṭu arai ārkku(m)mē
will tie in the waist a cobra with five hoods.
avaṉākil
if he has these attributes.
atikai vīraṭṭam āmē
he is the Lord in atiki vīraṭṭam.
[[variant readings: (1) cāmavētakkantiruvam (2) pacuvēṟum (3) emparama]]
{6:4}__2+
{$} ēṟu ēṟi ēḻ ulakum uḻitarvāṉē
Civaṉ will wander in all the seven worlds riding on a bull.
imaiyavarkaḷa toḻutu ētta irukkiṉṟāṉē
is present to be worshipped with joined hands and prised by the celestials who do not wink.
pāṟu ēṟu paṭutalaiyil pali koḷvāṉē
will receive alms in a dead skull on which the kite sits for eating flesh.
paṭa aravam taṭamārpil payilvittāṉē
cused on the big chest cobra with hoods, to be crowded.
nīṟu ēṟu ceḻumpavaḷak kuṉṟu oppāṉē
is like the hill of flawless coal on which sacred ash is thickly coated.
neṟṟimēl oṟṟaikkaṇ niṟaivittāṉē
he filled his forehead by creating a single eye.
āṟu ēṟu caṭai muṭimēl piṟai vaittāṉē
placed a crescent on the caṭi coiled into a crown on which the river stays.
avaṉ ākil atikai vīraṭṭaṉ āmē
[[see 1st verse.]]
{6:4}__3+
{$} muṇṭattiṉ polintu ilaṅkum (1) neṟṟiyāṉē
Civaṉ has a form which appears grand and emits rays by the three horizontal lines of the sacred ash worn on the forehead.
mutal āki, naṭu āki, muṭivu āṉāṉē
is the beginning, the middle and the end of all things.
kaṇṭattil veṇ maruppiṉ kāṟaiyāṉē
wears in the neck a necklet made of the hogs' white tusk;
[[kaḻuttiṉiṟ kāṟaiyoṭum ... polinta iruṭīkēcaṉ; kāṟai pūṇum (periyāḻwar tirumoḻi, 1-7-3, 3-7-8); At present this ornament has gone out of fasion]]
katanākam koṇṭu āṭum kāṭciyāṉē
has the appearance of dancing, adorning himself with an angry cobra.
piṇṭattiṉ iyaṟkaikku ōr peṟṟiyāṉē
is the support for the ultimate realities of which are the cuses for the body.
perunilam, nīr, tī, vaḷi, ākācam āki
being himself the big earth, water, fire, air and the space.
aṇṭattukku appāl āy ippālāṉē
pervades the universe both inside and outside.
avaṉ ākil atikai vīraṭṭaṉ āmē
[[see 1st verse]]
[[Variant reading: (1) mēṉiyāṉē]]
{6:4}__4+
{$} ceyyaṉē
Civaṉ has a body red in colour.
kaṇṭam kariyaṉē
has a black neck.
paiṅkaṇ veḷ eyiṟṟu āṭu aravaṉē
has a dancing cobra which has eyes fuming with anger, and white teeth.
viṉaikaḷ pōka veyyaṉē
is the enemy for the acts, good and bad to leave his devotees of their own accord.
taṇkoṉṟai milaitta ceṉṉic caṭaiyaṉē
has a caṭi on his head on which he wears cool koṉṟi flowers.
viḷaṅkumaḻuc cūlam ēntum kaiyaṉē
holds on his hands a shining battle axe and a trident.
kālaṅkaḷ mūṉṟu āṉāṉē
is the past, present and future.
karuppu vil taṉik (1) koṭum pūṇ kāmaṟ kāynta aiyaṉē
is the father who destroyed Kāmaṉ who wears a curved ornament and holds a bow of sugar-cane.
paruttu uyarnta āṉ ēṟṟāṉē
has a bull which is bulky and tall.
avaṉākil ... vīraṭṭaṉ āmē
see 1st verse.
[[Variant reading: (1) koṭum pūṭkāmaṟkāynta]]
{6:4}__5+
{$} nālvēta(m)mum oḻiyāmē pāṭumē
Civaṉ will chant with music all the four Vetams without ceasing;
[[naṉṟu āynta nīḷ nimircaṭai, mutumutalvaṉ vāy pōkātu, oṉṟu purinta īriraṇṭiṉ āṟu uṇarnta orumutunūl (puṟam, 166-1-4).]]
paṭar caṭaimēl oḷi tikaḻap paṉi veṇ tiṅkaḷ cūṭumē
will wear a white crescent on the spreading caṭi, to shine with brilliance.
araitikaḻat tōlum pāmpum cuṟṟumē
will wind round the waist a skin and a cobra to shine.
toṇṭai vāy umai ōr pākam kūṭumē
will unite with him as a half Umi who has lips like the fruit of the common creeper of the hedge.
kuṭamuḻavam vīṇai tāḷam kuṟu naṭaiya ciṟupūtam muḻakka
the small pūtam of short quick and graceful paces to play on Kuṭamuḻavam, and vīṇi and to keep time with cymbals. (1)
mākkūttu āṭumē
will perform the great dance.
am taṭakkai aṉal ēntu(m)mē
will hold in the beautiful and big hand fire.
avaṉākil ... vīraṭṭaṉ āmē
see 1st verse.
[[Variant reading: (1) mākūttu]]

{$} uḷkuvār (1) uḷḷattu uḷḷa uṟupiṇiyum ceṟupakaiyum oḻittiṭumē
Civaṉ will completely destroy the accumulated acts and the six internal enemies
[[they are: kāmam, kurōtam, ulōpam, mōkam, matam, māṟcariyam]]
kōlālampaṭavarai naṭṭu aravu cuṟṟik kurai kaṭalait tirai alaṟak kaṭaintu koṇṭa ālālam uṇṭu iruṇṭa kaṇṭattāṉē
has a neck which became black by consuming the poison which was got by churning the roaring sea to make the waves to roar, by setting up the mountin as churning rod and serpent as cord used for turning the churn-staff, the celestials to shout with uproarious noise.

{$} kuḻalōṭu kokkarai kaittāḷam montai kuṟaṭpūtam muṉpāṭat tāṉ āṭum(m)mē
Civaṉ will dance when the dwarf pūtams play on the flute, Kokkari, monti, and keep time with the hand.
kaḻal āṭu tiruviralāl karaṇam ceytu kaṉaviṉ kaṇ tiruvuruvam tāṉ kāṭṭu(m)mē
will show his holy form in the dream, performing gestures with the toes of the leg which wear Kaḻal
eḻil ārum tōḷ vīci naṭamāṭu(m)mē
will dance swinging the beautiful shoulders.
īmap puṟaṅkāṭṭil ēmam tōṟum aḻal āṭum(m)mē
will dance in the fire at every night in the cremation ground which has funeral pyres.
aṭṭamūrtti āmē
has eight visible forms, they are the five elements, the sun, the moon, and performer of sacrifices.
avaṉākil......āmē

{6:4}__9+
{$} cempoṉāl ceytu aḻaku peytāṟpōlum ceñcaṭai em perumāṉē
our god who has a red caṭi which appears to be made of superior gold and beauty added into it.
teyvam nāṟum (1) vampiṉ nāḷ malark kūntal umaiyāḷ kātal maṇavāḷaṉē
the husband of loving Umayāl who has tresses of hir of divine fragrance and is wearing fragrant flowers blossomed at day-break.
valaṅkai maḻuvāḷaṉē
who holds on the right hand the weapon of a battle-axe.
nampaṉē
whom people can repose their trust.
nāṉmaṟaikaḷ toḻaniṉṟāṉē
who is eternal to be prised by the four vētam.
naṭuṅkātār, purammūṉṟum naṭuṅkac ceṟṟa ampaṉē
who has an arrow with which he destroyed all the three cities of the enemies who never before trembled with fear, to tremble with fear.
aṇṭa (2) kōcarattu uḷāṉē
who is in the countries within the universe.
avaṉākil ... āmē
see 1st verse.
[[Variant reading: (1) vampiṉārmalark, vampulāmalark (2) kōcattu]]
{6:4}__10+
{$} eḻuntatirai natit (1) tivalai naṉainta tiṅkaḷ iḷanilāt tikaḻkiṉṟa vaḷarcaṭaiṉē
Civaṉ has a growing caṭi in which the light of the evening moon which is wet with the drops of the river in which waves rise.
koḻumpavaḷac ceṅkaṉivāyk kāmak kōṭṭi koṅkai iṇai amar porutu kōlam koṇṭa taḻumpu uḷavē varai mārpil
in the chest which can be compared to a mountin there are impressions which aquired beauty when the two breasts of the goddess, Kāmakkōṭṭi (Kāmakshi) who has lips like the flawless coral and the red fruit of the common creeper of the hedges embraced the chest as if they were waging war aginst it. (varaimārpil)
veṇṇūl uṇṭē
in the chest which can be compared to a mountin there is the white sacred thread.
cāntamoṭu cantaṉattiṉ aḷaṟu taṅki aḻuntiyacentiruvuruvil veṇṇīṟṟāṉē
has white sacred ash in his holy form of red colour in which the semi-liquid sandal is smeared and sandal paste of thick consistency is staying becoming pressed.
avaṉ ākil ... āmē
[[see 1st verse]]
[[Variant reading: (1) tuvalai]]
{6:4}__11+
{$} neṭiyāṉum nāṉmukaṉum nēṭikkāṇā nīṇṭāṉē
Civaṉ who rose as a tall column of fire which could not be searched for by Māl of tall form and Piramaṉ of four faces!
nēr oruvar illātāṉē
who has none as his equal!
koṭiēṟu(1) kōlamāmaṇikaṇṭaṉē
who has a neck like the beautiful sapphire in which there is the cobra (koṭi is ākupeyar for cobra)
kol vēṅkai ataḷaṉē kōvaṇavaṉē
who has the dress of the skin of the tiger which can kill; who wears a mens' loin-cloth.
poṭi ēṟu mēṉiyaṉē
who has a body on which the sacred powder is abundantly smeared.
aiyam vēṇṭip puvalōkam tiriyumē
who wanders in the world next above this world desiring alms (puvalōkam includes other worlds also, Implying something that has not been made explicit by expressing another thing associated or connected with it; this is called upalakkaṇam (upalakkaṇam))
purinūlāṉē(2)
who wears a sacred thread of strands.
aṭiyārai amarulakam āḷvikku(m)mē
who cuses his votaries to rule over heaven (amarar ulakam has become amarulakam)
avaṉākil ... āmē
see 1st verse.

{4:19}__1+
{$}
cūlappaṭaiyāṉai
Civaṉ who has a weapon of a trident
cūḻ āka vīḻ aruvik kōlat tōḷkuṅkumam cēr kuṉṟu eṭṭu uṭaiyāṉai
who has beautiful shoulders like the hills on which saffron is smeared, which is like the streams descending down, flowing in all directions
[[EXPL: the saffron paste that flows down the shoulders is compared to streams that flow down the hills.]]
pāl otta meṉ moḻiyāḷ paṅkaṉai
who has as a half a lady who speaks soft words which are as sweet as milk.
pāṅku āya ālattiṉ kīḻāṉai nāṉkaṇṭatu ārūrē
it is in Ārūr that I obtined vision of the god seated under the beautiful banyan tree.
{4:19}__2+
{$}
pakkamē pāriṭaṅkaḷ cūḻa
surrounded on all sides by pūtams.
paṭutalaiyil pukka ūrppiccai ēṟṟu uṇṭu
eating the alms received in the villages he entered in a begging bowl of a dead skull.
polivu uṭaittāyk kokku iṟakiṉ tūval koṭi eṭutta kōvaṇattōṭu akku aṇinta ammāṉai
who is our father who adorned himself with a string of black beads along with a loin-cloth which was held aloft in the banner, and the feather of a crane which is beautiful.
[[PP: kokkiṉ tūvalum kūviḷaṅkaṇṇiyum, mikka veṇtalaimālai viricaṭai, nakkaṉ (nāvukkaracar, tirunāriyur - tirukkuṟuntoki, 4);]]
[[PP: kokku iṟaku ceṉṉi uṭaiyāṉ kaṇṭāy (Do. tirumaḻapāṭi, 2);]]
[[PP: kokku iṟakar kuḷirmatic ceṉṉiyar (Do. aṉpil ālantuṟi 5)]]
nāṉ kaṇṭatu ārūr
see 1st verse.

cēya ulakamum celcārvum āṉāṉai
Civaṉ is the highest world above all worlds and the support of all;
irappavarkkuc celcār oṉḷu īvōr (nāṉmaṇikkaṭiki, 39);
mullai naṟumalar ūti iruntumpi celcārvu uṭaiyārkku iṉiyavāy (aintiṇi aimpatu, 6)]]
māyappōr vallāṉai
who can destroy the three impurities of the soul which are metaphorically the three cities.
mālai tāḻ mārpāṉai
who has a garland of koṉṟi flowers hanging down on the chest;
[[vaṇṇamārpil tārum koṉṟai (puṟam 1.2)]]
vēy otta tōḷiyartam meṉ mulai mēl taṇ cāntiṉ āyattiṉ iṭaiyāṉai
who is in the midst of female attendants who witness the festivals in Ārūr, who smear on their soft breasts cool sandal paste and who have shoulders resembling bamboo;
[[PP: arumaṇittaṭam pūṇmulai arampayarōṭu aruḷippāṭiyar;
vaṭikoṇ vēyt tōḷ vāṉ ara maṅkaiyarpiṉ cella
(nāvukkaracar, tiruvārūr, (4) 3; (5) 8)]]

ēṟu ēṟṟamā ēṟi
riding on a bull to give it pre-eminence
eṇ kaṇamum piṉpaṭara
the eight groups of worshippers to follow Civaṉ
[[PP: eṇkaṇam iṟaiñcum kōḷilip peruṅkōyil uḷāṉai
(cuntarar, kōlakkā, 8)]]
māṟu ēṟṟār val araṇam cīṟi
destroying the strong forts ofthese acurar who were opposed to Civaṉ.
nīṟu ēṟṟa mēṉiyaṉāy
having a body smeared with holy ash.
nīḷ caṭaimēl nīrtatumpa āṟu ēṟṟa antaṇaṉai
and the brahmin who received a river on his long caṭi for the water to overflow.
nāṉ kaṇṭatu ārūrē
(see the 1st verse)

tām kōla veḷḷelumpu pūṇṭu
adorning himself with the beautiful white bones.
[[PP_(elumpu):
elumpāl aṇi iṟai
(kōviyār, 197)]]
tam ēṟu ēṟi
riding on a bull which belongs to him.
pāṅku āṉa ūrkku ellām cellum paramaṉār
the supreme god who goes to all villages that are nearby. (paramaṉār: ār is a honorific plural suffix).
(1) tēm kāvi nāṟum tiruvārūrt toṉṉakaril pūṅkōyil uḷ makiḻntu pōkātu iruntār
Civaṉ stayed without leaving it with his heart full of joy, in the Pūṅkōyil in the ancient city of Tiruvārūr where the blue nelumbo flowers with honey spread their fragrance.
[[pūṅkōyil is the special name of the temple in Tiruvārūr;
pūtamyāvaiyiṉ uḷ alar pōteṉa, vēta mūlam veḷip paṭum mētiṉikkātal maṅkai itaya kamalamām mātu ōr pākaṉār ārūr malarntatāl (periya purāṇam, tirumalicciṟappu, 33); as Tiruvārūr is compared to the lotus shaped heart of the earth who is personified as a lady, it is called Pūṅkōyil)]]
[[Variant reading: (1) tēṅkāviṉūṟuntiruvārūrppoṉṉakaril]]

em paṭṭam paṭṭam uṭaiyāṉai
Civaṉ who has the right of mastery over cittu, acittu and citacittu
[[cittu: intelligent beings; acittu: non-intelligent beings; citacittu - beings endowed with both]]
ēr matiyiṉ num paṭṭam cērnta nutalāṉai
who has the beautiful crescent as a plate of gold on his forehead
[[The significance of num is not clear.]]
antivāyc cempaṭṭu uṭuttu
dressing in a red silk which is like the red sky.
ciṟumāṉ uri āṭai am paṭṭu acaittāṉai
and who wore as a beautiful silk-dress the skin of a young deer.

pōḻ otta veṇ matiyam cūṭi
wearing a white crescent which is like the segment of a circle;
[[PP:
pōḻ iḷaṅkaṇṇiyiṉāṉai;

(1) polintu ilaṅkum vēḻattu uri pōrttāṉ veḷvaḷaiyāḷ tāṉ veruva
Civaṉ covered himself with the skin of an elephant glittering and appearing grand when Umi wearing white bangles was frightened at that sight.
ūḻittī aṉṉāṉai
who is like the fire that rises at the end of the world.
ōṅku oli māppūṇṭatu ōr āḻittēr vittakaṉai
and who is the able god who has a round car to which are yoked tall and neighing horses
[[āḻittēr: the special name for the car of Tiyākarācar]]

the fragrance of the old koṉṟi flowers that blossom in winter to be cool.
nīramutu kōtaiyōṭu āṭiya nīḷ mārpaṉ
Civaṉ who has a long chest and who danced with a lady who wears a garland which resembles water by the white flowers of which it is made;
[[PP: nīr tiraṇṭaṉṉa kōtai piṟakkiṭṭu nīr tiraṇṭāṟ pōṉṟa veḷḷiya pūkkaḷāl ceyta mālaikaḷai ............ koṇṭaiyilē muṭittu (maturikkāñci, 562 and its commentary)]]
pēr amutam uṇṭārkaḷ uyyap peruṅkaṭal nañcu āramutā uṇṭāṉai
who drank the poison that rose in the big ocean as rare nectar, in order that the tevar who had partaken the nectar which was got from the ocean, might be saved from death.
nāṉ kaṇṭatu ārūr

koṭṭiya pāṇi eṭuttiṭṭa pātamum kōḷ aravum naṭṭam niṉṟu āṭiya nātar nallūriṭam koṇṭavarē
Civaṉ who dwelt in Nallūr, and the master who danced with a killing cobra, and a rised foot in accordance with measuring time by the sound of cymbal
aṭṭu miṉ ilpali eṉṟu akamkaṭai tōṟum vantu
coming often to the entrance of houses saying, Please give me alms in this house.
maṭṭu aviḻum kuḻalār vaḷaikoḷḷum vakai eṉ kolō
what is the idea of snatching the bangles of ladies from whose tresses of hir honey is flowing?
[[The ladies referred to here are the wives of the sages in tārukāvaṉam]]
{4:97}__2+
{$}
peṇ iṭṭam paṇṭaiyatu aṉṟu
love on ladies is not old
ivai pey palikku eṉṟu uḻalvār naṇṇiṭṭu vantu maṉai pukuntārum kaṟaikkaṇṭarē
wandering for alms placed in the bowl, it is quite new for the one who entered into the house apporaching it, is Civaṉ with a blue neck.
nallūrakattē paṇ iṭṭapāṭalar āṭalarāyp paṟṟi nōkki niṉṟu kaṇṇiṭṭup pōyiṟṟuk kāraṇam uṇṭu
there is some reason for Civaṉ to make gestures with looks at the ladies, singing songs with music, and dancing, in nallūr.
{4:97}__3+
{$}
paṭam ēr aravu alkul pāvai nallīr
Beautiful ladies like the Kollippāvi, having a waist like the cobra of rised hood!
pakalē oruvar iṭuvāriṭaip pali koḷpavar pōla vantu ilpukuntu
entering into the house as if he (Civaṉ) came to receive alms from those who give it, in the day time.
naṭavār
he will not move from that place
aṭikaḷ naṭam payiṉṟu āṭiya kūttar kolō
is Civaṉ the dancer who danced after much practice? (kol, ō : expletives)
vaṭapāṟ kayilaiyum teṉpāl nallūrum tam vāḻpatiyē
his dwelling places are kayili in the north and nallūr in the south, (kayili and nallur are of equal importance; kūttar vāḻpati is the syntactical link.)

ceñcuṭarc cōtip pavaḷattiraḷ tikaḻ muttu aṉaiya nañcu aṇi kaṇṭaṉ
Civaṉ who has a neck adorned by the poison which is like a pearl shining in the round mass of coral which has bright red colour.
nallūr uṟai nampaṉai
and the god who dwells in nallūr
nāṉ oru kāl tuñciṭaikkaṇṭu kaṉaviṉ talait toḻutēṟku
when I saw him once in my dream when I was asleep and worshipped him with joined hands.
avaṉ tāṉ neñciṭai niṉṟu akalāṉ
he did not leave my mind.
palakālamum niṉṟaṉaṉ
stayed there for a very long time.
[[Perhaps the poison was of white colour, so it is compared to pearl;
mēlāyatavattōr vēṭam taṉaip pūṇṭu iṅku ēlātaṉalē iyaṟṟiṉīr mātavattīr, pālākit tōṉṟip parukiṉār āvi koḷḷum ālāla nīrmaittē aiyar iyaṟkaiyatē (kantapurāṇam, vaḷḷiyammi tirumaṇappaṭalam)]]
{4:97}__5+

viṇṇōrkaḷ toḻa veṇmati cūṭi viḷaṅka niṉṟāṉ (ai)
Civaṉ stood renowned having worn a white crescent, to be worshipped by the celestials.
naṇ ilayattoṭu pāṭal aṟāta nallūrakattē tiṇnilayam koṇṭu niṉṟāṉ
stood having a strong temple in nallūr where songs with appropriate measure of time, never cease.
tiripuram mūṉṟu erittāṉ
burnt the three wandering cities.
[[tiripuram as it is followed by mūṉṟu. It is a Tamiḻ word meaning wandering.]]
kaḻaṟcēvaṭi kaṇṇuḷum neñcattakattum uḷa
his red feet wearing armour dwell always in my eyes and in the recess of my heart.
{4:97}__6+
{$}
tēṟṟappaṭat tiru nallūrakattē civaṉ iruntāl
as Civaṉ dwells in Tirunallūr to be seen clearly by devotees
tōṟṟappaṭac ceṉṟu kaṇṭu koḷḷārtoṇṭar tuṉ matiyāl
some devotees will not go there and have a vision of him to become conspicuous in his presence, through their folly.
āṟṟil keṭuttuk kuḷattiṉil tēṭiya ātaraippōl
like those foolish people who having lost something in a river searched for it in a tank.
(1) kaṟṟiṉ kaṭuttu ulaku elām tiritarvar kāṇpataṟkē
will wander as swift as the wind throughout the world, to have his vision.
[[āṟṟil keṭuttuk kuḷattiṉil tēṭutal is a proverb current during Nāvukkaracar's time. This has nothing to do with Cuntarar throwing all the gold he got in Viruttācalam in the river, Maṇimuttāri, and getting it back in the Kamalālayam tank of Tiruvārūr]]
[[Variant reading: (1) kāṟṟiṟ keṭuttu]]
{4:97}__7+
{$}
nāḷ koṇṭa tāmaraippūttaṭam cūḻnta nallūrakattē
in Nallūr, which is surrounded by lotus tanks which blossom in the morning.
kīḷ koṇṭa kōvaṇam kā eṉṟu colli
having sid, `keep the loin-cloth with the waist-band, safe'
ciṟipaṭattāṉ vāṭkoṇṭa nōkki maṉaiviyoṭum aṅku ōr vāṇikaṉai āṭkoṇṭavārttai uraikkum aṉṟō iv akaliṭamē
Do not the people of this world speak highly of the news of admitting a merchant in that place with his wife whose eyes were like sword, by uttering a lie.
[[The name of the merchant is Amarnīti Nāyaṉār; he is spoken in Tiruttoṇṭan Tiruvantāti as follows:
miṇṭum poḻiṟ paḻaiyāṟai amarnīti veṇpoṭiyiṉ, muṇṭam taritta pirāṟku nallūril muṉkōvaṇam nēr, koṇṭu iṅku aruḷ eṉṟu taṉ peruñcelvamum taṉṉaiyum taṉ, tuṇṭamati nutalāṉaiyum īnta toḻiliṉaṉē (verse 7; author Nampi Aṇṭar Nampi);
Amarnīti was a native of Paḻiyāṟi near Kumpakōṇam. This incident took place at Nallūr where Amarnīti had been to attend the big annual festival; This is elaborately told in Periya Purānam, (7) amarnīti nāyaṉār purāṇam: Amarnīti was earlier than Nāvukkaracar.]]

aṟai malku paiṅkaḻal 1. ārppa niṉṟāṉ
Civaṉ who danced standing to make the golden armours of his feet which produce a big sound to make a jingling sound.
aṇi ārcaṭaimēl naṟaimalku koṉṟai amtār uṭaiyāṉum
who has a garland of koṉṟi having profuse honey on his beautiful caṭi.
nallūrakattē paṟaimalku pāṭalaṉ āṭalaṉākip paricu aḻittāṉ
destroyed the nature of people by kindling love in their minds, by his songs to the accompaniments of instruments, and by his dances.
piṟai malku ceñcaṭai tāḻa niṉṟu āṭiya piññakaṉē
one who had an adornment of peacock's feather for his head and danced to make the red caṭi on which the crescent is thriving, to hang low.
[[niṉṟālum, uṭaiyāṉum paricu aḻittāṉum piññakaṉē is the syntactical link]]
[[Variant reading: 1. ārkka]]

maṉṉiya māmaṟaiyōr makiḻntu ētta
the great brahmins who have learnt the eternal vētams to prise Civaṉ with joy.
maruvi eṅkum tuṉṉiyatoṇṭarkaḷ iṉṉicai 1. pāṭittoḻutu
other devotees who crowd everywhere joining together to worship with joined hands and sing sweet music.
nallūrkkaṉṉiyar (tām vum) kaṉaviṭai uṉṉiya kātalarai, aṉṉiyar aṟṟavarkku maṅkaṇaṉē aruḷ eṉpar
will pray, one who has grace in his eyes to those who do not think anyone as a stranger grant to the girls of marriageable age their lovers whom they thought of in their dreams (the lover in this stanza is Civaṉ himself, cf. verse, 4).
[[Variant reading: 1. pāṭat]]

tiruamar tāmarai
the lotus desired by tiru (goddess of wealth; it will also mean lotus in which tiru is seated).
cīrvaḷar ceṅkaḻunīr
purple indian water-lily of increasing eminence.
koḷneytal
neytal which is much desired.
kuru amar kōṅkam
common cung which has a bright colour.
kurā
bottle-flower
makiḻ
pointed leaved ape flowers.
ceṇpakam
champak flowers
koṉṟai
koṉṟi flowers.
vaṉṉi
the leaves of indian mesquit.
maru amar nīḷ koṭi māṭam mali maṟaiyōrkaḷ nallūr
in nallūr where the storeys of brahmins have long flags and fragrance of the above-mentioned leaves and flowers, are in abundance.
uru amar pākattu umaiyavaḷ pākaṉai uḷkutumē
we shall meditate upon Civaṉ who has Umayavaḷ who has a half of beautiful form, as one half.

cellō koṭiyaṉ
my conduct is bad
[[koṭiyaṉ: verb in the first person]]
civaṉ peruṅkōyil civapuramum pukavum vallēṉ
I am qualified to enter into Civapuram which is the big temple of Civaṉ.
matilcūḻ ilaṅkaiyar kāvalaṉaik kal ārmuṭiyoṭu tōḷiṟac ceṟṟa kaḻal aṭiyāṉ
one who has feet wearing armour, who became angry to crush the shoulders and crown set with precious stones, of the king of the inhabitants of ilaṅki surrounded by a wall of enclosure.
nammai āḷpavaṉē nallūr irunta pirāṉ allaṉō
and is he not the master who admitted us into his grace and who stayed in nallū?

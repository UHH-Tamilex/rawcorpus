{2:82}__1+
{$} paṇ nilāviya moḻi umai paṅkaṉ
Civaṉ who has as a half Umi whose words resemble melody-types by their sweetness.
em perumāṉ
our Lord.
viṇṇil vāṉavar kōṉ, vimalaṉ, viṭai ūrti
the king of the tēvars in heaven, and the spotless god, who has a bull as his vehicle.
teṇ nilā mati tavaḻ tirumāḷikait tēvūr aṇṇal cēvaṭi aṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in the red-lotus feet of the god in tēvūr where in the mansions the moon with clear light crawls.
allal oṉṟu(m) ilamē
we do not have even the slightest suffering.
{2:82}__2+
{$} maṇ talattōr muḻutu (um) ōti uyya
all the people of this world to save themselve by singing his prises.
veṟpu ēṟu cōti vāṉavaṉ tuti ceya makiḻntavaṉ
Civaṉ who rejoiced when the sun who rises up in the mountin, worshipped him (it is a poetical covention that the sun rises in the morning in a mountin, utayakiri and sets in a mountin known as attamaṉa Kiri)
tū nīrt tītu il paṅkayam terivaiyar mukam malar tēvūr āticēvaṭi aṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in the red-lotus feet of the primordial Civaṉ in tēvūr where in the pure water the flawless lotus blossoms like the faces of ladies;
[[varumulai aṉṉa vaṇmukai uṭaintu tirumukam aviḻnta teyvattāmarai (Ciṟupāṇāṟṟuppaṭi, 72-73.)]]
allal oṉṟum ilamē
see 1st verse.
{2:82}__3+
{$} maṟaikaḷāl mika vaḻipaṭu māṇiyaik kolvāṉ
in order to kill th e bachelor, markaṇtēyaṉ who worshipped Civaṉ with mantirams of vētams.
kaṟuvu koṇṭa akkālaṉaik kāynta em kaṭavuḷ
our god who destroyed that Kālaṉ who was angry with him.
ceṟuvil vāḷaikaḷ cēl (avai) poruvayal tēvūr aṟavaṉ cēvaṭi aṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in the red-lotus feet of Civaṉ who is the abode of virtue in tēvūr where in the fields scabbard fish and carnatic carp fight.
allal oṉṟum (m) ilamē
see 1st verse.
{2:82}__4+
{$} muttaṉ
Civaṉ who is naturally free from all bordage
cilpalikku ūr toṟum muṟai muṟai tiriyum pittaṉ
who is called pittaṉ and wanders from village to village by turns for a small quantity of alms.
ceñcaṭaip piññakaṉ
who has a tucked a peacock`s feathers in his red caṭi.
taṉ aṭiyārkaḷ cittaṉ
and who is in the minds of his devotees who worship him.
ceḻumati tavaḻ poḻil tēvūr attaṉ cēvaṭi aṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in the red-lotus feet of our father in tēvūr which has gardens on which the beautiful moon seems to crawl.
allal oṉṟu(m) ilamē
see 1st verse.
{2:82}__5+
{$} icai pāṭuvār
devotees who sing music.
pal poruṭpayaṉ ukantu aṉpāl kūṭuvār
who gather with love desiring fruits of many things.
tuṇaik koṇṭa tam paṟṟu aṟappaṟṟit tēṭuvār
who seek him holding for the attachments which they formerly considered as their support, to became extinct.
poruḷ āṉavaṉ
the real object of those people.
ceṟi poḻil tēvūr āṭuvāṉ aṭi aṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in the feet of the dancer in tēvūr where there are dense gardens.
allal oṉṟu(m) ilamē
see 1st verse.

{2:82}__6+
{$} poṅku pūṇmulaip, purikuḻal, varivaḷaip 1. poruppiṉ maṅkai 2. paṅkiṉaṉ
Civaṉ who has the dughter of the mountin who wears bangles with lines, tresses of curly hirs, breasts wearing ornaments swelling with joy.
kaṅkaiyai vaḷar caṭai vaittāṉ
who placed Kaṅki on the growing caṭi.
tiṅkaḷ cūṭiya tī niṟakkaṭavuḷ teṉ tēvūr aṅkaṇaṉ taṉai āṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in Civaṉ of gracious looks in tēvūr in the south and the god who is red coloured like the fire and who wears the crescent on his head.
allal oṉṟu(m) ilamē
see 1st verse.
[[Variant reading: 1. poruppaṉ 2. paṅkaṉ taṇ kaṅkaiyai]]
{2:82}__7+
{$} vaṉ puyatta attāṉavar puraṅkaḷai eriyattaṉ puyattu uṟattaṭavarai vaḷaittavaṉ
Civaṉ who bent the big mountin to come into contact with his shoulder, to make the cities of those acurar who had strong shoulders, to be consumed by fire.
takka teṉ tamiḻkkalai terintavar poruntiya tēvūr aṉpaṉ cēvaṭi aṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in the red-lotus feet of Civaṉ who is the embodiment of love in tēvūr where people who made research in the arts in the beautiful and excellent tamiḻ.
allal oṉṟu(m) ilamē
see 1st verse.
{2:82}__8+
{$} taru uyarnta veṟpu eṭutta attacamukaṉ nerintu veruva ūṉṟiya tiruviral nekiḻttu vāḷ paṇittāṉ
Civaṉ granted a sword by relasing the holy toe which he fixed firmly to frighten and break, that ten-faced īrāvaṇaṉ who lifted the mountin which had tall trees.
teruvu tōṟum nal teṉṟal vantu ulaviya tēvūr aravu cūṭiyai aṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in Civaṉ who adorned himself with cobras, and who is in tēvūr where the pleasant balmy breeze blowing from the south moves in every street.
allal oṉṟu(m) ilamē
see 1st verse.
{2:82}__9+
{$} muntik kaṇṇaṉum nāṉ mukaṉum (m) avar kāṇā entai
our father who could not be seen by Kaṇṇaṉ and the four-faced Piramaṉ in the distant past.
tiṇ tiṟal iruṅkaḷiṟu uritta emperumāṉ
our god who flayed a big elephant of great vigour and strength.
centu iṉattu icai aṟupatam mural tēvūr antivaṇṇaṉai aṭaintaṉam
we took refuge in Civaṉ who has the colour of the evening sky and who is in tiruttēvūr where in the gardens bees with six legs hum the music of the group of centu.

{$} nalla centamiḻ vallavaṉ ñāṉacampantaṉ kāḻiyarkku atipaṉ
the chief of the residents of Kāḻi, Ñāṉacampantaṉ who is well-versed in the good and refined tamiḻ.
ellai il pukaḻ malkiya eḻil vaḷar tēvūrt tollai nampaṉaic colliya pattum vallārē
those only who can recite all the ten verses composed by him on the primordial Civaṉ who is in Tēvūr of increasing beauty and limitless fame which is always on the increase.
allal iṉṟi viṇ, āḷvarkaḷ
will reign in heaven without any sufferings.

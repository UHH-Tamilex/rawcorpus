{4:27}__1+
{$} keṭilavēli atikai vīraṭṭaṉār (ē)
Civaṉ in Atiki Vīraṭṭam bounded by the river, Keṭilam.
puliyiṉ tōlai maṭakkiṉār
wore round the waist the tiger's skin.
māmaṇi nākam kaccā muṭakkiṉār
would the waist the cobra with a big gem, as a belt.
moy caṭaikkaṟṟai taṉmēl mukiḻ veṇtiṅkaḷ (1) toṭakkiṉāṉ
bound on the bundle of dense caṭi a crescent just rising.
toṇṭaic cevvāyttuṭiyiṭaip paravai alkul aṭakkiṉār
contined on the left half a lady who has red lips like the fruit of the common creeper of the hedge, a waist like the drum like the sand glass and broad sides.
[[Variant reading: (1) tuṭakkiṉār]]

{$} keṭilavēli atikai vīraṭṭaṉār (ē)
[[see 1st verse]]
cūṭiṉār kaṅkaiyāḷai
were the lady, Kaṅki on the head.
cūṭiya tuḻaṉi kēṭṭu aṅku ūṭiṉāḷ naṅkaiyāḷum
even the lady of distinction became sulky hearing the sound of the water which he wore.
ūṭalai oḻikkavēṇṭi
to remove the sulkiness.
pāṭiṉār cāmavētam
sang the cāma vētam.
(1) pāṭiya pāṇiyālē āṭiṉār
danced to the measure of time suited to that music.
[[Variant reading: (1) pāṭiyappāṇiyālē]]

{$} keṭilavēli atikai vīraṭṭaṉār
[[see 1st verse]]
kompiṉār kuḻaitta vēṉal kōmakaṉ kōla nīrmai nampiṉār kāṇalākā vakaiyatu ōr naṭalai ceytār
cused suffering to the beautiful nature of cupid who reigns the spring when all branches thrive and sprout, so that those who reposed trust in him could not see his body.
vempiṉār matilkaḷ mūṉṟum villiṭai erittu vīḻtta ampiṉār
has an arrow which he fixed in the bow and cused the fall of all the three forts of the angry enemies;

{$} keṭilavīraṭṭaṉār
Civaṉ in Vīraṭṭam on the bank of the Kēṭilam.
maṟipaṭak kiṭantakaiyar
has in his hand a young one of a deer, united with it.
vaḷar iḷam maṅkai pākam ceṟi paṭakkiṭanta cekkarc ceḻumatik koḻuntu cūṭi
wearing a waxing crescent on the caṭi which resembles the red sky and which is dense, and having on one half a young lady whose youthfulness is always increasing without descreasing.
poṟipaṭak kiṭanta nākam pukai umiḻntu aḻala vīkki
tying a cobra which has spots, to spit fumes and be angry.
kīṟipaṭa naṭappar (pōlum)
conducts himself to cut practical jokes.

{$} varivarāl ukaḷum teṇṇīrk kaḻaṉicūḻpaḻaṉavēli, arivarāl vayalkaḷ cūḻnta atikai vīraṭṭaṉār (ē)
the Lord in Atiki Vīraṭṭam surrounded by fields where people who harvest engage in that work, and bound by tanks surrounded by fields of clear water where the fish varāl with lines frisks.
narivarāl kavvac ceṉṟu nal tacai iḻantatu otta (1) terivar (āl)
people with intelligence will discern the moral of the story of the fox losing its good flesh by trying to seize the varāl fish.
māl koḷ cintaitīrppatu ōr cintai ceyvar
will think of removing the confusion which is in their minds.
[[variant readings: (1) terivarāṉ māla cintai, terivarāṉ mācil cintai]]

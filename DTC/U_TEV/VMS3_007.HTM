{3:7}__1+
{$} kaṇṇutalāṉum
the Lord who has an eye on the forehead.
veṇṇīṟṟiṉāṉum
and who has smeared himself with the sacred white ash.
kaḻal ārkkavē paṇ icai pāṭa niṉṟu āṭiṉāṉum
danced standing, (the pūtams) to sing music with paṇ, to make the anklets to sound.
parañcōtiyum
and the supreme divine light.
puṇṇiyam nāṉ maṟaiyōrkaḷ ēttum pukalinnakarp peṇṇiṉ nallāḷoṭum vīṟṟirunta perumāṉ aṉṟē
is definitely the great god who is seated majestically with a noble lady in the city of pukali which is prised by the brahmins who have learnt the four vētams and enjoy the fruits of virtuous acts.
{3:7}__2+
{$} cāmpalōṭum taḻal āṭiṉāṉum
the Lord who danced in the fire covered by ash
[[This refers to the dance of Civaṉ, after destruction of the worlds.]]
caṭaiyiṉ micaip pāmpiṉōṭum mati cūṭiṉāṉum
and wore on his caṭi the crescent and cobra.
pacu ēṟiyum
and mounted on a bull
[[pacu here denotes the species; not the cow;]]
[[PP: pacu ēṟum eṅkaḷ paramaṉ- (Kōḷaṟu patikam, 9)]]
pūmpaṭukal iḷa vāḷai pāyum pukalin nakark kāmpu aṉa tōḷiyoṭum irunta kaṭavuḷaṉṟē
is definitely the god who was seated with the lady who has shoulders like bamboo, in the city of pukali where the young scabbard fish leaps in the tank having flowers.
{3:7}__3+
{$} karuppu nal vārcilaik kāmaṉ vēvak kaṭaik kaṇṭāṉum
the Lord who looked at Kāmaṉ who has a good and long bow of sugar-cane, by the corner of the eye to burn him.
[[kaṭaikkaṇṭāṉum: a verb coined from the noun kaṭaikkaṇ meaning corner of the eye.]]
maruppu nal āṉaiyil īruri pōrtta maṇāḷaṉum
and the bridegroom who covered himself with the skin of the elephant with well formed tusks, wet with blood.
[[īr urivai - eppōtum īramāṉa tōl; īruri - īram aṟāta uri ; īr - īram (takkayākapparaṇi, 149, 551, commentary);]]
[[PP: nitta maṇāḷar nirampa aḻakiyar (tiruvācakam, aṉṉippattu, 3)]]
poruppu aṉa mā maṇi māṭam ōṅkum pukalin nakar viruppiṉ nallāḷoṭum vīṟṟirunta vimalaṉ aṉṟē
is the spotless god who sat majestically with a loving lady in the city of pukali where the big and beautiful storeys which are like mountins, rise high into the sky.
{3:7}__4+
{$} aṅkaiyil (aṅku) aḻal ēntiṉāṉum
the Lord who held in his palm fire.
aḻakākavē kaṅkaiyaic ceñcaṭai cūṭiṉāṉum
and adorned on the red caṭi Kaṅki to appear beautiful.
kaṭali(ṉ)ṉiṭaip poṅkiya nañcu amutu uṇṭavaṉum
and drunk the poison, which shot up in the sea, as nectar.
pukalin nakar maṅkai nallāḷoṭum vīṟṟirunta maṇavāḷaṉē
is definitely the bridegroom who sat majestically with a good-natured lady in the city of pukali.
{3:7}__5+
{$} cāma nalvētaṉum
the god who sang the good cāmavetam
takkaṉ taṉ vēḷvi takarttāṉum
and destroyed the sacrifice of takkaṉ.
nāmam nūṟāyiram colli vāṉōr toḻu nātaṉum
the chief who is worshipped by the celestials by uttering his names which are lakh in number
[[nūṟāyiram here means countless.]]
pū malku taṇ poḻil maṉṉum antaṇ pukalin nakark kōmaḷa mātoṭum vīṟṟirunta kuḻakaṉ aṉṟē
is definitely the youth who sat majectically with the tender-natured lady in the city of beautiful and cool pukali where there are permanent cool parks abounding in flowers.
{3:7}__6+
{$} iraviṭai oḷḷeri āṭiṉāṉum
the Lord who danced in the fire after destruction of the worlds when darkness envelops everything.
imaiyōr toḻac ceruviṭai muppuram tī eritta civalōkaṉum
and resides in Civalōkam, buring with fire the three cities in the battle, to be worshipped by the tēvar.
poruviṭai oṉṟu ukantu ēṟiṉāṉum
and mounted on a fighting bull, desiring it.
pukalin nakar araviṭai mātoṭum vīṟṟirunta aḻakaṉ aṉṟē
is definitely the beautiful Lord who sat majestically in the city of pukali, with a lady having a waist resembling serpent.
[[araviṭai : cf. notes: campantar, pukali, (7) 5]]
{3:7}__7+
{$} (1) cērppu (atu) tiṇcilai mēviṉāṉum
the Lord who desired as his place the strong mountin (Kailācam)
tikaḻ pālaṉ mēl vērppatu ceyta veṅkūṟṟu utaittāṉum
and kicked the cruel god of death who was angry with the brilliant youth
[[pālaṉ here refers to Mārkkaṇṭēyaṉ who was sixteen years of age.]]
vēḷvippukai pōrppatu ceytu aṇi māṭam ōṅkum pukalin nakarp pārppatiyōṭu uṭaṉ vīṟṟirunta paramaṉ aṉṟē
is the supreme being who sat majestically in company with Pārppati in the city of Pukali where beautiful storeys rise high which are enveloped in the smoke rising from sacrificial pits.
[[pārppati - pārvati - parvatam becomes paruppatam in Tamiḻ the dughter of paruppatam becomes pārppati;]]
[[PP: paruppatattuk kayal poṟitta pāṇṭiyar kulapatipōl (periyāḻvār, 5-4-7)]]
[[variant readings : (1) cērp poḻutiṉ malaivēli, cērp poḻutu malai vēli]]
{3:7}__8+
{$} kalneṭu mālvaraikkīḻ arakkaṉ iṭar kaṇṭāṉum
the Lord who inflicted suffering on arakkaṉ who has cught under the big and long mountin (Kailācam) which is as strong as a stone.
vil neṭum pōr viṟal vēṭaṉ āki
Assuming the form of a victorious hunter wall skilled in fighting with a bow.
vicayaṟku oru poṉ neṭuṅkōl koṭuttāṉum
and gave Vicayaṉ (Arjunaṉ) a long golden arrow (Pācupatam)
am taṇ pukalin nakar aṉṉa mel naṭai maṅkaiyoṭum amarntāṉ aṉṟē
is definitely the Lord who resides gladly in the beautiful and cool city of Pukali with a young lady having a git like that of the swan.
{3:7}__9+
{$} poṉ niṟa nāṉmukaṉ paccaiyāṉ eṉṟa ivar pukkuḻit taṉṉai iṉṉāṉ eṉak kāṇpu ariya taḻaṟ cōtiyum
god who was the effulgence of the fire that could not be seen, when the four-faced Piramaṉ and green-coloured Māl started to find out, but could not find out who it was that assumed the form of fire.
puṉṉaip poṉ tātu utir malkum amtaṇ pukalin nakar miṉ iṭai mātoṭum vīṟṟirunta vimalaṉ aṉṟē
is definitely the spoteless one who sat majestically with a lady who has a waist like lightning, in the beautiful and cool city of pukali which is filled with the golden pollen of puṉṉi (mast-wood tree) which drops from the flowers.
{3:7}__10+
{$} piṇṭiyum pōtiyum (1) pēṇuvār pēcciṉaip pēṇātatu ōr toṇṭarum kātal cey cōtiyāya cuṭarc cōtiyāṉ
the effulgent light and god who is loved by the devotees who do not regard the words of those who hold in high estem the acōka tree and the pōti tree.
[[For Jains, the acōka tree is important as their god is seated under that tree;]]
[[pōti (aracamaram: pipal tree) is important for buddhists as Buddha got enlightenment when he did penance under that tree.]]
puṇṭarīkam malarp poykai cūḻnta pukalin nakar vaṇṭu amar kōtaiyoṭum irunta maṇavāḷaṉē
is definitely the bride-groom who was seated along with a lady as soft as a garland on which bees settle, in the city of pukali which is surrounded by natural tanks having lotus flowers.
[[Variant reading: (1) pēṇuvār pēṇaip pēṇāta tōr]]
{3:7}__11+
{$} pūṅkamaḻ kōtaiyoṭum iruntāṉ pukalinnakarp pāṅkaṉai
on the companion who was in the city of pukali in company with a lady as soft as a fragrant beautiful garland.
ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa tamiḻ pattu ivai
these ten verses composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ.
āṅku amarvu eytiya ātiyāka icai vallavar
those who are able to sing with music thinking them to be the god himself who is residing gladly in pukali.
ōṅku (1) amarāvatiyōr toḻac celvatum uṇmai
it is definitely true that they will ascend into heaven to be esteemed by the superior residents of Amarāvati.
[[Amarāvati is the capital of Intiraṉ's world.]]
[[Variant reading: (1) amarāpatiyōr]]

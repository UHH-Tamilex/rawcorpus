{$} uṇṇāmulai umaiyāḷoṭum uṭaṉ ākiya oruvaṉ
the unequalled god who is united with Uṇṇāmuli Umi, having fixed her in the left half of this body.
[[uṇṇāmulai the name of the goddess in this shrine]]
peṇ ākiya perumāṉ malai
the mountin which belongs to the Lord who is completely a lady.
tirumāmaṇi tikaḻa
the big gems of sapphire to shine

maṇ ārttaṉa aruvittiraḷ maḻalai(m) muḻavu atirum aṇṇāmalai toḻuvārviṉai vaḻuvāvaṇṇam aṟum (ē)
the acts, good and bad, of those who worship with joined hands Aṇṇāmali, where the collection of streams which are full of fertile silt and which make a gentle sound, will be severed from them without fail.
{1:10}__2+
{$} tēmāṅkaṉi kaṭuvaṉ koḷa viṭukompoṭu tīṇṭi
as the branch beats, when it is releaved after taking the fruits of sweet mango by the male monkey.
tūmāmaḻai
as the big and black cloud.
(1) tuṟukalmicai ciṟunuṇtuḷi citaṟa
scatters small and minute drops of rin on the boulders.
āmāmpiṇai aṇaiyum poḻil aṇṇāmalai aṇṇal pūmāṅkaḻal puṉaicēvaṭi niṉaivārviṉai ilarē
those who fix their thoughts on the red feet which wear beautiful kaḻal which are like the mango leaf in form, of the god in Aṇṇāmali which has gardens which the wild cow approaches.
[[āmāppiṇai have become āmāmpiṇai to suit rhyme]]
[[Variant reading: (1) tuṟuvaṇṭicai]
{1:10}__3+
{$} pīli(m) mayil peṭaiyōṭu uṟai toḻumcūḻkaḻaimuttam
the pearl that is produced in the bamboo surrounded by gardens in which the male peacock with its beautiful til lives with its female.
cūla(m)maṇi taraimēl niṟai coriyum viricāral āli(m) maḻai tavaḻum poḻil aṇṇāmalai aṇṇal kālaṉ valitolai cēvaṭi toḻuvāraṉa pukaḻē
the fame belongs to those who worship the lotus-red foot which destroyed the strength of the Kālaṉ (god of death), of the chief in Aṇṇāmali which has gardens on which the clouds with hils in the expansion slopes where those pearls become mature, shed on the ground,
[[$$cūli and adverbial participle from the verb-root cūl:
cūlāmai cūliṉpaṭumtuṉpam (ciṟupañcamūlam, 75)]]
[[pīlim mayil, cūlammaṇi, ālimmaḻai: the underlined letters are augmentation for rhythm]]
[[hil is compared to pearl (Cīvakaciṇtāmaṇi, 2786)]]
{1:10}__4+
{$} utirum mayir iṭuveṇtalai kalaṉā ulaku ellām, etirum pali (1) uṇavu ākavum erutu ēṟuvatu allāl
in addition to riding on the bull to receive the alms which are given unasked for, as food, in the whole world, in the bowl of the white skull left in the cremation ground from which hirs have fallen out.
mutirum caṭai iḷaveṇpiṟai muṭimēlkoḷa
to place on the head in the caṭi which has grown old, a young crescent.
aṭimēl atirum kaḻal aṭikaṭku iṭam aṇṇāmalai (atuvē)
Aṇṇāmali is the place for the deity who wears on his leg resounding kaḻal
[[Variant reading: (1) uṇal ākavum]]
{1:10}__5+
{$} maravam, cilai, taraḷam miku maṇi untu veḷ aruvi, aravam ceya
the streams which push in a large measure pearls, cili (a kind of tree) and common cadampa make a loud sound;
[[cilai a kind of tree]]
[[PP_EXT: arimāṉ iṭittaṉṉa amcilai valvil
(Kalittoki, 15-1)]]
[[PP_EXT: cilaimāṇvalvil cuṟṟi
(Akam, 69-15)]]
muravampaṭum aṇṇāmalai aṇṇal
the chief in Aṇṇāmali where the drum, muravam, is played
[[PP: muravam montai muḻāolikka;
naṭamāṭaloṭu poṅkum muravam (Campantar, Tirukkaṇṭiyūr Vīrattam, 5, Tiruttēvūr, 8)]]
uravu amcaṭai ulavum puṉal uṭaṉ āvatum ōrār
without considering that there is water (Kanki) united with him which moves in the strong caṭi.
kuravam kamaḻ naṟumeṉkuḻal umai pulkutal kuṇamē
is it proper for him to embrace Umi who has naturally fragrant tresses of hir in which the bottle flowers spread fragrance?
{1:10}__6+
{$} poṭi aṇivār
Civaṉ adorns himself with the sacred ash.
perukum puṉal aṇṇāmalai
the Lord in Aṇṇāmali which has rising water.
piṟaicēr kaṭal nañcaip parukumtaṉai tuṇivār
he dared so much as to drink the poison which rose in the ocean in which the crescent was born.
atu paruki
having drunk it.
karukum miṭaṟu uṭaiyār
has a black neck as a result of that.
kamaḻcaṭaiyār kaḻalparavi urukum maṉam uṭaiyār tamakku uṟunōy aṭaiyāvē
many diseases will not approach those whose hearts melt, prising the feet of the Lord who has a fragrant caṭi.
{1:10}__7+
{$} karikālaṉa, kuṭā koḷvaṉa, kaḻutu āṭiya kāṭṭil
in the cremation ground where the peys which have charred legs and seize the intestines of corpses; dance.
nari āṭiya nakuveṇtalai utaiyuṇṭavai (1) uruḷa
the white skulls, which seem to lugh and which are kicked by the foxes, roll.
eri āṭiya iṟaivarkku iṭam iṉavaṇṭu icai (2) murala ari āṭiya kaṇṇāḷoṭum aṇṇāmalai (atuvē)
is Aṇṇāmali where the swarms of bees hum like music the place of Civaṉ who danced in fire and stays along with a lady in whose eyes there are lines in the white portion.
[[Variant reading: (1) uruḷavveri (2) muralavvari]]
{1:10}__8+
{$} oḷiṟūpuli ataḷ āṭaiyaṉ
Civaṉ wears a dress made of the shining tiger's skin.
umai añcutal poruṭṭu (āl) piḷiṟūkural matavāraṇa vataṉam piṭittu urittu
flaying the roaring elephant of must catching hold of its face in order to make Umi seized with fear.
veḷiṟūpaṭaviṉaiyāṭiya vikirtaṉ
the Lord who is different from the world and played without any seriousness.
irāvaṇaṉai aḷaṟūpaṭa aṭarttāṉ iṭam aṇṇāmalai (atuvē)
the place of the god who pressed down Irāvaṇaṉ to become a slough is Aṇṇāmali.
[[oḷiṟū, piḷiṟū, veḷiṟū, aḷaṟū: in these words the last letter is lengthened for the sake of rhythmic movement they are oḷiṟu, piḷiṟu, veḷiṟu, aḷaṟu]]
{1:10}__9+
{$} viḷavu ār kaṉipaṭa nūṟiya kaṭal vaṇṇaṉum
Māl who is blue in colour like the sea and killed the acuraṉ who stood as a wood apple tree full of delicious fruits.
vētakkiḷar tāmarai malarmēl uṟai kēṭu ilpukaḻōṉum
and Piramaṉ who is seated on a bright lotus flower, learnt the Vētam-s and has undying fame.
aḷavā va(ṇ)ṇam aḻal ākiya aṇṇāmalai aṇṇal
the god in Aṇṇāmali who took the form of fire so that both cannot measure his greatness.
taḷarāmulai muṟuval(l) umai talaivaṉ (ṉ) aṭicaraṇē
the feet of the Lord of Umi who has breasts which do not droop and who smiles, is our refuge.
[[the anecdote about Māl and Piramaṉ is mentioned in the 9th verse of every decade by Campantar; but in this decade it has added significance as that story is connected with Aṇṇāmali. As Umi has not fed any child with her breast milk and did not allow any child suckle them; they did not droop]]
{1:10}__10+
{$} vērvantuṟa, mācu ūrtara veyil niṉṟu uḻalvārum
the amanar who wander standing in the hot sun to perspire and the dirt to spread over the body.
mārpam putai malicīvaram maṟaiyāvaruvārum
and the buddhists who cover their chests with a long CiVaram hiding them from being seen.
ārampartam urai koḷḷaṉmiṉ
do not accept the words of those two who are only beginners in philosophy.
aṇṇāmalai aṇṇal
the chief in Aṇṇāmali.
kūrveṇ maḻuppaṭaiyāṉ nalla kaḻalcērvatu kuṇamē
It is good to approach the feet that do good, of the god who has a weapon of sharp and white battle-axe.
{1:10}__11+
{$} ampu unti mūeyil eytavaṉ vempu untiya katirōṉ oḷi vilakum viricāral aṇṇāmalai ataṉai
on Aṇṇāmali which has expansive slopes where the light of the extremely hot sun steps aside and gives way, and which belongs to Civaṉ who shot the three forts by discharging an arrow.
kompu untuva kuyil āluva kuḷir kāḻiyuḷñāṉa, campantaṉa tamiḻvallavar aṭipēṇutal tavamē
It is equal to penance if one cherishes with love the feet of those who are able to recite the tamiḻ verses of Ñāṉacampantaṉ in Kāḻi where indian cuckoos sing being an accompaniment to the instrument's horn.

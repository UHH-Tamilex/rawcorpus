{3:108}__1+
{$} vētavēḷviyai nintaṉai ceytu uḻal ātam illi amaṇoṭu tērarai vātil veṉṟu aḻikkat tiruvuḷḷamē
is it your will to destroy, having won victory in religious disputation with tērar and amaṇar who have no regard towards other religions and go about scorning Vētam-s and sacrifices mentioned in them.
pāti mātuṭaṉ āya paramaṉē
the supreme god who is united with a lady as one half!
ñālam niṉpukaḻē vēṇṭum
only your fame should increase in this world.
ālavāyil uṟaiyum em ātiyē
Civaṉ who is the cuse of all things and who dwells in Ālavāy!
{3:108}__2+
{$} vaitikattiṉvaḻi oḻukāta akkaitavam uṭaik kār amaṇ tērarai (1) eyti vātuceyat tiruvuḷḷamē
is it your will to approach the tērar (buddhists) and the black amaṇar who practise deceit and do not follow the duties enjoined in the Vētam-s.
mai tikaḻtaru māmaṇi kaṇṭaṉē
Civaṉ who has a beautiful neck where the poison is shining!
ñālam ... ātiyē
[[see 1st verse]]
[[Variant reading: (1) eytavātu ceyyat]]
[[ceyyat: this reading will apply to the following nine verses]]
{3:108}__3+
{$} maṟai vaḻakkam ilāta māpāvikaḷ paṟitalaik kaiyar pāy uṭuppārkaḷai muṟiya
to defeat the low people amaṇar who wear mats, who pluck their hirs on the head, who are great sinners and who do not observe the duties enjoined in the Vētam-s.
vātu ceyat tiruvuḷḷamē
is it your will to enter into a disputation with them?
maṟiulām kaiyil māmaḻuvāḷaṉē
Civaṉ who has a big battle-axe in the hand where the young one of a deer stands!
ñālam ... ātiyē
see 1st verse.
{3:108}__4+
{$} aṟutta aṅkam āṟu āyiṉa nīrmaiyaik (1) kaṟutta vāḻ amaṇ kaiyarkaḷ tammoṭum ceṟuttu
suppressing the low amaṇar who exist and are angry with the nature of the six aṅkams which have well determined the meaning of the vētams.
vātu ceyat tiruvuḷḷamē
is it your will to enter into a disputation with them?
muṟitta vāḷmatikkaṇṇi mutalvaṉē
Civaṉ, the first cuse, who wears a curved chaplet of a crescent!
ñālam ... ātiyē
see 1st verse.
[[Variant reading: (1) kaṟuttu]]
{3:108}__5+
{$} antaṇāḷar puriyum arumaṟai cintaiceyyā arukar tiṟaṅkaḷaic cinta
to scatter the units of arukar (amaṇar) who do not care for the acts lid down in the abstruse vētams, which are performed by the branmins.
vātu ceyat tiruvuḷḷamē
see 2nd verse.
venta nīṟu(atu) aṇiyum vikirtaṉē
god who is different from the world and who smears himself with well-burnt sacred ash!
ñālam ... ātiyē
see 1st verse.
{3:108}__6+
{$} vēṭṭu vēḷvi ceyum (1) poruḷai viḷi (2) mūṭṭu cintai muruṭṭu amaṇ kuṇṭarai ōṭṭi
to drive away the low amaṇar who are obstinate and who despise the meaning of sacrifices which are performed with desire.
vātu ceyta tiruvuḷḷamē
see 2nd verse (vātu ceytu ōṭṭa is the sense).
kāṭṭil āṉai uritta em kaḷvaṉē
our robber who flayed an elephant that lives in the forest! (kāṭṭil āṉai: the underlined word is a generic epithet, as the elephant that Civaṉ flayed was produced in the sacrificial fire)
[[variant readings: (1) poruḷaivviḷi (2) mūṭṭuccintai]]
{3:108}__7+
{$} aḻal(atu) ōmpum arumaṟaiyōr tiṟam viḻalatu eṉṉum (1) arukar tiṟattiṟam kaḻala
the doctrines and minor doctrines of the arukar (amaṇar) who deride as worthless the doctrines of the brahmins who have studied the abstruse vetams and mintin sacrificial fires, to disappear.
vātu ceyat tiruvuḷḷamē
see 2nd verse.
taḻal ilaṅku tiruvuruc caivaṉē
Civaṉ who has a holy form which shines like fire!
[[Variant reading: (1) arukartiṟaṅkaḷaik]]
ñālam ... ātiyē
see 1st verse.
{3:108}__8+
{$} nīṟṟumēṉiyar āyiṉarmēl uṟṟa kāṟṟuk koḷḷavum nillā amaṇarait tēṟṟi
clarifying the mistakes of amaṇar who do not brook even the breeze to touch them which came in contact with the civites who smear their bodies with sacred ash.
vātu ceyat tiruvuḷamē
see 2nd verse.
āṟṟa vāḷ arakkaṟkum aruḷiṉāy
you granted with great grace a sword to the undeserving arakkaṉ too.
ñālam ... ātiyē
see 1st verse.
{3:108}__9+
{$} nīla mēṉi amaṇartiṟattu niṉ cīlam vātu ceyat tiruvuḷḷamē
is it your will to enter into a disputation with the amaṇar who have black bodies, about your religious doctrine.
mālum nāṉmukaṉum kāṇpu ariyatu ōr kōlamēṉi (atu) ākiya kuṉṟamē
Civaṉ who assumed the form of a mountin of fire which had a beautiful shape which could not be seen by Māl and Piramaṉ of four faces!
ñālam ... ātiyē
see 1st verse.
{3:108}__10+
{$} aṉṟu muppuram ceṟṟa aḻaka
Beautiful god who destroyed the three cities in the distant past!
kaṉṟu cākkiyar kāṇāt talaivaṉē
the master who could not be known by the cēkkiyar of hardened minds!
niṉ tuṉṟu poṟkaḻal pēṇā arukarait teṉṟa
to scatter the arukar (amaṇar) who do not love your golden feet which are close to each other (teṉṟa: the root is teṉṟu)
[[teṉṟit ticaiticai vīḻac ceṟṟāy (periyāḻvar, tirumoḻi, 5-3-9)]]
ñālam ... ātiyē
[[see 1st verse]]
{3:108}__11+
{$} kūṭal ālavāyk kōṉai viṭaikoṇṭu
getting the permission of the King at ālavāy in Kūṭal.
vāṭal mēṉi amaṇarai vāṭṭiṭa
to destroy the amaṇar of lean bodies
[[as the amaṇar fasted on many important days their bodies became lean; ōvātu iraṇṭu uvavum aṭṭamiyum paṭṭiṉiviṭṭu oḻukkam kāttal - tāvāt tavam eṉṟār (cintāmaṇi, 1547); uṇṇā nōṉpitaṉṉoṭum cūḷuṟṟu (manimēkani, 3-102); uṇṇāmaiyiṉ uyaṅkiya maruṅkiṉ āṭāppaṭivattu āṉṟōr pōla (akam, 123, 1-2).]]
māṭak kāḻic campantaṉ matitta ippāṭal vallavar pākkiyavāḷar(ē)
those who are able to recite these verses esteemed by campantaṉ of Kāḻi which has storeyed buildings, are definitely fortunate.

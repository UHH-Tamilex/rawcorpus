{3:91}__1+
{$} kōṅkamē kuravamē koḻumalarp puṉṉaiyē kokuṭi mullai vēṅkaiyē (2) ñāḻalē vimmupātirikaḷē viravi
the common cungs, bottles flower trees, mast-wood trees of fertile flowers, Vokuṭimulli, east indian Kino trees, fetid cassia trees, big trumpet-flowered trees combining together
eṅkum ōṅku mā kāviri vaṭakarai aṭai karai kuraṅkāṭutuṟai vīṅku nīrc caṭaimuṭiyaṭikaḷār iṭam eṉa virumpiṉār
the god who has copious water on his caṭi into a crown, desired as his place Kuraṅkātutuṟi on the northern bank of the big Kaviri which spreads its swollen floods everywhere
[[Variant reading: (1) nāḻalē]]
{3:91}__2+
{$} mantamāy iḻi matakkaḷiṟṟiḷa maruppoṭu poruppil nalla cantam ār ākiloṭu cātiyiṉ palaṅkaḷum takaiya mōti
Having dashed with force the young tusks of male elephant from which must flows in small quantity, sandal-wood trees and rare eagle-wood trees and fragrant and medicinal nutmeg fruits to fall in the water to make it beautiful
untu mā kāviri vaṭakarai aṭai kuraṅkāṭutuṟai entaiyār iṇai aṭi imaiyavar toḻutu eḻum iyalpiṉār
our father who is in Kuraṅkāṭutuṟi on the northern bank of the great Kāviri which pushes those things, is of such a nature that the celestials who do not wink, worship his two feet and then rise.
{3:91}__3+
{$} muttu mā maṇiyoṭu muḻai vaḷar āramum mukantu nunti ettum mā kāviri vaṭakarai aṭai kuraṅkāṭutuṟai mattam māmati poti caṭaimuṭi aṭikaḷ tammēl cittam ām aṭiyavar
the devotees who fix their thoughts on the god who conceals in his caṭi coiled into a crown dhatura flowers and a big crescent and who is in Kuraṅkāṭutuṟi on the northern bank of the great Kāviri which dashes pushing and taking in a large measure sandal-wood which grows in caves, pearls and other valuable precious stones
civakati peṟuvatu tiṇṇam
will definitely obtin liberation. (It is a popular fallacy that pearls are produced in bamboos)
{3:91}__4+
{$} kaṟiyum māmiḷakoṭu kataliyiṉ palaṅkaḷum kalantu nunti
pushing forward, mingling plintin fruits along with black pepper which is eaten by biting.
eṟiyum mākāviri vaṭa karai aṭai kuraṅkāṭutuṟai
in Kuraṅkātutuṟi on the northere bank of the great Kāviri which throws them on the banks.
maṟi ulām kaiyiṉar malar aṭi toḻutu eḻu maruvum uḷḷak kuṟiyiṉār avar
those who have the aim of worshipping the lotus feet of Civaṉ on whose hand a young deer is leaping
mikak kūṭuvar nīṭu vāṉulakiṉūṭu
will reach everlasting Civalōka, and live permanently there.
{3:91}__5+
{$} kōṭiṭaic corintēṉ ataṉoṭum koṇṭavāy viṇṭa 1. muṉṉīr kāṭuṭaip pīliyum kaṭaṟu uṭaip paṇṭamum kalantu nunti
pushing forward making the honey which flows down from the branches of trees and water drawn by clouds and poured by them, feathers of peacock of the forest and products growing in the forests to mingle.
ōṭu uṭaik kāviri vaṭakarai aṭai kuraṅkāṭu tuṟai pīṭu uṭaic caṭaimuṭi aṭikaḷār iṭam eṉap pēṇiṉār
Civaṉ who has a great cati coiled into a crown desired as his place Kuraṅkāṭutuṟi on the northern bank of the Kaviri which flows swiftly.
[[Variant reading: 1. munnīr]]
{3:91}__6+
{$} kōla mā malaroṭu tūpamum cāntamum koṇṭu pōṟṟi vāliyār vaḻipaṭap poruntiṉār
Civaṉ was pleased with the worship of vāliyār adoring him with beautiful big flowers, incenses and sandal paste
tiruntu māṅkaṉikaḷ unti
pushing fully ripe mango fruits
ālum mā kāviri vaṭakarai aṭai kuraṅkāṭutuṟai nīlamā maṇi miṭaṟṟu aṭikaḷai niṉaiya valviṉaikaḷ vīṭē
one will be free from the irresistible Karmams, if he can meditate on the god who has a neck resembling a sapphire and who is Kuraṅkāṭutuṟi on the north bank of the great Kāviri which flows with a great noise.

VMS9 / {3:91}__PIFI8+
{$} varumtiṟal kāviri vaṭakarai aṭai kuraṅkāṭutuṟai aruntiṟattu iruvarai allal kaṇṭu ōṅkiya aṭikaḷār
the diety who rose as a column of fire to make the two persons, Māl and Piramaṉ who had rare strength, to suffer, and who is in Kuraṅkāṭutuṟi on the northern bank of the Kaviri which comes with great force
porum tiṟal peruṅkai mā urittu umai añcavē oruṅku nōkkip peruntiṟattu anaṅkaṉai anaṅkamā viḻittum perumai pōlum
perhaps it the greatness of the deity to have opened his frontal eye to destroy anankaṉ to lose his form completely who has rare prowess, by looking at him with all his looks, and to make Umi frighten by flaying the elephant with a big trunk which is capable of fighting. (peruntiṟattu anaṅkaṉ with so many handicaps cupid has conquered all the three worlds)

{$} kaṭṭu amaṇ tērarum kaṭukkaḷ tiṉ kaḻuk kaḷum kacivu oṉṟu illāp piṭṭar tam aṟavurai koḷḷalum
Do not accept the religious instructions of the pitiless people who are heretics and who are as stout as the stake for impaling criminals and who eat the gall-nuts, and buddhists and amaṇar who are capable of fabrication. (koḷḷalum-koḷḷaṉmiṉ plural verb in the imperative mood)
peruvaraip paṇṭam unti eṭṭu mā kāviri vaṭakarai aṭai kuraṅkāṭutuṟaic ciṭṭaṉār aṭi toḻac civakati peṟuvatu tiṇṇamām
It is definite that if one worships the feet of the estimable god in Kuraṅkāṭutuṟi on the northern bank of the great Kāviri which reaches the sea pushing the produce of the big mountin in its floods, will obtin the supreme bliss

{$} tāḻ iḷaṅkāviri vaṭakarai aṭai kuraṅkāṭutuṟaip pōḻ iḷamati potipuritaru caṭaimuṭip puṇṇiyaṉai
on Civaṉ who is the embodiment of all virtues, who has a twisted cati coiled into a crown on which a rising crescent is concealed and who resides in Kuraṅkāṭutūṟi on the northern bank of the Kaviri which has soft water which flows and spreads (in the fields)
kāḻiyāṉ arumaṟai ñāṉacampantaṉa karutu pāṭal
the songs which are worthy of meditation in order to obtin good, composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ, a mative of Kāḻi who knows the abstruse vetam.
kōḻaiyā aḻaippiṉum nīṭu vāṉulakiṉūṭē kūṭuvār
will join the Civalōkam which knows no end, even if people recite them with a throat choked with phlegm so that it is difficult to make out the sense

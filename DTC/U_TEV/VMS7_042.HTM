eṟikkum katir vēy utir (1) muttamoṭu ēlam ilavaṅkam takkōlam iñci ceṟikkum puṉaluḷ peytu koṇṭu
having poured into the water hoarding up pearls which drop from bamboos and have shining rays, cardamon, cloves, cubeb and ginger.
maṇṭi
flowing swiftly.
tiḷaittu eṟṟu
dashing aginst the bank without ceasing
ciṟṟāṟu ataṉ kīḻkkaraimēl
on the Eastern bank of Ciṟṟāṟu.
muṟikkum (2) taḻai māmuṭap puṉṉai ñāḻal kurukkattikaḷ mēl kuyil (3) kūval aṟā veṟikkum kalaimā veñcamāk kūṭal vikirtā
Civaṉ who is different from this world and who remins in Veñcamākkūṭal where started stags live and where the indian cuckoos perching on the mastwood tree which has a curved trunk and leaves that can be broken, feted cassia, and common delight of the woods, cry without ceasing!
aṭiyēṉaiyum vēṇṭuti (ē)
desire me also to admit (me) into the company of your devotees.
[[Variant readings: (1) muttamoṭum (2) tāḻai (3) kūvalāl]]

kuḷaṅkaḷ palavum kuḻiyum niṟaiya
to fill many tanks and pits with water.
kuṭa māmaṇi cantaṉamum akilum peytukoṇṭu
storing up in the water moving to and fro, big gems found in the west, sandal wood and eagle-wood.
maṇṭi
flowing swiftly.
tiḷaittu eṟṟu
dashing aginst the banks without ceasing
ciṟṟātuataṉ kīḻkkaraimēl
on the eastern bank of ciṟṟaṟu.
valamkoḷ (1) matil māḷikai kōpuramum maṇimaṇṭapamum ivai mañcu taṉṉuḷ viḷaṅkum mati tōy veñcamayak kūṭal vikirtā
Civaṉ who is different from the world and who remins in vencamākkūṭal where the moon that shines in the cloud touches the wall of fortification of beauty, mansions, towers and beautiful and square or rectangular halls with flat roof.
aṭiyēṉaiyum vēṇṭuti (ē)
see 1st verse.
[[Variant reading: (1) māṭamāḷikai]]

varaimāṉ aṉaiyār
ladies who are like the deer that live in the mountin.
mayilcāyal nallār
who are as tender as the peace cock.
vaḻavēṟkaṇ nallār/ palar vantu iṟaiñca
and many ladies who have eyes like the sharp vēl to come and worship by bowing before god after taking a bath.
tiraiār puṉaluḷ peytukoṇṭu maṇṭit tiḷaittu eṟṟu
storing up many things offered by them in the water full of waves, flowing swiftly and dashing aginst the banks without ceasing.
ciṟṟāṟu ataṉ kīḻkkaraimēl
on the eastern bank of ciṟṟāṟu.
nirai ār kamukum neṭuntāṉ teṅkum kuṟuntāḷ palavum viraiār poḻil cūḻ veñcacamāk kūṭal vikirtā
Civaṉ who is different from the world and who is in vencamākkūṭal surrounded by fragrant gardens which are cool as areca-palms which grow in rows, cocoanut trees with long trunks and jack tree with short trunks are mixed!
aṭiyēṉaiyum vēṇṭuti (ē)
see 1st verse.

{$} paṇ nēr moḻiyāḷai ōr paṅku uṭaiyāy
Civaṉ who has as a half a lady whose words are as sweet as melody types.
paṭukāṭṭu akattu eṉṟum ōr paṟṟu oḻiyāy
you do not leave the attachment towards the cremation ground, always.
taṇ ār (1) akilum nalacāmaraiyum alaittu eṟṟu ciṟṟātu ataṉ kīḻakkaraimēl
on the eastern bank of the ciṟṟāṟu which dashes aginst the bank without ceasing and cuses to wander back and forth cool eagle-wood trees and defectless yak tils.
maṇ ār muḻavum kuḻalum iyampa
muḻavu on the head of which a pasts is smeared for toning it, and flute being played on.
maṭavār naṭam āṭum maṇi araṅkil viṇār matitōy veñcamāk kūṭal vikirtā
Civaṉ who is different from the world, and who is in veñvamākkuṭi which has beautiful stages in which ladies dance and which are so high as to touch the moon in the sky!
aṭiyēṉaiyum vēṇṭuti(ē)
see 1st verse.

god who has a form in which a white men's ear-ring with hole and white women's ear-ring with rolls are moving in the ear hanging low.
(1) ka(ḷ)ḷaiyē kamaḻum malarkkoṉṟaiyiṉāy

kalantārkku aruḷceytiṭum kaṟpakamē
God who is like the divine Kaṟpakam tree to people who have approached him.
pi(ḷ)ḷai veṇpiṟaiyāy! piṟaṅkum caṭaiyāy

piṟavātavaṉē
one who is not born like mortals!
peṟutaṟku ariyāy
one who is difficult to get!
ve(ḷ)ḷai mālviṭaiyāy
one who has a big white bull!

{$} toḻuvārkku eḷiyāy
Civaṉ who is easy of approach to people who worship him!
tuyar tīra niṉṟāy
you were always their companian for their sufferings to go away from them!
curumpu ār malarkkoṉṟai tuṉṟum caṭaiyāy
Civaṉ who has a caṭi which wears the koṉṟi flowers on which the bees hum loudly!
uḻuvārkku ariya viṭai ēṟi
who rides on a bull which is difficult to get for people who plough fields! (ēṟi is a noun)
oṉṉār puram tī eḻa ōṭuvittāy
you cused the fire to spread and engulf the cities of the enemies!
(1) aḻaku ār muḻavu ār oli pāṭaloṭu āṭal aṟā mutukāṭu araṅkā naṭamāṭa vallāy
God who is capable of dancing in the cremation-ground using it as a stage where the music of the beautiful muḻavu and vocal music and dance never cease!
viḻavu ār (1) maṟukiṉ veñcamāk kūṭal vikirtā
Civaṉ who is different from the world and who stays in veñcamākkūtal in whose streets there is always festivals.
aṭiyēṉaiyum vēṇṭuti (ē)
see 1st verse.

striking aginst the forest, mountins and the country-side.
katirmāmaṇi cantaṉamum(m) arilum kēṭaṉ uṟaiyum(m) iṭamtāṉ virumpi
desiring to enter into the lower world where cētāṉ dwells, carrying big gems emitting rays, sandal wood and eagle-wood.

on the eastern bank of the ciṟṟāṟu which dashes aginst the banks without ceasing.
pāṭal muḻavum kuḻalum iyampa
the muḻavu and flute to be played as accompaniments to vocal music.
paṇaittōḷiyar pāṭaloṭu āṭal aṇā vēṭar virumpum veñcamākkūṭal vikirtā
Civaṉ who is different from the world and who remins in veñcamākkūṭal desired by hunters, where the vocal music of ladies who have shoulders like bamboos, and their dances never cease!
aṭiyēṉaiyum vēṇṭuti (ē)
see 1st verse.

koṅkuār malarkkoṉṟai am tāravaṉē
Civaṉ who wears on the chest a garland of koṉṟi flowers full of honey!
koṭukoṭṭi ōr vīṇai uṭaiyavaṉē
who has an instrument called Koṭukoṭṭi and a vīṇi!
poṅku āṭu aravum puṉalum caṭaimēl potiyum puṉitā
the holy god who contins in his caṭi an angry and dancing cobra, and water!
puṉam cūḻntu aḻaku ār tuṅku ār puṉaluḷ peytu koṇṭu maṇṭi
flowing swiftly holding into the excellent and beautiful water, the produce of the forest tract, surrounding the forest region (tuṅku-tuṅkam)
tiḷaittu eṟṟu ciṟṟātu ataṉ kīḻkkarai mēl
on the eastern bank of the ciṟṟāṟu which, being full of floods, dashes aginst the banks without ceasing.
veṅkār vayal cūḻ veñcamāk kūṭal vikirtā
god who is different from the world and who is in veñcāmakkutal surrounded by fields which have an odour cused by fresh water let into them when they are parched and hot

vañcinuṇ iṭaiyar, (1) mayil cāyal aṉṉār, vaṭivēṟkaṇ nallār palar vantu iṟaiñcum veñcamākkūṭal vikirtā aṭiyēṉaiyum vēṇṭutiyē eṉṟu tāṉ virumpi
Civaṉ who is different from the world and whom the ladies who have a small waist like the rattan of South India, who are as tender as the peacock and who have eyes resembling in shape the sharp vēl, bow prostrating himself desiring to be admitted among one of his devotees.
vañciyātu aḷikkum vayal nāvalarkōṉ
the chief of the inhabitants of nāvalūr which has fields whivh yield produce without disappointing farmers.
vaṉappakai appaṉ
the father of vaṉappaki.
vaṉṟoṇṭaṉ coṉṉa ceñcol tamiḻmālaikaḷ pattum vallār civalōkattu iruppatu tiṇṇam (aṉṟu, ē)
It is a continty that those who are able to recite the ten garlands of verses which were composed by vaṉṟoṇṭaṉ, with words which have their direct primary significance, will dwell in civalōkam

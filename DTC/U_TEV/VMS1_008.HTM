{1:8}__1+
{$} puṇṇiyar
Civaṉ who is the embodiment of all virtuous acts.
pūtiyar
the master of all kinds of riches.
puṭaipaṭuvār tam maṉattār
who is in the minds of those who approach him.
tiṅkaṭ kaṇṇiyar
who wears the crescent as a chaplet.
eṉṟu eṉṟu kātalāḷar kaitoḻutu ētta iruntaūr ām
is the place where the pious people prise him with joined hands, without ceasing.
viṇuyar māḷikai māṭavīti viraikamaḻcōlai culāvi eṅkum paṇ iyal pāṭalaṟāta āvūrppacupapatiyīccaram pāṭu: nāvē!
my tongue! sing the fane of pacupati iccaram in āvūr where songs blending with music never ceases, where gardens which spread fragrance surround, and where there are streets of storeys in mansions which rise into the sky.
[[Āvūr is the name of the place; Pacupatīccaram is the name of the temple.]]
{1:8}__2+
{$} muttiyar
one who has the joy of liberation.
mūppu ilar
who does not grow old.
āppiṉuḷḷār
is in pegs;
[[Civaṉ was worshipped in āppu in mayilāppūr, and Kaṉṟappūr.]]
mukkaṇar
one who has three eyes.
takkaṉ taṉ vēḷvicāṭum attiyar eṉṟu eṉṟu aṭiyar ēttum aiyaṉ aṇaṅkoṭu irunta ūrām
the place where our father who was with a divine damsel and whom devotees prise often as the supplicant who destroyed the sacrifice of Takkaṉ;
[[attiyar supplicants who beg for some thing;]]
[[PP: attikaḷ ātal kaṇṭālum (tirunūṟṟantāti, 44)]]
tottu iyalum poḻil māṭu vaṇṭu tutaintu eṅkum tūmatup pāya
when honey is flowing everywhere in the gardens having bunches of flowers in which bees are swarming.
kōyil (1) pattimaip pāṭal aṟāta āvūrp pacupatiyīccaram pāṭu; nāvē!
my tongue! sing the fame of Pacupati Īccaram in Āvūr where in the temple songs which produce piety never stop.
[[Variant reading: (1) patticai]]
{1:8}__3+
{$} poṅkivarum puṉal ceṉṉivaittār
Civaṉ placed on his head the water which came foaming and raging.
pōmvaḻivantu
being born as human beings who can save themselves from being born agin.
iḻivu ēṟṟam āṉār, iṅku uyar ñāṉattar vāṉōr ēttum iṟaiyavar eṉṟum iruntaūr ām
the place where god who is prised by those who become low by doing evil acts and elevate themselves by doing good acts, dwells always.
teṅku uyar cōlai cēr ālai cāli tiḷaikkum viḷaivayal cērum poykaip paṅkaya maṅkai virumpum āvūrp pacupatiyīccaram pāṭu; nāvē
my tongue! sing the prises of pacupati īccaram in ācūr which is desired by the goddess of wealth seated in a lotus, and has crowded natural tanks, fields where superior paddy is produced always, and sugar-cane crushing mills, and groves of cocoanut trees growing tall.

{$} tēvi ōr kūṟiṉar
Civaṉ has Umi on one half.
ēṟu atu ēṟum celviṉar
is rich enough to ride on a bull.

nalkuravu eṉṉai nīkkum āviyar
is like the life within me who removes my poverty.
antaṇar allal tīrkkum appaṉār
the father who removes the sufferings of the brahmins.
aṅkē amarnta ūr ām
the place where he dwells with desire.
pū iyalum poḻil vācam vīca
when the gardens which have flowers spreading their fragrance.
purikuḻalār cuvaṭu oṟṟi
and when the ladies with curly tresses of hir measure time by the impressions of their toes.
pā iyal pāṭal aṟāta āvūrp pacupatiyīccaram pāṭu; nāvē
my tongue! sing the prises of pacupati īccaram in āvūr where the songs which have a sound that travels a great distance, never cease.
{1:8}__5+
{$} intu aṇaiyum caṭaiyār
Civaṉ has a caṭi with which the crescent comes into contact.
viṭaiyār
has a bull.
ippiṟappu eṉṉai aṟukka vallār
is able to grant me liberation as he can completely destroy this birth.
vantu aṇaintu iṉṉicai pāṭuvārpāl maṉṉiṉar maṉṉi irunta ūr ām
the place where Civaṉ who is always with those who come near him and sing sweet music, without leaving them, stays permanently.
kontu aṇaiyum kuḻalār viḻavil kūṭṭam iṭai iṭai cērum vītip pantu aṇaiyum viralār tam āvūrp pacupatiyīccaram pāṭu; nāvē
my tongue! sing the prises of pacupati īccaram in āvūr where ladies who play with balls in the streets and where the crowds of ladies who adorn their tresses with bunches of flowers gather at frequent intervals during festivals.
{1:8}__6+
{$} kuṟṟam aṟuttār
Civaṉ removed all the faults committed by his devotees.
kuṇattiṉ uḷḷār
is wherever good qualities are found.
kumpiṭuvār tamakku aṉpu ceyvār
will love those who worship him with joined hands.
oṟṟai viṭaiyiṉar
has a single bull.
neṟṟikkaṇṇār
has an eye on the forehead.
uṟaipatiyākum
the dwelling place of such a god.
cuṟṟiya vācaliṉ mātar viḻāc coṟ kavipāṭa
when the ladies sing songs composed with good words in the portals surrounding the houses, during the festivals.
nitāṉam nalkap paṟṟiyakaiyiṉar vāḻum āvūrp pacupatiyīccaram pāṭu; nāvē
my tongue! sing the greatness of pacupati īccaram in āvūr where liberal persons are having gold in their hands to offer as prizes to the singers, live.
{1:8}__7+
{$} nīṟu uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has smeared the holy ash on his body.
neṭumāl vaṇaṅkum nimircaṭaiyār
has a caṭi which stands upright and who is worshipped by the tall Māl.
niṉaivārtam uḷḷam kūṟuuṭaiyār
has the hearts of those who meditate on him, as his share.
uṭaikōvaṇattār
has a loin-cloth only as his dress.
kuvalayam ētta irunta ūr ām
the place where he stays to be prised by the people of this world.
tāṟu uṭai vāḻaiyil kūḻai manti tarukaṉi uṇṭu miṇṭiṭṭu
female monkeys of short tils having eaten the ripe fruits in the cluster of the fruits of the plintin tree, push.
iṉattaip pāṟiṭap pāyntu payilum āvūrppacupatiyīccarampāṭu; nāvē
my tongue! sing the fame of pacupati īccaram in āvūr where the monkeys pounce upon their kind to flee and roam about in the gardens.
{1:8}__8+
{$} veṇtalai mālai viravippūṇṭa mey uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has a body in which he wears garlands of white skulls mixed with flower-garlands.
viṟal ār arakkaṉ vaṇṭu amar pūmuṭiceṟṟu ukanta maintariṭam
the place of the strong god who became prominent by crushing the crowns on which bees settle, of the arakkaṉ who had previously victories to his credit.
eṅkum vaḷam ōṅki
riches being abundant everywhere.
(1) kaṇṭavar cintaik karuttiṉ mikkār
is uppermost in the idea of the minds of those who had seen him in their vision.
kati aruḷ' eṉṟu kai ārakkūppi
joining their hands to their satisfaction say, give us liberation?
paṇṭu alar koṇṭu payilum āvūrp pacupatiyīccarampāṭu nāvē
my tongue! sing the greatness of Pacupati Īccaram in Āvūr where such people crowd holding fruits and flowers;

{1:8}__9+
{$} mālum ayaṉum vaṇaṅki nēṭa maṟṟu avarukku eri āki nīṇṭacīlam aṟivu aritu āki niṉṟa cemmaiyiṉār avar cērum ūr ām
the place of the eminent Civaṉ whose easy accessibility in rising tall as a column of fire before those two, Māl and Ayaṉ, who bowed to him and searched for him everywhere, which act could not be understood by little minds.
kōlaviḻāviṉ araṅku atu ēṟi
ascending upon the stage with decorations, during festivals.
koṭiiṭai mātarkaḷ maintarōṭum pāl eṉavē moḻintu ēttum āvūrp pacupatiyīccaram pāṭu; nāvē
my tongue! sing the greatness of pacupati īccaram in āvūr where ladies with slender waist like creepers in company with youths prise god, speaking words as sweet as milk.
{1:8}__10+
{$} piṉṉiya tāḻ caṭaiyār
Civaṉ has a interwoven caṭi which hangs low.
pitaṟṟum pētaiyar ām camaṇ cākkiyarkaḷ taṉ iyalum urai koḷḷakillāc caivar iṭam
the place of the civaṉ who is not the subject matter of the words which ignorant camaṇar and cākkēyar prate about him.
taḷavu ēṟucōlait tuṉṉiya mātarum maintar tāmum cuṉaiyiṭai mūḻki
the young girls and the youth who crowd in the gardens which has plenty of red-coloured arabian jasmine, bathe in the tanks.

[[PP_X: ciralvāy uṟṟa taḷaviṉ

toṭarnta cintaip paṉṉiya pāṭalpayilum āvūrp pacupatiyīccaram pāṭu; nāvē
my tongue! sing the greatness of pacupati īccaram in āvūr where (they) practise songs in prise of Civaṉ with concentration and without distraction from outside things.
{1:8}__11+
{$} eṇticaiyārum vaṇaṅki ēttum emperumāṉai
on our god, Civaṉ before whom all the people in the eight directions bow and prise.
eḻilkoḷ āvūrppaṇṭu uriyār cilar toṇṭarpōṟṟum pacupatiyīccarattu ātitaṉmēl
and on the one who is the beginning of all things in pacupati īccaram in beautiful āvūr where he is worshipped by some devotees who have the honour of being such people from generation to generation.
kaṇṭalkaḷ miṇṭiya kāṉal kāḻik kavuṇiyaṉ ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa koṇṭu
with the help of the verses composed by Ñāṉacampantaṉ born in Kavuṇiya Kōttiram, in Kāḻi which has sea-shore groves in which mangroves grow densely.
iṉitā icai pāṭi āṭik kūṭum avar uṭaiyārkaḷ vāṉē
those who sing with pleasure and dance crowding there, have heaven as their residence after death.

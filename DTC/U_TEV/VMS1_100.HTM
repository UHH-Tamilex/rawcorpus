[[This shrine is famous as the shrine of Murukaṉ in Caṅkam literature works such as Paripāṭal and Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭi and Akam.]]

{1:100}__1+
{$} nīṭu alar cōti veṇpiṟaiyōṭu nirai (1) koṉṟai cūṭalaṉ
Civaṉ wears on his head a row of koṉṟi flowers along with a white crescent which spreads its light to a long distance.
(2) antic cuṭar eriēntic cuṭukāṉil
in the cremation ground which is scorching, holding a bright fire, in the evening.
am (3) colaṇi iḻaiyāḷai orupākam āṭalaṉ
dances placing on one half a lady who speaks beautiful words and wears beautiful ornaments.
pāṭalaṉ mēya naṉṉakar (pōlum) paraṅkuṉṟē
Paraṅkuṉṟu is the good city of Civaṉ who sings and dwells there with desire.
[[pōlum' is an expletive. (also in verses 2,3,6 and 7)]]
[[(Variant readings: (1) koṉṟaic cūṭalaṉ (2) antīccuṭareri (3) collaṇiyiḻaiyāḷai]]
{1:100}__2+
{$} aṅkam ōr āṟumceytu arumaṟai nāṉkum(m) aruḷceytu
having revealed the six aṅkams and the four abstruse vētams.
poṅku veṇṇūlum poṭi aṇi mārpil polivittu
cusing the bright white sacred thread to shine on the chest which is adorned with sacred ash.
tiṅkaḷum pāmpum tikaḻcaṭai vaittu
placing on the shining caṭi the crescent and the cobra.
ōr tēṉmoḻi paṅkiṉaṉ mēya naṉṉakar (pōlum) paraṅkuṉṟē
paraṅkuṉṟu is the good city where Civaṉ who has on one half a lady whose words are as sweet as honey, dwells with desire.
{1:100}__3+
{$} nīr iṭam koṇṭa nimircaṭai taṉmēl nirai (1) koṉṟai cīr iṭam koṇṭa emiṟai (pōlum)
our Lord who had a renowned place for rows of koṉṟi on his erect cati on which water had its own place.
cēytu āya ōr uṭampuḷḷē umai orupākam uṭaṉ āki
being united with Umi as a half in the body which is red in colour.
pāriṭam pāṭa iṉitu uṟai kōyil paraṅkuṉṟē
Paraṅkuṉṟu is the temple where he dwells with pleasure when the pūtams sing.
[[Variant reading: 1.koṉṟaiccīr]]
{1:100}__4+
{$} vaḷarpūṅkōṅkam mātaviyōṭu (1) mallikaik kuḷirpūñcāral vaṇṭu aṟai cōlaip paraṅkuṉṟam
Paraṅkuṉṟam which has gardens where bees hum noisily in the cool and beautiful slopes where the growing common cung, common delight of the woods and jasmine, spread their fragrance.
taḷirpōl mēṉit taiyal nallāḷōṭu orupākam naḷirpūṅkoṉṟai cūṭiṉaṉ mēya nakar (tāṉ, ē)
is the city where Civaṉ who wore cool koṉṟi flowers, being united with a good and young lady whose body has the colour of the tender shoots of leaves.
[[Variant reading: 1. mallikai kuḷir]]
{1:100}__5+
{$} poṉiyal koṉṟai poṟikiḷar nākam puricaṭait tuṉṉiya cōti ākiya īcaṉ
the Lord of the universe who is the light in whose twisted caṭi koṉṟi which is golden in colour and cobra with conspicuous spots stay close to one another.
tolmaṟai paṉṉiya pāṭalaṉ āṭalaṉ mēya paraṅkuṉṟai uṉṉiya cintai uṭaiyārkku
to those who always think of Paraṅkuṉṟu where Civaṉ sang the ancient Vētam with skill and performed dances.
illai uṟu nōy(ē)
the disease that may attack them will not do so.
{1:100}__6+
{$} kaṭai neṭumāṭak kaṭiaraṇmūṉṟum kaṉalmūḻka
all the three fortifications which were well-guarded and had tall storeys and entrances to be engulfed by fire.
toṭai navilkiṉṟa villiṉaṉ
Civaṉ has a bow with which he practised shooting an arrow.
(1) antic cuṭukāṉil
in the evening at the cremationground.
puṭainavil pūtam pāṭa niṉṟu āṭum porucūlappaṭai navilvāṉtaṉ naṉṉakar (pōlum) paraṅkuṉṟē
Paraṅkuṉṟu is the good city of Civaṉ who practises the trident which is useful for fighting and who dances when the pūtams which crowd by his side, sing.
[[Variant reading: 1. antīccuṭukāṉil]]
{1:100}__7+
{$} ayil uṭaivēlōr
Civaṉ has a sharp trident
aṉal pulku (1) kaiyil ampu oṉṟāl eyilpaṭa eyta em iṟai mēya iṭam (pōlum)
the place desired by our Lord who shot a single arrow to destroy the forts with his hand in which there is fire.
mayil peṭai pulki mānaṭam āṭum vaḷar cōlaip payil peṭai vaṇṭu pāṭal aṉāta paraṅkuṉṟē
is Paraṅkuṉṟu where the humming of the bees joining with their females in the flourishing gardens never stops and where the male peacock performs a great dance embracing its female.
[[Variant reading: (1) kaiyil]]
{1:100}__8+
{$} maittaku mēṉi vāḷ arakkaṉtaṉ makuṭaṅkaḷ pattiṉa tiṇtōḷ irupatum ceṟṟāṉ paraṅkuṉṟaic cittam (atu) oṉṟi
concentrating the mind on Paraṅkuṉṟu of Civaṉ who destroyed all the twenty shoulders and ten crowns of the cruel arakkaṉ who had a body like the black cloud.
ceykaḻaluṉṉi
thinking of the feet which do everything.
civaṉ eṉṟu nittalum ētta
to prise dily that Kuṉṟu as Civaṉ himself,
tolviṉai nammēl nillā(ē)
our acts done in many previous births will not stay with us.
{1:100}__9+
{$} munti ivvaiyam tāviya mālum moyoḷi untiyil vantu aṅku arumaṟai īnta uravōṉum
Piramaṉ of knowledge who gave out the abstruse Vētam-s to the world, coming into existence in the nevel of great brightness and Māl who measured this world long ago.
cintaiyiṉālum terivu aritu ākit tikaḻcōti
the shining light which could not be known to both of them even by their minds.
pantu (1) iyal amkai maṅkai ōrpaṅkaṉ paraṅkuṉṟē
this is Paraṅkuṉṟu of the Lord who has on one half a young lady who has a ball in her hand to play with.
[[Variant reading: (1)iyaluṅkai]]
{1:100}__10+
{$} kuṇṭāy muṟṟum tirivār
amaṇar being persons of completely low character and wandering naked.
kūṟai mey pōrtta miṇṭāy miṇṭar pēciya pēccu mey alla
the talks by the buddhists who are strong and ignorant and who cover their bodies with a cloth, do not contin any truth.
paṇṭu āl nīḻal mēviya īcaṉ paraṅkuṉṟait (1) toṇṭāl ētti
to prise by performing devoted service, Paraṅkuṉṟu of the Lord of the universe who sat under the shade of the banyan tree in the distant past.
tolviṉai nammēl nillā (ē)
acts done in previous births will not stay with us.
[[Variant reading: 1. toṇṭāy ētta]]
{1:100}__11+
{$} paṭam malinākam araikku acaittāṉtaṉ paraṅkuṉṟai
on Paraṅkuṉṟu of Civaṉ who tied in the waist a cobra of spreading hood.
taṭammali poykaic caṇpaimaṉ ñāṉacampantaṉ toṭaimali pāṭal pattum vallār
those who are able to recite all the ten verses which contins the five modes of versification done by Ñāṉacampantaṉ, the chief of Canpi, which has a natural tank, Pirama tīrttam, for which there are many banks.
tamtuyar pōy
as their sufferings leave them.
viṭam mali kaṇṭaṉ aruḷpeṟum taṉmai mikkōrē
they are geat persons in the nature of receiving the grace of Civaṉ who has a neck in which poison is conspicuous.

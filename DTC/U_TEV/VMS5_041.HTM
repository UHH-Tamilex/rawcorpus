kōvaṇam uṭaiyar
Civaṉ has a loin-cloth as his dress
oṉṟum kuṟaivilar
he is not wanting in any thing
paṭai koḷ pāriṭam cūḻnta paiññīliyār
Civaṉ in Paiññīli who is surrounded by pūtams holding weapons.
caṭaiyil kaṅkai taritta caturarai aṭaiya vallavarkku illai avalam
there will be no sufferings to those who are capable of approaching the able god who bore on his caṭi Kaṅki
[[ñīli
this shrine got this name as ñīli is a variety of plantin tree under which the liṅgam is established]]
{5:41}__2+
{$}
matta mā malar cūṭiya maintaṉār
the strong god who wore big flowers of dhatura flowers.
cittarāyt tirivār viṉai tīrppar
will remove the Karmams of those who meditate on him in their minds.
pattar tām toḻutu ēttu paiññīli em attaṉait toḻa vallavar nallarē
those who are capable of worshipping our master in piññili which pious people worship with joined hands and prise, are good people.
{5:41}__3+
{$}
viḻutu cūlattaṉ
Civaṉ who has a trident to which fat is sticking
veṇ maḻu vāṭpaṭaik kaḻutu tuñcu iruḷ kāṭṭakattu āṭalāṉ
who dances in the cremation ground at night when even the pey sleeps, and has a bright battle-axe.
paḻutu oṉṟu iṉṟip paiññīlipparamaṉait toḻutu celpavar tam viṉai tūḷiyē
the Karmams of those who spend their lives worshipping the supreme god in piññili without any fault, will be reduced to dust
{5:41}__4+
{$}
mālum piramaṉum tammil oṉṟi
by Māl and Piramaṉ joining among themselves.
niṉṟa cūḻal aṟivu ariyāṉ iṭam (paiññīli)
piññili is the place of the god whose place of stay could not be known
ceṉṟu pāriṭam ēttu paiññīliyuḷ eṉṟum mēviyirunta aṭikaḷē
the god dwells with desire always in piññili to which the putams go and worship him.
{5:41}__5+
{$}
vēḻattiṉ uri pōrtta vikirtaṉār
the god different from the world who covered himself with the skin of an elephant
tāḻac ceñcaṭaimēl piṟai vaittavar
he placed the crescent to stay on his red caṭi.
tāḻait taṇ poḻil cūḻnta paiññīliyār
who is in piññili surrounded by cool gardens having screw-pines (tāḻai may also mean cocoanut trees; this meaning will be more appropriate to the region in which the temple is located)
yāḻiṉ pāṭṭai ukanta aṭikaḷē
is the god who rejoiced to hear the songs played on a lute.
{5:41}__6+
{$}
kuṇṭupaṭṭu
being low
kuṟi aṟiyāccamaṇ miṇṭaroṭu paṭuttu
having fallen in the company of the camaṇar who do not know the aim in life
nāṉ uyyap pōntu
and I having left them to save myself
kaṇṭam kār vayal cūḻnta paiññīli em aṇṭavāṇaṉ aṭi aṭaintu 1. uyntaṉē
I saved myself by approaching the feet of our god who dwells in the universe, in piññili surrounded by black fields in which sugar-cane grows (kaṇṭam : jaggery; it here means the cane from which it is obtined)
[[Variant reading: 1. uynteṉē;
This is better reading, as uyntēṉ has become uynteṉ; kuṟukkalvikāram shortening of the long vowel into a short one]]
{5:41}__7+
{$}
varippai āṭu aravu āṭṭi
cusing the cobra which has the nature of dancing and which has lines and a hood, to dance.
matakari urippai mūṭiya uttamaṉār uṟai tiruppaiññīli ticai toḻuvārkaḷ
those who worship tiruppiññili where the superior god who has covered himself with a skin of a frenzied elephant looking towards the direction in which it is located
pōy
entering into heaven after death
vāṉavarōṭu iṉitāka iruppar
will be in the company of the celestials, happily.
{5:41}__8+
{$}
kōṭal kōṅkam puṟavu aṇi mullaimēl, pāṭal vaṇṭu icai kēṭkum paiññīliyār
the god in piññili where the sound of the bees can be heard like music huming on the arabian jasmine flowers which beautify the forest region which has white species of malabar glory lily and common cung
pēṭum āṇum piṟar aṟiyātatu ōr aṭikaḷ
is the god whom others cannot find out whether he is hermophrodite and male. (aṟiyātatu ōr aṭikaḷ is the syntactical link)
āṭu nākam acaitta aṭikaḷ
is the god who tied a dancing cobra.
{5:41}__9+
{$}
kār ulām malark koṉṟai am tāriṉāṉ
Civaṉ who wears a garland of koṉṟi flowers which blossom in winter when clouds wander in the sky.
vār ulām mulai maṅkai ōr paṅkiṉaṉ
has as a half a lady on whose breasts a bodice is tied loosely
tēr ulām poḻil cūḻnta paiññīli em ār kilā amutai aṭaintu uymmiṉ
(People of the world!) save yourselves by approaching our insatiate nectar (civan) in piññili surrounded by gardens where the southern balmy breeze is moving.
[[tēr it is a poetical covention to describe the southern balmy breeze as the chariot of Kāmaṉ (cupid); it may mean also the chariots of those who enjoy staying in gardens in summer]]

tarukkic ceṉṟu taṭavarai paṟṟalum nerukki ūṉṟa
when (Civaṉ) pressed down with his toe to crush (iravaṇaṉ) who seized the big mountin going there with pride.
civaṉaiyē niṉaintu arakkaṉ pāṭa
when the arakkaṉ sang the prises thinking of Civaṉ only and nothing else.
aruḷum emmāṉ iṭam irukkai ñīli eṉpārkku iṭar illai
there will be no sufferings to those who say that ñīli is the place of residence to our master who grantedd his grace (to arakkan)

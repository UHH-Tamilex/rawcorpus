{1:132}__1+
{$} ēricaiyum vaṭavāliṉkīḻ iruntu
sitting under the banyan tree which has the beauty of spreading.
aṅku īriruvarkku iraṅkiniṉṟu
taking pity on four sages there.
nēriya nāṉ maṟaipporuḷai uraittu, oḷicēr neṟi aḷittōṉ niṉṟa kōyil
the temple where Civan who showed the illumined path, expounding the right and proper meaning of the four Vētam-s, stayed permanently.
pār icaiyum paṇṭitarkaḷ palnāḷum payiṉṟu ōtum ōcaikēṭṭu
hearing the sound when on many days men of learning and erudition of this world, practise and recite.
vērimali poḻiṟkiḷḷai vētaṅkaḷ poruṭcollum miḻalai ām
is Miḻali where the parrots in the gardens having much honey, expound the meaning of the Vētam-s.
{1:132}__2+
{$} poruppē mattāka
using the mountin, mantaram as the churning staff.
poṟi aravam atu cuṟṟi
and winding the serpent, Vācuki with spots, as a cord for turning the churn staff.
puttēḷir kūṭi
the tēvar combining (with acurar)
maṟikaṭalaik kaṭantiṭṭaviṭam uṇṭakaṇṭattōṉ maṉṉum kōyil
the temple where Civaṉ who stationed the poison that rose when the ocean of milk with rolling waves was churned, in the neck, stays permanently.
ceṟi (1) itaḻt tāmarait tavicil tikaḻntu ōṅkum ilaikkuṭaikkīḻ
under the umbrella of the shining leaves of the lotus and using the lotus which has dense petals, as a seat.
cey ār cennel veṟikatirc cāmaraiiraṭṭa iḷa aṉṉam vīṟṟirukkum miḻalai ām
is Miḻali where the young swans sit majestically when the corns of red and superior Variety of paddy of fragrance, wave alternately on opposite sides as fly-whisk in a procession.
[[Variant reading: (1) itaṭṭāmarai]]
{1:132}__3+
{$} eḻuntu ulakai nalintu uḻalum avuṇarkaḷ tampuram mūṉṟum
all the three cities of the avunar who rose in war and wandered harassing people of the world.
eḻil kaṇṇāṭi uḻuntu uruḷum aḷavaiyiṉ
in such a short time that a seed of black-gram would take to roll down a beautiful glass.
oḷeri koḷa veñcilaivaḷaittōṉ uṟaiyum kōyil
the temple where Civaṉ who bent the cruel bow, to catch fire.
koḻuntaraḷam nakai kāṭṭa
the mature pearls appear like the teeth of ladies.
cōkanatam mukam kāṭṭa
the lotus appears like the face.
kutittu nīrmēl viḻunta kayalviḻikāṭṭa
the carps which fall on the water jumping appear like the eyes.
viṟpavaḷam vāykāṭṭum miḻalaiām
is Miḻali where bright coral appears like the lips.

{$} (1) uraicērum eṇpattu nāṉku nūṟāyiramām yōṉi pētam (2) niraicērappaṭaittu
having created eighty-four lakhs of species of living beings which are famous, in the regular order.
avaṟṟiṉ uyirkku uyirāy aṅkaṅkē niṉṟāṉkōyil
the temple of Civaṉ who stood as the soul of those souls in their bodies.
(3) varai cērum mukil muḻava
the clouds that are on the peak of mountins thunder gently like muḻavu.
mayilkaḷ pala naṭamāṭa
the many peacocks dance spreading their tils.
vaṇṭu pāṭa
the bees hum music.
(4) viraicēr poṉ iḻitara
the koṉṟi trees give golden flowers which have fragrance.
meṉkāntaḷkai ēṟkum miḻalai ām
is miḻali where the soft malabar glory lilies which resemble the hand, strentch out to receive them in supplication.
[[The thunder of the clouds is compared to muḻavu, peacocks are dancers, bees are singers, the koṉṟi tree sheds its flowers like gold and the flowers of the malabar glory lily are hands]]

{1:132}__5+
{$} kāṇumāṟu ariyaperumāṉ āki
Being the god who is difficult to be seen by the physical eyes.
kālam āy
being the past, the present and the future tenses.
kuṇaṅkaḷ mūṉṟāy
being the three qualities which are cattuvam, rajas, and tamas.
pēṇum mūṉṟu uru ākippēr ulakam paṭaittu aḷikkum (1) perumāṉ kōyil
the temple of Civaṉ who assumes the three forms of Piramaṉ, Māl, and Uruttiraṉ to create, protect and destroy the great worlds.
iṟaiñcīr eṉṟu
bow to Civaṉ.
tāṇuvāy niṉṟa paratattuvaṉai
who is the supreme ultimate reality who stood firm as a pillar.
uttamaṉai
and the supreme god.
vēṇuvārkoṭi viṇṇōr tamai vaḷippapōl ōṅku
is Miḻali where the long flags which are fixed to bamboos rise high as if they summon the celestials.
[[Variant reading: (1) pemmāṉ]]
{1:132}__6+
{$} akaṉ amarnta aṉpiṉarāy
having sincere love towards god in their hearts.
aṟupakai ceṟṟu
having destroyed the six emotions (the inner enemies) which disturb the mind and corrupt the soul.
aim pulaṉum aṭakki
having controlled the five senses.
ñāṉam pukal uṭaiyōr
those who have spiritual knowledge of Civaṉ as their refuge.
tam uḷḷap puṇṭarikattuḷ irukkum purāṇar kōyil
the temple of the ancient god who dwells in the heart which is like a lotus flower.
takavu uṭai nīr maṇittalattuc caṅku uḷavarkkum tikaḻa
when the species of conches which are like relations are prominent in the places where the precious stones are fixed.
calacat tīyil
in the fire of the lotus flowers (the red lotus flower is compared to fire).
mika uṭaiya puṉku malarp pori aṭṭa maṇam ceyyum miḻalai ām
is Miḻali where marriages are performed when the large numbers of indian beech trees shed their flowers which are like parched paddy.
[[the flowers of puṉku are compared to parched paddy.]]

{1:132}__7+
{$} āṟu āṭucaṭaimuṭiyaṉ
Civaṉ has on his caṭi coiled into a crown a river (Kaṅki)
aṉal āṭum malarkkaiyaṉ
has on his hand which is like the lotus fire.
imayappāvai kūṟu āṭutiruvuruvaṉ
has a sacred body in which there is a half of the beautiful dughter of the Imayam.
kūttāṭum kuṇam uṭaiyōṉ kuḷirum kōyil
the temple where Civaṉ who has the nature of dancing stays.

cēṟu āṭu ceṅkaḻunīrt tātu āṭi
wallowing in the pollen of the purple indian water-lily which grows in the mire.
matu uṇṭu
and drinking the honey in it.
civanta vaṇṭu
the bee which has become red.
vēṟu āya uru ākic cevvaḻi naṟpaṇpāṭum miḻalaiām
is Miḻali where, its colour having become changed, hum like the good melody type of cevvaḻi.
{1:132}__8+
{$} karuppam mikum uṭal aṭarttu
attacking with a body which was in the mother's uterus for a long time.
kāl ūṉṟi
fixing the legs firmly.
kaimaṟittu
and checking with the forearm.
kayilai eṉṉum poruppu eṭukkal uṟum arakkaṉ poṉmuṭi tōḷneritta viral puṉitarkōyil
the temple of the spotless Civaṉ who pressed down with his toe the beautiful crowns and shoulders of the arakkaṉ who tried to lift the mountin, Kayili.
taruppam miku calantaraṉ taṉ uṭal taṭinta cakkarattai vēṇṭi
Desiring to get the discus which cut into two the body of calamtaraṉ who was on the verge of pride.
īṇṭu
in the place.
mālviruppoṭu vaḻipāṭuceyya iḻivimāṉamcērmiḻalaiām
is Miḻali when Māl worshipped with love, turret of the temple surrounding the cell in which the chief image is placed, descended from heaven and reached this temple, which Māl dedicated to Civaṉ.
{1:132}__9+
{$} centaḷir māmalarōṉum tirumālum ēṉamōṭu aṉṉam āki
when Piramaṉ who is seated in a big red lotus flower which has a leaf, and Tirumāl, transformed themselves into a pig and a swan (Māl became a pig; Piramaṉ became a swan).
antam aṭi kāṇātē avar ētta veḷippaṭṭōṉ amarum kōyil
the temple where Civaṉ manifested himself when they both prised him, as they could not find out the head and feet, stays.
pantiyiṉ nāṉ maṟaivaḻiyē pulparappi neycamitai kaiyil koṇṭu
holding in the hands sacrificial fuel and ghee and spreading the sacred kus grass in rows according to rules lid down in the four vētams.
ventaḻaliṉ vēṭṭu
performing sacrifices in the fire.
ulakil mikaaḷippōr cērumūr miḻalai ām
is Miḻali where brahmins who protect the world, live crowded.
{1:132}__10+
{$} eṇ iṟanta amaṇarkaḷum
countless amaṇar.
iḻi toḻilcēr cākkiyarum
and buddhists who do low acts.
eṉṟum taṉṉai naṇṇa ariyavakai mayakki
confusing them so that they cannot approach him always.
taṉ aṭiyārkku aruḷpuriyum nātaṉ kōyil
the temple of the master who grants his grace to his devotees.
paṇ amarummeṉmoḻiyār pālakaraip pārāṭṭum ōcaikēṭṭu viyappu eyti
hearing the sound of ladies of soft words which are like melody-types fondling their infants, being wonder-struck.
viṇṇavarkaḷ vimāṉattōṭum iḻiyum miḻalai ām
is Miḻali where the celestials descend to the earth with their self-moving serial cars.
{1:132}__11+
{$} miṉiyalum maṇimāṭam miṭaivīḻi miḻalaiyāṉ viraiārpātam ceṉṉimicaikkoṇṭu oḻukum cirapurakkōṉ
the chief of the inhabitants of cirapuram who conducts himself bearing the fragrant feet of Civaṉ in Miḻali where storeys set with bright precious stones are crowded.
ceḻumaṟaikaḷpayilum nāvaṉ
who practises with his tongue the vētams which are full of meanings.
paṉṉiya cīr miku ñāṉacampantaṉ parintu uraitta pattum ētti iṉṉicaiyāl pāṭavallār
those who are able to sing with sweet music prising Civaṉ with the ten verses which were composed with love by Ñāṉacampantaṉ who has fame fit to be repeated.
irunilattil īcaṉ eṉṉum iyalpiṉōrē
will have the nature of being considered as Civaṉ in this wide world.

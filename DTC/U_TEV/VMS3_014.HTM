{3:14}__1+
{$} aṭikaḷ
God
āriṭam pāṭilar
sang the Vētam-s which he revealed to the sages
kāṭu alāl ōr iṭam kuṟaivu ilar
has nothing wanting in abode except the cremation-ground.
kōvaṇam uṭaiyar
has a loin-cloth as his dress
nīr iṭam caṭai
caṭi is the place of the water (Kaṅki)
viṭai ūrti
the bull is his vehicle
nittalum pāriṭam paṇi ceyum
the pūtams do service to him dily
payil paiññīliyē
his place of stay is piññili.
{3:14}__2+
{$} 1. maruvilār tiripuram eriya mālvarai paruvilāk kuṉitta paiññīli mēvalāṉ
Civaṉ who is staying in piññili, bent as a big bow the big mountin, to burn the three cities of the enemies.
uru ilāṉ
has no form of his own except what the devotees meditate upon.
perumaiyai uḷam koḷāta at 2. tiruvilār avar taṉait teruṭṭalākumē
is it possible to convince those who have not the good fortune to grasp in thier minds his greatness?
[[Variant reading: 1. maruvalār 2. tiruvilātavarkaḷai]]
{3:14}__3+
{$} am curumpu aṇimalar amutam māntittēṉ pañcuram payiṟṟu paiññīli mēvalāṉ
Civaṉ who is in piññili where the beautiful curumpu having drunk the honey which is equal to nectar, found in flowers, teach the bees, called tēṉ the melody of pañcuram
veñcuram taṉil umai veruva vantatu ōr kuñcaram paṭa uri pōrtta koḷkai
what is the idea in covering himself with a skin killingthe elephant which came in the hot forest, to make Umi trembles?
{3:14}__4+
{$} kōṭalkaḷ puṟavu aṇi kollai mullaimēl pāṭal vaṇṭu icai mural payil paiññīliyār
Civaṉ in piññīli where humming bees buz without ceasing on the arabian jasmine flowers in the woods adjoining the forest region which are made beautiful by the white species of malabar glory lilies.
pēṭu alar āṇ alar; peṇṇum allatu ōr āṭalai ukanta em aṭikaḷ allarē
is not our deity who felt glad to dance, and who is neither hermaphroditi, nor male nor female.
{3:14}__5+
{$} viḻiyilā naku talai, viḷaṅku iḷam piṟai cuḻiyil ār varu puṉal cūḻtāṅkiṉāṉ
Civaṉ who bore on his coiled caṭi a lughing skull without eyes, shining young crescent, and water with eddies.
paḻi ilār paravu paiññīli pāṭalāṉ kiḻiyilār vēṇmaiyaik keṭukkal ākumē
is it possible to destroy he intimacy of those who have as the bag contining money, Civaṉ who stays singing in piññili which is prised by blemishless devotees?
{3:14}__6+
{$} viṭaiyu�ṭuk koṭi valaṉ ēnti veṇmaḻup paṭaiyuṭaik kaṭavuḷ paiññīli mēvalāṉ
Civaṉ who is piññili, the god who has a white battle-axe, and who holds on the right side a banner on which the bull is drawn.
tuṭiyiṭaikkalai alkulāḷ ōr pākamāccaṭaiyiṭaip puṉal vaitta caturaṉ allaṉē
is he not a clever person who placed water on his caṭi, when there was already a lady who wear a cloth on her waist and which is as slender as the centre of a drum tapering towards the middle portion?
{3:14}__7+
{$} tūyavaṉ
Civaṉ is pure.
tūya veṇṇīṟu mēṉimēl pāyavaṉ
who spread on his body pure white sacred ash
pāyapaiññīli kōyilā mēyavaṉ
who is in extensive piññili as his temple.
vēypuraitōḷi pākamā ēyavaṉ
who is united with a lady whose shoulders are like bamboos, as a half.
eṉaic ceyum taṉmai eṉ kol
How wonderful is his nature of transforming me, a human being, into Civaṉ!
{3:14}__8+
{$} tottiṉa tōḷmuṭi uṭaiyavaṉ talai pattiṉai neritta paiññīlimēvalāṉ
Civaṉ who is in piññili and who crushed the ten heads of the one who had a bunch of shoulders and crowns.
muttiṉai muṟuval ceytāḷ ōr pākamāp pottiṉaṉ tiruntaṭi porunti vāḻmiṉ
(People of this world!) Lead your life approaching the flawless feet of Civaṉ who embraced the lady who has pearls as her teeth on one half.
{3:14}__9+
{$} nīr uṭaippōtu uṟai vāṉum mālumāyc cīr uṭaiya kaḻal aṭi ceṉṉi kāṇkilar
Piramaṉ who dwells in the flower which grows in water, and Māl could not find out the head, and feet wearing armour (The first two lines are etir niral niṟai i.e., reversed sequence; a syntactical arrangement of a series of predicates in an order which is the reverse of that in which their respective nominatives stand)
pār uṭaik kaṭavuḷ
the god who has the world as his possession
paiññīli mēviya tāruṭaik koṉṟai amtalaivar taṉmaiyē
these are the nature of the master who wears a garland of koṉṟi and who is in piññili.
{3:14}__10+
{$} pīliyār perumaiyum
the greatness of amaṇar who have a collection of peacock's feathers
piṭakar nūṉmaiyum
the subject-matter of the buddhist's tripiṭakam (As the buddhists follows the three piṭakams they are called piṭakar)
cāliyātavarkaḷaic cātiyātatu ōr kōliyā aruvarai kūṭi eyta paiññīyāṉ kaḻalaṭi niṉaintu vāḻmiṉ
(People of this world!) spend your lives remembering the feet wearing armour of the god in piññili who shot an arrow bending a mountin difficult to be lent and which could not be achieved by those two who achieved nothing.
{3:14}__11+
{$} kaṇ puṉal viḷai vayal kāḻik kaṟpakam naṇpu uṇar arumaṟai ñāṉacampantaṉ
Ñāñacampantaṉ who knows the abstruse Vētam-s and has intimacy with Civaṉ who can be compared to the divine tree, Kaṟpakam, born in Kāḻi which has fields yielding paddy and which has water everywhere.
paṇpiṉar paravu paiññīli pāṭuvār
who sing the fame of Paiññili with his verses, which is prised by good natured people
uṇ piṉa ulakiṉil ōṅkivāḻvar
will live prosperously in this world which is the place for enjoying the fruits of action done in previous births

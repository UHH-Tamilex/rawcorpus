{4:77}__1+
{$} kaṭumpakal naṭṭam āṭi
dancing in the noon
kaiyil ōr kapālam ēnti
holding a skull in the hand.
iṭumpalikku illam tōṟum uḻitarum iṟaivaṉīrē
Civaṉ who stays in everything and wanders in every house for the alms, the house wives place in the skull!
neṭum poṟai malaiyarpāvai, nēriḻai neṟimeṉ kūntal koṭuṅkuḻai
the beautiful daughter of the high imālaya mountain which has hillocks, who wears fine jewels, has soft and curly tresses of hair and wears crooked ear-rings.
pukunta aṉṟum kōvaṇam araiyatēyō
was the loin-cloth on your waist even on that day when she entered into your house as your wife?
{4:77}__2+
{$} yāvarē eḻutuvārē
who can draw a picture of Civaṉ.
kōvaṇam uṭuttavāṟum
having a dress of a loin-cloth.
kōḷ aravu acaittavāṟum
tying a cobra which can kill.
tīvaṇac (1) cāmpar pūcittiruuru iruntavāṟum
state of the holy body which has the colour of fine sacred ash smearing on it.
pūvaṇak kiḻavaṉārai
the primordial god dwells in pūvaṇam (pūvaṇam is one of the 14th shrines in paṇṭiya naṭu)
puliuri araiyaṉārai
who has tied the skin of a tiger in the waist.
ēvaṇaccilaiyiṉārai
and who has a bow in which the arrow is fixed? (People who have obtained a vision of him by his grace only can draw a picture of him there is none to do so)
[[Variant reading: (1) cāmpaṟ pūcciṟṟiruvuru]]
{4:77}__3+
{$} viḷakkiṉār peṟṟa iṉpam meḻukkiṉāl patiṟṟi ākum
those who clean the floor of the temple with cow-dung water is tenfold greater than the benefit derived by those who sweep the temple.
tuḷakkiṉ nal malar toṭuttāl tūyaviṇ ēṟal ākum
if one knits garlands with good and bright flowers he can ascent into civalōkam which is pure.
viḷakku iṭṭār pēṟu colliṉ (1) meyññeṟi ñāṉam ākum
if we speak about the benefits one obtains by lighting lamps it will lead to knowledge of god by the truthful path.
kītam coṉṉārkku aṭikaḷ (tām) aruḷumāṟu aḷappila
the ways by which god grants his grace to those who sing songs of his praise are limitless.
[[Variant reading: (1)meynneṟi]]
{4:77}__4+
{$} cantiraṟ caṭaiyil vaitta caṅkaraṉ
the dispenser of good things who wears the crescent on his caṭai.
cāmavēti
who chants the cāmavētam
antarattu amarar pemmāṉ
the god of the immortals in heaven.
āṉ nal veḷ ūrtiyāṉtaṉ mantiram (1) namaccivāya āka nīṟu aṇiyap peṟṟāl
if one smears beautifully sacred ash uttering the mantiram of Civaṉ (namaccivāya who has a good white vehicle of a bull)
cevvaḻal viṟaku iṭṭu aṉṟē viṉaiyum nōyum ventu aṟum
the two kinds of acts and diseases which afflict as a result of those two, will be burnt and destroyed as fire-wood which is placed in fire is burnt, at once.
[[Variant reading: (1) namaccivāyamāka]]
{4:77}__5+
{$} puḷḷuvar aivar kaḷvar
the five sense-organs which are deceitful and which practise theiving.
puṉattiṭaip pukuntu niṉṟu
entering into the farm of the body.
tuḷḷuvar
jump with joy.
cūṟaikoḷvar
they loot
tūneṟiviḷaiya oṭṭār
they would not allow to come into being the pure path to salvation.
muḷ uṭai avarkaḷ tammai mukkaṇāṉ pātanīḻal uḷḷiṭai maṟaintu niṉṟu aṅku uṇarviṉāl eyyalāmē
we can discharge an arrow and kill them who are like thorns, with the knowledge of god, concealing ourselves in the feet of the god of three eyes from there lying in ambush.
[[Variant reading: (1)viḷaiyaloṭṭār]]
{4:77}__6+
{$} toṇṭaṉēṉ
I who am a slave to worldly pleasure.
[[iḷaiyakālam emmāṉai aṭaikilāt tuḷaiyilāccevit toṇṭarkāḷ (nāvukkaracar, tiruvañcuḻi (1) 3)]]
vāḷāpiṟantu
born without any use.
tolviṉaik kuḻiyil vīḻntu
falling into the pit of acts done in many births.
piṇṭamē cumantu
carrying the burden of this body.
nāḷum periyatu ōr avāvil paṭṭēṉ
I was stuck up daily in a pit of a big desire.
aṇṭaṉē
God!
amarar kōvē
the King of the immortals!
aṟivaṉē
the embodiment of pure knowledge!
teṇtiraik kaṅkai cūṭum tikaḻtaru caṭaiyiṉāṉē
Civaṉ who wears on his shining caṭai Kaṇkai which has clear waves!
añcal eṉṉāy
comfort me by saying Do not be afraid
[[Variant reading: (1) vāḷāttolviṉaik]]
VMS7. & VMS8.
(Verses 7 and 8 are lost).
VMS9 / {4:77}__PIFI7+
{$} pāvi neñcē
my cruel mind!
[[cf. aḻukkāṟeṉa orupāvi; iṉmaiyeṉa oru pāvi (Kuṟaḷ 168, 1042)]]
paṉṟipōl aḷaṟṟil paṭṭup pāṟiṉāy
you ruined yourself like the pig which has fallen into the mire (aḷaṟu: birth)
[[narakameṉṟatu īṇṭup piṟaviyai; vīṭu pēṟṟu iṉpattoṭu cārtta narakamum cuvarkkamum oru nikaraṉa ātaliṉ narakameṉṟār (Kōvaiyār, 166, pērāciriyar).]]
tēṟi nī niṉaiti āyiṉ civakati tiṇṇam ākum
it is a certainty that you will attain eternal bliss if you become clear and meditate on Civaṉ.
ūṟal ēy uvarppu nāṟi
issuing a bad odour of saltishness which flows like a spring.
utiramē (1) oḻukum vācal
the nine apertures through which blood flows
kūṟaiyāl mūṭakkaṇṭu kōlamāk karutiṉāyē
you thought the body to be beautiful by seeing it covered by clothes.
[[Variant reading: (1) oḻukumpācam]]
VMS10 / {4:77}__PIFI8+
{$} uytta kāl utayattu umpar
the leg that was directed went beyond the mountain where the sun rises, (it is poetical convention that the sun rises in the east in a mountain).
umaiyavaḷ naṭukkam tīra vaittakāl arakkaṉōtaṉ vālmuṭi taṉakku nērntāṉ
the arakkaṉ dedicated his pure crown to the foot which was placed on him to remove the trembling of Umayavaḷ.
moyttakāṉ mukiḻveṇ tiṅkaḷ mūrtti
the god who has a visible form and who wears on his head a young white crescent which has abundant fragrance (kāṉ fragrance)
[[kāṉalaṅkāvum-maṇattaiyuṭaiya kāviṉum (paripātal 16-17).]]
eṉ ucci taṉmēl vaittakāl varuntum eṉṟu vāṭi nāṉ oṭuṅkiṉēṉ (ē)
I became weak and reduced when I thought that the feet which were placed on my head, would feel pain.

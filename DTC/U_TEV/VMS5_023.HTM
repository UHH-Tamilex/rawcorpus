{5:23}__1+
{$} neṭuṅkaṇ maṅkaiyar āṭṭu ayar niṉṟiyūr
in Niṉṟiyūr where young girls of long eyes dance.
kaṭuṅkaik kūṟṟu utaittiṭṭa karuttar
God the origin of all things who kicked the god of death of cruel act.
koṭuṅkaṇ veṇtalai koṇṭu
holding a white skull from which the eyes have been scooped out and the sockets are ugly to look at.
kuṟai vilaippaṭum kaṇ oṉṟu ilarāy
and having no place to sell his good at a cheap price.
palitērntu uṇpar
He will eat the alms which he gets by begging
{5:23}__2+
{$} niṉṟiyūrvētam ōti
chanting the vētam in Niṉṟiyūr
viḷaṅku veṇ tōṭṭarāy
and having worn a white and shining womens' ear-ring on the left ear
[[as it is made of conch the ear-ring is described as white]]
kātil veṇkuḻaivaitta em kaḷvarē
our thief who placed on the right ear a men's ear-ring which is white.
vītivēl neṭuṅkaṇṇiyar veḷvaḷai koḷappālatu nītiyē
Is it right conduct on your part to take away the white bangles of the ladies who have long eyes resembling Vēl, who come into the streets.
{5:23}__3+
{$} puṟṟiṉ ār aravam pulittōl micaic cuṟṟiṉār
the god wound round on the tiger's skin a cobra that lives in the ant-hill.
cuṇṇap pōrvai koṇṭār
He smeared so much holy ash on his body as to appear like covering himself with a white cloth.
cuṭar neṟṟikkaṇ uṭaiyār
He has a frontal eye which is fire.
niṉṟiyūr paṟṟiṉārai viṉai pāvam paṟṟā
evil acts and sins will not seize those who have held firmly as their support, Niṉṟiyūr.
{5:23}__4+
{$} paṟaiyiṉ ōcaiyum
the sound of drums.
pāṭaliṉ ōcaiyum
the sound of songs.
maṟaiyiṉ ōcaiyum
the sound produced when maṟi is chanted.
malki
being on the increase.
(1) ayal ellām niṟaiyum pūmpoḻil cūḻtiru niṉṟiyūr uṟaiyum īcaṉai eṉ uḷḷamuḷkum
my mind will always think of Civaṉ who dwells in Tiru Niṉṟiyur surrounded by beautiful gardens, where the above-mentioned sounds are full on all sides
[[niṟaiyum: this qualifies niṉṟiyūr]]
[[Variant reading: (1) vayal]]
{5:23}__5+
{$} niṉaiyum taṇvayal cūḻtaru niṉṟiyūrp paṉaiyiṉ īruri pōrtta paramē
the supreme being in Niṉṟiyūr which is surrounded by cool fields highly valued, who has covered yourself with the wet skin of an elephant;
[[paṉai-: palmyra; here it means an elephant which has a trunk resembling palmyra tree in colour, form and rough surface]]
cuṉaiyuḷ nīlam cuḷiyum neṭuṅkaṇāḷ
the lady with long eyes which look angrily at the blue nelumbo flowers growing in tanks
[[cuṉai: this is used in the sense of an ordinary tank]]
iṉaiyaṉ eṉṟu eṉṟum ēcuvatu eṉ kolō
what is the reason for decrying you always as one who deserted ladies who loved you?
{5:23}__6+
{$} num ucciyuḷāṉ taṉai uraippakkēṇmiṉ
Please listen what I say about the god who is seated on the crown of your head.
niraip poṉ māmatil cūḻ tiruniṉṟiyūr, uraippoṟkaṟṟaiyar ivar ārō eṉil
if you want to know about this person who has a collection of caṭi glittering like the gold which is used to test the fineness of gold, in Tiru Niṉṟiyūr surrounded by big and beautiful rows of walls of enclosure.
tiraittup pāṭit tiritarum celvar
he is the Lord, Civaṉ, who roves like waves, singing
{5:23}__7+
{$} niṉṟiyūrpatiyāka nilāyavaṉ
the god has as his permanent abode, Niṉṟiyūr
veṉṟi ēṟuṭai eṅkaḷ vikirtaṉ
our Lord who is different from the world and has a victorious bull
kaṉṟi ūr mukil pōlum karuṅkaḷiṟu iṉṟi ēṟalaṉ (āl)
without using the elephant which is like the sable cloud that crawls in the sky, for riding, and without using the bull what is the reason for his coming walking?
{5:23}__8+
{$} nilai ilā veḷḷai (1) mālaiyaṉ
the god wears a garland of white bones of persons who are not permanent
[[nilaiyilā- refers to Māl and Ayaṉ who are not permanent]]
[[It would be better reading if the words were `nilaiyilār']]
nīṇṭatu ōr kolaivilāl eyil eyta koṭiyavaṉ
he is cruel in as much as he shot an arrow at the forts (of his enemies) from a long and death dealing bow
nilaiyiṉ ār vayal cūḻ tiruniṉṟiyūr uraiyiṉāl toḻuvār viṉai ōyum
the Karmam of those who prise by their words, Tiru Niṉṟiyūr surrounded by fields of unchanging nature, will gradually shrink.
[[Variant reading: (1)mālayaṉ]]
{5:23}__9+
{$} iñci māmatil eytu imaiyōr toḻak kuñci vāṉpiṟai cūṭiya kūttaṉ niṉṟiyūrai neñcam; añci ākilum aṉpu paṭṭākilum niṉai
my mind! think of Niṉṟiyūr of the dancer who wears on his head the white crescent to be worshipped by the celestials who do not wink, having shot an arrow at the forts made of gold, silver and iron, either out of fear or out of love.
[[the eighteen groups of celestials worship the crescent on the head of Civaṉ; piṟai nutalvaṇṇam ākiṉṟu; appiṟai, patiṉeṇ kaṇaṉum ēttavum paṭumē (puṟam, 1-9-10)]]
[[vāḻi: expletive]]
[[iñci, māmatil, will include puricai also; irumpuṟumāmatil poṉṉiñci veḷḷip puricai aṉṟu ōra, turumpu uṟac ceṟṟavaṉ (Kōviyar, 167)]]
{5:23}__10+
{$} (1) eḷiyaṉā moḻiyā ilaṅkaikku iṟai, kaḷiyiṉāl kayilāyam eṭuttavaṉ neḷiya ūṉṟavallāṉ amar niṉṟiyūr aḷiyiṉāl toḻuvār viṉai alkum
the karmam of those who worship Niṉṟiyūr out of love, where the god who was capable of pressing down the King of Ilaṅki who did not speak words of humility and who lifted Kayilāyam being intoxicated with his strength, to make his body wriggle, will diminish gradually
[[eḷiyaṉāmoḻiyā: who was not spoken as a weak one is another meaning]]
[[Variant reading: (1) eḷiyeṉā]]

[[maṟumāṟṟam: reply. According to periya purāṇam, this decade was in reply to the order of Makentira Varmaṉ, the pallavā King, sent through his ministers to appear before him.]]

{6:98}__1+
{$} nām ārkkum kuṭiallōm
we, who are the slave of Civaṉ are not the subject of any king.
namaṉai añcōm
we are not afrid of the god of death.
narakattil (1) iṭarppaṭōm
we will not undergo torture in the hell, after death.
naṭalai illōm
we will not suffer distress in this life.
ēmāppōm
we will be feeling joy always.
piṇi aṟiyōm
we will not know what diseases are.
paṇivōm allōm
we won't go to anybody and stand before them submissively wanting anything.
iṉpamē, ennāḷum tuṉpam illai
we will always enjoy happiness only; we have no suffering at any time.
tām ārkkum kuṭi allāt taṉmaiāṉa caṅkaraṉ
Caṅkaraṉ, who is not the subject of anyone however great he might be.
nal caṅkaveṇkuḻai ōr kātil kōmāṟkē nām eṉṟum mīḷā āḷāy
having become the slave who cannot be freed from bondage to the Lord who wears in the right ear a men's ear-ring made of defectless white conch.
koymmalarc cēvaṭiiṇaiyē kuṟukiṉōm (ē)
we approached as refuge the two red feet which are like the flower just blossomed and plucked.
[[Variant reading: iṭappaṭōm]]
{6:98}__2+
{$} akaliṭamē (1) iṭam āka
the whole world to be our place.
ūrkaḷtōṟum aṭku uṇpār iṭku uṇpār aiyam vivakkār
those people who cook their food and eat after giving alms, will not prevent us from getting alms.
pukum iṭam ām ampalaṅkaḷ
open spaces for the use of the public are places where we can stay.
(2) pūmitēvi uṭaṉ kiṭantāl puraṭṭāḷ
If we lie on the earth mother earth will not roll us down.
poyaṉṟu; meyyē
this is not falsehood; this is truth
[[cf. ūrelām aṭṭa cōṟu nammatē; uvari cūḻnta, pār elām pāyal; tuṉṉaṟ kōvaṇam parikkum āṭai; cīrelām ciṟanta cāntam teyvanīṟu; aṇipūṇkaṇṭi, nīrelām cumanta vēṇi niruttaṉ āṭkoṇṭa aṉṟē (parañcōti tiruviḷayāṭālpurāṇam, 58-85)]]
ikal uṭaiya viṭai uṭaiyāṉ ēṉṟu koṇṭāṉ
Civaṉ who has a bull capable of fighting, accepted us.
iṉi ētum kuṟaivu ilōm
we will not be wanting in anything hereafter.
iṭarkaḷ tīrntōm
we are free from all sufferings
tukil uṭuttu
wearing costly clothes.
poṉpūṇṭu
and wearing ornaments of gold.
tirivār collum col kēṭkak kaṭavōmō
are we bound to obey the words of those kings who wander?
turicu aṟṟōm (ē)
we are free from all faults.
[[variant readings: (1) mēviṭamāka (2) pūmatēvi]]
{6:98}__3+
{$} vār āṇṭakoṅkaiyar cēr maṉaiyil cērōm
we have renounced the house-holder's life when we are united with the wife who wears a bodice on her breasts.
mātēvā, mātēvā eṉṟu vāḻtti
hiling by uttering matēvā, mātēvā'
nīr āṇṭa purōtāyam āṭappeṟṟōm
we bathed before sunrise which is the time for waking up from sleep
[[nīr āṭappeṟṟōm is the syntactical link;
purōtāyam is a corruption of pūrvōtayam (pūrvōtayam) which means before sunrise;
munti vantu purōtāyam mūḻki muṉikaḷpalar, entai pemmāṉ eṉa niṉṟu iṟaiñcumiṭam eṉparāl (campantar, tirukkētāram, 3)]]
nīṟu aṇiyum kōlamē nikaḻap peṟṟōm
we are shining in the beauty of the sacred ash with which we have smeared our body.
kār āṇṭa maḻaipōlak kaṇṇīr cōra
the tears to flow like the rin which pours in winter.
kalmaṉamē nalmaṉamāyk karaiyappeṟṟōm
our heart which was hitherto like the stone melted like a tender heart.
pār āṇṭu (1) pakaṭu ēṟit tirivār collum paṇi kēṭkak kaṭavōmō
are we bound to obey the orders given by Kings who wander riding on an elephant, ruling over the earth?
paṟṟuaṟṟōm (ē)
we have given up all attachments.
[[Variant reading: (1) pakkuēṟi varuvār]]
{6:98}__4+
{$} uṟavu āvār uruttira palkaṇattiṉōrkaḷ
our relations are many hosts of devotees of Civaṉ,
[[oṭṭiṭṭa paṇpiṉ uruttirapal kaṇattārkku, aṭṭiṭṭal (campantar, tirumayilāpūr, 1)]]
uruppaṉa kōvaṇattoṭu kīḷ uḷaām (aṉṟu, ē)
our dress is a loin-cloth and a strip of cloth used as a waist band.
ceṟuvārum ceṟamāṭṭār
our enemies will not be angry with us.
tīmaitāṉum naṉmaiyāyc ciṟappatē
when sinful acts will become definitely eminent by being transformed into virtuous acts.
piṟappil cellōm
we will not be born agin.
naṟavu ār poṉitaḻi naṟuntārōṉ cīrār (1) namaccivāyac colla nāvāl vallōm
we are able to utter the excellent namaccivāya which is the name of Civaṉ who wears a fragrant garland of koṉṟi which has abundant honey and is golden in colour, by our tongue.
cuṟavu ārum koṭiyāṉaip poṭiyākkaṇṭa cuṭarnayaṉac cōtiyaiyē toṭarvuṟṟōm ē)
we are clinging to the divine light who with his frontal eye of fire cused the cupid who had the flag of a shark, to fall as ashes.
[[Variant reading: (1) namaccivāyam colla]]
{6:98}__5+
{$} eṉṟum nām yāvarkkum iṭaivōm allōm
we will not fall back, being second to anyone at any time, however great he may be.
irunilattil emakku etir āvārum illai
there is none who is equal to us, in this wide world
[[etir may also mean opposed to us
(there is none superior to us)]]
ceṉṟu nām ciṟuteyvam cērvōm allōm
we will not approach the inferior deities going to them for help.
civaperumāṉ tiruvaṭiyē cērappeṟṟōm
we approached the feet of the Lord, Civaṉ only.
oṉṟiṉāl kuṟaivu uṭaiyōm allōm
we are not deficient in anything.
aṉṟē uṟupiṇiyār (1) ceṟal oḻintiṭṭu ōṭippōṉār
the acute diseases filed from us without inflicting suffering from the day when we left the company of camaṇar
[[piṇiyār: the suffix ār is added and the predicate ōṭippōṉār are used to denigrate the disease]]
poṉṟiṉār talaimālai aṇinta ceṉṉip puṇṇiyaṉai naṇṇiya puṇṇiyattu uḷōm (ē)
we are enjoying the fruits of the virtue of having been united with Civaṉ who is the embodiment of all virtues and who wears on his head a garland of skulls belonging to those who had died.
[[Variant reading: (1) ceṟivoḻintiṭṭu]]
{6:98}__6+
{$} mūuruviṉ mutal uruāy
being the first form of the three forms of Ayaṉ, Māl and Uruttiraṉ
[[the cuse of the three forms of Ayaṉ, Māl and Uruttiraṉ is another meaning]]
irunāṉku āya mūrttiyē eṉṟu muppattumūvar tēvarkaḷum mikkōrum ciṟantu vāḻttum cempavaḷat (1) tirumēṉic civaṉē eṉṉum nā uṭaiyār
those who are able to utter the name of the Civaṉ who has a holy form like the red coral, and who is prised by the superior human beings and the thirty three celestials, as the god who has eight forms
[[muppattu mūvar amararkku muṉ ceṉṟu (āṇtāḷ tiruppāvi, 20)]]
namai āḷa uṭaiyār aṉṟē
only those have the right to Lord over us (therefore)
nāvalantīviṉukku nātar āṉa kāvalarē ēvi viṭuttārēṉum kaṭavamallōm
we are not to submit to the orders of the King even if he be the Lord of whole of India
[[the island that derives its name from the jumoon plum tree]]
[[The idea is that the King who rules over the whole of India, orders me to appear before him]]
kaṭumaiyoṭu kaḷavu aṟṟōm (ē)
the reason for it is that we are without cruel acts and deceit.
[[Variant reading: (1) tikaḻmēṉic]]

{$} niṟpaṉavum naṭappaṉavum
being things which do not move and things which move.
nilaṉum nīrum
the earth and the water.
neruppiṉoṭu kāṟṟu āki
being the fire and the air.
neṭuvāṉ āki
being the extensive sky.
(1) aṟpamoṭu perumaiyum āy
being the small and big things.
arumai āki
being the rare things.
aṉpuṭaiyārkku eḷimaiyatu āy
within easy reach of those who have love towards him.
aḷakkal ākāt taṟparam āy
being the supreme being which could not be measured by any means.
catācivam āy
being Catācivam
tāṉum yāṉum ākiṉṟa taṉmaiyaṉai
Civaṉ who has the nature of being himself and myself.
naṉmaiyōṭum poṟpuṭaiya pēcak kaṭavōm
we are duty bound to talk about Civaṉ good and graceful things. (2)
pēyar pēcuvaṉa pēcutumē
shall we speak things which people with low intelligence, speak?
piḻai aṟṟōm (ē)
(as) we are without any fault.
[[variant readings: (1) aṟpaṉoṭu (2) poyyar]]

{$} nittalum niṉaiyappeṟṟōm
we meditated dily (on)
īcaṉai
the Lord of the universe.
evvulakiṉukkum iṟaivaṉtaṉṉai
the master of every world.
imaiyavartam perumāṉai
the master of the celestials who do not wink.
eriāy mikka tēcaṉai
who has much brightness like the fire.
cemmēṉi veṇṇīṟṟāṉai
who has smeared white sacred ash on his red body.
cilampuaraiyaṉ poṟpāvai nalam ceykiṉṟa nēcaṉai
who loves, and is loved by the beautiful dughter of the King of mountins.
niṉṟu uṇpār emmai niṉaiyac coṉṉa vācakam ellām maṟantōm aṉṟē
we completely forgot on that day itself all words that we were asked to remember, by the camaṇar who eat from their hands using them as plates, in the standing posture.
vantīr ār? maṉṉavaṉ āvāṉtāṉ ār (ē)
who are you, that have come on one as kings mission? who is he that is named king?

{$} caṭaiuṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has a caṭi.
caṅkakkuḻai ōr kātaṉ
wears a men's ear-ring in the right ear.
cāmpalum pāmpum aṇinta mēṉi viṭai uṭaiyāṉ
has a bull, and a body on which he wears cobras and smears sacred ash.
vēṅkai ataḷ mēlāṭai
has as upper cloth the tiger's skin.
veḷḷipōl puḷḷi uḻaimāṉ tōl cārnta uṭai uṭaiyāṉ
we are as dress in the waist a skin of a deer which has white spots like silver;
[[puḷḷi uḻaimāṉtōl oṉṟu uṭuttup pulittōl piyaṟku iṭṭu (Kārikkāl ammiyār, tiruvālaṅkāṭu mūtta tiruppatikam (2) 5),
puḷḷi uḻaimāṉ tōlāṉ kaṇṭāy (nāvukkaracar, tirumaṟikkāṭu (5) 3)]]

has us as his protege.
[[kaṇṭīr: expletive]]
ummōṭu maṟṟum uḷarāy niṉṟa paṭai uṭaiyāṉ paṇikēṭkum paṇiyōy allōm
we have no business to obey the command of the King who has a standing army, along with you.
pācam aṟa vīcum paṭiyōm nām (ē)
we are in the nature of completely destroying all bondage.

{$} nā āra nampaṉaiyē pāṭappeṟṟōm
we had the good fortune of singing the prises of Civaṉ to the complete satisfaction of our tongue.
nāṇ aṟṟār naḷḷāmē viḷḷappeṟṟōm
we were separated from the camaṇar who discarded wearing clothes without any sense of shame, without befriending us.
āvā' eṉṟu emai āḷvāṉ amararnātaṉ
the master of the immortals admits us as protege, taking pity on us.

civaṉ

eṉcintai cērntu iruntāṉ

teṉticaickōṉ tāṉē vantu kōaṭik kuṟṟēval ceyka' eṉṟālum

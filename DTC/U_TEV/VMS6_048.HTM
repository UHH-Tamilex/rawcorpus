nallāṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ is the good god.
nāṉ maṟaikaḷ āyiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is himself the four Vētam-s.
nampaṉ (kāṇ)
has the name of Nampaṉ
naṇukātār puram mūṉṟu eyta vallāṉ (kāṇ)
has a bow from which he discharged an arrow on all the three cities of the enemies.
(1) viṇṇavarkkum mēlāṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is superior to the celestials.
melliyalāḷ pākaṉ (kāṇ)
has as a half a lady of tender nature.
vēta vēḷvic collāṉ (kāṇ)
is in the form of words which describe sacrifices mentioned in the vētams.
cuṭar mūṉṟum āyiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is himself the three lights viz., sun, moon and fire.
toṇṭu ākip paṇivārkkut tolvāṉ īyavallāṉ (kāṇ)
is capable of granting his world [[which]] is older than all other worlds. (it is Civalōkam)
vāṉavarkaḷ vaṇaṅki ēttum valivalattāṉ (kāṇ)
is in Valivalam where the celestials bow to him and prise him.
avaṉ eṉ maṉattu uḷāṉ (ē)
he is in my mind
[[All the kāṇ in this and the following verses are expletives]]
[[Variant reading: (1) mēlavarkku]]

muṉṉavaṉ kāṇ piṉṉavaṉ kāṇ
Civaṉ existed before the worlds were created, he survives even after all the worlds are destroyed;
[[mūppāṉ iḷaiyavaṉ muṉṉavaṉ piṉṉavaṉ (Kōviyarn 312 and pēraciriyar's commentary)]]
mūvāmēṉi 1. mutalavaṉ (kāṇ)
is the beginning whose body does not show signs of age.
muṭivu avaṉ (kāṇ)
is the end of all things.
mūṉṟu cōti aṉṉavaṉ (kāṇ)
is like the three lights viz., the sun, moon and the fire.
aṭiyārkku aṇṭattārkkum aṇiyavaṉ (kāṇ) cēyavaṉ (kāṇ)
is near at hand to his devotees and far away from the celestials,
aḷavu il cōti miṉṉavaṉ (kāṇ)
is the lightning which has brilliance which cannot be measured.
urum avaṉ (kāṇ)
is the thunder.
tirumāl pākam vēṇṭiṉaṉ (kāṇ)
desired tirunāl to stay on one side.
īṇṭu puṉaṟ kaṅkaikku eṉṟum maṉṉavaṉ (kāṇ)
is the husband of the Kaṅki which descended with great speed.
vāṉavarkaḷ ....... maṉattuḷāṉē

paṅkayattiṉ mēlāṉum
Piramaṉ who is seated on a lotus
1. pālakaṉāy
assuming the form of a young boy
ulaku aḷanta paṭiyāṉum
Māl who had the tall body for measuring the world.
paravikkāṇātu aṅkai vaitta ceṉṉiyarāy aḷakkamāṭṭā aṉalavaṉ (kāṇ)
is the column of fire which could not be measured by both of them who got wearied placing their palms on their heads, as they were unable to see him by prising him

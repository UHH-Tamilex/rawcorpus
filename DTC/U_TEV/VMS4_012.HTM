{4:12}__1+
{$} col mālai payilkiṉṟa kuyiliṉaṅkāḷ
flocks of indian cuckoos which practise the order of words without ceasing.
collīrē
will you please tell (Civaṉ)
pal mālai varivaṇṭu paṇ miḻaṟṟum paḻaṉattāṉ
the god who is in Paḻaṉam where many rows of bees which have lines on them, hum softly like melodies.
muṉ mālai naku tiṅkaḷ mukiḻviḷaṅku muṭicceṉṉippoṉ mālai mārpaṉ eṉ putu nalam uṇṭu ikaḻvāṉō
will Civaṉ who wears a golden garland on his chest and has a head in which the caṭai coiled into a crown on which the crescent that rises in the early part of the evening is shining, neglect me after enjoying my fresh beauty, after my coming of age? (He will not)
{4:12}__2+
{$} kaṇṭaṅkāḷ! muṇṭaṅkāḷ! kaitaikāḷ! neytalkāḷ!
white long flowered Nail-Dyes! Water thorns! fragrant screwpines! Blue nelumbo flowers.
paṇṭaraṅkavēṭattāṉ
Civaṉ who has the dress appropriate to the dance of Pāṇṭaraṅkam.
pāṭṭu ōvāppaḻaṉattāṉ
who is paḻaṉam where music is never-ceasing
vaṇṭu ulām taṭam mūḻki
Bathing in the tank where bees are wandering (from flower to flower)
avaṉ eṉ taḷir vaṇṇam koṇṭa nāḷ aṟivāṉ kuṟikkoḷḷātu oḻivāṉō
will Civaṉ who knows that day when he enjoyed my complexion as beautiful as tender leaves, abandon me without guarding me carefully?
{4:12}__3+
{$} maṉaikkāñci iḷaṅkurukē
young crane which is perched on the river portia which grows in the garden of the house!
maṟantāyō
did you forget?
paṉaikkaimmā uripōrttāṉ palar pāṭum paḻaṉattāṉ niṉaikkiṉṟa niṉaippu ellām uraiyāyō
will you not tell all my thoughts to Civaṉ in Paḻaṉam where devotees sing his praise, and who covered himself with the skin of an elephant which has a trunk like the palmyra tree in colour and shape?
nikaḻvaṇṭē
Bright bee!
cuṉaikkuvaḷai malarkkaṇṇāḷ coltūtāyc cōrvāḷō
will the lady-messenger who has eyes as beautiful as the blue nelumbo flowers in the mountain spring, will forget me on account of the love she developed towards him or will she faulter in speech?
{4:12}__4+
{$} putiyaiyāy iṉiyaiyām pūnteṉṟāl
O! southern balmy breeze which is fresh, fragrant and delightful!
patiāvatu itu' eṉṟu palarpāṭum paḻaṉattāṉ
Civaṉ in Paḻaṉam which is sung by many as ``this is his favourite city''
matiyātār vēḷvitaṉai matittiṭṭamati kaṅkai vitiyāḷaṉ eṉ uyirmēl viḷaiyāṭal viṭuttāṉō
Did the ordainer of fate who wears the crescent and Kaṅkai and destroyed by his fury the sacrifice (performed by Takkaṉ and other tēvar) send you to play with my life?
{4:12}__5+
{$} (1)vīṭupēṟāy niṉṟāṉai
Civaṉ who was freedom from sufferings and attainment of eternal bliss.
maṇporuntivāḻpavarkkum
to the people who live in this earth,
mātīrttavētiyarkkum
to the very pure brahmins.
viṇporuntu tēvarkkum
to the tēvar who live in heaven.
paṇ porunta icaipāṭum paḻaṉamcēr appaṉai
and my father in Paḻaṉam where music is sung to be appropriate to melodies.
eṉ kaṇ poruntum (2) pōtattum kaiviṭa nāṉ kaṭavēṉō
will I give up thinking of him even at the time of my death?
[[Variant reading: (1) vīṭupeṟā (2) pōḻtattum]]
{4:12}__6+
{$} poṅku ōtam māl kaṭaliṟ puṟam puṟam pōy iraitērum ceṅkāl veṇmaṭa nārāy
white young crane which goes out from the big ocean which has surging waves, in search of prey!
ceyaṟpaṭuvatu aṟiyēṉ nāṉ
I do not know what is to be done now.
am kōlavaḷai kavarntāṉ aṇipoḻilcūḻ paḻaṉattāṉ
Civaṉ in Paḻaṉam surrounded by gardens beautifying it, who robbed me of my beautiful collection of bangles.
tam kōla naṟuṅkoṉṟaittār aruḷātu oḻivāṉō
will (he) not grant me his beautiful and fragrant koṉṟai garlands?
{4:12}__7+
{$} tuṇai āramuyaṅkip pōyttuṟai cērum maṭa nārāy
young crane going to the ghat after embracing tightly your mate!
paṇai āravārattāṉ; pāṭṭu ōvāp paḻaṉattāṉ
Civaṉ in Paḻaṉam where songs never cease and there is always the loud noise of drums.
kaṇai āra iru vicumpil kaṭi araṇam poṭi ceyta iṇai āram mārpaṉ eṉ eḻil nalam uṇṭu ikaḻvāṉō
will Civaṉ who wears two garlands on his chest and who reduced to dust the well-guarded forts that were wandering in spacious sky to fall a prey to the arrow, having enjoyed my beauty and other excellences, neglect me?
{4:12}__8+
{$} kūvaivāy maṇivaraṉṟik koḻittu ōṭum kāvirippūmpāvai vāy muttu ilaṅkap pāyntu āṭum paḻaṉattāṉ
Civaṉ in Paḻaṉam which is inundated by the damsel of the Kaviri of dancing waves in which pearls shine and which flows wafting on the banks the precious stones which are found in heaps.
[[kūvai-tiraḷ
koṭuviṟ kūḷiyar kūvaikāṇiṉ (malaipaṭukaṭām, 422)]]
kōvaivāy malaimakaḷ kōṉ
the husband of the daughter of the mountain who has red lips like the common creeper of the hedge
kol ēṟṟiṉkoṭi āṭaip pūvaikāḷ
Bush-mynas which perch on the cloth of the banner of a killing bull!
maḻalaikāḷ
female bush-mynas which prattle sweet words!
pōkātapoḻutu uḷatu
I have days that hang on me long.
{4:12}__9+
{$} puḷ uyarttāṉ maṇi nākap paḷḷiyāṉ puḷḷimāṉ poṟi aravam toḻutu ētta irukkiṉṟa paḻaṉattāṉ
Civaṉ who stays in Paḻaṉam where Piramaṉ who holds aloft the swan in his banner and Māl who is lying on a bed of a serpent having a jewel in its hood, worship and praise the spotted deer skin and cobra with a spot.
ulaku ellām (1) uḷḷuvār viṉaitīrkkum eṉṟu uraippar
all the people of the world say that Civaṉ would remove the karmams of those who meditate on him.
kaḷḷiyēṉ nāṉ ivarkku eṉ kaṉavaḷaiyum kaṭavēṉō
Not only have I who am a deceitful person been not rid of my karnams but should I also lose my heavy bangles?
[[As the love-sick lady could not bear the pangs of separation, even bangles become a burden]]
[[Variant reading: (1) uḷkuvār]]
{4:12}__10+
{$} pañcikkāṟ ciṟaku aṉṉam parantu ārkkum paḻaṉattāḷ vañcittu eṉ vaḷaikavarntāṉ vārāṉē āyiṭiṉum
though the Lord in Paḻaṉam where the swans with feet and feathers as soft as cotton make a loud noise spreading themselves, robbed me of my bangles by deceiving me, does not come to me.
añcip pōyk kali meliya aḻal ōmpum appūti kuñcippūvāy niṉṟa cēvaṭiyāy
the deity that resides in the circles of Kūṭal, which placed its red feet as a flower that is worn on the head of Appūti who maintains the three sacrificial fires to make the evils of Kaliyukam to become weak.
kōṭu iyai
Let the lines for Kūtal join together.
[[Drawing Kūtal is one of the ways by which love-sick ladies get satisfaction. This is mentioned in Tēvāram by Appar. (Kōḻampam, 4; tirumarukal 8);

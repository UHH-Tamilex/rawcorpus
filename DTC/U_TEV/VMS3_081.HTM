{3:81}__1+
{$} caṅku amarum muṉkai maṭa mātai oru pāl uṭaṉ virumpi
Having placed on one half with him, a young lady who has on her forearm bangles made of conches
aṅkam uṭal mēl uṟa aṇintu
and having profusely adorned the body with bones.
piṇi tīra aruḷ ceyyum eṅkaḷ perumāṉ iṭam eṉattakum tōṇipuram ām
tōṇipuram can be sid to be the place of our great one who bestows his grace to remove the three ills pertining to the soul (the three ills are āṇavam, māyai, kaṉmam)
muṉaikkaṭaliṉ muttam, tuṅkamaṇi, ippi karaikku varutōṇipuram
tōṇipuram whose banks the pearls, superior precious stones and oysters are carried by the energetic waves, reach.
{3:81}__2+
{$} callari yāḻ muḻavam montai kuḻal tāḷam (atu) iyampa
when a kind of drum by name callari, yaḻm, mulavu, monti, flute, and cymbals are played.
kal ariya māmalaiyar pāvai oru pākam nilai ceytu
Having placed permanently on one half the beautiful and only dughter of the big mountin (imayam)
erikai ēnti al naṭamāṭu caṭai aṇṇal iṭam eṉpar
people say that the place of the eminement god with caṭi who dances at night holding the fire in his hand.
col ariya toṇṭar tuti ceyya vaḷar tōṇipuram ām
it is tōṇipuram which is always increasing in fame, as the countless devotees prise him. (The yi in first line has been inserted to prevent hiatus; normally according to grammer i will appear only after consonants; naṉṉūl, 206; this is what is called ārshaprayōkam)
{3:81}__3+
{$} vaṇṭu aravu koṉṟai vaḷar puṉcaṭaiyiṉ mēl matiyam vaittu
having placed the crescent moon on the golden caṭi which grows with garlands of Kōṉṟi which is pecked by bees.
paṇṭu aravu taṉ araiyil ārtta paramēṭṭi
the Lord who is in the most elevated place and who has tied in his waist cobras from very ancient times.
paḻitīrak kaṇṭu
Having in view the removal of the blemish (The blemished was that the tēvar went to churn the ocean of milk without the previous permission of the Lord).
arava oṇ kaṭaliṉ nañcam amutu uṇṭa kaṭavuḷ ūr
the place of the god who drank the poison which came out of the roaring brigh t ocean, as if it were nectar.
toṇṭar avar miṇṭi vaḻi pāṭu malku tōṇipuram ām
is tōṇipuram where devotees throng and are zealous in their act of worship.
{3:81}__4+
{$} kollai viṭai ēṟu uṭaiya kōvaṇavaṉ
the Lord wearing a loin-cloth has a bull of the forest on which he rides.
nā aṇavu mālai ollai uṭaiyāṉ
has the sound of the garlands of verses sung by devotees (nāvaṇavu mālai the epithet nāvaṇavu is given to differentiate this from the garlands of flowers)
aṭaiyalār araṇam oḷ aḻal viḷaitta villai uṭaiyāṉ
has a bow with which he set fire to the fort of the enemies.
miku virumpu pati
the place which he very much desires
mēvivaḷar teṇṭar collai aṭaivāka iṭartīrttu aruḷ cey tōṇipuram ām
is tōṇipuram which bestows grace after removing the sufferings, having as a pretext the prise of devotees who have as their support god, and increase in their devotion.
{3:81}__5+
{$} tēyum matiyam caṭai ilaṅkiṭa
The waning moon to shine in the caṭi.
vilaṅkal mali kāṉil kāyum aṭutiṉ kariyiṉ īr urivai pōrttavaṉ
the Lord who covered himself with the skin, wet with blood, of the strong, killing and angry elephant wandering in the forest where they are many hills.
niṉaippār tāy eṉa niṟaintatu oru taṉmaiyiṉar naṉmaiyoṭu vāḻvu
the place where the Lord who has the nature of pervading everywhere when people think of him as the mother for their help, resides conferning benefits.
tūya maṟaiyāḷar muṟai yōti niṟai tōṇipuram āmē
is tōṇipuram where holy brahmins recite the vētams in the proper manner, crowd in that temple.
{3:81}__6+
{$} paṟṟalartam ppuram erittu
Having burnt the three cities of the enemies.
aṭi paṇintavarkaḷ mēlaik, kuṟṟam atu oḻittaruḷu koḷkaiyiṉaṉ
has the nature of completely destroying the balance of acts remining after enjoying their fruits, belonging to those who fell at his feet
[[aruḷu-aruḷ + u this form is also in usage;
virain teṉmēl cīṟṟattait tīrt taruḷu (peruntēvapāṇi, veṇpā)
aruḷu niṉ tāḷ kaḷai eṉakkē (tiruvimoḻi 8-1-9);

veḷḷil mutukāṉil, paṟṟavaṉ
Has a desire to stay in the cremation ground which has funeral biers.
icaikkiḷavi pāriṭam atu ētta naṭamāṭum, tuṟṟacaṭai attaṉ uṟaikiṉṟapati tōṇipuram āmē
the city where the father of thick caṭi dances and resides to be prised by the pūtams with articulate words, is tōṇipuram.
{3:81}__7+
{$} paṇ amarum nāṉ maṟaiyar
the Lord who gave the four vētams which have melody types of their own.
nūl muṟai payiṉṟa tiru mārpil, peṇ amarum pēṉiyiṉar
Has a lady on his beautiful chest on which the sacred thread is always worn in the proper manner;
[[vil niṟa vāḷ nutal vēl niṟakkaṇ melliyalai mallal taṉ niṟam oṉṟil iṟutti niṉṟōṉ (Koviyar, 58); though praciriyar has given the meaning as form, the word niṟam will mean also chest]]
tam perumai pēcum aṭiyār meyt, tiṇ amarum valviṉaikaḷ tīra aruḷ ceytal uṭaiyāṉ ūr
the place of the god who bestows his grace to vinsh of their own accord the real, very strong and irresisitible acts of devotees who always speak his greatness.
tuṇṇeṉa virumpu cariyait toḻilar tōṇipuram āmē
is tōṇipuram where people who have taken upon themselves several services with fear whether any fault might creep inadvertantly.
[[cariyai will include others of its kind; kiriyai, yōkam, and ñāṉam; Kiriyi, yōkam and ñāṉam]]

the place of the able god who pressed down the shoulders matching each other, which won victories having conquered the rulers in the four quarters, of the king of ilanki which is situated in the south, and then bestowed his grace upon him.

{3:81}__9+
{$} nāṟṟam miku māmalariṉ mēl ayaṉum nāraṇaṉum nāṭi, āṟṟal ataṉāl mika aḷappariya vaṇṇam eri āki
Having risen as a column of fire which cannot be measured by nāraṇaṉ, and Ayaṉ who is seated on a big (lotus) flower of much fragrance though they both tried to do so with their own efforts
ūṟṟam miku kīḻulakum mēlulakum ōṅki eḻutaṉmait tōṟṟam 1. ariyāṉ uṟaivu tōṇipuram āmē
tōṇipuram is the reisidence of the god whose nature of prominent appearence ascending and rising high in the upper worlds and the netherworlds of great strength, is immeasurable even today.

{$} mūṭutuvar āṭaiyiṉar vēṭa nīlai kāṭṭum amaṇ ātar, kēṭu pala colliṭuvar
Buddhists who cover their bodies with yelow robes and ignorant jins who exhibit that their hollowness of their knowledge is like their nudity, will talk certinly words which will ruin listeners.
ammoḻi keṭutta aṭaiviṉāṉ, ak, kāṭu patiyāka naṭam āṭi maṭamātoṭu irukātil, tōṭu kuḻai peytavar tamakku uṟaivu tōṇipuram āmē
tōṇipuram is the residence of the Lord who cused those words to be destroyed and who danced in that cremation ground and wore in both the ears a woman's ear-ring and men's ear-ring, being united with a young lady (tōṭu is worn on the left ear; kuḻai is worn on the right ear)
[[keṭutta aṭaiviṉāṉ : have become keṭuttaṭaiviṉāṉ it can be also split into keṭuttu aṭaiviṉāṉ;
am moḻi keṭuttu aṭaiviṉāṉ tōṇipurattaic cērvīrkaḷ
is another meaning. Having destroyed those words you will reach tōṇipuram in the proper manner]]

{$} tuñcu iruḷil niṉṟu naṭamāṭi miku tōṇipuram mēya, mañcaṉai vaṇaṅku tiru ñāṉacampantaṉ col mālai
the garland of verses composed by tiruñāṉacampantaṉ who worships the strong god who danced standing in the darkness at the end of the world.
tañcam eṉa niṉṟu icaimoḻinta aṭiyārkaḷ
the devotees who recited these with music thinking them to be their support
taṭumāṟṟam, vañcam ilar
will not have perplexity and deceit.
neñcu iruḷum nīṅki aruḷpeṟṟu vaḷar vārē
will proper receiving the grace of god, their ignorance having left from their minds.

{3:10}__1+
{$} alai vaḷar taṇ matiyōṭu ayalē aṭakki
having controlled the water of the Kaṅki along with waxing cool crescent by the side.
[[alai - alaikaḷaiyuṭaiya nīr]]
umai mulai vaḷar pākam muyaṅka valla mutalvaṉ mu(ṉ)ṉi
Civaṉ, the first cuse who can embrace the half of the body of Umi in which the breast are mature, thinkng it to be the proper place.
ilai vaḷar tāḻaikaḷ vimmu kāṉal irāmēccuram talai vaḷar kōla nalmālaiyaṉ tāṉ iruntu āṭci(ē)
iramēccuram which has seashore gardens in which the fragrant screw-pines whose leaves grow long are flourishing, is the place whre Civaṉ who wears a beautiful garland of skulls, reigns.
{3:10}__2+
{$} tēviyai vavviya teṉ ilaṅkait tecamāmukaṉ pū iyalum muṭi poṉṟu vitta paḻi pōy aṟa
the sin of having cused the death of the heads wearing crowns and victorious garland of the arakkaṉ of ten big faces who was the King of ilaṅki in the south and who carried off his queen, to disappear completely, having gone away from him.
ē iyalum cilai aṇṇal ceyta irāmēccuram mēviya cintaiyiṉārkaḷ tammēl viṉai vīṭum (ē)
the acts, good and bad of those who always think of īrāmeccuram established by the chief (irāmaṉ) who had a bow from which arrows could be discharged, will vanish.
{3:10}__3+
{$} māṉ aṉa nōkki vaitēvi taṉṉai oru māyaiyāl kāṉatil vavviya kār arakkaṉ uyirceṟṟavaṉ īṉam ilāp pukaḻ aṇṇal
the chief who had fame without meanness attached to it and who destroyed the life of the black arakkaṉ who carried off vitēvi who has startled looks like the deer, from the forest by deception.
[[vitevi is the corruption of the sanskrit word vitēhi; vitevi is used in nālāyira tivyappirapantam;
vār aṇinta mulaimaṭavāy vaitēvī,
māṉamarum meṉṉōkki vaitēvi,
maittaku māmalark kuḻalāy vaitēvī (periyāḻvār tirumoḻi, 3-10-4, 5,8);
māṉ amarum meṉṉōkki vaitēvi iṉtuṇaiyā,
kentuvarvāy, vār ār vaṉamulaiyāṉ vaitēvi kāraṇamā (periya tirumoḻī, 11-5+1; periyāḻvār tirumaṭal, 41);
maṉṉaṉ irāmaṉ piṉ vaitēvi eṉṟuraikkum, aṉṉa naṭai aṇaṅku (do.53)]]
ceyta irāmēccuram
īrāmēccuram established by him.
ñāṉamum naṉporuḷum āki niṉṟatu oru naṉmai (ē)
is the sacred place which grants spiritual knowledge and the fruit of salvation derived from it.
{3:10}__4+
{$} urai uṇarātavaṉ kāmam eṉṉum uṟu vēṭkaiyāl varai poru tōḷ iṟac ceṟṟa villi makiḻntu ēttiya viṟai maruvum kaṭal ōtam malkum irāmēccurattu
in irāmēccuram where the waves of the sea which has fragrance, increase, and which was prised by the archer irāmaṉ, who destroyed the shoulders of Irāvaṇaṉ who did not mind the curse that befell him, by the excessive desire of sexual pleasure.
arai aravu āṭa niṉṟu āṭal pēṇum ammāṉ nallaṉ (ē)
the father who desires dancing in the standing posture the cobra, tied round his waist to dance along with him, is Civaṉ.
{3:10}__5+
{$} ūṟu uṭai(ya) veṇtalai kaiyil ēntip pala ūr toṟum vīṟu uṭai(ya) maṅkaiyar aiyam peyya
holding a white skull which has the sense of touch and going to every village for the ladies of unique beauty to place the alms in it.
viṟal ārntatu ōr ēṟu uṭai(ya) vel koṭi entai mēya irāmēccuram pēṟu uṭaiyāṉ peyar ēttum māntar piṇi pērum (ē)
the physical diseases and those pertining to the soul of those who prise the names of Civaṉ who has īrāmēccuram as a place worth obtining, in which our father who has a victorious flag of a very strong bull, will go away from them.
{3:10}__6+
{$} aṇai alai cūḻkaṭal aṉṟu aṭaittu vaḻi ceytavaṉ
irāmaṉ who created a path on that day blocking the ocean in which the waves are moving, by a dam.
paṇai ilaṅkum muṭi pattu iṟuttavaṉ paḻi pōkkiya iṇaiili eṉṟum irunta kōyil irāmēccuram
in irāmēccuram where the god who is unequalled and who removed the sin of irāmaṉ who cut down the ten heads of Irāvaṇaṉ which were shining being big in size, dwells for ever
tuṇai ili tūmalarp pātam ēttat tuyar 1. nīṅkum (ē)
all sufferings will go away if one prises the feet which are like the defectless flowers, of Civaṉ who has no comparison;
tuṇaiyili
: one of the names of Civaṉ;
[[iṇai ili tollait tillai- (Kōviyār, 2)]]
[[Variant reading: 1. tīrumē]]
{3:10}__7+
{$} caṉi putaṉ, ñāyiṟu veḷḷi tiṅkaḷ pala tīyaṉa 1. muṉivatu ceytu ukantāṉai veṉṟu
having won Irāvaṇaṉ who esteemed his strength as great by getting angry with the planets saturn, mercury, sun, venus, and moon which do evil also, (ñāyiṟu, tiṅkaḷ, putaṉ, veḷḷi, caṉi should be the proper order; for the sake of poetry the order is changed)
avviṉai mūṭiṭa
that sin when encompassed him.
iṉi aruḷ nal kiṭu eṉṟu aṇṇal ceyta irāmēccuram
irāmēccuram which was established by the chief who requested Civaṉ, grant me now your grace
paṇimati cūṭi niṉṟu āṭavalla paramēṭṭi(ē)
is the God in the most exalted place, who wears a cool crescent and is able to dance in the standing posture.
[[Variant reading: 1. muṉivaṉa]]
{3:10}__8+
{$} peruvarai aṉṟu eṭuttu ēntiṉāṉ taṉ peyar cāy keṭa
tirumāl who lifted and held long ago a big mountin to lose his fame and brilliance.
aruvaraiyāl aṭarttu aṉṟu nalki
granting his grace after pressing down Irāvaṇaṉ with the great mountin.
ayaṉ māl eṉum iruvarum nāṭi niṉṟu ēttu kōyil irāmēccurattu oruvaṉumē pala āki niṉṟatu oru vaṇṇam(ē)
the unequalled god who is in the temple at irāmēccuram where both Ayaṉ and Māl having searched the head and feet and failed in their attempt prise Civaṉ, is a beauty that he has taken many manifestations.

{$} cākkiyar vaṉ camaṇkaiyar meyyil taṭu māṟṟattār vākku iyalum(m) urai paṟṟu viṭṭu
leaving attachments to the spoken words of inconsistency in their religious doctrines of the low and strong camaṇar and cākkiyar (buddhists).
mati oṇmaiyāl
by the brilliance of your intellect
ēkku iyalum cilai aṇṇal ceyta irāmēccuram ākkiya celvaṉai ētti
prising the god who had converted irāmēccuram established by the cheif who has a bow which is useful by the arrow, into a shrine.
aruḷ aka vāḻmiṉ(ē)
lead such a life as to obtin the grace of Civaṉ.

{$} pakalavaṉ mītu iyaṅkāmaik kātta patiyōṉ taṉai ikal aḻivittavaṉ ēttu kōyil irāmēccuram
the temple in irāmēccuram prised by irāmaṉ who killed in battle Irāvaṇaṉ who well guarded his city so that the sun would not pass over his city.
pukaliyuḷ ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa tamiḻ puntiyāl akal iṭam eṅkum niṉṟu ētta vallārkku
to those who can prise in all the places in this wide world, with intention, the tamiḻ, verses composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ of pukali.
allal illai (ē)
there will be definitely no sufferings

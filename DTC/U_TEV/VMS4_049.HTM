[[Note: Vīraṭṭam is a corruption of vīraṭṭāṉam, which is itself a corruption of vīrastāṉam; a place where Civaṉ performed heroic deeds; it was here that Cupid (Kāmaṉ) was burnt]]

{4:49}__1+
{$} ātiyil piramaṉār (tām) aṭi iṇaikkīḻ arccittār
Piramaṉ performed worship at the feet of the Lord by scattering flowers and uttering his names at the beginning of creation.
ōtiya vēta nāvar uṇarumāṟu uṇaral uṟṟār
Civaṉ understood it so that Piramaṉ who chanted the vētam can be conscious of it.
pal pūkkōti vaṇṭu aṟaiyum cōlaik kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār
the Lord in Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭam which has gardens where the bees hum with great noise, having worked through many flowers (for gathering honey)
cōtiyuḷ cuṭarāyt tōṉṟi
appearing as the light of happiness in the effulgent light of spiritual illumination.
colliṉai iṟantār
he transcends words that try to describe him.
{4:49}__2+
{$} nīṟṟiṉai niṟaiyap pūci
Besmearing his body profusely with holy ash.
nittalum niyamañceytu
and carrying out dily his routine worship
āṟṟunīrpūrittu āṭṭum
bathing god with the water of the Kaviri, having filled the pots with it.
(1) cāṟṟu nāḷ aṟṟatu eṉṟu antaṇaṉāraik kolvāṉ
to take away the life of the antaṇaṉ thinking that the span of life granted to him came to an end.
kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār tarumarācaṟkāy vanta kūṟṟiṉaik kumaippar
Civaṉ in Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭam trod upon the god of death who came ordered by Tarumarācaṉ
[[Tarumarācaṉ is the god of death, yamaṉ;
kūṟṟu (Kūṟṟu) is his servant who does his bidding;
tāmā iruvarum tarumaṉum maṭaṅkalum (Paripāṭal, 3-8);
tarumaṉ - yamaṉ ; maṭaṅkal avaṉ ēvalceyyum kūṟṟam (Do. Parimēḷaḻakar's commentary)
cf. Appar, Cāykkaṭu, Tiruviruttam, 1)
[[Variant reading: (1) cāṟṟi]]
{4:49}__3+
{$} taḻaittatu ōr āttiyiṉkīḻ
under the ātti tree which grew luxuriantly.
tāparam maṇalālkūppi
heaping the sand in the river, maṇṇi, in the form of a civaliṅkam.
aḻaittu
inviting Civaṉ to condescend to be present in that liṅkam
aṅkē
on that civaliṅkam
āviṉpālaik kaṟantu koṇṭu āṭṭakkaṇṭu
seeing (his son bathe the civaliṅkam with the milk which was milked from the cows.
piḻaitta taṉ tātai tāḷaip peruṅ koṭumaḻuvāl vīca
when he cut the leg of his father who committed a great sin by kicking the milk-pots, with a big and cruel battle-axe.
kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār kuḻaittatu ōr amutam īntār
the Lord in Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭam gave him a well-cooked food
[[kuḻaittatu ōr amutam figuratively means eternal bliss;
cf: pātamē cōṟu paṟṟiṉavā tōṇōkkam
(tiruvācakam, tōṇōkkam, cf.apparcāykkāṭu, tiruviruttam,6)]]
{4:49}__4+
{$} cilantiyum āṉaikkāvil tiru niḻaṟ pantar ceytu
even the spider which constructed a sacred panṭal in Āṉikkā to provide shade to the civalinkam, with its web.
avaṉ ulantu iṟantapōtē
as soon as it died there by being done to death by the elephant.
kōcceṅkaṇāṉumāka
as Kōcceṅkaṇaṉ, who was fit to wear a crown
kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār piṟappittār
Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭaṉār made it to be born
kalanta nīrkkāviricūḻ cōṇāṭṭuc cōḻar taṅkaḷ kulantaṉil
in the family of the kings who ruled Coṇāṭu girt by the Kāviri into which many rivers mingle.
[[cōṇāṭṭuc cōḻar: here cōḻar is used in the sense of ruler
(cf. appar, Cāykkāṭu, tiruviruttam, 3)]]
{4:49}__5+
{$} uṭaṉ ēṟu ēḻ aṭarttāṉ
Māl in the incarnation of Kaṇṇaṉ, controlled seven bulls simultaneously
āyiram pū eṇṇik koṇṭu
having in his hand thousand flowers having counted them.
āṟuṭaic caṭaiyiṉāṉai aṭiyiṇaikkīḻ arccittāṉ
performed worship by scattering flowers at the feet of the Lord who has on his Caṭi a river (kaṅki)
ōr pūkkuṟaiya
when one flower was missing
vēṟum ōr (pūvēṇṭi)
to fill up the number of thousand and when he wanted one more flower.
meym malark kaṇṇai (1) miṇṭa
when he removed his eye which was like the flower, from his body.
kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār kūṟum ōr āḻi īntār
Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭaṉār gave him a discus which he wished for
[[cf. Appar, cāykkatu, tiruviruttam, 9]]
[[Variant reading: (1) īṇṭa]]
{4:49}__6+
{$} kalliṉāl eṟintu
after pelting the civaliṅkam with stones
kañcitām uṇṇum cākkiyaṉār
the Cākkiya nāyaṉar who was in the habit of having kañci after that.
nelliṉ ār cōṟu uṇāmē nīḷvicumpu āḷavaittār
made him rule over the Civalōkam so that he may not even eat rice got from paddy.
[[The idea is he attined bliss without the necessity of having to eat rice in the next birth]]
elli āṅku
at night.
erikai ēnti
holding fire in his hand
eḻil tikaḻ naṭṭam āṭi
performing beautiful dances
kolli ām paṇ ukantār kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār
kuṟukki vīraṭṭaṉar liked the melody-type kolli.
{4:49}__7+
{$} kāppatu ōr villum ampum ōr iṟaiccippāram kaiyatu
in the hand there were a bow for protection, an arrow and a heavy weight of cooked meat.
[[kaiyatu is singular; it should be linked to vil, ampu and pāram]]
tōṟperuñ cerupput toṭṭu
Removing with a big sandal made of leather, the flowers and leaves on the head of Civaliṅkam
tūyavāykkalacam āṭṭi
bathing the god with the water brought in the pot, his pure mouth.
peruṅkaṇkaḷ tī ceyya kuruti nīr oḻuka
when the blood which is red like fire was flowing from the eyes.
taṉkaṇ kōppatum
as soon as Tiṇṇaṉār inserted an arrow in his eye.
kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār paṟṟik koṇṭār
the Lord in Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭam cught his hand securely.
[[The story is as follows: God wanted to show Tiṇṇaṉar's devotion to Civakōcariyār; on the sixth day blood was flowing from the right eye. Tiṇṇaṉar tried all cures to stop it but of no avil. At last he removed his eye and grafted it on the lord's eye. When he was in ecstasy God made blood flow from the eye also. Now tiṇṇaṉar was happy as he had succeeded in stopping the flow of blood in the left eye. He thought: I have one more eye. I shall remove it. God could not bear the sight and firmly held his hand and sid thrice kaṇṇappa! stop this!]]
{4:49}__8+
{$} niṟai maṟaikkāṭutaṉṉil
in Maṟikkāṭu which was worshipped by the Vētam which contins many truths before kaliyukam was born.
kaṟai niṟantu eli
when the black rat
taṉ mūkkuc cuṭṭiṭa nīṇṭu eritīpam taṉṉaikkaṉaṉṟu tūṇṭa
trimmed with anger the light that was burning very high as it burnt its nose, as it came there with the intention of eating the ghee in the hollow of the lamp.
kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār koṭuppar
The god in Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭam gave
niṟai kaṭal maṇṇum viṇṇum nīṇṭavāṉ ulakum ellām kuṟaivaṟa
without anything to be desired in the next birth, to rule over this earth surrounded by the sea full of water, heaven and the world where there is ever-lasting bliss.
[[cf: veṇṇey uṇṇa eṇṇupuvantu, nantā viḷakkai nuntupu peyartta, tāvupul elikku mūvulaku āḷa, noytiṉal aḷitta kaivaḷam pōṟṟi (11th tirumuṟi, kōyil nāṉmaṇimāli, 40-31-34)]]
{4:49}__9+
{$} aṇaṅku umai pākamāka aṭakkiya āti mūrtti
God who has a form which is the cuse of all things and contins within him as a part Umi, the divine damsel.
vaṇaṅkuvār iṭarkaḷ tīrkkum maruntu
who is the nectar for removing all the sufferings of those who bow to him
[[This is partial metaphor as sufferings are not compared to diseases.]]
aruntavatta kaṇam pullarkku aruḷkaḷ ceytu
bestowing several kinds of grace upon kaṇampullar who had done severe penance (to be rid of the cycle of birth and death)
kātalām aṭiyārkku eṉṟum kuṇaṅkaḷaik kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār koṭuppar
the Lord in Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭam will give the eight qualities to the devotees who always think of him with love.
[[The eight qualities are:
1.muṟṟaṟivu (omniscience)
2.varampiliṉpa (Happiness that has no finality)
3.iyaṟkaiyaṟivu (Knowing all things without being tught)
4.taṉ vayam (Being self-dependant)
5.varampil āṟṟa (unlimited power)
7.taṉ uṭampu (pure body)
8.iyalpākavē pācaṅkaḷ illāmai (being naturally free from attachments)

{$} eṭuttaṉaṉ eḻiṟkayilai ilaṅkaiyar maṉṉaṉ taṉṉai aṭuttu oru viralāl ūṉṟa
when the king of the people of Ilaṅki who uprooted and held the beautiful Kayili, god pressed him down with a single toe, at once.
alaṟippoy avaṉum vīḻntu viṭuttaṉaṉ
such a strong one as he roared and gave up that effort falling down.
kai narampāl vētakītaṅkaḷ pāṭa
when he sang songs from the Vetam with the help of the strings of the lute removed from his hand.
kuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉār koṟṟavāḷ nāḷ koṭuttaṉar
The Lord in Kuṟukki Vīraṭṭam granted him a victorious sword and long life.

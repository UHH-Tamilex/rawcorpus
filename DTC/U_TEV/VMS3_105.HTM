A note on the shrine: Kalikkāmūr is now known as Aṉṉappaṉpētti

{3:105}__1+
{$}
maṭal varai il matu vimmu cōlai vayal cūḻntu aḻaku ārum (kalikkāmūr)
in (Kalikkāmūr) which is full of beauty as it is surrounded by fields and gardens where from the petals of flowers limitless honey is flowing.
kaṭal varai ōtam kalantu muttam coriyum kalikkāmūr
is Kalikkāmūr where the waves of the sea which are like mountins combine among themselves and pour forth pearls.
uṭal varaiyiṉ uyir vāḻkaiyāya oruvaṉ kaḻal ētta
to prise the feet of the unequalled one who is the cuse for the life of the soul within the body

sufferings will not follow; twin acts, good and bad, will be destroyed; one will receive God's grace

maivaraipōl tiraiyōṭu kūṭi
having joined with the waves which are like the hills on which clouds settle.
ōtam puṭaiyē malintu
the tides increasing by the side of the shore.
kaivaraiyāl vaḷar caṅkam eṅkum mikukkum kalikkāmūr
in Kalikkāmūr where they (waves) heap conches on the shore, in every place.
mey varaiyāṉ makaḷ pākaṉ taṉṉai virumpa
if one loves the Lord who has on half of his form the dughter of the king of the mountins.
uṭal vāḻum aivarai ācaṟuttu āṟum eṉpar
(wise people) say that he will put an end to to the mischief of the five senses that are in the body
[[aintu is spoken of as aivar (rational beings) on account of anger]]
atuvum caratamē
that too is certin.
{3:105}__3+
{$}
kāviyiṉ nērviḻi mātar
ladies whose eyes resemble the blue nelumbo flowers.
tūviya nīr malar ēnti vaiyattavar toḻutu ētta
and the males of this world worship and prise the Lord holding in their hands flowers to scatter at his feet and water to bathe him
[[tūviya -- tūvum poruṭṭu; adverbial participle (viṉaiyeccam)]]
eṉṟum kaviṉ ār kalikkāmūr mēviya īcaṉai empirāṉai virumpi vaḻipaṭṭāl
if one worships with desire the chief who is our master and who resides in Kalikkāmūr which is always full of beauty.
ātimūrtti, amarar perumāṉ
the Lord of primordial form, the chief of the immortals.
āviyuḷ nīṅkalaṉ
will not leave the soul (and will be always in it).
{3:105}__4+
{$}
kuṉṟukaḷ pōl tirai unti -- am taṇ maṇi ārtara
Beautiful and cool pearls which have been pushed out by the waves which are like hills, being abundant.
mēti āyam kaṉṟuṭaṉ pulki maṉai cūḻ kalikkāmūr
in Kalikkāmūr where the herds of buffaloes combine with their calves and surround the houses.
eṉṟu uṇar ūḻiyum vāḻum entai perumāṉ
the great one who is our father and who is from that aeon the world knew the existence of the sun.
aṭi ētti niṉṟu
continously prising his feet.
uṇarvārai niṉaiya killār nīcar
those who do not think highly about those who meditate on them (feet) are low people.
namaṇ tamarē
and they can be compared to the servants of Namaṉ (god of death) in respect of cruelty.
{3:105}__5+
{$}
vāṉiṭai vāḷmati māṭam tīṇṭa
the storeys are being touched by the bright moon in the sky.
maruṅkē kaṭal ōtam kāṉiṭai nīḻavil kaṇṭal vāḻum kaḻi cūḻ kalikkāmūr
in Kalikkāmūr which is surrounded by backwater, where in the shade of the sea-shore garden the fragrant screw-pines are in a flourishing state, as they are dashed by the waves nearby.
āṉiṭai aintu ukantu āṭiṉāṉai
one who bathed gladly in the five products of the cow.
amarar toḻutu ētta
to be worshipped and prised by the immortals.
nāṉ aṭaivu ām vaṇam aṉputanta nalamē niṉaivōm
I shall think about his good act of bestowing his love on me, so that I might be fit to be his devotee.

tuṟai vaḷar kētakai mītu vācam cūḻ vāṉ mali teṉṟal
along with the balmy breeze from the screw-pine which grows in the tuṟi (ghat)
kaṟai vaḷarum kaṭal ōtam eṉṟum kalikkum kalikkāmūr
in Kalikkāmūr where the waves in the black sea are always roaring.
maṟai vaḷarum poruḷ āyiṉāṉai
He is the person who is prised in maṟi above all other gods.
maṉattāl niṉaintu ētta
if one thinks about him and prise him.
niṟai vaḷarum pukaḻ eytum
increasing fame which spreads everywhere will reach him.
vātai niṉaiyā
distresses will not think of afflicting him.
viṉai pōmē
even the twin actions will leave him.

{$}
kōla nal mēṉiyiṉ mātar maintar koṇar maṅkaliyattil
with the auspicious things brought by young men and ladies with beautiful bodies.
kālamum poykkiṉum tām vaḻuvātu iyaṟṟum kalikkāmūr
in Kalikkāmūr where they perform worship without any deficiency, even if the seasonal rins fail,
[[kālam here stands for seasonal rins]]
ñālamum tī vaḷi ñāyiṟu āya nampaṉ
the god in whom we can repose confidence and who is the world, fire, air and sun
[[By the conjuction um in ñālamum, we have to supply his other four forms: space, water, moon and the person who performs sacrifices.]]
kaḻal ētti olamiṭātavar
those who do not cry aloud for protection, worshipping his feet.
ūḻi eṉṟum uṇarvait tuṟantār
though they live for many ages, have permanently left their clear discernment.

ūr aravam talai nīḷ muṭiyāṉ oli nīr ulaku āṇṭu
ruling over the world surrounded by noisy sea having a crown of matted locks of hirs on the head which has crawling serpents.
kār aravak kaṭal cūḻa vāḻum pati ām kalikkāmūr
Kalikkāmūr, a city which prospers surrounded by the blue sea of roaring noise.
tēr aravu alkul am pētai añcat tiruntu varai pērttāṉ
on one who removed the perfect mountin for the young lady who has a waist resembling the central seating place of chariot and the expanded hood of a snake to fear.
ār aravam paṭavaitta pātam uṭaiyāṉ iṭam ām
is the place of the god who placed his foot to make him cry aloud.

aruvarai ēntiya mālum
Māl who held up (as an umbrella) the pre-eminent mountin (gōvardhanam)
maṟṟai alar mēl uṟaivāṉum
and the other one who resides on a (lotus flower).
iruvarum añca eri uruvāy eḻuntāṉ kalikkāmūr
in Kalikkāmūr where the god who rose as a column of fire for both of them to be afrid of.
oru vāraiyāṉ makaḷ pākaṉ taṉṉai uṇarvāl toḻutētta
if one worships and prises with consciousness the god who has on his half the dughter of the mountin which has no equal.
tirumaruvum
wealth will come to him.
citaivu illai
it will not perish.
cemmait tēcu avar pāl uṇṭu
there will be spotless lustre on their bodies.

mācu piṟakkiya mēṉiyārum
those who have abundant dirt on their bodies (jins)
maruvum tuvarāṭai mīcu piṟakkiya meyyiṉārum
those who cover their bodies with shining robes soaked in myrtle dye
[[mīcu -- mītu]]
avar tōṟṟam aṟiyār
do not know his real nature.
kāciṉi nīrttiraḷ maṇṭi eṅkum vaḷam ār kalikkāmūr
in Kalikkāmūr where every place is full of fertility as floods are moving swiftly.
īcaṉai entai pirāṉai ētti niṉaivār viṉaipōm
the actions of those who prise and fix their thoughts on the master who is also our father and Lord, will leave them of their own accord.

āḻiyuḷ nañcu amutu āra uṇṭu aṉṟu
having drunk on that day the poison that rose in the ocean as nectar.
[[PP: ālattiṉāl amirtu ākkiya kōṉ
(Kōviyār, 27)]]
amutu uṇṇa ūḻitoṟum uḷarā amararkku aḷittāṉ
on him who gave the immortals a long life to live in every age as they partook of nectar.
ulakattu uyarkiṉṟa kāḻiyuḷ ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa tamiḻāl
with the Tamiḻ verses sung by Ñāṉacampantaṉ of Kāḻi which is pre-eminent in this world.
kalikkāmūr emmāṉai vaṇaṅki ēttappiṇi maruvā
diseases will not go near them if people bow to our Lord and prise him, in Kalikkāmūr.

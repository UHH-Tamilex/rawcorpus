{1:20}__1+
{$} taṭam nilaviya malainiṟuvi
having set up a spacious mountin (mantaramali)
ōrtaḻal umiḻtarupaṭaaravu koṭu
using the serpent with a hood and vomitting fire, as a cord for turning the churn-stiff.
acuraroṭu amararkaḷ alaikaṭal kaṭaivuḻi
when the acurar and the immortals cheerned the roaming ocean (of milk).
eḻummiku ciṉaviṭam aṭaitaru miṭaṟu uṭaiyavaṉ
Civaṉ has a neck which the poison which rose with great fury reached as its refuge.
viṭaimicaivarum avaṉ uṟaipati
the abode of the god who comes riding on a bull.
tiṭam malitaru maṟaimuṟai uṇar maṟaiyavar niṟai tirumiḻalaiyē
is tirumiḻi where brahmins who understood the vētams in the proper manner as they possess steadness of mind in an abundant measure;
{1:20}__2+
{$} taraiyoṭu tavitalam nalitaru takutiṟal uṟucalataraṉatu varai aṉatalaivicaiyoṭu varutikiriyai aripeṟa aruḷiṉaṉ
Civaṉ granted the discus which served and brought the head of calataraṉ which was like a mountin with speed and had such prowess to harass heaven as well as this earth.
urai malitaru curanati matipoti caṭaiyavaṉ uṟaipati
the place where Civaṉ who contins in his caṭi the Kaṅki, by the river of gods and a crescent.
mikutiraimalikaṭal maṇal aṇipeṟutiṭar vaḷartirumiḻalaiyē
is tirumiḻali where high level grounds that grow in extent and are made beautiful by the sound of the sea where there are many waves.
{1:20}__3+
{$} malaimakaḷ taṉai ikaḻvatu ceytamati aṟuciṟumaṉavaṉatu uyar talaiyiṉoṭu
along with the head of takkaṉ of little mind and devoid of good sense who despised pārvati.
aḻal uruvaṉa karam aṟa muṉivu ceytavaṉ uṟaipati
the abode of Civaṉ who cut off the seven hands of the god of fire to be served (as the hands of the god of fire are seven in number the plural case ending is used)
kalai nilaviya pulavarkaḷ iṭar kaḷaitaru koṭai payilpavarmiku, cilaimalimatiḷ puṭai taḻuviya tikaḻ poḻil vaḷar tirumiḻalaiyē
is tirumiḻali where thriving gardens are by the side of the wall of enclosure built of stones and where liberal donors are on the increases who completely remove the needs of poets in whom knowledge is permanent
[[veyyavaṉ aṅki viḻuṅkattiraṭṭiya, kaiyaittaṟittāṉ eṉṟu untī paṟa, kalaṅkiṟṟu vēḷvi eṉṟu untīpaṟa (tiruvācakam, tiruvuntiyār, 7).]]
{1:20}__4+
{$} maruvalar puram eriyiṉil maṭitara orukaṇai cela niṟuviya peruvaliyiṉaṉ
Civaṉ of great strength who fixed an arrow to destroy the cities of the enemies by fire.
nalamalikaraṉ
has hands which do good.
uram miku piṇam amar vaṉa iruḷiṭai aṭai uṟavoṭu naṭavicai uṟuparaṉ
the supreme god, Civaṉ who dances with speed together with pūtams which reach him, in the darkness of night in the cremation-ground there are very strong corpses.
iṉitu uṟaipati
the city where he dwells with pleasure.
teruviṉil varu peru viḻavu oli malitaravaḷar tirumiḻalaiyē
is flourshing tirumiḻali where during big festivals when the deities are taken in procession in the streets, the bustle increases.
{1:20}__5+
{$} aṇipeṟu vaṭamara niḻaliṉil amarvoṭum aṭiyiṇai iruvarkaḷ paṇitara
when the four sages two on each side made obeisance to the feet, being seated under the shade of the beautiful banyan tree.
aṟaneṟi maṟaiyoṭum aruḷiya paraṉuṟaivu iṭam
the abode of the supreme god who expounded the right conduct with vētams.
oḷimaṇiporuvarum marakatam nilam mali puṉal aṇaitaru vayal aṇitirumiḻalai
is tirumiḻali which is made beautiful of fields where abundant water is in the ground, which is like the emerald which has no comparison with any other brilliant precious stone, becuse of its depth.
tiṇipoḻil tarumaṇam matunukar aṟupatam mural tirumiḻalai
tirumiḻali where bees having six legs hum having sucked the fragrant honey in the dense gardens.
{1:20}__6+
{$} vacai aṟuvalivaṉacaravuruvatu koṭu
assuming the form of a hunter possessing strength without any blemish, and fighting strongly with vacayaṉ who performed such severe penance that even sages could not think of, so that pārvati can know his strength.
acaivu ilapaṭai aruḷ puritarum avaṉ uṟaipati atu
the city where the god who granted a weapon which knows no defeat, dwells.
mikutaru ticaiyiṉil malar kulaviya ceṟipoḻil malitaru tirumiḻalaiyē
is tirumiḻali where there are many dense gardens where flowers are shining wherever one turns his looks.
{1:20}__7+
{$} nalam malitaru maṟaimoḻiyoṭu, nati uṟupuṉal, pukai, oḷi mutal, malar avaikoṭu vaḻipaṭutiṟal maṟaiyavaṉ uyir atukoḷavaru, calam malitaru maṟalitaṉ uyirkeṭa utaicey araṉ uṟaipati
the city where Civaṉ who kicked to remove the life of the god of death who came with where araṉ who kicked to remove the life of the god of death who came with malicious anger to take away the life of the brahmin (mārkkaṇtēyaṉ) who had the strength of mind to worship Civaṉ with mantirams of vētams which do good, water from the river (Kaṅki), incense, lamps and flowers.
tilakam itu eṉa ulakukaḷ pukaḻtarupoḻil aṇi tirumiḻalaiyē
is tirumiḻali adorned by gardens, which is prised by all the worlds as being eminent among the cities.
{1:20}__8+
{$} araṉ uṟaitaru kayilaiyai nilaikulaivatu ceyta tacamukaṉatu karam irupatum neritara viral niṟuviya kaḻal aṭi uṭaiyavaṉ
Civaṉ who has feet which wear Kaḻi, and who set up his toe to crush the twenty hands of the arakkaṉ of ten faces who shook the mountin, Kayili where araṉ dwells, from its position.
varaṉmuṟai ulaku avaitarum malar vaḷar maṟaiyavaṉ vaḻi vaḻuviya ciram atukoṭu
holding the skull of maṟiyavaṉ who is thriving on a lotus flower, who erred from the right path, and who created all the worlds according to tradition.
palitiritaru civaṉ uṟaipati tirumiḻalaiyē
tirumiḻali is the place where Civaṉ who wanders for alms, dwells.
{1:20}__9+
{$} ayaṉoṭum eḻil amarmalar makaḷ makiḻ (1) kaṇaṉ aḷaviṭal oḻiya
Kaṇṇaṉ who is the joy of the beautiful goddess of wealth who is seated in the red lotus, along with Ayaṉ, to give up measuring (kaṇṇaṉ has become kaṇaṉ)
ōrpayam uṟuvakai taḻal nikaḻvatu ōrpaṭi uru atuvara
assuming a form which was shining as a mass of five to be frightened.
cayacaya eṉa miku tuticeyaveḷi uruviya avaṉ uṟaipati
the abode of Civaṉ who went beyond the sky for both of them to prise very much saying victory, victory!
ceyam nilaviya matilmati atu tavaḻtara uyartirumiḻalaiyē
is tirumiḻali where on the wall of fortification which was always victorious and so high that the moon had to crawl on it.
[[Variant reading: (1) vaṇaṉ]]
{1:20}__10+
{$} ikaḻ uruvoṭu paṟitalai koṭum iḻitoḻil malicamaṇ virakiṉar, tikaḻ tuvaruṭai uṭal potipavar keṭa
the camaṇar who are cunning and commit mean acts with bodies to be despised and with plucking the hirs on their heads, and the buddhists who cover their bodies with a robs soaked in shining yellow ochre, to meet with ruin.
aṭiyavar mika aruḷi
granting his grace to devotees in an abundant measure.
pukaḻ uṭaiiṟai uṟaipati
the city where the master with fame dwells.
puṉal aṇikaṭalpuṭai taḻuviya puvi tikaḻ curartaru (1) nikarkoṭaiyiṉar ceṟivoṭu tikaḻtaru miḻalaiyē
is eminent tirumiḻali where in this earth which is surrounded by the ocean of beautiful water, liberal donors who can be compared to the Kaṟpakam, the divine tree, are conspicuous by their modesty.
[[Variant reading: (1) nikaḻ]]
{1:20}__11+
{$} ciṉam malikari uriceyta civaṉ uṟaitaru tirumiḻalaiyai
about Tirumiḻali where Civaṉ who flayed an angry elephant, dwells.
mikutaṉamaṉar cirapuranakar iṟaitamiḻ virakaṉatu urai orupatum
the ten verses of the expert in tamiḻ who is the chief of the city of cirāpuram (cīkāḻi) where great wealthy people live.
maṉam makiḻvoṭu payilpavar
those who practise singing them with their hearts full of joy.
eḻilmalarmakaḷ, kalaimakaḷ, cayamakaḷ, iṉammalipukaḻmakaḷ icaitara irunilaṉiṭai iṉitu amarvar
will live happily in this wide world, as the goddess of wealth, goddess of knowledge, goddess of victory and goddess of fame which is of several kinds, being united in them.

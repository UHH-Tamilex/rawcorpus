[[It is wrongly spelt as cīrkāḻi [although] it is [in fact] a corruption of (śrī + kāḻi); śrī becomes cī; so cīkāḻi is the correct name; if it were (cīr + kāḻi), it should according to canti rules be cīrkkāḻi (cīrkkāḻi)]]

{1:24}__1+
{$} pū ār koṉṟaip puripuṉcaṭai īcā! kāvāy
Lord who has a red and twisted caṭi adorned with shining flowers of indian laburnum! protect us!
eṉaniṉṟu ēttum kāḻiyār
so saying the Lord in Kāḻi who is prised by human beings and celestials, standing in his presence.
mēvār puram mūṉṟu aṭṭār avar (pōlām) pāār iṉcoṟ payilum paramarē
is the supreme god who is the meaning of the sweet words that are abundant in poetry, and who destroyed the three cities of the enemies
[[pōlām this is an expletive in this and the following verses]]
{1:24}__2+
{$} imaiyōr, entai!' eṉṟu pukuntuīṇṭi, kantam mālaikoṇṭu cēr kāḻiyār
the god in Kāḻi where the celestials who do not wink, enter into this world and crowd with fragrant garlands and sandal-wood passte prising our father.
venta nīṟṟar
one who smears his body with well burnt sacred ash.
vimalar avar(pōlām) anti naṭṭam āṭum aṭikaḷē
the spotless one is the deity who dances in the evening.
[[The dance is called cantiyātāṇṭavam]]
{1:24}__3+
{$} tēṉaiveṉṟa moḻiyāḷ orupākam, kāṉamāṉ kaikkoṇṭa kāḻiyār
the god in Kāḻi who has a lady whose words are superior to the sweetness of honey, on one half and a deer of the forest on the hand.
[[kāṉamāṉ this is a generic epithet, as that deer which Civaṉ is holding in his hand was created in the sacrificial fire]].
vāṉam ōṅku kōyilavar (pōlām), āṉaiṉpam āṭum aṭikaḷē
is the deity who bathes in the five sweet products of the cow and who resides in the temple which rises into the sky.
{1:24}__4+
{$} māṇāveṉṟik kālaṉ maṭiyavē, kāṇā māṇikku aḷitta kāḻiyār
the god in Kāḻi who granted his grace to the piramacari who did not care to look at any other thing except Civaṉ, to make the god of death (Kālaṉ) of inglorious victory, die.
nāṇ ār vāḷi toṭṭāravar (pōlām) pēṇārpuraṅkaḷ aṭṭaperumāṉ
is the great one who destroyed the cities of the enemies by discharging an arrow which was fixed in the string of the bow.
{1:24}__5+
{$} māṭē ōtam eṟiya vayaṟ cenneṟkāṭu ēṟiccaṅkīṉum kāḻiyār
the god in Kāḻi where the conches climb up the red Variety of paddy growing in the fields like a forest, where the tide throws on the sides, and bear pearls in them.
vāṭāmalarāḷ paṅkaravar (pōlām), ēṭu ārpurammūṉṟu eritta iṟaivar
and the one who has on his half a lady whose flowers do not fade, is the god who burnt the three cities which had blemish attached to them.
[[vāṭāmalarāḷ the flowers worn by Umi do not fade; this explins that her chastity is divine.]]
[[ēṭu blemish;
PP: ēṭēṟu peruñcelvattueḻil maṟaiyōr (periya tirumoḻi, 4-1-7)]]
{1:24}__6+
{$} koṅku cerunti koṉṟai malarkūṭak kaṅkai puṉainta caṭaiyār kāḻiyār
the god in Kāḻi who has a caṭi on which is adorned Kaṅki in addition to the flowers of indian laburnum and fragrant flowers of panicled pear tree.
aṅkaṇ aravam āṭṭumavar (pōlām) ceṅkaṇ arakkar purattai erittār
he is the one who burnt the cities of the arakkar of red eyes, and makes the cobras worn on his person, to dance.
{1:24}__7+
{$} kollai viṭaimuṉ pūtam kuṉittu āṭum, kallavaṭattai ukappārkāḻiyār
the god in Kāḻi who feels glad with the instrument Kallavaṭam which is played upon by the pūtams which bend their forms and dance before the bull belonging to the sylvan region.
alla iṭattum naṭantār avar (pōlām), palla iṭattum payilum paramar
the supreme one who is pervading in many places, went to places where his greatness is not understood and prised without thinking whether they are high or low.
{1:24}__8+
{$} eṭutta arakkaṉ neriyaviral ūṉṟik, kaṭuttu muriya aṭarttār kāḻiyār
the god in kāḻi who pressed down angrily by gently fixing his his toe to crush the arakkaṉ who lifted (the mountin)
eṭutta pāṭaṟku iraṅkumavar (pōlām), (1) poṭikkoḷ nīṟu pūcum puṉitarē
the pure one who smears the ash which is dust, took pity (on him) as he began to prise god by singing cāma vētam.
[[Variant reading: (1) poṭikkaṇīṟu]]
{1:24}__9+
{$} āṟṟal uṭaiya ariyum piramaṉum, tōṟṟam kāṇā veṉṟik kāḻiyār
the victorious god in Kāḻi whose origin both the ari (Māl) of great power and Piramaṉ could not find out.
ēṟṟam ēṟu (aṅku) ēṟum avar (pōlām), kūṟṟam maṟukak kumaitta kuḻakarē
is the young one who destroyed the god of death to become bewildered and who rides on a superior bull.
{1:24}__10+
{$} perukkap pitaṟṟum camaṇar cākkiyar karakkum uraiyai viṭṭār kāḻiyār
the god in Kāḻi who was away from the cunning words of camaṇar and cākkiyar who prate incessantly without any truth.
irukkiṉ malintaiṟaivā avar (pōlām), aruppiṉ mulaiyāḷ paṅkattu aiyarē
he who is much prised in the rik-vētam is the Lord worthy of respect who has a lady whose breasts resemble buds of (Lotus)
[[arumpu: has become aruppu to be in line with the assonance etukai. irukku this may mean the Rig-veda or all the four vetams).
{1:24}__11+
{$} kār ār vayalcūḻ kāḻikkōṉtaṉaic, cīrār ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa, pārār pukaḻap parava vallavar
those who are capable of prising the god to be luded by the people of this world, with the verses with which famous ñāṉacampantaṉ prised the Lord in Kāḻi surrounded by fields where paddy harvested in the riny season grows.
ēr ār vāṉattu iṉitāiruppārē
will dwell happily in the beautiful heaven.

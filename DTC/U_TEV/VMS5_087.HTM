{$}
paṭṭa neṟṟiyar
Civaṉ wears a plate of gold on the forehead
nāḷ toṟum niṉṟu naṭṭam navilpavar
dily dances standing
ciṭṭar vāḻ tiru ār maṇañcēri em vaṭṭavār caṭaiyār vaṇṇam vāḻttum
(people of this world!) prise the nature of the Lord who has tied his long cati into the form of a circle, in Maṇañcēri which has abundant wealth and where people of good conduct live.
{5:87}__2+
{$}
tuṉṉuvār kuḻalāḷ umaiyoṭum
in addition to placing Umiyaḷ who has long and thick tresses of hir
piṉṉuvār caṭaimēl piṟai vaittavar
Civaṉ placed the crescent on the intertwining long caṭi.
maṉṉuvār
who is staying permanently (in maṇañcēri)
maṇañcēri maruntiṉai uṉṉuvār viṉai āyiṉa ōyumē
the old Karmams of those who think about the nectar in maṇañcēri will become enfeebled
[[maṉṉuvār ākiya maruntiṉai]]
{5:87}__3+
{$}
puṟṟil āṭu aravu āṭṭum puṉitaṉār
the pure Civaṉ who makes the cobra living in the anthill to dance.
teṟṟiṉārpuram tī eḻac ceṟṟavar
He destroyed the cities of the perverse enemies to make the flames to consume them.
cuṟṟiṉār matil cūḻ maṇañcēriyār
the Lord in maṇañcēri surrounded by a wall of enclosure
paṟṟiṉār (avar) paṟṟu avar
he is the refuge of those who seek him as their support.
kāṇmiṉ
(people of this world!) you understand this (and act according to that)
{5:87}__4+
{$}
mattamum matiyamum vaḷar ceñcaṭai muttar
Civaṉ who is naturally free from all bondage and on whose head dhatura flowers and the crescent are prospering
[[muttaṉ mutti vaḻaṅkum pirāṉ (Kōviyār, 127)]]
mukkaṇar
who has three eyes.
mūcu aravam aṇi cittar
who possesses mystic powers and adorns himself with a cobra that makes a hissing noise
tīvaṇar
who has the colour of fire.
maṇañcēri em vittar tām
and who is the origin of all things in maṇañcēri
viruppārai virupparē
desires those who desire him (Civaṉ)
{5:87}__5+
{$}
tuḷḷu māṉmaṟi tū maḻuvāḷiṉar
Civaṉ has a young frisking deer and a pure battle-axe (vāḷ is used in the sense of a weapon, not sword)
caṭaimēl avar veḷḷa nīr karantār
he concealed on his caṭi water of Kaṅki which descended torrentially
aḷḷal ār vayal cūḻ maṇañcēri em vaḷḷalār kaḻal vāḻttal vāḻvu āvatē
that life is worth living if one prises the feet of our munificent god in maṇañcēri which is surrounded by fields of mire.
{5:87}__6+
{$}
nīr paranta nimir puṉ caṭaiyiṉ mēl ūr paranta urakam aṇipavar
Civaṉ adorns cobras that crawl, on his caṭi which stands upright and is golden in colour, and on which the water (of Kaṅki) is spreading.
cīr paranta tirumaṇañcēriyār, ēr parantu aṅku ilaṅku cūlattarē
he is in maṇañcēri whose fame has spread far and wide, and has a trident shining becuse its beauty has spread everywhere.
{5:87}__7+
{$} cuṇṇattar
Civaṉ besmears himself with powdered holy ash.
cuṭu nīṟu ukantu āṭalar
he bathes with joy in the well burnt holy ash
viṇṇattu am mati cūṭiya vētiyar
the brahmin among gods who wore the beautiful crescent that shines in the sky.
maṇṇattu am muḻavu ār maṇañcēriyār
the god in maṇañcēri where the beautiful muḻavu is played on marriage occasions
[[maṇṇattu = maṇattu; ṇ is an augmentation to suit yetuki
(etukai); maṇ attu muḻavu muḻavu on which black paste is smeard on the head of the drum, to increase its resonance]]
vaṇṇattu am mulaiyāḷ umai vaṇṇar
he has the nature of placing on one half Umi who has breasts with beauty and shining colour
{5:87}__8+
{$}
tuṉṉa(m) āṭaiyar
Civaṉ who wears a sewn dress (tuṉṉam - taittal sewing)
tū maḻuvāḷiṉar
who has a pure battle-axe
piṉṉu ceñcaṭaimēl piṟai vaittavar
who placed on his red caṭi which is intertwined, a crescent.
maṉṉuvār poḻil cūḻ maṇañcēri em maṉṉaṉār kaḻalē toḻa
if we worship the feet of our King in maṇañcēri surrounded by long and prospering gardens.
vāykkum
we shall get what all we want.

{$}
cittar tēvarkaḷ māloṭu nāṉ mukaṉ pukaḻavē puttar (1) tēr amaṇkaiyar mattar (tām) aṟiyār
when the people with mystic powers, tēvar, Māl and Piramaṉ of four faces prise Civaṉ, the buddhists and low amaṇar who live in groups, and who are mad men do not know him
[[Amaṇar always lived in groups;
cf. amaṇkuṭi (Naṉṉūl, 211, Cankaranamaccivāyar's commentary)]]
maṇañcēri em attaṉār aṭiyārkku allal illaiyē
there are no sufferings to those devotees of our father in Maṇañcēri
[[Variant reading: (1) tēr]]

kaṭutta mēṉi arakkaṉ kayilaiyai eṭuttavaṉ
the arakkaṉ of detestable body who lifted Kayili
neṭu nīḷ muṭi pattu iṟappaṭuttalum
when Civaṉ cused the very tall ten crowns to be crushed.
maṇañcēri! aruḷ eṉa
when he (arakkaṉ) begged The Lord in maṇañcēri Bestow your grace on me
koṟṟavāḷoṭu nāmam koṭuttaṉaṉ
he (Civaṉ) gave him a victorious sword and the title of īcvaraṉ (īccuvaraṉ) (irāvaṇēccuvaraṉ irāvaṇēcvaraṉ)

{7:77}__1+
{$} karavuil aruvi kamuku uṇṇa
the floods that arrive without failure to submerge the areca-palms
teṅkaṅkulaikkīḻkkaruppālai aravam tiraikkāvirikkōṭṭattu aiyāṟu uṭaiya (1) aṭikēḷō (ōlam)
of God having Aiyāṟu on the bank of Kāviri of waves which make a noise along with the sound of the sugar-cane press, under the coconut tree with clusters of fruits as your place! (please help me)
paravum paricu oṉṟu (um) aṟiyēṉ nāṉ
I do not know even a little of the proper manner of prising you.
paṇṭē ummaip payilātēṉ
as I did not come to you in previous births, to worship you
iravum pakalum niṉaintālum
Though I think of you night and day
eyta niṉaiyamāṭṭēṉ nāṉ
I do not adequately concentrate on you
[[The ō aṭikēḷō is an exclamation entreating succour in distress; this will apply to all the verses in this decade;
aṇmaiyiṉ iyalpum īṟuaḻivum cēymaiyiṉ, aḷapum pulampaliṉ ōvum ākum (nāṉṉūl, 313)]]
[[Variant reading: (1) aṭikaḷō; this is to be taken as the Variant reading in the following nine verses]]

{7:77}__3+
{$} paruvi vicci malaic cāral paṭṭai koṇṭu pakaṭu āṭi
Having played with elephants with the barks of trees in the slopes of the mountin belonging to Vicci, which is situated in the region of Paruvi
[[NOTE_HISTORY:
It is argued that these three names are of three different sons who originated three different branches of the family, viz. Paruvi, Kaivra and Talakād. But the records of Avini of Talakād show that Paruvi-vishaya i.e. Paruvi region was within him
(A History of Karnātāka by Dr.P.B. Desi, 1970; page 66-67)]]
[[NOTE_HISTORY:
The Ganga records of this period speak distinctly of two houses, the Pāruvis and the members of Taḷakād house of which the former seems to have been befriended by the Pallavas. (The Kongu country by Dr.M.Arokiaswami p.118);]]
[[PP_X: A chieftin by name Vicci is mentioned in Kuṟuntoki, as having fought with the kings who had the right to wear crowns
(Kuṟuntoki, 328, Kapilar)]]
[[PP_X: the court-poet of Pāri, took his dughters and requested Vicci to marry them, which he refused for fear of incurring the enmity of the three Tamiḻ Kings
(Puṟam, 200)]]
kuruvi oppikkiḷikaṭivārkuḻalmēl mālaikoṇṭu ōṭṭantantu
flowing swiftly snatching the garlands on the tresses of hir of girls who drive small birds of flee and scare away parrots (in the millet farms)
aravantiraik kāvirik karaikkōṭṭattu aiyāṟu uṭaiya aṭikēḷō
oh God! who has aiyāṟu as your place which is on the bank of the Kāviri with noisy waves!
maruvippiriya māṭṭēṉ nāṉ
I shall not leave you after coming into close contact with you so many times.
vaḻiniṉṟu oḻintēṉ oḻikilēṉ
I stood steadfast in the way allotted to me; I shall not deviate from it at any time (ōlam please help me)

{$} vāḻaikkulait teṅku koṇarntu karaimēl eṟiyavē aḻaku ārtiraikkāvirik kōṭṭattu aiyāṟu uṭaiya aṭikēḷō
oh! God who has aiyāṟu as your place which is on the bank of the Kāviri with waves made beautiful by throwing on the banks the cluster of plintin fruits and clusters of cocoanuts!
ikaḻātu umakku āṭpaṭṭōrkku
before those who became your protege without despising you
ēkapaṭam oṉṟu araiccārtti
one who wears on the waist a single cloth
kuḻakā
Youth!
paḻakā niṉṟu paṇiceyvār peṟṟapayaṉ oṉṟu aṟikilēṉ
I am unable to understand the benefit that those who have rendered service to you having been familiar with you for a long time
(Those who perform any service to you perform it without the idea of expecting any reward; I shall also do likewise).

malaiyum nilaṉum koḷḷāmai

kaḻaikkoḷ piracam kalantu

piḻaiyaittīrappaṇiyāy (ōlam)

{$} tēcam eṅkum (1) teḷittu āṭa
the people in the country to bathe sprinkling the water on their heads.
teṇṇīr aruvi koṇarntu eṅkum vācam tiraikkāvirikkōṭṭattu aiyāṟu uṭaiya aṭikēḷō (ōlam)
oh God in the shrine in aiyāṟu on the bank of the Kāviri which stays everywhere bringing with it streams with clear water! (please help me)
kūci aṭiyār iruntālum
though the devotees are shy to express explicitly their wants.
kuṇam oṉṟu illīr
you do not have the nature of fulfilling their wants understanding them
kuṟippu illīr
you do not have even the idea to do so
tēca vēntaṉ tirumālum malarmēl āyaṉum (2) kāṇakilār
tirumāl, the master of the world and Ayaṉ who is seated on a (lotus) flower, are unable to see you (when those two great gods were unable to see you how can other small people see you?)

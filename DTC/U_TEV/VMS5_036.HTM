{5:36}__1+
{$}
kāṉ aṟāta kaṭipoḻil vaṇṭiṉam, tēṉ aṟāta tiruc cempoṉpaḷḷiyāṉ
Civaṉ in Tiruccempoṉpaḷḷi where honey does not cease even after the swarm of bees drink it, in the bright gardens where fragrance never leaves them
[[kāṉ : fragrance.]]
[[PP: kāṉalaṅkāvum kayamum tukuttiyum
(Paripāṭal, 16-17);]]
[[PP: maṇattaiyuṭaiya kāviṉum, kayaṅkaḷiṉum, āṟṟiṭaik kuṟaiyiṉum; kāṉalaṅkā eṉ puḻi allum ammum cāriyai
(Do. Parimēlaḻakar's commentary)]]
ūṉ aṟātatu ōr veṇtalaiyil pali tāṉ aṟātatu ōr koḷkaiyaṉ kāṇ miṉ
has the nature of not leaving begging alms in a white skull from which flesh has not left; you know this.
{5:36}__2+
{$}
eṉpum āmaiyum pūṇṭu aṅku uḻitarvarkku iṉi aṉpum āyiṭum; āy iḻaiyīr!
Ladies who wear choice ornaments love Civaṉ who is wandering adorning himself with bones and the shell of a tortoise, now itself.
cempoṉpaḷḷi uḷāṉ civalōkaṉai
one who is in civalokam and is in cempoṉpaḷḷi for our benefit
nam poṉ paḷḷi uḷka viṉai nācamē
our Karmams will be destroyed if we meditate upon him in our beautiful minds.
{5:36}__3+
{$}
āṇ alar peṇ alar vēṟu kōlattar
Civaṉ is neither male nor female but assumes a different form so that it is difficult to say exactly this is his form.
kīṟu kōvaṇa 1. aitukil āṭaiyar
wears a beautiful loin-cloth torn from a big cloth?
cempoṉpaḷḷi āṟu cūṭiya aṇṇal avaṉait, tēṟal āvatu oṉṟu aṉṟu
it is difficult to have a clear idea about Civaṉ who adorns himself with a river and who is in cempoṉpaḷḷi
[[Variant reading: 1. aintukil]]
{5:36}__4+
{$}
aruvarātatu ōr veṇtalai ēnti vantu
coming holding a white skull which he does not consider loathsome
iruvarāy iṭuvār kaṭai tēṭuvār
will enquire after the entrance of the houses of those who give alms, being himself and Umai.
teru elām uḻalvār
will wander in all the streets
cempoṉpaḷḷiyār oruvar tām palapēr uḷar, kāṇmiṉ
those Civaṉ in cempoṉpaḷḷi is the unequalled one he has many names.
{5:36}__5+
{$}
pū ulām caṭaimēl puṉal cūṭiṉāṉ
Civaṉ bore water on the caṭai on which there are flowers.
val ēvāl eyil mūṉṟum erittavaṉ
burnt all the three forts with a strong arrow.
tēvar ceṉṟu iṟaiñcum cempoṉpaḷḷiyāṉ
the god in cempoṉpaḷḷi where the celestials go and make obeissance to him.
mūvarāy mutalāy niṉṟa mūrtti
is the one with a form who is the creator, protector and destroyer and the origin of those three gods
{5:36}__6+
{$}
tilaka nīḷ muṭiyār cempoṉpaḷḷiyār
Civaṉ in cempoṉpaḷḷi who has a crown superior to other crowns
[[tilaka nīḷ muṭittēvar (civakacintāmaṇi, 246; cūḷamaṇi, cuyamvaraccarukkam, 69)]]
kula villāl eyil eyta kūttar
and the dancer who shot an arrow on the forts with a superior bow.
calavarāy
carrying the water of the Kaṅkai
pāmpoṭu taṇ matik kalavarāvatiṉ kāraṇam eṉ (kol, o)
what is the reason for joining with it the cobra and the cool crescent?
{5:36}__7+
{$}
kai koḷ cūlattar
Civaṉ has a trident in his hand
kaṭṭuvāṅkattiṉar
has a battle-axe.
maikoḷ kaṇṭattar āki
and having a black neck.
iru cuṭar
is the form of the sun and the moon.
ceyya mēṉi veṇṇīṟṟar
smears white holy ash on his red body.
cempoṉpaḷḷi aiyar kaiyatu ōr aintalai nākamē
a five hooded cobra is in the hands of the father in cempoṉpaḷḷi
{5:36}__8+
{$}
veṅkaṇ nākam veruvuṟa ārttavar
Civaṉ tied a cobra of cruel eyes to frighten people
paiṅkaṇ āṉaiyiṉ īr uri pōrttavar
covered himself with the skin of an elephant with eyes fuming with anger, wet with blood
ceṅkaṇ māl viṭaiyār
has as his bull Māl who has red eyes
cempoṉpaḷḷiyār
Civaṉ in cempoṉpaḷḷi
aṅkaṇāy aṭaintār viṉai tīrppar
will remove the Karmams of those who take refuge in him as their support.
{5:36}__9+
{$}
naṉṟi nāraṇaṉ nāṉ mukaṉ eṉṟa ivar
both nāraṇaṉ who has all good things and Piramaṉ of four faces.
cempoṉpaḷḷiyāṉ niṉṟa cūḻalil nīḷ eri āki
when the god in cempoṉpaḷḷi rose as a column of fire in the surroundings in which he stood.
niṉṟa nīḷ muṭiyōṭu aṭikāṇpuṟṟuc ceṉṟu kāṇpariyāṉ
Civaṉ whose long and permanent head and feet could not be seen though they went in search of them, flying in the sky and digging the earth, respectively.
{5:36}__10+
{$}
tiriyum mummatil aṉal ceṅkaṇai oṉṟiṉāl eriya eytu
shooting a single straight arrow which is the god of fire on the wandering three forts to cause them to burn.
ilaṅkaik kōṉ neriya ūṉṟiyiṭṭār cempoṉpaḷḷiyār (avar)
Civaṉ in cempoṉpaḷḷi who pressed with his toe to crush the King of ilaṅkai
ariya vāṉam aruḷ ceyvar
will grant to his devotees bliss in civalokam which is difficult to attain (that same god who snubbed the pride of Irāvaṇaṉ grants civalokam to his devotees, so avar is used

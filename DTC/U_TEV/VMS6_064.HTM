kūṟṟuvaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ is the destroyer of all things;
[[PP: tōlā nallicai nālvaruḷḷum kūṟṟottīyē māṟṟarun cīṟṟam
(Puṟam, 56-10-11)]]
[[PP: maṇi miṭaṟṟōṉaik kūṟṟam eṉṟatu, aḻittal toḻil uṭaimaiyiṉ
(do. old commentary)]]
kūṟṟuvaṉaik kumaitta kōṉ (kāṇ)
is the King who trod upon the god of death and killed him.
kuvalayaṉ (kāṇ)
is the earth.
kuvalayattil nīr āṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is the water in the world.
kāṟṟu avaṉ (kāṇ)
he is the air.
kaṉalavaṉ (kāṇ)
he is the fire.
kalikkum miṉ (kāṇ)
is the quick lightning.
kaṉa pavaḷac cemmēṉi kalanta veḷḷai nīṟṟavaṉ (kāṇ)
has sacred ash which is smeared in the red body which is like the precious coral.
nilā ūrum ceṉṉiyāṉ (kāṇ)
has on his head a crescent that crawls.
niṟai(ya) ārnta puṉal kaṅkai nimircaṭaimēl ēṟṟavaṉ (kāṇ)
received on the erect caṭi Kaṅki which is full of water.
eḻil ārum poḻil ār kacci ēkampaṉ (kāṇ) avaṉ eṉ eṇṇattāṉ (ē)
is the Lord in Ēkampam of Kacci which has many gardens full of beauty; he is always in my thoughts.
[[All the kāṇ in this as well as in the following verses should be taken as expletives]]

pal uyirkaḷ āki eṅkum parantavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ pervaded everywhere being himself many living beings.
paṇintu eḻuvār pāvamum viṉaiyum pōkat turantavaṉ (kāṇ)
drove away the sins and the twin acts of sin and virtue to leave those who wake up with the idea of worshipping god.
tūmalar am kaṇṇiyāṉ (kāṇ)
wears a chaplet of pure flower (koṉṟi).
tōṟṟam nilai iṟutip poruḷ āy vanta maruntu avaṉ (kāṇ)
he is the rare nectar who is the creator, preserver and destroyer.
1. vaiyakaṅkaḷ poṟai tīrppāṉ (kāṇ)
will remove the burden of the worlds.
malar tūvi niṉaintu eḻuvār uḷḷam nīṅkātu iruntavaṉ (kāṇ)
dwelt in the hearts of those of those who rise remembering him, scattering flowers, without leaving them.
eḻil ārum poḻil ār kacci ēkampaṉ kāṇ avaṉ eṉ eṇṇattāṉē
see 1st verse.
[[Variant reading: 1. vaiyakaṅkaḷ]]

nīṟṟavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ smears himself with sacred ash.
nīr ākit tī āṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is the water and the fire.
niṟai maḻuvum tamarukamum aṉalum kaiyil tōṟṟavaṉ (kāṇ)
he holds in his hand very heavy battle-axe, a drum which tapers in the middle like the sun-glass; and fire.
tōṟṟak kēṭu illātāṉ (kāṇ)
has no birth and death.
tuṇai ili (kāṇ)
has no equal;
[[taṉakkuvamai illātāṉ (Kuṟal, 7)]]
tuṇai eṉṟu toḻuvar uḷḷam pōṟṟavaṉ (kāṇ)
protects the minds of those who worship him with hands joined thinking him to be their companion.
pukaḻkaḷ tamaip paṭaittāṉ (tāṉ-kāṇ)
has many kinds of fame.
viri caṭaimēl poṟi aravam kaṅkaip puṉalum eṟṟavaṉ (kāṇ)
received on his spreading caṭi cobras with spots and water of the Kaṅki.
eḻilār ...... eṇṇattāṉē
see 1st verse.

1. ulakiṟkut tāyavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ is the mother to all beings in this earth.
taṉ oppu illāt tattuvaṉ (kāṇ)
is the ultimate reality which has no equal to him.
malai maṅkai paṅkā eṉpār vāyavaṉ (kāṇ)
is in the mouths of thsoe who say Civaṉ who has as no half the dughter of the mountin.
[[Civaṉ remins in the words of devotees and grants the fruits of their devotion]]
vāṉavarkkum tāṉavarkkum maṇ uḷōrkkum varum piṟavi nōy tīrppāṉ (kāṇ)
will remove birth which is like a disease, which is both cuse and effect to the celestials, acurar and human beings in this earth.
niṉaiyārkkuc cēyavaṉ (kāṇ)
he is at a great distance to those who do not think of him.
cittam ārat tiruvaṭiyē uḷki niṉaintu eḻuvār uḷḷam ēyavaṉ (kāṇ)
stayed in the hearts of those who wake up from sleep thinking without ceasing the holy feet, to the complete satisfaction of the mind.
eḻil ...... eṇṇattāṉ
see 1st verse.
[[Variant reading: 1. ulakukkut]]

ayil vāy ampāl eyil mūṉṟum aḻittavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ destroyed all the three forts with an arrow of sharp end.
aiyāṟum iṭaimarutum āḷvāṉ (tāṇ-kāṇ)
rules over aiyāṟu and iṭimarutu.
paḻittavaṉ (kāṇ) aṭaiyārai
does not love those who do not approach him.
aṭaivār taṅkaḷ paṟṟavaṉ (kāṇ)
he is the support of those who approach him.
puṟṟu arava nāṇiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
wears a waist cord which is a cobra living in the anthill.
muṭik kaṅkai cuḻittavaṉ (kāṇ)
controlled Kaṅki in his head.
aṭiyē pōṟṟum tūya māmuṉivarkkāyp pārmēl niṟka iḻintavaṉ kāṇ
let down Kaṅki to remin in the world for the benefit of the sages who prise his feet only, for their bath and ablutions
[[If the reading is singular as muṉivarkkā then it will mean the sage pakirtaṉ;
tavam purintu uyarnta māmuṉi koṇarnta kaṅkai (periya tirumoḻi, 1-4-8)]]

pāṭal pēṇi naṭam āṭi acaintavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ moved about dancing, and singing songs with desire.
aḻal vaṇṇattil aṭiyum muṭiyum tēṭap pacaintavaṉ (kāṇ)
was kind towareds (Māl and Piramaṉ) when both of them searched the feet and head of the form of a column of fire.
pēykkaṇṅkaḷ paravi ēttum pāṉmaiyaṉ (kāṇ)
he is so easy of approach that groups of pēy prise him in the second and third persons.
pāvi niṉaintu eḻuvār tampāl kacintavaṉ kāṇ
grew tender hearted towards those who wake up prising in the second person and thinking of him.
kariyiṉ uri pōrttāṉ (tāṉ-kāṇ)
covered himself with a skin of an elephant.
kaṭalil viṭam uṇṭu amararkku amutam īya icaintavaṉ (kāṇ)
consented to give the nectar to the celestials himself consuming the poison that appeared in the ocean.
eḻil ...... eṇṇattāṉē

vaṉ kūṟṟai muṭittavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ put an end to the life of the strong god of death.
cīṟṟat tīyāl valiyārtam puram mūṉṟum vēvac cāpam piṭittavaṉ (kāṇ)
held in his hand a bow to burn all the three cities of the strong acurar, by the fire of his anger.
piññakaṉām vēṭattāṉ (kāṇ)
has a form in which he adorns himself with a peacock's feather on his head.
piṇaiyal veṟi kamaḻ koṉṟai aravu ceṉṉi muṭittavaṉ (kāṇ)
decorated his head with koṉṟi flowers which spread fragrance and joined together as a garland, and a cobras.
mū vilai nal vēliṉāṉ (kāṇ)
has a trident of three blades.
urum eṉattōṉṟum maḻai āy
being the cloud from which thunder is produced.
miṉṉi iṭittavaṉ (kāṇ)
thundered and emitted lightning.
eḻil ...... eṇṇattāṉē
see 1st verse.

maṉam uruki niṉaiyātārkku 1. viruntavaṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ is the cuse of sufferings to those who do not think of him with melted hearts.
2. vañcakaṉ (kāṇ)
plays deceit towards them.
añcu eḻuttum niṉaivārkku eṉṟum maruntavaṉ (kāṇ) vāṉ piṇikaḷ tīrum vaṇṇam
is the medicine for curing the great diseases for those who meditate upon the mantiram consisting of five letters.
vāṉakamum maṇṇakamum maṟṟum ākip parantavaṉ (kāṇ)
pervades everything being himself the sky, the earth and other things.
paṭar caṭai eṭṭu uṭaiyāṉ (kāṇ)
has eight caṭi which spreads in eight directions;
[[This omits the caṭi that is on the centre of the head;
oṉpatu pōlavar kōlak kuḻaṟcaṭai (nāvukkaracar vitam tirtta tiruppatikam, 9)]]
paṅkayattōṉ taṉ cirattai ēnti ūr ūr irantavaṉ (kāṇ)
begged alms going to every village, holding the skull of Piramaṉ who is seated in a lotus flower.
eḻil ...... eṇṇattāṉē

Civaṉ covered himself with the skin of a tiger of desirable honour, flaying it from it body.
vētattiṉ poruḷāṉ (kāṇ) eṉṟu iyampi vimmā niṉṟu aḻavārkaṭku aḷippāṉ (tāṉ-kāṇ)

ayaṉ cirattai aṟuttāṉ (kāṇ)
Civaṉ cut off one head of Ayaṉ (Piramaṉ).
amarar vēṇṭa āḻ kaṭaliṉ nañcu uṇṭu
consuming the poison which appeared in the deap ocean, at the request of the immortals.
aṅku aṇi nīrk kaṅkai ceṟuttāṉ
contined within his caṭi the Kaṅki of beautiful water.
tēvarkkum tēvaṉ (kāṇ)
is superior god even to the celestials.
ticai aṉaittum toḻutu ēttak kalaimāṉ kaiyil poṟuttāṉ (kāṇ)
bore a young stag in the hand to be prised and worshipped with hands joined, by all beings in all the directions.
pukal iṭattai naliya vantu porukayilai eṭuttavaṉ taṉ muṭi tōḷ 1. nāl añcu iṟuttāṉ kāṇ
broke the crowns and twenty shoulders of the arakkaṉ who lifted Kayili which was an obstacle to him, having come there to make the people of the world quiver
[[poru can also mean Kayili to which there is no comparision,; it can be compared only to itself.]]

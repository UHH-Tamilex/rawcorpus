{5:61}__1+
{$}
moy muttu ūrum puṉal ariciṟkaraip puttūraṉ aṭi pōṟṟi eṉpār elām
all those who pray [invoking] the feet of Civaṉ in Puttūr on the bank of the river Aricil which has water on which pearls move slowly thronging
poyttu ūrum pulaṉ aintoṭu pulkiya maittu ūrum viṉaimāṟṟavum vallarē
have the ability to remove the Karmam-s which have mastery over the soul, and are black in colour and are combined with the five sense-perceptions which take upon themselves untruth and lead to destruction.
{5:61}__2+
{$}
piṟaikka(ṇ)ṇiccaṭai emperumāṉ eṉṟu, kaṟaik kaṇittavar
those devotees who realised their faults listing them and praying, `our Lord who has a cati on which the crescent is like a chaplet'.
kaṇṭavaṇakkattāy
are really worthy of paying obeisance.
uṟakkaṇittu urukā maṉattārkaḷaip puṟakkaṇittiṭum puttūrp puṉitaṉ
the holy god in Puttūr will neglect those who do not melt in their hearts thinking much of the fame of Civaṉ.
{5:61}__3+
{$}
(People of this world!)
ariciliṉ karaimēl aṇi ārtaru puricai nam tirupputtūrp puṉitaṉaip paricoṭum paravip paṇivārkku elām
to all those who pay obeisance by prising in the proper manner our pure and holy Civaṉ in Puttūr which has a beautiful wall of fortification and is situated on the bank of the river Aricil.
turicu il naṉṉeṟi tōṉṟiṭum
the good and faultless way leading to eternal bliss, will appear before them.

eṉ maṉam
my heart
niṉaintu
thinking of,
vētaṉai
Civaṉ who chants the vetams.
miku vīṇaiyil mēviya kītaṉai
who is the music that is produced in the superior vīṇi.
kiḷarum naṟuṅkoṉṟai am pōtaṉai
who adorns himself with the brilliant and fragrant koṉṟi flowers.
puṉal cūḻnta puttūraṉai
who is in puttur surrounded by water.
nātaṉai
and the Lord.
naiyum
loses its hardness and becomes soft.
{5:61}__5+
{$}
aruppuppōl mulaiyār allal vāḻkkai mēl viruppuccēr nilaiviṭṭu
giving up the state of desiring the life of sufferings by living with women who have breasts like buds.
nal iṭṭamāy
with good piety.
tirupputtūraṉaic cintai ceyac ceya
when I meditate upon Civaṉ in tiruputtur without ceasing.
karuppuc cāṟṟilum aṇṇikkum
he is sweeter than the juice of the sugar-cane.
kāṇmiṉ
(People of this world!) you know this.

pāmpoṭumatiyum pūm puṉalum paṭar puṉ caṭaip potinta puttūruḷāṉ
Civaṉ is in puttūr who has concealed the cobra, crescent and beautiful water on the spreading golden cati.
nām paṇintu aṭi nāḷ toṟum pōṟṟi
when we, his devotee fall at his feet and prise him dily.
cāmpal eṉpu 1. taṉakku aṇi ākumē
holy ash and bones, add beauty to him. (pōṟṟiṭap puttūr uḷāṉ is the syntactical link)
[[Variant reading: 1. tamakku aṇivākumē]]
{5:61}__7+
{$}
kaṉal aṅkai taṉil ēnti veṅkāṭṭiṭai
-in the hot cremation ground holding fire in the palm.
aṉal aṅku eyti niṉṟu āṭuvar pāṭuvar
will dance in the fire reaching it, and will sing.
1. miṉal am ceñcaṭai mēl 2. piṟaiyum taru puṉalum cūṭuvar (pōlum) puttūrarē
Civaṉ in puttūr will adorn his red cati which is matted, a crescent and water descending from heaven.
[[For the reading pilayam taru the meaning is deluge of water.]]
[[Variant reading: 1. piṉalam ceñcaṭai 2. pilayan taru, milayan taru]]
{5:61}__8+
{$}
kāṟṟiṉum kaṭitāka naṭappatu ōr ēṟṟiṉum(m) icaintu ēṟuvar
will ride with desire on a bull which has a git faster than the wind.
eṉ poṭu nīṟṟiṉai aṇivar
will adorn himself with bones and sacred ash.
niṉai vāyttamaip pōṟṟi eṉpavarkku aṉpar puttūrar
Civaṉ in puttūr loves those who say `Let you protect me, with good intention.
{5:61}__9+
{$}
muṉṉum muppuram ceṟṟaṉarāyiṉum
though he destroyed the three cities in the past.
1. alantu aṭaintārkku elām aṉṉam 2. oppar
is like the food which removes hunger, to those who reach him having suffered much.
viricaṭai miṉṉum oppar
has a spread out caṭi which is like the brilliant lightning.
mēṉi cempoṉṉum oppar puttūr em puṉitar
our pure Lord in puttūr has a body glittering like pure gold.
[[Variant reading: 1. vantu 2. opparāl]]
{5:61}__10+
{$}
ceruttaṉāl taṉa tēr cela uyttiṭum karuttaṉāy
with the idea of driving his chariot to fight enemies.
kayilai eṭuttāṉ uṭal parutta tōḷ keṭap pātattu oru viral poruttiṉār poḻil ārnta puttūrar
Civaṉ in puttūr which is full of gardens, fixed one toe to destroy the strength of the big shoulders and the body of the arakkaṉ who lifted Kayili.

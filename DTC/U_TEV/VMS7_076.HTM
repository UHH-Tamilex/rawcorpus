poruvaṉār
Civaṉ has the greatness to be compared to other tēvar but no other tēvar can be compared to him;
[[PP: piṟarkku nī vāyiṉallatu niṉakkup piṟar uvamam ākā oru peruvēntē
(patiṟṟupattu, 73-2-3);]]
[[PP: piṟarkku uvamam tāṉ allatu taṉakku uvamam piṟar illeṉa
(puṟam, 377-10-11);]]
[[PP: porunaṉeṉṟatu tāṉ piṟarkku uvamikkappaṭuvāṉ eṉṉum poruṭṭu
(maturikkāñci, 42, nacciṉārkkiṉiyār)]]
purinūlar
wears a sacred thread of three strands.
puṉalmulai umaiyavaḷōṭu maruvaṉār
is united with Umayaval who has breasts close to each other.
maruvār pāl varuvatum illai nam aṭikaḷ
our god does not even come near those who do not approach him.
tiruaṉār paṇintu ēttum tikaḻ tiruvāñciyattu uṟaiyum oruvaṉār
the unequalled god who dwells in eminent tiruvāñciyam who is pid obeisance and prised by ladies who are as beautiful as the goddess of wealth.
aṭiyārai (1) ūḻviṉai naliya oṭṭār
will not tolerate deeds done in former births considered as the cuse of the present life with all its fortunes, afflicting his devotees.
[[Variant reading: (1) uḷaviṉai]]
{7:76}__2+
{$}
toṟuvil āṉ iḷavēṟu (1) tuṇṇeṉa iṭikural veruvi
being afrid of the thunderous bellowing sound of the young bull which is in the midst of cows, to make one frightened.
ceṟuvil vāḷaikaḷ ōṭa
the scabbared fish in the fields run for safety.
ceṅkayal paṅkayattu otuṅka
the red carp fish to hids themselves in the lotus.
kaṟuvu ilā maṉattārkaḷ kāṇṭaku vāñciyattu aṭikaḷ
the god in vāñciyam where people who have no hostility in their minds are beautiful to look at.
maṟuilāta veṇṇīṟupūcutal (maṉum) oṉṟu uṭaittu
smearing himself with white holy ash which has no defect, has an idea behind it
[[NOTE: maṉ um are expletives]]
[[Variant reading: (1) tuṇiṉeṉa]
[[The idea is that Civaṉ is the support for the world.]]
{7:76}__3+
{$}
cuṭunuṉaippakaḻi (atu) oṉṟāl tūrttar mū eyil eytu
discharging an arrow which has a sharp end and which is burning, on the three forts of the wicked persons.
pārttaṉār tiraḷtōḷmēl pal nuṉaip pakaḻikaḷ pāycci
having thrust into the globular shoulders of pārttaṉ many arrows of sharp ends.
tīrtta mā malarppoykai tikaḻtiruvāñciyattu aṭikaḷ
the god in tiruvāñciyam where natural ponds of holy water in which big flowers grow, are eminent.
cāttum māmaṇikkaccu aṅku orutalaivalatalai uṭaittu
the sash of big rubies which he ties in the waist has many heads on one side; what a wonder!
[[Variant reading: (1) poykaittikaḻ]]
{7:76}__4+
{$}
caḷḷai
a kind of fish by name caṭi (grey river mullet).
vaḷḷai veṇ malar veḷḷai amkuruku (nāṉ atu) ām eṉakkaruti añci
being afrid of the white flowers of the creeping bindweed mistaking it to be the white crane.
maṟuki ōr vāḷaiyiṉ vāyil tuḷḷu teḷḷunīrp poykaittuḷai malku vāñciyattu aṭikaḷ
the god in vāñciyam where ghats in the natural ponds in which there is clear water are many, leap into the mouth of the seabbard fish, confused in mind.
veḷḷai nuṇpoṭippūcum vikirtam oḻikilar
does not give up the state of being different from the world, by smearing fine and white holy ash.
{7:76}__5+
{$}
(1) maikoḷ kaṇṭar
Civaṉ has a neck in which there is poison.
eṇ tōḷar
has eight arms.
malaimakaḷuṭaṉ uṟai vāḻkkaik koyta kūviḷamālai kulaviya caṭaimuṭik kuḻakar
is the youth who is living with the dughter of the mountin and who on his caṭi coiled into a crown wears a garland of bel leaves plucked and strung into a garland.
kaitaineytal am (2) kaḻaṉi kamaḻ pukaḻ vāñciyattu aṭikaḷ
the god in famous vāñciyam where the fragrance of the screw-pine spreads in the fields in which white indian water-lily grows.
paitalveṇ piṟaiyōṭu pāmpu uṭaṉ vaippatu paricē
has the nature of keeping on his head the tender crescent along with a cobra.
[[Variant reading: (1) maiyakaṇṭar (2) kaḻaṉikkamaḻ]]
{7:76}__6+
{$}
tamparicāl
according to his nature.
karantai kūviḷamālai kaṭimalark koṉṟaiyum cūṭi
having adorned his head with garlands of fragrant basil, garlands of bel and fragrant garlands of koṉṟi.
paranta pāriṭam cūḻavaruvar nam paramar
our superior god will be coming to be surrounded by pūtams, spreading everywhere.
tiruntu māṭaṅkaḷ nīṭu tikaḻtiruvāñciyattu uṟaiyum maruntu a(ṉ)ṉār aṭiyārai valviṉai naliya oṭṭār
Civaṉ who is like the nectar and who dwells in eminent tiruvāñciyam where the perfect storeys are tall, will not brook the irresistible Karmams inflicting suffering on his devotees.

aruvipāytaru kaḻaṉi alartarum kuvaḷaiam kaṇṇār
when the ladies who have eyes resembling the blue nelumbo flowers which blossom in the fields where streams of water flow.
kuruvi (1) āykiḷicōppa
scare away with stones thrown from the slings, the small sparrows and parrots which are in search of ears of corn in the fields
[[PP: cōppa
nantaṉ maṉaivi kaṭaitāmpāl cōppūṇṭu (periyāḻwar tirumoḻi, 2-1-5)]]
[[PP: ēṟumāl yāṉaiyē civikaiyantaḷakam īccōppivaṭṭil (campantar, tiruvārūr, (3) 7);]]
[[PP: iṭākiṉikaḷ irumaruṅkum īccōppi paṇimāṟa
(Kalinkattuppuṟam 7-3);]]
[[NOTE: many have read this word as cērppa]]
kurukiṉam iritarukiṭaṅkil
from the moat where the flocks of cranes flee in all directions.
paimpoḻil vāñciyattu uṟaiyum iruvarāl aṟiyoṇṇā iṟaivaṉatu aṟaikaḻal caraṇē
the feet of Civaṉ wearing jingling Kaḻal, who could not be known by both Māl and Ayaṉ, and who dwells in vāñciyam which has verdant gardens into which.
paruvarāl kutikoḷḷum
the bulky murrel fish leaps.
[[Variant reading: (1) pāy]]
{7:76}__8+
{$} kaḷaṅkaḷ ārtaru kaḻaṉi aḷitara
when the fields among which there are many threshing-floors for threading grin yield mature crops.
kaḷitaruvaṇṭu uḷaṅkaḷ ār (1) kalippāṭal umparil olittaṭum kāṭci
with the scenery of intoxicated bees humming out of joy like music of loud sound which fill the hearts with joy.
kuḷaṅkaḷ āl niḻaṟkīḻ naṟkuyilpayil vāñciyattu aṭikaḷ viḷaṅku tāmaraippātam niṉaippavar
those who meditate on the feet which are like the shining lotus flowers of the god in vāñciyam where under the shade of the banyan trees growing on the banks of the tanks sweet-singing indian cuckoos live in large numbers.
viṉainalivu ilar
will not be afflicted by Karmams.
[[Variant reading: (1) kavippāṭal]]

vāḻaiyiṉ kaṉi(tāṉ) um matuvimmu varukkaiyiṉ cuḷaiyum kūḻaivāṉaram tammil kūṟu itu ciṟitu eṉakkuḻaṟi
the share which is due to me of the plintin fruits and pulps of the jack-fruit in which there is abundant honey flowing, is small the stupid monkeys babbling among themselves.
tāḻai vāḻai am taṇṭāl cerucceytu tarukku vāñciyattuḷ ēḻaipaṅkaṉai allāl
except the Lord who has on his half a lady and who is in Vāñciyam where (the monkeys) fight with the stem of the fragrant screw-pine and plintin trees and feel elated.
iṟai eṉakkarututal ilam
we do not think of any other god as master.

cennel aṅku alaṅku kaḻaṉittikaḻ tiruvāñciyattu uṟaiyum iṉ alaṅkal amcaṭai em iṟaivaṉatu aṟaikaḻal paravum poṉ alaṅkal nalmāṭappoḻil aṇināval ārūraṉ
ārūraṉ who was born in nāvalūr made beautiful by gardens and perfect storeys adorned with lace-garlands who worships the feet of Civaṉ wearing jingling Kaḻal, and who adorns his caṭi with garlands pleasing to look at and who dwells in tiruvāñciyam which is eminent by fields where the ears of red variety of superior paddy is dangling in the air.
[[PP_(alaṅkaḻaṉi)
eṭuttanaṟaviṉ kulaiyalaṅkāntaṉ (Kalittōki, 40-12)]]
paṉ alaṅkal naṉ mālaipāṭumiṉ; pattaruḷīrē
People who are in the company of pious people! sing the good garlands of verses which were composed with great consideration.

pāṇattāl matil mūṉṟum erittavaṉ
Civaṉ burnt all the three forts with one arrow.
pūṇat(tāṉ) aravu āmai poṟuttavaṉ
bore a cobra and the shell of a tortoise to adorn himself as ornaments.
(1) kāṇattāṉ iṉiyāṉ
it is a pleasure to have a vision of him
kaṭal nākaikkārōṇattāṉ eṉa nam viṉai ōyumē
our Karmams will come to an end if we say Civaṉ in Nākaikkārōṇam on the shore of the sea.
[[nākaikkārōṇam has been split into nākaikkā and rōṇattāṉ;
This is called in prosody as vakaiyuḷi scansion in complete disregard of speech-rhythm.]]
[[Variant reading: (1) pēṇattāṉ, tēṇattāṉ]]

kaṇṭalum
as soon as we have a vision of
vaṇṭu alampiya vārcaṭai īcaṉai
Civaṉ who has a long caṭai on which bees hum.
viṇtalam paṇintu ēttum vikirtaṉai
the god who is different from the world and who is praised by paying obeisance by the inhabitants of heaven.
kaṇṭal (am) kamaḻ nākaikkārōṇaṉai
who in nākaikkārōṇam where the fragrant screw-pine spreads its fragrance.
[[kāṇṭalum has become kaṇṭalum to suit etukai (rhyme);
kāṇṭalum a new form of adverbial participle found in post-caṅkam literature;
pāttiram ēntiya pāvaiyaik kāṇṭalum;
mātavi makaḷum mātavark kāṇṭalum;
piṇippu aṟu mātavaṉa pīṭikai kāṇṭalum (maṇimēkalai, 18-118, 24-90, 25-34).]]
viṉaiyāṉa kaḻalum
all the Karmams will slip off.

puricaṭai naṉaiyum māmalar cūṭiya nampaṉai māmalar koṇṭu puṉaiyum
(People of this world!) adorn Civaṉ who bore on his twisted caṭai flowers wet with honey, with big flowers.
[[puṉaiyum verb in the imperative mood; plural]]
kaṉaiyum vārkaṭal nākaikkārōṇaṉai 1. niṉaiyavē viṉaiyāyiṉa nīṅkum
Karmams will take leave of us if we fix our thoughts on Civaṉ in nākaikkārōṇam on the shore of the expensive sea which is always roaring.
[[Variant reading: 1. niṉaiya veṉ viṉaiyāṉavai nīṅkumē]]

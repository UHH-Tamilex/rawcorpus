[[Note: this shrine is now known as pāṭi]]
{1:3}__1+
{$} pattaroṭu palarum poli am malar aṅkaip puṉal tūvi
scattering shining beautiful flowers and sprinkling water from the palm; many brahmins along with pious people of other castes.
otta colli
chanting the mantirams of the Vētam in a uniform accent.
ulakattavar tām toḻutu ētta
to be prised by the people of this world with joined hands.
uyar ceṉṉi mattam vaitta perumāṉ piriyātu uṟaikiṉṟa valitāyam, cittam vaitta aṭiyār (avar) mēl aṭaiyā (maṟṟu) iṭar nōyē
sufferings and diseases will not reach the devotees who always think of Valitāyam in their minds, in which the god who placed on his lofty head datura flowers, dwells without leaving it.
{1:3}__2+
{$} paṭai ilaṅku karam eṭṭu uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has eight arms on which weapon are glittering;
[[cf. cilaittalai mikutta paṭai eṇkaram viḷaṅka (campantar, tirukkōkaraṇam, 4)]]
kaṉal ēnti
holding fire.
paṭiṟākak kaṭai ilaṅku maṉaiyil palikoṇṭu uṇum kaḷvaṉ
the chief who eats receiving alms in houses having shining entrances, falsely.
uṟaikōyil
the temple where he dwells.
maṭai ilaṅku poḻiliṉ(ṉ) niḻalvāy matuvīcum valitāyam aṭaiya niṉṟa aṭiyārkku aṭaiyāviṉai allal tuyar (tāṉ, ē)
mental sufferings and physical sufferings which are the result of acts done, will not reach the devotees who reach Valitāyan where in the shades of gardens which have prominent sluices, and which give honey in plenty [SIC!].
{1:3}__3+
{$} aiyaṉ
Civaṉ, the beautiful god, (the wonderful god)
[[ai viyappu ākum (tolkāppiyam, uri iyal, 87; Rajam edition).]]
noyyaṉ
is light in weight.
aṇiyaṉ
is near at hand to devotees.
piṇi illavar eṉṟu toḻutu ētta
to be prised always by devotees who are not bound by āṇavam, with joined hands.
ceyyaṉ
has red colour.
veyya paṭai ēnta vallāṉ tirumātōṭu uṟaikōyil
the temple where he who is able to hold cruel weapons dwells with a dear lady.
vaiyam vantu paṇiya
when the people of this world come and pay homage.
piṇi tīrttu uyarkiṉṟa valitāyam uyyum vaṇṇa(n) niṉaimiṉ
(People of this world!) think to save yourselves, valitāyan which is great by curing the diseases.
niṉaintāl viṉaitīrum
if you think of that shrine, your acts will leave you.
nalam ākumē
happiness will come to you.
{1:3}__4+
{$} oṟṟai ēṟu (atu) uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has an unequalled bull which is the embodiment of righteousness and which could not be counted along with other bulls.
ōr pūtap paṭaicūḻa naṭam āṭi
performer of dance, surrounded by an army of pūtams.
puṟṟil nākam arai ārttu uḻalkiṉṟa emperumāṉ
our god who wanders tying in his waist a cobra that lives in the anthill, (puṟṟilnākam: the underlined word in a generic epithet).
maṭavāḷōṭu uṟṟa kōyil
the temple where he stays with a lady.
ulakattu oḷi malkiṭa uḷkum valitāyam
is valitayam which the world thinks to be filled with lustre.
paṟṟi vāḻum(m) atuvē caraṇāvatu pāṭum(m) aṭiyārkkē
for devotees who sing the prises of God is the refuge catching hold of that shrine.
{1:3}__5+
{$} punti oṉṟi niṉaivār viṉai āyiṉa tīra
in order that the acts of those who meditate with their intellect without being dregged by the sense-organ, may leave them.
poruḷ āya anti aṉṉatu oru pēr oḷiyāṉ amarkōyil
the temple where he who is the object of meditation and who has the great brilliance like the evening sky, dwells with desire.
ayal eṅkum
in all place on its sides.
manti (1) vantu kaṭuvaṉoṭum kūṭivaṇaṅkum valitāyam
is vālitāyan which is worshipped by the female monkey with its male.
cintiyāta avartam aṭum ventuyar tīrtal eḷitu aṉṟu (ē)
the removal of the acute sufferings which afflict those who do not think of that shrine, is not easy.
[[Variant reading: (1) kaṭuvaṉṉoṭu kūṭi]]
{1:3}__6+
{$} ūṉ iyaṉṟa talaiyil paṇikoṇṭu
receiving alms in a skull which has flesh.
ulakattu uḷḷavar ētta
to be prised by the people of this world.
kāṉ iyaṉṟa kariyi(ṉ) uripōrttu uḻal kaḷvaṉ
the thief who wanders covering himself with a skin of an elephant that lives in the forest (kāṉ iyaṉṟa kari; the underlined words are generic epithets)
caṭaitaṉ mēl vāṉ iyaṉṟa piṟai vaitta em āti makiḻum valitāyam tēṉ iyaṉṟa naṟumāmalar koṇṭu ētta teḷivu āmē
clear spritual knowledge will come to those who prise valitāyam of our first cuse who placed on his caṭi a crescent which moves in the sky, with fragrant flowers having honey.
{1:3}__7+
{$} kaṇ niṟainta viḻiyi(ṉ) aḻalāl varukāmaṉ(1) uyir vīṭṭi
killing the Kāmaṉ who came aginst Civaṉ to discharge floral arrows, by the fire that issued from the eye-ball which occupied the eye.
peṇ niṟainta orupāl makiḻvu eytiya pemmāṉ uṟaikōyil
the temple where the god who rejoiced in having a half occupied fully by a lady.
maṇniṟainta pukaḻkoṇṭu aṭiyārkaḷ vaṇaṅkum valitāyattu uḷniṟainta perumāṉ kaḻal ētta
to prise the feet of the god who occupied completely the shrine valitāyam to which the devotees pay homage by prostrating with the fame which has filled this world.
nam uṇmaik kati ākum
we will attin salvation which is permanent.
[[Variant reading: (1) uruvīṭṭi; This is a better reading]]
{1:3}__8+
{$} kaṭaliṉ nañcam amutu uṇṭu
consumed the poison that rose in the ocean, as nectar.
imaiyōr toḻutu ētta naṭamāṭi
and dancing to be prised with joined hands by the celestials who do not wink.
aṭal ilaṅkai araiyaṉ valiceṟṟu aruḷ ammāṉ amar kōyil
the temple in which the god who destroyed the strength of the King of ilaṅki who was strong and then granted his grace.
maṭal ilaṅku kaḻukiṉ palaviṉ matuvimmum valitāyam uṭal ilaṅkum uyir uḷḷaḷavum toḻa
to worship with joined hands valitāyam where the honey from the flat leaf of the areca palms and jack-trees is increasing, as long as the life in the body shines.
uḷḷat tuyar pōmē
the mental sufferings of those devotees will go away.
{1:3}__9+
{$} periya mēru varaiyē cilaiyā malaivuṟṟāreyil mūṉṟum eriya eyta oruvaṉ
the unequalled god who shot an arrow to burn all the three forts of those who came to fight with him, having as a bow the big mountin; mēru.
iruvarkku aṟivu oṇṇā vaṭivu ākum eri(atu) āki uṟa ōṅkiyavaṉ valitāyam toḻutu ētta uriyarāka uṭaiyār
those devotees who have the right to prise and worship with joined hands valitāyam of Civaṉ who grow very tall as such a great column of fire that as the two, Māl and Piramaṉ could not see that.
periyār eṉa uḷku(m)m ulakōrē
people of this world will esteem them as great souls
[[uḷkum is used as the predicate of the subject ulakōrē it is aginst the use of that form of the verb which is generally not used to denote plural in uyartiṇai]]
{1:3}__10+
{$} āci āra moḻiyār amaṇ cākkiyar allātār
those who are outside the pale of civam, the camaṇar who do not have the mind to say blessings to the satisfaction of their hearts, cākkiyar (buddhists).
kūṭi
all of them joining together.
ēci īram ilarāy moḻi ceytavar collaip poruḷ eṉṉēl
Do not consider as having any substance the words of those people who have no love, and talk only despisingly about Civaṉ.
vāci tīra aṭiyārkku aruḷ ceytu vaḷarntāṉ valitāyam pēcum ārvam uṭaiyār aṭiyār eṉap pēṇum periyōrē
great people will esteem such people as have the devotion to speak highly of valitāyam of Civaṉ who grew into fame having bestowed his grace on devotees without any distinction.
{1:3}__11+
{$} vaṇṭu vaikum maṇam malkiya cōlai vaḷarum valitāyattu aṇṭa vāṇaṉ aṭi uḷkutalāl aruḷ mālait tamiḻāka kaṇṭal vaiku kaṭaṟkāḻiyuḷ ñāṉacampantaṉ tamiḻpattum koṇṭu
having all the ten tamiḻ verse done by Ñāṉacampantaṉ in Kaḻi surrounded by the sea where mangroves are found, as a garland of tamiḻ having the grace of Civaṉ as he meditated on the feet of the Lord who pervades all the universe, and who dwells in valitāyam where the gardens of increasing fragrance in which bees are staying, are flourishing.
vaiki icai pāṭa vallār
who are capable of singing them with music dwelling there.
kuḷir vāṉattu uyarvārē
will reach Civalōkam which is higher than the cool heaven.

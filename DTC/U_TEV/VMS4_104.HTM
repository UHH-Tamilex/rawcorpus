{4:104}__1+
{$} mācuil oḷvāḷ pōl maṟiyum maṇi nīrttokuti ūcalai āṭi
having played in the swing of the collection of water which has gems in it and rolls up and down like the bright sword which has no blemish
[[vāḷ pōlnīr:
vāḷniṟam koṇṭa aruvittē (Kalittoki, 42-11)]]
aṅku oṇciṟai aṉṉam (1) uṟaṅkal uṟṟāl
if the swan with bright feathers goes to sleep.
pācaṭai nīlam parukiya vaṇṭu paṇ pāṭal kaṇṭu
hearing humming of the bees, which drank the honey in the blue nelumbo flowers which has green leaf, resembling music.
vīcum keṭila (2) vaṭakaraittē entai vīraṭṭam (ē)
Vīraṭṭam of our father is on the North bank Ketilam which gives gems found in it, liberally.
[[pācaṟai is a scribal error]]
[[variant readings: (1) uṟṟa, uṟṟār (2) vaṭakaraikkē; vaṭakaraittē has been read as vaṭakaraikkē as palm leaf manuscripts ka and ta can be mistaken; this reading occurs in all the following verses]]
{4:104}__2+
{$} veṅkāl kurucilai vīraṉ aruḷ vaitta vīraṭṭam (ē)
the Vīraṭṭam on which Civaṉ who has bright bow with cruel ends was gracious enough to dwell there.
paiṅkāl tavaḷai paṟai koṭṭa
the frog with green legs to make a sound like that of the drum.
pācilai nīrppaṭukar amkāl kuvaḷaimēl āvi uyirppa
the tanks with water and green leaves to waft the fragrance of the blue nelunbo flowers with beautiful stems.
aruku ulavum ceṅkāl kuruku ivai cērum ceṟikeṭilak karaittē
is on the bank of the Keṭilam full of water where the swans with red legs, which are moving to and fro near that place, reach those flowers.
{4:104}__3+
{$} añcaṉam ammalarkkaṇṇiyar centuvarvāy iḷaiyār
young girls who have eyes coated with collyrium and resembling beautiful blue nelumbo flowers and have red lips like coral.
vemmulaiccāntam vilai peṟumālai uṭuttavarkaḷ
whose chose sandal paste smeared on the warn breasts and highly valuable garlands to adorn themselves.
tam maruṅkuṟku iraṅkār
without feeling pity for their slender waist.
taṭantōḷ meliyakkuṭaivār
who plungo and bathe to make their big shoulders exhusted.
(1) vimmupuṉal keṭilak karaittē entai vīraṭṭam (ē)
vīraṭṭam of our father is on the bank of the Keṭilam full of water (kuṭaivārkkup puṉal uṭaiya keṭilam is the syntactical link)
[[Variant reading: (1) vimmum puṉaṟkeṭilak]]
{4:104}__4+
{$} mīṉ uṭaittaṇpuṉal vīraṭṭarē
Lord in Vīraṭṭam which has cool water abounding in fish!
nummai vēṇṭukiṉṟatu yāṉ uṭaic cil(1) kuṟai oṉṟu uḷatu (āl)
there is a small request that I want to make to you.
naṟuntaṇ erukkiṉ tēṉ uṭaik koṉṟaiccaṭaiuṭaik kaṅkait tirai tavaḻum kūṉ uṭaittiṅkaḷ kuḻavi eppōtum kuṟikkoṇmiṉ (ē)
guard carefully always the curved crescent which crawls in the waves of the Koṅki which is in your caṭi which is adorned with cool and fragrant yarcum flowers and koṉṟi flowers having honey, from being drowned; (naṟuntaṇ erukku; the underlined epithets are generic.)
[[Variant reading: (1) kuṟaivu]]
{4:104}__5+
{$} viri nīrp paravaic cūr aṭṭavēlaṉ tātaiyaic cūḻ vayal ār atikai vīraṭṭattāṉai virumpar arumpāvavētaṉaiyē
the trouble of born out of the sin committed in previous birth which cannot be avoided as I did not cherish with love Civaṉ in Atiki Vīraṭṭam which has field and the father of Vēlavaṉ (Murukaṉ) who killed Cūraṉ in the expensive ocean filled with water.
ār aṭṭatēṉum irantu uṇṭu
eating by begging the food cooked by anybody, without enquiring who cooked it and whether it is fit for me to eat, to appease my hunger.
akam akavaṉ tirintu
and being a wandered from house to house.
vēr aṭṭa niṟpittiṭukiṉṟatu (āl)
cuses me to stand before house the sweat to flow down the body (vēr sweat)

{4:104}__6+
{$} paṭaṟ poṟ caṭaiyum, pakuvāy aravum, paṉi matiyum, cuṭalaip poṭiyum ellām uḷavē
Civaṉ has all the distinctive marks of a golden caṭi which is spread out, a cobra with a wide open mouth, a cool crescent and ash of the cremation ground (I did not follow after him and seek him as my refuge out of my ignorance).
avar tūya teṇṇīrk keṭilakkarait tiru vīraṭṭar āvar
he is the god who dwells in the tiruvīraṭṭam on the bank of the river, Ketilam which has holy and clear water.
keṭṭēṉ
woe is mine.
avar nāmaṅkaḷē aṭaintār naṭalaikku naltuṇaiyākum (kaṇṭīr)
his names will be good companion to remove the sufferings of those who approached him.
{4:104}__7+
{$} kāḷam kaṭantatu ōr kaṇṭattar āki
having a neck which was victorious over the poison
[[The victory consists in stationing the poison in the neck neither allowing it to go into the body nor to go out; More black than the dark cloud is another meaning]]
kāḷam
black cloud.
kaṇ ār keṭila nāḷ aṅkaṭikku ōr (1) nakaramum
and being a city to the morning bazaar of Keṭilam which is pleasing to look at. (kaṇ may also mean sluices for water to flow into the fields)
mātiṟku naṉku icainta tāḷaṅkaḷ koṇṭum, kuḻal koṇṭum, yāḻ koṇṭum (3) vēḷaṅkaḷ koṇṭum vicumpu celvār (avar) (1) vīraṭṭarē
Civaṉ in vīraṭṭam will travel to his abode, Kayili with the cymbals which are very appropriate to the lady, Umi, flute, yāḻ, and quarters of his own. (vēḷam quarters, 5. Inscriptions Vol. III, 217; Tamil Lexicon); (vēḷam may also mean fortified place where ladies of rank captured in war were kept as slaves)

{1:40}__1+
{$} poṭiyuṭai mārpiṉar
Civaṉ who has besmeared holy ash on his chest.
pōrviṭai ēṟi
riding upon a fighting bull.
pūtakaṇam puṭai cūḻa
surrounded by groups of pūtam.
koṭiyuṭai ūr tirintu
wandering in places where flags are flying
aiyam koṇṭu palapala kūṟi
receiving alms and speaking many words.
vaṭivuṭai vāḷ neṭuṅkaṇ umai pākam āyavaṉ vāḻkoḷi puttūr
in vaḻkoḷiputtūr which is the abode of Civaṉ who has as a half Umi whose long eyes are like the word, and who has a beautiful body.
kaṭikamaḻ māmalar iṭṭuk kaṟai miṭaṟṟāṉ aṭikāṇpōm
we will worship the feet of the god who has a black neck by scattering fragrant flowers.
{1:40}__2+
{$} araikeḻu kōvaṇa āṭaiyiṉ mēl ōr āṭu aravam acaittu
Having tied a cobra that can dance, on the loin-cloth tucked at the back of the waist.
[[kōvaṇa āṭai the folds of the cloth passed between the legs and tucked at the backside of the waist.]]
aiyam puraikeḻu veṇṭalai ēnti
holding a white skull of holes to receive alms.
pōrviṭai ēṟippukaḻa
riding of a fighting bull to be prised (by all)
varaikeḻu maṅkaiyatu ākam ōrpākam āyavaṉ, viri caṭaiyāṉ
one who has spreading caṭi and has as half the body of the dughter of the (himālaya) mountin.
vāḻ koḷiputtūr virai (1)kamaḻ māmalar tūvi aṭicērvōm
we shall meditate upon Civaṉ's feet in vāḻkoḷiputtūr by scattering flowers which spread fragrance.
[[Variant reading: (1) keḻu]]
{1:40}__3+
{$} pūṇ neṭunākam acaittu
fastening long cobra as an ornament
aṉalāṭi
and dancing in the fire.
puṉtalai aṅkaiyil ēnti
holding an unclean skull in the palm.
ūṇ iṭupiccai aiyam uṇṭi eṉṟu palakūṟi
telling many times my food in picci; ayyam is my food
[[aiyam is what is given to people who are fit to receive it. piccai is what is given to ascetics and brahmachāris.]]
[[PP: aiyamum piccaiyum āmtaṉaiyum kaikāṭṭi (āṇṭāl, tiruppāvi, 2);
piṭippiccai piṉiṟai aiyam (ciṟupañcamūlam, 64);
orupiṭiyuḷ aṭaṅkum piccai, viral iṟaiyuḷ aṭaṅkum piccai, uṇṭō illaiyō eṉṟu aiyappaṭum piccai (Do. old commentary)]]
vāḷ neṭuṅkaṇ umai maṅkai ōr pākam āyavaṉ vāḻkoḷiputtūr
in vāḻkoḷiputtūr belonging to Civaṉ who has as his half a young lady, Umi who has long eyes like the word.
tāḷ neṭu māmalar iṭṭu
scattering big flowers having long stalks.
talaivaṉa tāḷniḻal cārvōm
we shall reach the protection of the feet of the master
[[talaivaṉa-talaivaṉ+a, a is the plural suffix of the genitive case.]]
{1:40}__4+
{$} tār iṭu koṉṟai ōr veṇmati kaṅkai tāḻcaṭai mēlavai cūṭi
having borne on the low-hanging caṭi Kaṅki, a white crescent and koṉṟi flowers that blossom in the form of a garland.
ūr iṭu piccai koḷ celvam uṇṭi eṉṟu pala kūṟi
telling many times, our wealth and food are the alms that is given by the people of the village.
vār iṭu meṉmulaimātu ōr pākam āyavaṉ kaṟaimiṭaṟṟāṉ vāḻkoḷiputtūr
in vāḻkoḷiputtūr of the god who has a black neck and has his half a lady of soft breasts on which she wears a bodice.
kār iṭu māmalar tūvi
scattering big flowers that blossom during winter.
aṭi kāṇpōm
we shall have vision of his feet.
{1:40}__5+
{$} kaṉamalark koṉṟai alaṅkal ilaṅka
when the garland of flowers of koṉṟi which is golden in colour, shines.
kātil ōr veṇkuḻaiyōṭu puṉamalar mālai puṉaintu
having adorned himself with flowers in the forest region, and a white ear-ring in the ear.
ūrpukuti eṉṟē palakūṟi
saying many times, enter into the village.
vaṉamulai māmalai maṅkai ōrpākam āyavaṉ vāḻkoḷi puttūr
in vāḻkoḷiputtūr belonging to Civaṉ who has as a half a young lady, dughter of the big mountin, having beautiful breasts.
iṉamalar ēyntaṉa tūvi emperumāṉ aṭicērvōm
we shall scatter kinds of flowers which are fit for worship and meditate upon the feet of our master.
[[Variant reading: kaṉamalar koṉṟai]]
{1:40}__6+
{$} aḷaivaḷar nākam acaittu aṉal āṭi
having tied a cobra that lives in the anthill, and dancing in the midst of fire.
alarmicai antaṇaṉ uccikkaḷai talaiyil palikoḷḷum karuttaṉē
Civaṉ who is in the minds of all and who receives alms in the skull that was removed from the top of the antaṇaṉ (Piramaṉ) who is seated on a (lotus) flower!
kaḷvaṉē eṉṉā
and addressing him as thief
vaḷai oli muṉkai maṭantai ōr pākam āyavaṉ vāḻkoḷi puttūr
in vāḻkoḷiputtūr which is the place of Civaṉ who has as a half a lady on whose fore-arm bangles make a jingling sound.
taḷai aviḻ māmalar tūvittalaivaṉa tāḷ iṇai cārvōm
we shall approach the feet of the master by scattering big flowers which unfold the petals that are closed.
[[talaivaṉa=talaivaṉ+a; see notes for the 3rd verse.]]
{1:40}__7+
{$} aṭarcevi vēḻattiṉ īruri pōrttu
covering himself with the skin wet with blood, of the elephant which has ears like plates.
aḻitalai aṅkaiyil ēnti
holding in the palm a skull which is life-less.
(1) uṭaliṭupiccaiyōṭu aiyam uṇṭi eṉṟu palakūṟi
saying many times the alms that was given by the ladies of tārukāvaṉam who lost self-control, and doubtful alms are my food.
[[piccai is the name given to the alms which is sought by one who begs; aiyam is the name given to alms which is given to those who beg, by inviting them.]]
[[PP: aiyam iṭṭu uṇ (ātticūṭi); piccai pukiṉum kaṟkai naṉṟē (veṟṟivēṟki)]]
maṭalneṭu māmalark kaṇṇi ōr pākam āyavaṉ vāḻkoḷi puttūr
in vāḻkoḷiputtūr, the residence of the god who has as a half a lady with long and big eyes resembling flowers having petals (lotus)
taṭamalar āyiṉa tūvit talaivaṉa tāḷ niḻal cārvōm
we shall approach the feet of the master as our protection, by scattering big flowers.
[[Variant reading: (1) uṭaṉiṭu piccai: alms that is given immediately when it is asked; it is a custom that people who come for alms should not be made to wait for a long time]]
{1:40}__8+
{$} uyar varai olka eṭutta arakkaṉ oḷirkaṭakakkai aṭarttu
Pressing down the hands which wore shining bracelet, of the arakkaṉ who lifted the high mountin to shake it from its position.
ayal iṭu picṅucayōṭu aiyam ārtalai eṉṟu aṭipōṟṟi
prising the feet saying you have as food picci given nearby and ayyam
[[piccai; aiyam: see notes on 3rd and 7th verse.]]
vayalvari nīla neṭuṅkaṇṇi pākam āyavaṉ vāḻkoḷi puttūr
in vaḻkoḷiputtūr, the residence of Civaṉ who has as a half a lady with long eyes resembling blue nelumbo flowers which blossom in the fields.
cayaviri māmalar tūvit tāḻcaṭaiyāṉ aṭi cārvōm
we shall approach the feet of the god whose caṭi hangs low, by scattering blossomed flowers (flowers of vāki)
[[cayaviri māmalar: flowers of vāki which are worn by warriors who have won the war; vaki is the tree under which the liṅkam has been established.]]
[[PP: vākai nuṇtuḷivīcum vāḻkoḷiputtūr uḷārē (campantar, vāḻkoḷiputtūr, 2nd decade, verse 1)]]
{1:40}__9+
{$} kariyavaṉ nāṉmukaṉ kaitoḻutu ēttak kāṇalum cāralum ākā eri uru āki
assuming the form of a fire which cannot be seen and approached by coming near, to be worshipped with joined hands by the black one and the one of four faces.
ūr aiyam iṭu pali uṇṇi eṉṟu ētti
prising god who has as his food the alms that is given by the people of the village.
vari aravu alkul maṭantai ōrpākam āyavaṉ vāḻkoḷi puttūr
in vāḻkoḷiputtūr where Civaṉ who has as his half a lady with sides like the hood of the cobra with spots.
virimalar tūvi vikirtaṉacēvaṭi cērvōm
we shall meditate upon the red feet of the Lord who is different from the world, by scattering blossomed flowers.
{1:40}__10+
{$} kuṇṭu amaṇar tuvark kūṟaikaḷ meyyil koḷkaiyiṉār puṟaṅkūṟa
when the low amaṇar (jins) and those who cover their bodies with yellow robes soaked in myrtle-dye, speak slandering words.
veṇṭalaiyil pali koṇṭal virumpiṉai eṉṟu viḷampi
saying you desired to receive alms in the white skull
[[koṇṭal-koḷḷal; a rare usage, in the sense of receiving;]]
[[PP: kaṉaveṉak koṇṭaliṉ iṉiyōrkkum uraiyāṉ (peruṅkati 4-7-133);
tāṉ kāval koṇṭal takum (puṟapporulveṇpāmāli, 179)]]
vaṇṭu amar pūṅkuḻal maṅkai ōrpākam āyavaṉ vāḻkoḷi puttūr
in vāḻkoḷiputtūr, where the god who has as his half a young lady with beautiful tresses of hir on which bees stay.
toṇṭarkaḷ māmalar tūvat tōṉṟi niṉṟāṉ aṭicērvōm
we shall meditate upon the feet of the Lord who manifested himself for the devotees to scatter flowers at his feet.
{1:40}__11+
{$} kal uyar mākkaṭal niṉṟu muḻaṅkum karaiporu kāḻiya mūtūr
in the ancient city of Kāḻi where the black sea roars and dashes aginst the shore, having waves like hills.
[[a: expletive (acai)]]
naluyar nāṉmaṟai nāviṉ naltamiḻ ñāṉacampantaṉ
ñāṉacampantaṉ who had knowledge of chasts Tamiḻ and learnt the four, good and eminent maṟi repeating them with his tongue.
valuyar cūlamum veṇ maḻuvāḷum vallavaṉ vāḻkoḷiputtūrc colliya pāṭalkaḷ vallārtuyarkeṭutal eḷitu ām
it is easy for the sufferings of those who are able to sing the songs composed on vāḻkoḷiputtūr, the residence of Civaṉ who can wield a white battle-axe, and strong eminent trident, to be destroyed.

tēṭu pali ūṉ (atu)

uṭaivēṭa miku vētiyar tiruntu patitāṉ

nāṭakam atu āṭa, maññai
when the peacocks are dancing
aripāṭa

kōṭal kaim maṟippa

{3:74}__2+
{$} kōḷ aravu koṉṟai naku veṇtalai erukku va(ṉ)ṉi kokku iṟakoṭum, vāḷaravu taṇ cala makaṭ kulavu ceñcaṭai varattu iṟaivaṉ ūr
the place of superior Civaṉ on whose shining red matted locks, the cobra which has the nature of killing, koṉṟi flowers, lughing white skull, yarcum flowers, leaves of indian mesquit, and flower resembling in colour and form the feather of a crane, are flourishing.
vēḷ aravu koṅkai iḷa maṅkaiyarkaḷ kuṅkumam viraikku maṇam ār tēḷ aravu teṉṟal teru eṅkum niṟaivu oṉṟi varu tēvūr (atuvē)
is Tēvūr where the balmy southern breeze fills all the streets, which adds its smell to the fragrance of saffron of the young ladies whose breasts the cupid torments, and is like the sting of the scorpion to them.
{3:74}__3+
{$} taṭavu paṇ colliṉ malai valli umai paṅkaṉ
Civaṉ who has on his half Umi, the dughter of the mountin who is like a tender creepers and speaks words like melodies played on the lute.
emai āḷum iṟaivaṉ
the Lord who admits us as his slaves.
eṇ taṭavu vāṉavar iṟaiñcu kaḻalōṉ iṉitu irunta iṭam ām
the place where he whose feet are worshipped by esteemed celestials, dwells with pleasure.
1. viṇ taṭavu vār poḻil ukutta naṟavu āṭi malarcūṭi virai ār ceṇ taṭavu māḷikai ceṟintu tiru oṉṟi vaḷar tēvūr (atuvē)
is tēvūr where the mansions which are close to each other and where the goddess of wealth flourishes as she is pleased in her mind; and they gently stroke the sky, are full of fragrance as they bathe in the honey that drops from the gardens which stroke the sky and wears on its top flowers shed by the gardens. (cēṇ has been shortened into ceṇ for the sake of rhyme.)
[[Variant reading: 1. vaṇṭaṭavu.]]
{3:74}__4+
{$} mācu il maṉam nēcar tamatu ācaivaḷar cūlataraṉ
Civaṉ who holds the trident on whom the love of the loving devotees whose minds are without any blemish, are likely to grow day by day.
mēlai imaiyōr īcaṉ
is the chief of celestials who do not wink and dwell in heaven.
maṟai ōti
one who revealed the vetams himself chanting them.
eri āṭi
one who dances in the fire.
miku pācupataṉ mēvupati (tāṉ)
the city where the eminent pācupataṉ dwells with desire.
vācamalar kōtu kuyil vācakamum mātaravar pūvaimoḻiyum tēca oli vīṇaiyoṭu kītam atu vīti tēvūr (atuvē)
is tēvūr where the sweet voice of the indian cuckoo which peck with their beaks into the fragrant flowers, the words of ladies, the words of the bush myna, and the music of the vīṇi which is played by people who came from other regions and the sweet sound of songs, fill the streets.
{3:74}__5+
{$} kāṉam uṟu māṉmaṟiyaṉ
Civaṉ holds in his hand a young deer that lives in the forest. (kāṉam uṟu is a generic epithet)
āṉai uri pōrvai
covers himself with the skin of an elephant.
kaṉal āṭal purivōṉ
dances in the fire at the end of the world.
ēṉa eyiṟu, āmai, iḷa nākam vaḷar mārpiṉ imaiyōr talaivaṉ ūr
the place of the chief of the tēvar who do not wink, on whose chest the hog's tusk, the shell of the tortoise and a young cobra are resting.
vāṉ aṇavu cūtam, iḷavāḻai, makiḻ, mātavi, palānilavi vārtēṉ amutu uṇṭu varivaṇṭu maruḷ pāṭi varu tēvūr atuvē
is tēvūr where mango-trees which touch the sky, young plintins, pointed-leaved ape flowers trees, common delight of the woods, jack fruit are in a flourishing condition, the bees with lines on their bodies drink the nectar of honey which flow from them and come singing maruḷ, a secondary melody-type of the Kuṟinci class.
{3:74}__6+
{$} āṟiṉoṭu kīṟu mati ēṟucaṭai ēṟaṉ
Civaṉ has a bull on whose matted locks the river, Kaṅki, the crescent have settled.
aṭaiyār nakarkaḷ tāṉ cīṟum avai vēṟupaṭa nīṟu ceyta nīṟaṉ namai āḷum araṉ ūr
the place of araṉ who admitted us as his protege, who smears himself with holy ash, and who burnt the cities of the enemies to become changed from their previous state.
vīṟumalar ūṟummatu ēṟi vaḷar vāya viḷaikiṉṟa kaḻaṉic cēṟupaṭu ceṅkayal viḷippa iḷavāḷaivaru tēvūr atuvē
is Tēvūr where the young scabbard fish come when invited by the red carp fish that are in the fields which yield abundantly from the growing as the honey that flows from the flowers of unique beauty rise up and enter into the fields.
{3:74}__7+
{$} kaṉṟi eḻa veṉṟi nikaḻ tuṉṟu puram aṉṟu aviya niṉṟu nakai cey eṉ taṉatu ceṉṟu nilai entai taṉa taṅkai amar iṉpanakartāṉ
the city that grants happiness to its residents, which is the abode of the father of my father, who is the refuge for me to go to him, and take shelter in him, and who lughed standing on the chariot to destroy the cities close to each other in the distant past, which gined victory after and rose with anger to destroy the worlds.
muṉṟil micai niṉṟa palaviṉ kaṉikaḷ tiṉṟu kaṟavaik kuruḷaikaḷ ceṉṟu icaiya niṉṟu tu(ḷ)ḷi oṉṟa viḷaiyāṭuvaḷar tēvūr atuvē
is Tēvūr where the calves of milch cows eat the fruits of the jack trees that grow in the front portions of houses, go from there and stand sgreeing among themselves, play jumping simultaneously, and grow in size.
[[kuruḷai used to denote calves is a new usage: tolkāppiyam, marapiyal, 8.]]

{$} vaṇṇamukil aṉṉa eḻil aṇṇaloṭu cuṇṇam mali vaṇṇamalarmēl naṇ avaṉum
the god who has the beauty of the sable cloud and Piramaṉ who stays in the colourful flower of lotus which has abundant pollen.
eṇ ariya viṇṇavarkaḷ kaṇṇa aṉalam koḷ patitāṉ
the place of Civaṉ who assumed the form of fire to make countless celestials to think of means to reach Civaṉ.
vaṇṇam vaṉanuṇṇiṭaiyiṉ eṇ ariya aṉṉa naṭai iṉmoḻiyiṉār
countless ladies who speak sweet words and have the git of a swan, who have a minute and beautiful waist and beautiful complexion.
tiṇṇa va(ṇ)ṇam māḷikai ceṟinta icai yāḻ maruvu tēvūr atuvē
is Tēvūr where the strong and colourful mansions are filled with the music played on the yāl (by the ladies).

{$} tuṅkam miku poṅku aravu taṅku caṭai naṅkaḷ iṟai
our master on whose matter locks excellent and angry cobras stay.
tuṉṟu kuḻul ār ceṅkayal kaṇ maṅkai umai naṅkai oru paṅkaṉ
who has on one half a lady of distinction, Umi, who has dense tresses of hir and eyes resembling red carp fish.
amar tēvūr ataṉ mēl
on Tēvūr where he stays with desire
paiṅkamalam aṅku aṇi koḷ tiṇ pukali ñāṉacampantaṉ urai cey, caṅkam mali centamiḻkaḷ pattum ivai vallavarkaḷ caṅkai ilar
those who are able to recite all the ten verses rendered in refined Tamil and fit to be considered as approved by the caṅkam, by Ñāṉacampantaṉ of imperishable Pukali, where fertile lotus flowers lend beauty, have no doubt about their salvation.

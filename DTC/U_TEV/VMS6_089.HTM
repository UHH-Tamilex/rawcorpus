iṉṉampart tāṉ tōṉṟi īcaṉār
Civaṉ who is self-existent in Iṉṉampar
alli malar nāṟṟuttu uḷḷār
gives pleasure by being inside the fragrance of flowers which have inner petals
aṉpu uṭaiyār cintai akalār
will not leave the minds of pious people
colliṉ arumaṟaikaḷ tāmē
is himself the abstruse vetam-s having words with pregnant meanings
tū neṟikku vaḻikāṭṭum toḻilār
does the function of showing the path to attain knowledge of the ultimate reality
villiṉ puram mūṉṟu erittār
burnt with a bow the three cities
vīṅku iruḷum nam veḷiyum āṉār
is the dense darkness and bright light.
elli naṭam āṭa vallār
is able to dance in the night
[[All the pōlum in this verse and the following verses are expletives.]]

iṉṉam part tāṉ tōṉṟi īcaṉār
see 1st verse
kōḻik koṭiyōṉ taṉ tātai
is the father of one who has a cock in his banner.
kompaṉāḷ pākam kuḷirntār
rejoiced to have as a half in his body a lady who is tender and supple as the twing of a plant.
ūḻi mutal varum tāmē
is also the cause for the happenings during acons
uḷkuvār uḷḷattiṉ uḷḷār
stays in the minds of those who meditate on him
āḻittēr vittakarum tāmē
is also the skillful person who has a chariot with strong wheels
[[āḻi may also mean the sun and the moon which are circular; they served as wheels for the chariot on which Civaṉ went to fight against the three citites]]
[[PP: āḻittēr vittakaṉai nāṉ kaṇṭatu ārūrē
(Nāvukkaracar, Ārūr, Paṇ Cīkāmaram, 4:19_7)]]
aṭaintavar kaṭku aṉparāy niṉṟār
is a loving person to those who seek refuge in him
ēḻupiṟavikkum tāmē (kāraṇar)
is the cause for the seven kinds of living beings.

karuvuṟṟa kālattē eṉṉai āṇṭu
Having admitted into his grace me even at the time of my being in the mother's womb
kaḻaṟpōtu tantu aḷitta kaḷvar
is the robber who gave me his feet which are like flowers, and saved me
[[karuvuṟṟa nāḷ mutalāka uṉ pātamē kāṇpataṟku, urukiṟṟu eṉ uḷḷamum nāṉum kiṭantu alantu eyttu oḻintēṉ karuvāyk kiṭantu uṉ kaḻalē niṉaiyum karuttuṭaiyēṉ (tirunāvukkaracar, oṟṟiyūr, tiruviruttam 6; tiruppātirippuliyūr, tiruviruṭṭam, 6);
God is spoken of as kaḷvar , because he was within tirunāvukkaracar concealing himself, and after attaining spiritual wisdom realised that god was within him always]]
ceruvil puram mūṉṟum aṭṭār
destroyed all the three cities in the battle (ceruvil can be interpreted as the bow which he used in the battle)
maruvip piriyāta maintar
is the strong youth who does not leave people whom he has befriended.
malaraṭikaḷ nāṭi vaṇaṅkaluṟṟa iruvarkku oruvarāy niṉṟār
was the matchless chief for those two persons, Māl and Piramaṉ, who bowed before his feet which are like lotus flowers, after abandoning their futile search.

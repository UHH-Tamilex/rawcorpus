[[Note: aṉpil and ālantuṟai are two different shrines; Aṉpil was recently renovated, During the renovations the aṉpil plates were found belonging to Cuntara Cōḻaṉ, father of Rajaraja I. Ālantuṟai is a little to the East in a dilapidated condition, in which a civalinkam is found under a banyan tree; so it got its name of Ālaṉtuṟai.]]

{1:33}__1+
{$} kaṇai nīṭu erimāl aravam varai villā
utilising the arrow which was formed by the combination of fire which rises upwards and Māl, and Vācuki, the serpent, and the Mēru mountain as bow.
eyil mūṉṟum eritta iṟaivar
god who burnt all the three forts.
(1) piṇai māmayilum kuyil cēr maṭa aṉṉam aṇaiyum poḻil aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai which has gardens where the flock of young swans, indian cuckoos and male peacocks along with their females, approach.
[[Variant reading: (1) piṇaiyā]]
{1:33}__2+
{$} caṭai ār caturaṉ
Civaṉ, a capable person, who has a caṭai.
mutirāmaticūṭi
and wearing a crescent.
viṭaiār koṭi oṉṟu uṭai entai vimalaṉ
is our father, the spotless one who has a flag on which the form of a bull is drawn.
kiṭai ār oli ottu aravattu icai kiḷḷai aṭai ārpoḻil aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai which has gardens which are reached parrots which repeat the sound of reciting the vetam by bands of disciples learnt by rote.
{1:33}__3+
{$} ūrum aravam caṭaimēl uṟavaittup, pārum palikoṇṭu olipāṭum paramar
the supreme god who sings songs receiving alms in the world, having placed on his caṭai a crawling cobra.
nīr uṇkayalum vayal vāḷai varālōṭu ārumpuṉal aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai where in the water of the fields the carp which drinks water, scabbard and murrel are found in abundance.
{1:33}__4+
{$} piṟaiyum aravum uṟavaitta muṭimēl naṟai uṇṭu eḻuvaṉṉiyum maṉṉu caṭaiyār
Civaṉ who has on his caṭai leaves of indian mesquit of good fragrance, which is on the head on which are placed the crescent and the cobra close to each other.
maṟaiyum pala vētiyar ōta oliceṉṟu aṟaiyum puṉal aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai of abundant water, where the sound of the vētams which are chanted by many vētiyar (brahmins) spread and make a big sound.
{1:33}__5+
{$} nīṭum puṉal kaṅkaiyum muṭimēl taṅka, kūṭummalaiyāḷ orupākam amarntār
Civaṉ who kept on one half the daughter of the mountain who joined him, when the Kaṅkai of plentiful water stays on the head
[[kaṅkaiyum: um is expletive or it may include other things on the head.]]
māṭu(m) muḻavam atira(m) maṭamātar āṭumpati aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai where the young ladies dance to the accompaniment of muḻavu which is played on a high pitch, near them.
[[māṭum muḻavam, muḻavam matira atiram maṭamātar all the letters underlined are augmentation for the sake of rhythm.]]
{1:33}__6+
{$} nīṟār tirumēṉiyar
Civaṉ who has a divine body on which holy ash is sumptuously smeared.
ūṉam ilār pāl ūṟu ārcuvai ākiya umpar perumāṉ
the chief of the celestials, who is the sweet nectar which is the cause for the mouth to water, in people who have no defects.
vēṟu ār ākilum miku cantaṉam unti āṟu ārvayal aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai which has a river which irrigates the fields pushing the eagle-wood logs separated from the trees and superior sandal-woods.
{1:33}__7+
{$} ceṭi ārtalaiyil palikoṇṭu iṉitu uṇṭapaṭiyār paramaṉ, paramēṭṭi
Civaṉ who is in the most exalted place and the supreme being and who has the nature of eating with pleasure the alms which he received in the skull which has the stench of flesh.
taṉ cīraikkaṭiyār malarum puṉal tūvi niṉṟu ēttum aṭiyār toḻum aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai where the devotees praise his fame by scattering fragrant flowers and sprinkling water.
{1:33}__8+
{$} viṭattu ār tikaḻum miṭaṟaṉ
Civaṉ has a neck conspicious by the poison stationed there.
naṭam āṭi
performer of dances.
paṭattu ār aravam viravum caṭai āti
is the origin of all things on whose caṭai cobras with hoods are in combination with other things.
koṭittēr ilaṅkaikkōṉvarai āra aṭarntār aruḷ aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai who granted his grace after pressing down under the mountain the King of ilaṅkai who had a chariot adorned with flags.
{1:33}__9+
{$} malarmēl ayaṉum neṭu mālum piṇaṅki vaṇaṅki aṟikiṉṟilar perumai
auaṉ who is seated on a (lotus) flower and tall Māl being at Variance with each other, could not know his greatness, by bowing before him.
cuṇaṅkum mukattam mulaiyāḷ orupākam aṇaṅkum (1) nikaḻ aṉpil ālantuṟaiyār
is in aṉpil ālantuṟai where many divine phenomena occur, having on one half a lady with breasts on whose surface there are spreading yellow spots.
[[Variant reading: (1) tikaḻ]]
{1:33}__10+
{$} tukil pōrttu uḻalvār
buddhists who cover their bodies with yellow robes called cīVaram.
taṟiyārcamaṇkaiyar
the low camaṇar who are like wooden posts.
[[vāliyataṟikaḷ pōla matiyilār (appar, tiruvaiyāṟu, 4);
taṟipōlām camaṇar (campantar, irāmanantēccaram, 10)]]
neṟiyā uṇarā nilaikēṭiṉar
are in a bad state and do not know things in the proper way about Civaṉ.
nittal veṟiār malarkoṇṭu aṭivīḻum varai aṟivāravar aṉpil ālantuṟaiyārē
Civaṉ who is in aṉpil ālantuṟai will recognize those who fall at his feet scattering fragrant flowers daily (and will grant their wishes)
{1:33}__11+
{$} aravu ār puṉal aṉpil ālantuṟai taṉmēl
on Aṉpil Ālantuṟai which has water making a big noise (aravu=aravam noise)
karavātavar kāḻiyuḷ ñāṉacampantaṉ paravuār tamiḻ pattu icai pāṭavallār
those who are able to sing with music the ten tamiḻ verses which have the purpose of praising Civaṉ and were composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ in kaḻi where people who do not with hold what they can give, live.
pōy vāṉiṭaiviravu ākuvār
mix with the tēvar in heaven, going there after death.
vīṭueḷitām
eternal bliss is easy.

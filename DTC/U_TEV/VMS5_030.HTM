parappu nīrvaru kāvirit teṉkarait tirupparāyttuṟai mēviya celvar
the god who is in Tirupparāyttuṟi with desire, on the Southern bank of the Kāviri which brings extensive floods
kālam aṭaintavar tamviṉai karappar
will cuse to disappear the karmam-s of those who take refuge in him early in life.
kaṅkai ceñcaṭai curukkumāṟu vallār
is able to reduce the river Kaṅki in his red caṭi
{5:30}__2+
{$}
kaḷiyāṉaiyiṉ īr uri mūṭiṉār
Civaṉ covered himself with the wet skin of an elephant which is frenzied
maṟai nāṉkiṉōṭu āṟu aṅkam pāṭiṉār
He sang the four Vetams and the six Aṅkams
cēṭaṉār
has greatness
teṉparāyttuṟaic celvarait tēṭik koṇṭu ceṉṟu aṭiyēṉ kāṇpaṉ
I who am his servant shall see Civaṉ in parāittuṟi in the south searching for him and approaching him
{5:30}__3+
{$}
paṭṭam neṟṟiyar
Civaṉ wears a plate of gold on his forehead
pālmatik kīṟṟiṉar
wears a crescent as white as milk
naḷḷiruḷ ēmamum naṭṭam āṭuvar
dances at night when there is dense darkness.
[[ēmam-iravu;
īmap puṟaṅkāṭṭil ēmam tōṟum aḻal āṭumē (appar, tiruvatikivīraṭṭāṉam (13) 7)]]
ciṭṭaṉār
one who is well versed in vedic love
teṉ parāyttuṟaic celvaṉār iṭṭamāy iruppārai aṟivarē
the god in parāittuṟi in the south will know people who cherish him with love (aṟivar means knowing them he will grant all their wishes)

teṉ parāyttuṟai mēviya celvaṉār
Civaṉ who desires parāittuṟi in the south.
muṉpu elām cila mōḻaimai pēcuvar
will speak words of ridicule in the presence of many people
[[mōḻaimai stupidity is another meaning
muṉpu coṉṉa mōḻaimaiyāl muṭṭai maṉattīrē (cuntarar, tiruvetirkoḷpāṭi, 8)]]
eṉpu elām pūṇṭu aṅku uḻitarvar
will wander having as ornaments all the bones.
aṉparāy iruppārai aṟivar
will know people who bestow their love on him
[[He will know and grant all their requests]]

(People of the world!)
tātu aviḻcaṭaic caṅkaraṉ pātattuḷ
on the feet of Caṅkaraṉ who has a caṭi to which pollen lends its fragrance
pōtu tātoṭu puṉaintu uṭaṉ
in addition to adorning them with flowers contining pollen
vātai tīrkka eṉṟu ētti
prising him, Let you remove all distress
parāyttuṟaic cōtiyāṉait toḻutu eḻuntu uymmiṉē
save yourselves by worshipping the effulgent light in Parāyttuṟi waking from sleep

neruppiṉāl kuvittāl okkum nīḷcaṭai
Civaṉ who has a long caṭi which appears like a heap of fire
[[neruppiṉāl should be changed into neruppai]]
paruppatam matayāṉai urittavaṉ
who covered himself with the skin of a frenzied elephant resembling a mountin
tirupparāyttuṟaiyār tirumārpil nūl poruppu arāvi iḻiyuṉal pōṉṟatu
the sacred thread on the chest of the god in parāittuṟi resembles the clear water descending, grating the hill

eṭṭa viṭṭa iṭumaṇal ekkarmēl
on the mound of sand heaped high by the waves and wind
paṭṭa nuṇ tuḷi pāyum parāyttuṟaic ciṭṭaṉ cēvaṭi ceṉṟu aṭaikiṟṟirēl
if you approach the feet of the god well versed in vedic love who is in parāittuṟi where the fine drops of water dashing on it are spreading, going to that shrine.
viṭṭu namviṉaiyuḷḷaṉa vīṭumē
our Karmams which are still with us will disappear, leaving us.

neruppu arāy nimirntāl okkum nīḷ naṭai
the long caṭi of Civaṉ resembles the fire that is filed and standing erect.
maruppu arāy vaḷaintāl okkum vāḷ mati

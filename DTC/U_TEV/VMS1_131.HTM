{1:131}__1+
{$} meyttu āṟu cuvaiyum
by the six flavours that are tasted by the tongue in this body.
ēḻ icaiyum
by the seven notes of music.
eṇ kuṇaṅkaḷum
by the eight qualities.
virumpum nālvētattālum (1) teḷiya aṟivu oṇṇā naṭai
his nature which cannot be comprehended by all the four desirable vētam-s.
paḷiṅkēpōl arivai pākam ottu
united with the half of a lady and having colour like the clear crystal quartz
[[teḷiyaṭiteḷḷiya]]
āṟu camayaṅkaṭku orutalaivaṉ karutum ūr
the place which the unequalled master for the six religious sects intimately connected to caiva cittāntam, which are six in number desires.
[[cuvaiyālum, icaiyālum, vētattālum teḷiya aṟivu oṇṇā orutalaivaṉ is the syntactical link]]
ulavu teṇṇīr muttāṟu vetir utira nittilamvārik koḻikkum mutukuṉṟamē
is Mutukunṟam where the river, Muttāṟu, of clear moving water, takes in a sweep pearls which drop from bamboos and bring them to the surface.
[[Variant reading: (1) teḷiyap]]
{1:131}__2+
{$} vērimiku kuḻaliyoṭu vēṭuvaṉāy veṅkāṉil vicayaṉ mēvu pōril miku poṟai aḷantu pācupatam purintu aḷitta purāṇar kōyil
the temple of the primordial god who gave with love Pācupatam to Vicayaṉ in the fight which he fought with him in the hot forest measuring his patience, assuming the form of a hunter along with a lady whose tresses of hair have abundant honey.
kāriṉ mali kaṭipoḻilkaḷ kaṉikaḷ palamalar utirttuk kayam muyaṅki mūrivaḷam kiḷar teṉṟal tirumuṉṟil pukuntu ulavum mutukuṉṟamē
is Mutukuṉṟam where the strong and fertile southern breeze enters and moves into the holy countryard being united with the tanks and shedding flowers and many fruits in the fragrant gardens which are flourishing due to receiving water from rain.
{1:131}__3+
{$} takkaṉatu peruvēḷvi
in the big sacrifice performed by Takkaṉ.
cantiraṉ, intiraṉ, eccaṉ, arukkaṉ, aṅki, mikka vitātāviṉoṭum vitivaḻiyē taṇṭitta vimalarkōyil
the temple of the spotless god who punished according to rules the moon, intiraṉ the deity supposed to be present at a sacrifice and to accept the offerings given, the sun, fire god, and the renowned Piramaṉ.
kokku iṉiya koḻum varukkai, katali, kamuku, uyar teṅkiṉ kuvaikoḷcōlaikaḷ
in the garden which has mango trees, sweet and plumpy jack trees, plantain trees, areca palm and tall cocoanut of clusters.
mukkaṉiyiṉ cāṟu oḻukic cēṟu ularā nīḷvayal cūḻ mutukuṉṟamē
is Mutukuṉṟam which is surrounded by long fields where the mire which is formed by the juice of the mango-fruit, jack fruit and plantain fruit which flows, and does not get dry.
{1:131}__4+
{$} vemmai miku puravāṇar mikai ceyya viṟal aḻintu viṇṇuḷōrkaḷ, cemmalarōṉ, intiraṉ māl ceṉṟirappa
when the celestials, Piramaṉ in the red lotus flower, intiraṉ and Māl having lost their strength when the cruel residents of the cities did arrogant deeds, besought Civaṉ's help.
tēvarkaḷētēr(atu) āka
having the teVar themselves as a chariot.
maimmaruvu mēruvilum, mācuṇanāṇ, ari, eri, kālvāḷiyāka
having the mēru on which the clouds settle as bow, the serpent, vācuki as bow-string and the arrow whose components were are (mal) fire and the air.
mummatilum noṭiyaḷavil poṭi ceyta mutalvaṉ iṭam mutukuṉṟamē
Mutukuṉṟam is the place of the first cause who reduced to ashes all the three forts in an instant.
{1:131}__5+
{$} iḻaimēvu kalai alkul ēntiḻaiyāḷ orupālā
the lady who wears superior ornaments and has a waist wearing a cloth of fine fibre being on one half.
orupāl (1) eḷkātu uḻaimēvum, uri uṭutta oruvaṉ (2) iruppiṭam eṉpar
People say that (Mutukuṉṟam) is the dwelling place of the unequalled god who dressed in a deer skin on one side without feeling bashful.
umpar ōṅku kaḻaimēvu maṭamanti maḻaikaṇṭu makaviṉoṭum puka
the young female monkey which sits on the bamboo which grows high to touch the sky seeing the cloud about to rain enters into a place of safety.
oṇ kalliṉ muḻai mēvum (3) mālyāṉai iraitērum vaḷar cāral mutukuṉṟamē
is Mutukuṉṟam which has slopes where many trees grow where the big elephants which lives in the cave which has bright boulders go in search of food.
[[mutukuṉṟam oruvaṉ iruppiṭam eṉpar is the syntactical link.]]
[[Variant readings : (1) veḷkātu (2) viruppiṭam eṉpar (3) māvāṉai]]
{1:131}__6+
{$} nakai ār veṇtalai mālai muṭikku aṇinta nātaṉ iṭam
the place of the master who adorns his head with a garland of white skulls with teeth intact.
naṉ muttāṟu vakai āṟum varaip paṇṭam koṇṭu
the good Muttāṟu bringing the produces of the mountain which are of several kinds.
iraṇṭu karai arukum maṟiya mōti
dashing near both the banks to recede
takai ārum varampu iṭaṟi
striking against the ridges which are the boundaries of fields.
cāli, kaḻunīr, kuvaḷai cāyappāyntu
flowing to cause to bend the paddy crop, indian purple lily and blue nelumbo flowers.
mukai ār centāmaraikaḷ mukam malara vayal taḻuvum mutukuṉṟamē
is Mutukuṉṟam which is surrounded by fields where the red lotus buds blossom like faces.
{1:131}__7+
{$} aṟam kiḷarum nālvētam āliṉkīḻ iruntu aruḷi
initiating the four sages into the secrets of the four vētam-s which speak conspicuously about right conduct, sitting under the banyan tree.
amarar vēṇṭa
to fulfil the request of the immortals
niṟam kiḷar centāmaraiyōṉ ciram aintiṉ oṉṟu aṟutta nimalar kōyil
the shrine of the spotless Civaṉ who cut off one of the five heads of Piramaṉ who is seated in a red lotus of resplendent colour.
tiṟam koḷ maṇittaraḷaṅkaḷ varattiraṇṭu aṅku eḻiṟkuṟavar ciṟumimārkaḷ muṟaṅkaḷiṉāl koḻittu maṇi cela vilakki muttu ulaippeytu mutukuṉṟamē
is Mutukuṉṟam where in the river which brings different kinds of precious stones along with pearls, the young and beautiful girls of the KuṟaVar tribe sift with the winnow to separate the precious stones and pour into the pot of water set over the fire for boiling rice, pearls as rice.
{1:131}__8+
{$} katir oḷiya neṭumuṭi pattu uṭaiya kaṭal ilaṅkaiyarkōṉ kaṇṇum vāyum pitir oḷiya kaṉal piṟaṅkap peruṅkayilaimalaiyai nilai peyartta ñāṉṟu
on that day when the King of the inhabitant of Ilaṅkai surrounded by sea who had tall ten crowns, emitting rays removed the great mountain; Kayilai from its position, when in his eyes and mouth five which has scattered light glittered.
matil aḷakaikku iṟai murala
the King of Aḷakai which has a fortified wall to shout out of joy.
malaraṭi oṉṟu ūṉṟi
fixing one foot resembling flower
maṟaipāṭa āṅkē
when he sang the Cāma Vētam, then and there itself.
mutir oḷiya cuṭarneṭuvāḷ muṉ īntāṉ vāyntapati mutukuṉṟamē
is Mutukuṉṟam, the suitable place for Civaṉ who donated long ago a long sword of excessive brilliance.
{1:131}__9+
{$} pū ār poṉ taviciṉ micai iruntavaṉum pūntuḻāy puṉainta mālum ōvātu kaḻuku ēṉam āy uyarntu āḻntu uṟanāṭi uṇmai kāṇāt tēārnta tiruuruvaṉcērumalai
the mountain where the god of holy and divine body whose true nature could not be found out though making a thorough search by flying clove and going deep into the earth by Piramaṉ who is seated in a beautiful lotus flower and Māl who adorned himself with beautiful basil transforming themselves respectively into an eagle and a pig.
[[Piramaṉ assuming the form of an eagle is found in Tēvāram in other places and in the 11th Tirumuṟai;]
[[PP: ēṉam kaḻuku āṉavar uṉṉaimuṉ eṉkol, vāṉam talam maṇṭiyum kaṇṭilāvāṟē
(campantar tirumaṟaikkātu (2) 9);]]
[[PP: puṇṭarikattu uḷ irunta puttēḷ kaḻuku uruvāy aṇṭar aṇṭam ūṭuruva āṅkōṭi
(11th book, pōṟṟittirukkaliveṇpā, 4)]]
ceḻunilattai mūṭavanta mūvāta muḻaṅku olinīr kīḻ tāḻamēl uyarnta mutukuṉṟamē
is Mutukuṉṟam, which rose above when the water that is old and roaring and came to cover the fertile earth stayed low.
{1:131}__10+
{$} mēṉiyil cīvarattārum viritaru taṭṭu uṭaiyārum viral ākā ūṉikaḷāyuḷḷār coṟ koḷḷātu
without listening to the words of the people who have corporeal existence and who are not fit to be associated with, [i.e.] the buddhists who cover their bodies with a salmon-coloured dress and amanar who wear broad dress of mat.
uḷ uṇarntu aṅku uymmiṉ; toṇṭīr
votaries of Civaṉ! realise Civaṉ within you and save yourselves.
ñāṉikaḷāy uḷḷārkaḷ
people endowed with spiritual knowledge.
nāṉmaṟaiyai muḻutu(um) uṇarntu
having learnt all the four Vētam-s completely.
aimpulaṉkaḷ ceṟṟu
having subdued the five senses of the body.
mōṉikaḷāy muṉic celvar taṉittiruntu tavam puriyum mutukuṉṟamē
Mutukuṉṟam where the sages who have taken a vow of silence perform penance detached from company and being alone.
{1:131}__11+
{$} muḻaṅku olinīr muttāṟu valam ceyyum mutukuṉṟattu iṟaiyai
about Civaṉ in Mutukuṉṟam around which the river Muttāṟu which has roaring water, goes from left to right.
mūvāp paḻaṅkiḻamaip paṉṉirupēr paṭaittuṭaiya kaḻumalamē patiyākkoṇṭu
having Kaḻumalam which has twelve names which are its old names by right and which do not become old.
taḻaṅku erimūṉṟu ōmpu toḻil tamiḻ ñāṉacampantaṉ camaitta pāṭal
the songs composed by Ñāṉacampantaṉ proficient in Tamiḻ and who performs the function of maintaining the three sacrificial fires which produce a tremulous sound.
vaḻaṅkum icai kūṭum vakai pāṭum avar nīṭu ulakam āḷvar(tām, ē)
those who can sing according to their ability with music that is current will rule over the extensive world.

{4:55}__1+
{$} teṉ tirai (1) tēṅki ōtam ceṉṟu aṭivīḻum kālai
when the clear waves become stagnant and the water in them surge and fall at the feet of the god.
toṇṭu iraittu
the devotees making a roaring noise
aṇṭar kōṉaittoḻutu aṭi eṅkum vaṇaṅki
paying obeisance to the feet of the god who is the master of the tēvar and worshppping him with joined hands, everywhere
vaṇṭukaḷ matukkaḷ māntum valampurattu aṭikaḷ tammaik koṇṭu
meditating in their hearts the god in Valampuram where the bees drink different kinds of honey.
naṟkītam pāṭak kuḻakartām iruntavāṟē
How wonderful a sight it is that the youth was seated when they sang songs of praise
[[Variant reading:(1) ōṅki]]
{4:55}__2+
{$} maṭukkaḷil vāḷai pāya
as the scabbard fish leap in the deep pools.
vaṇṭu iṉam irinta poykaip piṭikkaḷiṟu eṉṉat tammil piṇai payiṉṟu aṇai varālkaḷ
the mural fish which embrace often their females like the male and female elephants in the natural tanks from which the swarm of bees speed for safety.
toṭutta naṉmālai entit toṇṭarkaḷ paravi ētta
the devotees holding the good garlands which they strung, praise in the second person and third person, Civaṉ.
vaṭittaṭaṅkaṇṇi paṅkar valampurattu iruntavāṟē
what a wonderful sight it is that the god who has on his half a lady of big eyes like the tender mango cut into two, dwells in Valampuram.
[[if the reading were payiṉṟaṉa it would make better sense]]
[[varālkaḷaiyuṭaiya valampuram is the syntactical link according to the existing reading]]
[[vaṭuvakirkkaṇṇi is the name of the goddess in this shrine]]
{4:55}__3+
{$} tēṉ uṭai malarkaḷ koṇṭu tiruntu aṭi poruntac cērtti
laying the flowers with honey to reach the perfect feet
āṉiṭai añcum koṇṭu aṉpiṉāl amara āṭṭi
bathing the god with love to please him, with the five products of the cow.
vāṉiṭai matiyam cūṭum valam purattu aṭikaḷ tammai nāṉ aṭaintu ēttappeṟṟu nalviṉaip payaṉ uṟṟēṉ
I, who am in every way low, got the fruit of my virtuous acts by reaching and praising the god in Valampuram who wears a crescent that shines in the sky.
{4:55}__4+
{$} muḷai eyiṟṟu iḷanal ēṉampūṇṭu
wearing the tusk of a hog which is young and strong
[[ēṉam-ēṉakkompu: the hog's tusk]]
moycaṭaikaḷ tāḻa
the crowded caṭai to hang low.
vaḷaieyiṟṟu iḷaiyanākam valittu arai icaiya vīkki
having securely tied fittingly in the waist seizing a young cobra of curved teeth.
(1) puḻaikaiya pōrvai pōrttu
covering himself with the upper garment got from an elephant
[[puḻaikkai: has become puḻaikai to suit rhyme]]
[[puḻaikai- elephant]]
puṉaloṭu matiyamcūṭi
adorning himself with water and a crescent.
vaḷaipayil iḷaiya rēttum valampurattu aṭikaḷ (tām, ē)
Civaṉ is the god in Valampuram where ladies wearing many bangles, praise him.
[[pūṇṭu, tāḻa, valittu, icaiyavīkkip, pōrttuccūṭi valampurattu aṭikaḷ āyiṉār: this is one kind of syntactical link.]]
[[cūṭi can be taken as a noun and the previous adverbial participles can be linked with that]]
[[ēttum can be interpreted in the passive voice as ēttappeṟum.]]
[[Variant reading: (1) puṉaipuḻaikaiya]]
{4:55}__5+
{$} curuḷ uṟu varaiyiṉ mēlāl tuḷaṅku iḷampaḷiṅku cinta
scattering the white rays like crystal which are moving on the ridges which are curved.
iruḷ uṟukatirnuḻainta iḷaṅkatirp pacalait tiṅkaḷ valampurattu aṭikaḷār aruḷuṟum aṭiyar ellām
the devotees who desire the grace of the god in Valampuram who wears a tender crescent of mild rays which penetrate into the ears of corn in the darkness of night.
aṅkaiyil malarkaḷ ēnta maruḷ uṟukītam kēṭṭār
listened to the music known as maruḷ when they were holding flowers in their palms to worship him
[[maruḷ: one of the secondary melody types of the Kuṟiñci class]]
[[The meaning of the first two lines is not clear]]
{4:55}__6+
{$} nīṇṭa puṉcaṭaiyiṉāṉē
Civaṉ who has a long and tawny caṭai!
valampuravaṉīrē
one who is in Valampuram!
neñcutaṉṉāl niṉaikkiṉṟēṉ
I am thinking of you in my mind.
aṉaittu uṭaṉ koṇṭu vantu (aṅku) aṉpiṉāl amaiya āṭṭi
bathing you properly with love bringing all things that are used for bathing
puṉaikkiṉṟēṉ poymmai taṉṉai
I increase falsehood
meymmaiyaip puṇara māṭṭēṉ
I am unable to be associated with truth.
(1) eṉakku nāṉ ceyvatu eṉṉē iṉi
what can I do now for my uplift?
[[Variant reading: (1) neṉakku, niṉaikkum]]
{4:55}__7+
{$} ceṅkayal cēlkaḷ pāyntu tēmpaḻam iṉiya nāṭi
Fish like red kayal and cēl [[kayal, cēl: different kinds of fish]] searching for sweet fruit which are tasty to eat.
tam kayam tuṟantu pōntu
having gone leaving their tanks
taṭam poykai aṭaintu niṉṟu
reaching the extensive big natural tank.
koṅkaiyar kuṭaiyum kālaik koḻuṅkaṉi aḻuṅkiṉārām maṅkalam maṉaiyil mikku ār valampurattu aṭikaḷārē
the god who is in Valampuram, when ladies plunged and bathed in the water, felt pity for the plumpy fruits where in the houses auspicious things are in abundance.
[[The meaning of the first three lines is not as clear as it should be]]
{4:55}__8+
{$} aruku elām kuvaḷai cennel akalilai āmpal neytal
By the side of the village there is blue nelumbo flowers, and crops of paddy and lilies of broad leaves, and white indian water-lilies that blossom in midst of that crop.
teru elām teṅkum māvum
coconut trees and mango trees are growing tall in all the streets.
paḻamviḻum paṭappai ellām
in the gardens fruits fall having become fully ripe.
kuruku iṉam kūṭi āṅkē kummalittu iṟaku ulartti
flocks of birds gather there, flap their wings and dry them.
maruval ām iṭaṅkaḷ kāṭṭum valampurattu aṭikaḷār
the god is in Valampuram which contains such places frequented by birds.
{4:55}__9+
{$} karuvarai aṉaiyamēṉik kaṭal vaṇṇaṉ (avaṉum) kāṇāṉ
even Māl who has the colour of the sea and has a big body like the block mountain, could not find him
[[sea is compared to the colour and the mountain for the big body]]
tiruvarai aṉaiya pūmēl ticai mukaṉ avaṉum kāṇāṉ
and Piramaṉ too who has four faces and who is seated in a lotus flower as big as a beautiful mountain, could not find him.
oruvarai ucci ēṟi ōṅkiṉār
Civaṉ rose high climbing up the peak of a mountain (Kayilai)
ōṅkivantu
having risen high and descending low
arumaiyil eḷiyar āṉār (avar) valampuravaṉār
[the] god in Valampuram became easy of access in the midst of his pre-eminence;
[[PP: arumaiyil eḷiya aḻakē pōṟṟi
(Tiruvācakam, Pōṟṟittiruvakaval, 126.)]]
{4:55}__10+
{$} vāḷ eyiṟu ilaṅka nakku
laughing to make the bright teeth to shine.
vaḷarkayilāyam taṉṉai āḷvalikarutic ceṉṟa arakkaṉai varaikkīḻ aṉṟu tōḷoṭu pattuvāyum tolaintu uṭaṉ aḻunta ūṉṟi
pressing down close under the mountain the arakkaṉ who went to uproot it thinking of his valour and strength, to make his twenty shoulders and all the ten mouths to perish
āṇmaiyum valiyum tīrppār (avar) valampuravaṉār
Civaṉ in Valampuram will remove his valour and strength.

{2:92}__1+
{$} paṭṭam pāl niṟam matiyam
Civaṉ wears a plate of gold and a crescent as white as milk;
[[PP: paṭṭa neṟṟiyar (campantar pallavaṉiccaram, 2);
piṟai nutal vaṇṇam ākiṉṟu (puṟam, 1-9)]]
paṭar caṭaic cuṭarviṭu pāṇi
bore sparkling water on his spreading caṭi.
naḷḷiruḷ naṭṭam āṭum nātaṉār naviṉṟu uṟai kōyil
the temple in which the chief who dances at midnight dwells with desire.
puḷtaṉ pēṭaiyoṭu āṭum pūm pukalūr
which is in pukalūr where birds play with their females.
toṇṭar pōṟṟi vaṭṭam cūḻntu aṭiparavum varttamāṉīc carattārē
is in varttamāṉiccaram where devotees prise and surround him on a circle and pay obeisance to his feet.
{2:92}__2+
{$} muyal vaḷāviya tiṅkaḷ vāḷ mukattatu arivaiyil terivai iyal vaḷāviyatuṭaiya iṉṉamutu entai em perumāṉ
our Lord and our father who has a lady who is as sweet as the sweet nectar and who has the nature of arivi and terivi whose face is like the full moon in which the black spot is compared to the hare;
[[it is a literary convention to describe the black spot in the moon as a hare;
oṇ katir vāḷ matiyam cērtalāl ōṅkiya, aṅkaṇ vicum muyalum toḻappaṭūum (nālaṭiyār 176);
piṟaiyatu vaḷarat tāṉum vaḷarntu uṭaṉ perukip piṉṉāḷ, kuṟaipaṭumatiyam tēyak kuṟumuyal tēyvatēpōl- (Civaka cintāmaṇi 254);
arivai, terivai
are names of ladies denoting their ages.]]
kayal vaḷāviya kaḻaṉik karuniṟakkuvaḷai kaṇ malarum vayal vaḷāviya pukalūr vartta māṉīccurattār
is in varttamāṉiccaram in pukalūr surrounded by fields in which the blue-nelumbo flowers blossom like the eyes and the paddy-fields full of carp fish.
{2:92}__3+
{$} toṇṭar taṇ kayam mūḻki
the devotees bathing in the cool tanks plunging into them.
tuṇaiyalum cāntamum pukaiyum koṇṭu aṭikoṇṭu paravi
carrying garlands, sandal-paste, incense for burning, and prising the feet of god fixing them in their minds.
kuṟippu aṟimurukaṉ cey kōlam kaṇṭu kaṇṭu kaṇkuḷira
the eyes being satisfied with seeing the decoration by Murukaṉ (a nāyaṉār, comtemporary of Campantar) who knows everything by his penetrative intellect.
kaḷiparantu oḷi malku kaḷ ār vaṇṭu paṇ ceyyum pukalūr vartta māṉīccurattār
is in varttamāṉiccaram in pukalūr where the bees having drunk the honey are lustrous, get intoxicated, and hum like melody-types.
{2:92}__4+
{$} paṇṇa vaṇṇattar āki
being the forms of melody types.
pāṭaloṭu āṭal aṟāta viṇṇattar āyaviri pukalūrar
Civaṉ in extensive pukalūr where songs and dances never stop and who has the form of the sky.
ōr pākam peṇṇa vaṇṇattar ākum peṟṟiyoṭu
with the nature of having, as one half, a lady.
āṇ iṇai piṇainta vaṇṇavaṇṇattu em perumāṉ
our Lord who has unadorned natural beauty and arifical body who is united with a lady, being himself a male.
varttamāṉīccarattār
is in varttamāniccaram.

Civaṉ
ēṟu amar kaṭavuḷ

iṉṉamutu
one who is as sweet as the nectar.

pūcum mācu il veṇṇīṟṟar
god who smears himself with spotless white holy ash.
polivuṭaip pūmpukalūril
in beautiful pukalūr of gret splendour.
mūcuvaṇṭu aṟai koṉṟai murukaṉ 1. muppōtum cey muṭi mēl vācamā malar uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has on his head koṉṟi on which swarming bees who loudly, and other fragrant flowers which muruka nāyaṉar adorns in the morning, noon, and evening. (muruka nāyaṉār was a contemporary of campantar, a native of pukalūr; he supplied flowers and garland to Civaṉ thrice in a day; therefore he is eulogized by campantar.)
varttamāṉīccarattār
is in varttamāṉiccaram

{2:92}__6+
{$} taḷir ilaṅkoṭi vaḷara
as the tender creeper grows with tender leaves.
taṇ kayam iriyavaṇṭu ēṟi
the bees climbing upon those creeper to bend low in the cool tanks.
kiḷar iḷam(m) uḻai nuḻaiyak 1. kiḻitaru poḻiṟ pukalūril
in pukalūr where in the gardens the mast-wood trees blossom flowers like gold tied in a piece of cloth when the spirited young deer creeps through a narrow passage
[[the flowers of mastwood trees are compared to gold tied in a piece of cloth;
pāṇ nikar vaṇṭiṉam pāṭap paim poṉ taru veṇkiḻitam, cēṇikar kāviṉ vaḻaṅkum puṉṉaittuṟaic cērppar- (Kōviyār, 183)]]
uḷar iḷañ 2. cuṉai malarum oḷi taru 3. caṭaimuṭi ataṉ mēl vaḷar iḷam piṟai uṭaiyār varttamāṉīccarattār
Civaṉ who has on his brilliant caṭi coiled into a crown a growing crescent and flowers just blossomed in the tanks which are moved by the wind.
[[Variant reading: 1. tarupūm pukalūril 2. iḷañciṉai malarum 3. caṭaimuṭi taṉmēl]]
{2:92}__7+
{$} teṉ col viñcu amar vaṭacol ticai moḻi eḻil narampu eṭuttu
singing in any one of the languages such as tamiḻ, excellent vaṭacol and words borrowed from the twelve countries bordering the country where chaste tamiḻ is spoken, playing songs on the strings of yāḻ and viṇi.
tuñcu neñcu iruḷ nīṅkat toḻutu eḻu tol pukalūril
in ancient pukalūr where devotees wake up from sleep worshipping with joined hands in order to dispel the ignorance in the mind which has been staying there for a long time.
añcaṉam pitirntaṉaiya alkaṭal kaṭaiya aṉṟu eḻunta vañca nañcu aṇi kaṇṭar varttamāṉīccarattār
Civaṉ who adorned his neck with the cruel poison which arose in the moving ocean which is like the collyrium scattered everywhere when it was churned, is in varttamāṉiccaram.
{2:92}__8+
{$} cāma vētam ōr kītam ōti attacamukaṉ paravum nāmatēyam (atu) uṭaiyār naṉku uṇarntu aṭikaḷ eṉṟu ētta
that ten-faced īravaṇaṉ realising well that Civaṉ was the god and prised him by chanting the cāmavētam which is musical, and has a name which is prised by him.
kāmatēvaṉai vēvak kaṉal eri koḷuviya kaṇṇār
has a frontal eye by which he set scorching fire to burn Kāmatēvaṉ (cupid).
vāma tēvar
has the appellation of vāmatēvar.
taṇpukalūr varttamāṉīccarattār
is in varttamāṉiccaram in cool pukalūr.
{2:92}__9+
{$} cīr aṇaṅku uṟaniṉṟa ceruvuṟu ticai mukaṉōṭu nāraṇaṉ karuttu aḻiya nakai ceyta caṭai muṭi nampar
Civaṉ who has a caṭi coiled into a crown and smiled to destroy the self-esteem of nāraṇaṉ along with Piramaṉ who has four faces each one for each quarter and who had divinity in him and quarrelled with nāraṇaṉ that he was superior to him;
ār aṇaṅku uṟum umaiyai añcuvittu aruḷutal poruṭṭāl
with the purpose of granting his grace and cusing fright to Umi who had rare beauty.
vāraṇattu uri pōrttār varttamāṉīc carattār
Civaṉ who coverd himself with the skin of an elephant is (pukalūr) in varttamāṉiccaram.
{2:92}__10+
{$} kaiyil uṇṭu uḻalvārum
camaṇar who wander eating their food using their hands as plates.
kamaḻ tuvar āṭaiyiṉāl tam meyyaip pōrttu uḻal vārum
and buddhists who wander covering their bodies with a robe soaked in myrthe die whose odour spreads everywhere.
uraippaṉa mey eṉa virumpēl
(People of this world!) Do not wish to listen to what they talk as truth.
ceyyil vāḷaikaḷōṭu ceṅkayal kutikoḷum pukalūr
in pukalūr where in the fields scabbard fish and red carp fish jump together.
mai koḷ kaṇṭattu em perumāṉ varttamāṉīccarattār
our Lord who has a black neck is in varttāmāṉiccaram.
{2:92}__11+
{$} poṅku taṇ puṉal cūḻntu pōtu aṇi poḻil pukalūril
in pukalur which has gardens made beautiful by flowers and surrounded by foaming and raging cool water.
maṅkul māmati tavaḻum varttamāṉīccarattārai
on Civaṉ in varttamāṉīccaram on which the big moon which dispels darkness seems to crawl.
[[maṅkul darkness;
maṅkul ñāyiṟṟu (paripāṭal, 13-1); maṅkul may also mean cloud]]
taṅku cīr tikaḻ ñāṉacampantaṉ taṇtamiḻ pattum eṅkum ētta vallārkaḷ
those who are capable of prising Civaṉ everywhere with the ten pleasant verses composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ of great frame which stays with hum.
eytuvar imaiyavar ulaku
will reach the heaven of the immortals who do not wink.

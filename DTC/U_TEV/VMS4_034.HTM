{4:34}__1+
{$} tēraiyum mēl kaṭāvi
driving the car over Kayilai
tiṇṇamāt teḻittu nōkki
looking at it with vigour uttering threats.
āraiyum mēl uṇarā āṇmaiyāl mikkāṉtaṉṉai
Irāvaṇaṉ who was extremely proud of his strength which did not think anyone to be superior to him.
pāraiyum viṇṇum añcap paranta tōḷ muṭi aṭarttu
pressing down the heads and shoulders which were extensive and made the people of this world and the celestials in heaven dread him.
kārikai! añcal' eṉpār kali maṟaikkāṭaṉār (ē)
the Lord in Maṟaikkāṭu of great bustle will assuage Umai saying, Lady! do not fear'
{4:34}__2+
{$} mukki muṉ vekuṇṭu eṭutta muṭicaṭai arakkarkōṉai (1) nakku iruntu ūṉṟi
fixing the toe without any sign of perturbation and laughing at the King of the arakkar who had ten heads, and who lifted the mountain with anger and with great strain like a woman in travail.
ceṉṉi nāḷmati vaitta entai
our father who wore on his head the fresh crescent.
akku, aravu, āmai, pūṇṭa aḻakaṉār
and the beautiful god who wore as ornaments bones, cobras, and the shell of a tortoise.
karuttiṉālē
with intention.
tekku nīrt tiraikaḷ mōtum tirumaṟaikkāṭaṉār (ē)
is the Lord in the sacred Maṟaikkāṭu where the waves that take in water dash against the shore.
[[tēkku has become tekku for the sake of rhyme]]
[[syntactical link: entai, aḻakaṉār, ūṉṟik kāṭaṉār]]
[[Variant reading: 1.ikkiruntu]]
{4:34}__3+
{$} mikap paruttu
being very stout
ulāva mikkāṉ
and extremely wandering.
nakku
with a derisive laughter
oru tēr kaṭāvi
driving a chariot
akappaṭuttu' eṉṟu
ordering the driver Bring it under your control'.
tāṉum (1) āṇmaiyāl mikku arakkaṉ
the arakkaṉ too being proud of his valour.
ukaittu eṭuttāṉ malaiyai
lifted the mountain, Kayilai jumping up.
āṅkē ūṉṟalum
as soon as Civaṉ fixed his toe then itself.
avaṉai nakaippaṭuttu aruḷiṉāṉ ūr nāṉmaṟaikkāṭu (tāṉ, ē)
Maṟaikkāṭu which was worshipped by the four Vētams is the place of Civaṉ who laughed at his foolishness and then granted his grace.
[[Variant reading: 1. vāṇmaiyāl vallarakkaṉ]]
{4:34}__4+
{$} antaram tēr kaṭāvi
driving the car in the space.
yār ivaṉ?' eṉṟu colli
and saying, who is this person?
untiṉāṉ māmalaiyai
Irāvaṇaṉ who pushed out the great mountain Kayilai.
ūṉṟalum
when Civaṉ fixed firmly his toe
oḷ arakkaṉ
the renowned arakkaṉ.
pantam ām talaikaḷpattum vāykaḷviṭṭu alaṟi vīḻa
to fall roaring opening his ten months which were on his head which were fixed to his body.
cintaṉai ceytuviṭṭār tiru maṟaikkāṭaṉār (ē)
the Lord in Tiru Maṟaikkāṭu thought as such.
{4:34}__5+
{$} taṭukkavum tāṅka (um) oṇṇāt taṉ valiuṭaiyaṉ āki
having strength that could not hindered and opposed by anybody.
kaṭukka ōr tēr kaṭāvi
driving a chariot with speed.
kai irupatukaḷālum (irupatu kaikaḷālum)
with all the twenty hands.
eṭuppaṉ nāṉ'
saying I shall lift this.
eṉṉa paṇṭam!'
what a small thing this is!
eṉṟu eṭuttāṉai ēṅki aṭukkavē vallaṉ ūr ām aṇi maṟaikkāṭu (tāṉ, ē)
beautiful Maṟaikkāṭu is the place of the Lord who is capable of pressing down the arakkaṉ who lifted the mountain to sob.
{4:34}__6+
{$} nāḷ muṭikkiṉṟa cīrāṉ (1) naṭuṅkiyē mītu pōkāṉ
the sun who ends the daytime never passed over above Ilaṅkai trembling with fear.
kōṉ piṭittu ārtta kaiyāṉ
Irāvaṇaṉ who caught hold of the planets and bound them in his hand.
koṭiyaṉ, (2) māvaliyaṉ eṉṟu
and a cruel person thinking of himself as a very strong person.
nīḷmuṭic caṭaiyar cērum nīḷvarai eṭukkal uṟṟāṉ tōḷmuṭi neriya vaittār tolmaṟaikkāṭaṉār (ē)
the Lord in ancient Maṟaikkāṭu placed his toe to crush the heads and shoulders of Irāvaṇaṉ who lifted the high mountain in which Civaṉ who has a long caṭai coiled into a crown stays.
[[Not only did the sun pass over Ilaṅkai but also the moon;
cf. irucuṭar mītu ōṭā ilaṅkaik kōṉai (nāvukkaracar tirumaḻapāḷi (1) 10);
irucuṭar iyaṅkāp perumūtu ilaṅkai, neṭuntōḷ irāmaṉ kaṭanta ñāṉṟai (puṟattirattu, 1333);
irucuṭar mītiṉil iyaṅkā mummatiḷ ilaṅkai (tirumankai āḻvar, tiruveḻukkuṟṟirukkai; 3-4)]]
[[Irāvaṇaṉ won a victory over the planets;
caṉiyum veḷḷiyum tiṅkaḷum ñāyiṟum muṉivaṉāy muṭipattu uṭaiyāṉ (nāvukkaracar, tiruviṉṉampar (3) 10)]]
[[Variant reading: 1.naṭuṅkiyō 2. māvaliyaṉaṉṟu]]
{4:34}__7+
{$} pattuvāy iraṭṭik kaikaḷ uṭaiyaṉ
Irāvaṇaṉ who had ten mouths and twenty hands.
māvaliyaṉ eṉṟu
thinking about himself I am a strong person.
vāypotti
closing the mouth with the finger
[[without making any noise is the idea]]
tīmai ceyta poruvali arakkarkōṉaik katti vāy kataṟa aṉṟu kālviral ūṉṟiyiṭṭār, muttu vāy tiraikaḷ mōtum mutu maṟaikkāṭaṉār (ē)
the Lord in ancient Maṟaikkāṭu where the waves which carry pearls dash against the shore, definitely fixed his toe on that day to make the King of the arakkar who committed sinful acts and had strength to fight with others, roar and cry with gaping mouths.
{4:34}__8+
{$} (1) pakkam mēviṭṭa kaiyāṉ pāṅku ilā matiyaṉ āki
Irāvaṇaṉ having hands that hang like branches on the sides and having no discerning intellect.
māmalaikkīḻp pukkaṉaṉ pōtumāṟu aṟiyamāṭṭāṉ
went under the big mountain and did not know how to come out of it.
mikka māmatikaḷ keṭṭu vīramum iḻantavāṟē
as he lost his valour and lost his great intellect, completely.
nāṉmaṟaik kāṭaṉār(ē) pūtam ellām nakkaṉa
the pūtams of the Lord in Maṟaikkāṭu worshipped by the four Vētam-s laughed derisively at him.
[[Variant reading: 1 pakkamēliṭṭa kaiyāṉ]]
{4:34}__9+
{$} nāṇ añcu kaiyaṉ āki
having hands that the bow string was afraid of.
nalmuṭi pattiṉōṭu
with his ten crowns of exquisite workmanship.
pāṇ añcumuṉ iḻantu
having lost his singing before he has frightened.
pāṅkuilāmatiyaṉ āki
having no discerning mind.
nīṇ añcu tāṉ uṇarā
without realising the greatness of the mantram of five letters which is to be chanted for a long time.
niṉṟu eṭuttāṉai aṉṟu ēṇ añcu kaikaḷ ceytār, eḻil maṟaikkāṭaṉār (ē)
the Lord in beautiful Maṟaikkāṭu made the hands to be afraid of Civaṉ's valour.

{4:34}__10+
{$} kaṅkainīr caṭaiyuḷ vaikkak kāṇṭalum, maṅkai ūṭa
where Umai the young lady was sulky as soon as she saw, Civaṉ placing the water of Kaṅkai inside the caṭai.
teṉkaiyāṉ
Irāvaṇaṉ who ruled in the south.
tēr kaṭāvi
driving his chariot.
ceṉṟu malaiyai eṭuttāṉ
went and lifted the mountain.
(1) muṉkai mā narampu veṭṭi muṉ irukku icaikaḷ pāṭa

ammaivāḷ aruḷiṉāṉ ūr aṇi maṟaikkāṭu (tāṉ, ē)
the place of Civaṉ who granted a beautiful small sword is beautiful Maṟaikkāṭu;

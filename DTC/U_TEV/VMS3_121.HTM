{3:121}__1+
{$} ulaku iṭaṟiṉār kūṟṟai; poṭi ceytār matilai' ivai colli eḻuntu ētta
the people of this world prise walking from sleep saying such things as ``Civaṉ kicked the god of death and reduced to dust the forts''
kaṭaṟiṉār āvar
he is in the forest;
kāṟṟu uḷār āvar
he is in the air
kātalittu uṟai taru kōyil
the temple where he dwells with great attachment
(1) koṭiṟaṉār
god who is like the pincers and who does not let down his devotees
yātum kuṟaivu ilār
he is not deficient in any thing
tām pōy
having gone
kōvaṇam koṇṭu kūttāṭum (2) paṭiṟaṉār pōlum pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacupati yār
Pacupatiyar who is in Pantaṇinallūr is perhaps the cruel person who dances wearing a loin-cloth
[[Pacupati is the name of Civaṉ in the temple]]
[[Variant reading: (1) koṭiṟiṉār (2)paṭiṟiṉār]]
{3:121}__2+
{$} kaḻiyuḷār eṉavum
Civaṉ is the back water,
kaṭaluḷār eṉavum
he is in the sea,
kāṭṭu uḷār eṉavum nāṭṭu uḷār eṉavum
he is in the forest and country side,
maṇ uḷār eṉavum viṇ uḷār eṉavum
he is in the earth and he is in the sky.
cuḻiyuḷār eṉavum
he is in the eddies of water
cuvaṭutām aṟiyār
as they do not know any means of ascertining his existence.
toṇaṭar vāy vantaṉa collum paḻi uḷār pōlum pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacu patiyār
perhaps our Pacupatiyār who is in Pantaṇinallūr has the blemish of what the devotees say whatever comes out of their mouth.
{3:121}__3+
{$} kāṭṭiṉār eṉavum
Civaṉ is in the forest
nāṭṭiṉār eṉavum
he is in the country-side
kaṭuntoḻil kālaṉaik kālāl vīṭṭiṉār eṉavum
he took away the life of the god of death whose work is cruel, by kicking him with the leg
cānta veṇṇīṟu pūci
besmearing himself the white holy ash as sandalpaste.
ōr veṇmati caṭaimēl cūṭṭiṉār eṉavum
and he wore on his caṭi a white crescent
[[cūṭṭiṉār- is used in the sense of cūṭiṉār; it is not the cusal form of cūṭiṉār]]
cuvaṭutām aṟiyār
the devotees not having any means of ascertining his existence
colluḷa collum nālvētap pāṭṭiṉār (pōlum) pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacupatiyār
our Pacupatiyār who is in Pantaṇinallūr has the four Vētam-s which are in the form of songs, and sing all the prises that he has.
{3:121}__4+
{$} murukiṉ ārpoḻil cūḻ ulakiṉār ētta
to be prised by the people of this world which has fragrant gardens
moytta palkaṇaṅkaḷiṉ tuyarkaṇṭu urukiṉār āki, uṟuti pōntu uḷḷam oṇmaiyāl
his heart having melted at the sight of the suffering of the groups that had thronged, with the knowledge determined in mind to help them
[[The meaning of the 2nd line is not clear]]
oḷi tikaḻ mēṉi karukiṉār ellām kaitoḻutu ētta
when the god whose shining bodies became black worshipped with joined hands.
kaṭaluḷ nañcai amutamā vāṅkip parukiṉār (pōlum) pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacupatiyār
our Pacupatiyār in Pantaṇinallūr drank the poison that rose in the ocean as nectar, taking it in his hands
{3:121}__5+
{$} poṉṉiṉ ār koṉṟai iruvaṭam kiṭantu poṟikiḷar pūṇanūl puraḷa
the sacred with shining spots is rolling over the chest on which two strands of garland made of koṉṟi flowers of golden colour are lying
miṉṉiṉ ār uruviṉ miḷirvatōr aravammēvu veṇṇīṟu meypūci
besmearing his body with white holy ash liked by him, and on that body which is as bright as lightning, there is a shining cobra.
tuṉṉiṉār nālvarkku aṟam amarntaruḷi
giving instruction in philosophy to four sages who came to him.
toṉmaiyār tōṟṟamum kēṭum paṉṉiṉār pōlum pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacupatiyār
our Pacupatiyār in Pantaṇinallūr spoke with great consideration about birth that comes from earliest times, and death.
{3:121}__6+
{$} oṇ poṉ (ār) aṉaiya aṇṇal vāḻka eṉavum
saying ``Let the god who is like the lustrous gold live long''
umaiyavaḷ kaṇavaṉ vāḻka eṉavum
and ``let the husband of Umayavaḷ live long!''
aṉpiṉār piriyār allum naṇpakalum aṭiyavar aṭiyiṇai toḻavē
when the devotees who have approached god do not leave him, and worship his feet night and day
naṇpiṉār ellām nallar eṉṟu ētta
and when all the affectionate devotees prise him as good god.
allavar tīyar eṉṟu ēttum paṇpiṉār pōlum pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacupatiyār
our Pacupatiyār who is in Pantaṇinallūr has the nature of being decried as a wicked person.
{3:121}__7+
{$} eṟṟiṉār ētum iṭaikoḷvār illai
without any good to themselves, having attacked,
irunilam vāṉ ulaku ellai teṟṟiṉār taṅkaḷ kāraṇamākac cerumalaintu
and fought a battle on account of those who destroyed the world and the boundaries of heaven
aṭiyiṇai cērvāṉ
and in order that they may reach his feet
muṟṟiṉār vāḻum mummatilvēva
to make the three forts in which the acurar hardened in their cruelty, lived
mū ilaic cūlamum maḻuvum paṟṟiṉār pōlum pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacupatiyār
our Pacupatiyār who is in Pantaṇinallūr holds in his hand a trident of three blades and a battle-axe
{3:121}__8+
{$} oli ceyta kuḻaliṉ
along with the music produced in a flute
muḻavam (atu) iyampa
when the muḻavu is also played upon
ōcaiyāl āṭal aṟāta kali ceytapūtam kaiyiṉāl iṭa
when the roaring pūtam which never ceases dancing along with those sounds, measure time with their hands
kāliṉāṟ pāytalum arakkaṉ vali koḷvar
god took away the strength of the arakkaṉ as soon as he pressed him down with his leg
puliyiṉ uri koḷvar
will dress in ten skin of a tiger
ēṉaivāḻvu naṉṟāṉum ōr talaiyil pali koḷvar pōlum pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacupatiyār
our Pacupatiyār who is in Pantaṇinallūr receives alms in a skull, though the opposite of that life is without any want

{$} cēṟṟiṉār poykait tāmaraiyāṉum
though Piramaṉ who is in the lotus which grows in the natural tank with mire
[[cēṟṟiṉār poykai is a generic epithet]]
ceṅkaṇmāl ivar iru kūṟāt tōṟṟiṉār
and Māl of reddish eyes came into being as two portions
tōṟṟat toṉmaiyai aṟiyār
they could not know the antiquity of Civaṉ assuming the form of a fire
(1) tuṇaimaiyum perumaiyum tammil cāṟṟiṉār
they spoke about the equality and greatness between them
cāṟṟi āṟṟalōm eṉṉa
when they sid ``we are not capable'' and after saying so
caraṇkoṭuttu
giving refuge to them
avar ceyta pāvam pāṟṟiṉār pōlum pantaṇai nallūr niṉṟa em pacupatiyār
our Pacupatiyār who is in Pantaṇinallūr destroyed the sins they had committed.

muṉtuṇai āyiṉāṉai
Civaṉ who is the companion from time immemorial
mūvarkkum mutalvaṉ taṉṉai
the origin of the trinity of Piramaṉ, Māl and Uruttiraṉ
coltuṇai āyiṉāṉai
who can be understood by the help of knowledge of ākamam - (who is the help to understand the correct meaning of vetams)
cōtiyai ātarittu
cherishing with love the light
uṟṟu uṇarntu uruti ūṟiuḷkacivu uṭaiyavarkku
for those whose hearts melt being immersed having realised him intimately,
nal tuṇai āvar (pōlum) naṉipaḷḷi aṭikaḷār
Civaṉ in naṉipaḷḷi will become a good companion and guide.
{4:70}__2+
{$}
pularntakāl pūvum nīrum koṇṭu aṭi pōṟṟa māṭṭā
without being able to worship the feet with water and flowers at daybreak ((māṭṭā - māṭṭāmal).
valam ceytu vāyil nūlāl vaṭṭaṇaip pantar ceyta cilantiyai araiyaṉ ākki
elevating the spider which constructed as round pantal with the saliva from its mouth, and going from left to right, as king (caṅkaṇṇāṉ)
cīrmaikaḷ aruḷavallār, nalam tikaḻ cōlai cūḻnta naṉipaḷḷi aṭikaḷār
Civaṉ in naṉipaḷḷi surrounded by beautiful gardens is capable of granting many kinds of fame.
{4:70}__3+
{$}
eṇpatum pattum āṟum eṉṉuḷē maṉṉiiruntu
staying within me permanently as the ninety six reals
kaṇ paḻakku oṉṟum iṉṟi
without the slightest of glow of kind feeling towards me
kalakka nāṉ alakkaḻintēṉ
I suffered as they confused my mind.
ceṇpakam, tikaḻum puṉṉai, ceḻuntiraḷ kuravam, vēṅkai naṇpu cey cōlai cūḻnta naṉipaḷḷi aṭikaḷārē
Civaṉ in naṉipaḷḷi surrounded by gardens where east indian kino tree, globular and fertile bottle-flower trees, bright mast wood trees and champak trees are close to each other! (aṭikaḷārē nominative of address.)

naṉipaḷḷi aṭikaḷār
Civaṉ in naṉipaḷḷi
paṇṇiṉ ār pāṭal āki
is songs that can be sung with heptatonic melody-type.
paḻattiṉil iratam āki
is the juice in fruits.
kaṇṇiṉ ār pārvai āki
is the power of vision in the eye
karuttoṭu kaṟpam āki
is the determination and ideas in the mind.
eṇṇiṉār eṇṇam āki
is the idea of thinkers
ēḻ ulaku aṉaittum āki
is all the seven worlds
naṇṇiṉur viṉaikaḷ tīrppār
will remove the karmams of those who approach him.

naṉipaḷḷi aṭikaḷār
Civaṉ in naṉipaḷḷi
iruḷtuñcu kālai mālai toṭarcciyai maṟantirātē
without forgetting the connection between god and souls both in the morning when the darkness departs, and in the evening.
añcu eḻuttu ōtiṉ nāḷum araṉ aṭikku aṉpu (atu) ākum
our love towards the feet of araṉ will increase day by day if we chant the mantiram consisting of five letters.
vañcaṉaip pāṟcōṟu ākki vaḻakku ilā amaṇar tanta nañcu amutu ākkuvittār
transformed by his grace the poison which the amaṇar without good manners mixed with food cooked in milk and gave me deceitfully, into nectar.
[[This is an auto-biographical element in nāvukkaracar's life; this is mentioned in detil in perriya purāṇam; tirunāvukkaracu nāyanār purāṇa, verses, 102-107]]

naṉi paḷḷi aṭikaḷār
Civaṉ in naṉipaḷḷi
cemmalark kamalattōṉum tirumuṭi kāṇamāṭṭāṉ,
Even the Piramaṉ who is seated in the red lotus flower was not able to find out the head.
āḻiyāṉ ammalarppātam kāṇpāṉ akaḻntum kāṇāṉ
Māl who holds a discus was not able to find the feet which are like beautiful flowers though he went digging the earth to find them.
niṉ malaṉ eṉṟu aṅku ēttum niṉaippiṉai aruḷināḷum nam malam aṟuppar (pōlum)
will rend a sunder our impurities of the soul, granting the idea of thinking of him as the spotless god.

aravattāl varaiyaic cuṟṟi
having wound a serpent round the mountin (which was the churning rod)
amararōṭu acurar kūṭi
the acurar joining with the immortals.
aravittuk kaṭaiya
churned with great noise.
tōṉṟum ālanañcu amutā uṇṭār
consumed the poison by name ālam that rose in the ocean, as nectar.
viravittam aṭiyar āki vīṭu ilāt toṇṭar tammai narakattil vīḻa oṭṭār naṉipaḷḷi aṭikaḷār
Civaṉ in naṉipaḷḷi will not tolerate his devotees who do not leave him, having become his slaves cultivating intimacy with Civaṉ, to fall into hell.

pattum ōr iraṭṭi tōḷāṉ pārittu malai eṭukka
when the arakkaṉ of twenty shoulders spread them and lifted the mountin.
pattum ōr iraṭṭi tōḷkaḷ paṭaruṭampu aṭara ūṉṟi
pressed with his toe to crush the body from which the shoulders spread
pattuvāy kītam pāṭap parintu avaṟku aruḷ koṭuttār pattar tām paravi ēttum naṉipaḷḷip paramaṉārē
the supreme god in naṉipaḷḷi who is always prised by the pious devotees, granted his grace, becoming affectionate when he sang songs with all the ten months.

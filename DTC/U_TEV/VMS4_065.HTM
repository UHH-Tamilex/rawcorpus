{4:65}__1+
{$} tōṭu ulām malarkaḷ tūvit toḻutu eḻu mārkkaṇṭēyaṉvīṭum nāḷ aṇukiṟṟu eṉṟu
thinking that the day on which Mārkkaṇṭēyaṉ who rose after worshipping scattering flowers of broad petals, should die has approached.
meykoḷvāṉ vantakālaṉ
the god of death who came to take away the life after separating it from the body.
pāṭu celalum
as he came near Mārkkaṇṭēyaṉ
añci
being afraid of him (the god of death)
pātamē caraṇam' eṉṉa
when he said Your feet are my refuge.
cāykkāṭu mēviṉār kālaṉ māḷac cāṭiṉār
the Lord in Cāykkātu destroyed Kālaṉ (god of death) to die.
{4:65}__2+
{$} vāṉavar acurarōṭu
the tēvar joining with acurar
vaṭam keḻumalai mattāka
using the mountain (round which the serpent vācuki was wound) as a chuming staff.
kaṭaintiṭa eḻunta nañcam kaṇṭu
on seeing the poison which rose when the ocean was churned.
paltēvar añci
many tēvars being afraid of it.
aṭaintu num caraṇam eṉṉa
and approaching the Lord, said we come to you for refuge.
aruḷ peritu uṭaiyar āki
having abundant grace.
cāykkāṭu mēviṉār taṭaṅkaṭal nañcam uṇṭār
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu drank the poison of the big (milky) ocean.
{4:65}__3+
{$} araṇ ilā veḷiya nāval aruniḻalāka
when the white jaumoon plum which had no protecting wall could not afford shade.
īcaṉ
to the Lord.
varaṇ iyalāki
as a protection that surrounded it.
taṉ vāy nūliṉāl pantarceyya
when the spider constructed pantal (web) with the thread of its saliva.
muraṇ ilāc cilanti taṉṉai muṭiyuṭai maṉṉaṉ ākki
having made the spider which was without any hatred, a king fit to wear a crown
[[This refers to Kōcceṅkaṭ Cōḷaṉ, who was a spider in the previous birth and which constructed a web over the lingam at Tiruvāṉaikka. The Lord made him to be born in the Cōḻa line. This story is mentioned in many places in Tēvāram.]]
cāykkāṭu mēviṉār taraṇi āḷavaittār
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu made him rule over the world.
{4:65}__4+
{$} arum peruñ cilaikkai vēṭaṉāy
assuming the disguise of a hunter who has a big and rare bow.
pārttaṟku aṉṟu uramperitu uṭaimaikāṭṭi
and showing on that day his possession of excessive strength to Pārttaṉ
[[Pārttaṉ - Arjuṉaṉ]]
oḷ amar ceytu mīṇṭē
giving a fair battle and after that,
varam perituṭaiyaṉākki
having made Pārttaṉ recipient of many boons.
vāḷ amar mukattil maṉṉum caram poli tūṇi īntār cāykkāṭu mēviṉār
The Lord in Cāykkātu presented him with a quiver shining with arrows, which will stay securely in the bright battle-field.
{4:65}__5+
{$} intiraṉ piramaṉ aṅki eṇvakai vacukkaḷōṭu mantiram maṟaiyatu ōti vāṉavar vaṇaṅki vāḻtti
the residents of the celestial world bowed to the Lord and praised him chanting the maṟai (maṟai) consisting of mantiram (mantram-s) joining with the eight vacu-s, god of fire, Piramaṉ (Brahma) and Intiraṉ, the ruler of the celestial world.
tantiram aṟiyāttakkaṉ vēḷviyaittakartta ñāṉṟu
on that day when the Lord destroyed the sacrifice of Takkaṉ who did not know the proper procedure.
cantiraṟkum aruḷceytār cāykkāṭu mēviṉārē
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu bestowed his grace upon the moon also.
[[Though the moon was reduced by the feet of the Lord, he wore it on his head as an ornament.]]
[[The um in ceytārum has been added to cantiraṉ]]
{4:65}__6+
{$} āmali pālum neyyum āṭṭi arccaṉaikaḷ ceytu
Having bathed the god with ghee and milk which can be had abundantly from cow, and having performed worship of many kinds with flowers.
pūmali koṉṟai cūṭṭa
to adorn on the head the flowers of koṉṟai tree (with abundant bunches of flowers)
poṟātataṉtātai tāḷaik kūr maḻu oṉṟāl ōcca
to raise the sharp battle-axe in order to amputate the feet of his father who did not exercise for-bearance.
kuḷircaṭaikkoṉṟai mālaittāmam naṟcaṇṭikku īntār cāykkāṭu mēviṉār
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu presented the good anti with the chaplet of bright koṉṟai which he was wearing on his cool matted locks of hair.
[[tāmam! brightness]]
[[āṭṭutal, arccaṉaikaḷ ceytal, cūṭṭutal, ōccutal are the acts of Antēcar]]
[[porumai is the action of his father]]
[[The story of Antēcar is mentioned in many places in Tēvāram by both Ñāṉacampantar and Appar. It is found in Tiruppallāṇtu, a later work:
tātaiyait tāḷ aṟavīciya caṇṭikku ivvaṇṭat toṭum uṭaṉē, pūtalattōrum vaṇaṅkap poṟ kōyilum pōṉakamum aruḷic, cōti maṇimuṭittāmamum nāmamum toṇṭarkku nāyakamum, pātakattukkupparicu vaittāṉukkē pallāṇṭu kūṟutumē]]
[[Variant reading: koṉṟai mālaittākumaṉattaṇṭikkīntār]]
{4:65}__7+
{$} mai aṟu maṉattaṉ āya pakīrataṉ varaṅkaḷvēṇṭa
as Pakīrataṉ who had a blemishless mind supplicated for boons.
aiyam il amarar ētta
and as the immortals who had no doubt about god coming to their help, praised the Lord.
āyira mukamatu āki
having thousand forms.
vaiyakam neḷiyap pāyvāṉ vantu iḻi kaṅkai eṉṉum taiyalaic caṭaiyil ēṟṟār cāykkāṭu mēviṉār
the Lord in Cāykkkāṭu received on his matted locks of hair Kaṅkai who descended in the form of a lady from heaven gushing out, to topple the world
[[As Kaṅkai is feminine gender in sanskrit, it is spoken of as a taiyal]]
{4:65}__8+
{$} kuvapperuntaṭakkai vēṭaṉ
the hunter (Tiṇṇaṉ) who had round and very big hands.
koṭuñcilai iṟaiccippāram
(as he was carrying) the bent bow and a heavy weight of flesh (in his hands)
tuvarp peruñ ceruppāl nīkki
after removing the flowers and leaves (placed the previous day) with the big and red coloured leather sandals.
tūya vāykkalacam āṭṭa
to bathe god with the water in the pure mouth (which he used as a pot).
uvapperuṅ kuruti cōra
the god made to flow blood profusely (from the eye) with gladness
[[uva - uvantu;
cf. varippuṉaipantu (Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai, 68)]]
oru kaṇai iṭantu aṅku appa
engrafted one eye after scooping it out
[[kaṇai iṭantu kaṇṇai iṭantu, kaṇaiyāl iṭantu;
(kaṇai arrow)
cāykkāṭu mēviṉār tavap peruntēvu ceytār
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu made him a very great god (to be worshipped by others)
[[It is mentioned in Periyapurāṇam that Ñāṉacampantar worshipped Kaṇṇappar after worshipping the Lord in kaḷatti]]
[[Variant reading: avap peruṅkuruti]]
{4:65}__9+
{$} nakku ulām malar paṉṉūṟu koṇṭu
Having several hundreds of well-blossomed (lotus) flowers from which fragrance spreads.
naṉ ñāṉattōṭu
with the wisdom of realising Civaṉ
mikka pūcaṉaikaḷ ceyvāṉ
(Māl) who performed many different kinds of pūcaṉai.
meṉ malar oṉṟu kāṇātu
on seeing that one soft flower was missing.
eṉ malarkkaṇ okkum eṉṟu
having decided `My eye which resembles the lotus will supply the deficiency.
oru kaṇai iṭantu aṅku appavum
as soon as he scooped out one eye and performed pūcaṉai without any deficiency.
cāykkāṭu mēviṉār cakkaram koṭuppar
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu granted one discus (to Māl)
[[This incident happened in Tiruvīḻimiḻalai:
tarai yoṭu tivitalam nalitaru takutiṟal uṟucalataraṉatu, varaiyaṉatalaivicaiyoṭu varu tikiriyai aripeṟa aruḷiṉaṉ
(Campantar, Tiruvīḻimiḻalai, tiruvirākam, 2)
taruppam miku calantaraṉ taṉ uṭal taṭinta cakkarattai vēṇṭi īṇṭu, viruppoṭu mālvaḻipāṭu ceyya iḻi vimāṉañ cēr miḻilaiyāmē
(paṇ mēkarākakkuṟiñci-8);
taṉtavam periya calantaraṉ uṭalam taṭintacakkarameṉakkaruḷeṉ, naṉṟarivaḻipaṭṭiḻicciya vimāṉat tiṟaiyavaṉ
(paṇ - puṟanīrmai - 7)
ēṟuṭaṉ ēḻ aṭar/ttāṉ eṇṇi āyiram pūkkoṇṭu, āṟuṭaic caṭaiyiṉāṉai arccittāṉ aṭiyiṇaikkīḻ, vēṟumōr pūkkuṟaiya meymmalarkkaṇṇai miṇṭak kūṟumōr āḻi īntārkuṟukkai vīraṭṭaṉārē
(appar, kuṟukkai vīrattam (1) 5)
alar āyiram tantu vantittu māl āyiram karattāl alar ārkaḻal vaḻi pāṭu ceytāṟku aḷavil oḷikaḷ, alarāvirukkum paṭai koruttōṉ
(kōvaiyār, 180)]]
{4:65}__10+
{$} arakkaṉ puyaṅkam aiññāṉkum pattum āya koṇṭu ōṭi
the arakkan having twenty shoulders and ten heads, ran.
civaṉ tirumalaiyaip pērkka
and tried to remove the tirumalai (kailācam) from its place
[[Kailāśam is spoken of as tirumalai in Saiva literature]]
tirumalark kuḻali añca
when the beautiful lady with tresses of hair adorned with flowers, became frightened.
viyaṉ peṟa eyti vīḻa
to fall down changing his idea of removing the mountain.
viral ciṟitu ūṉṟi
pressed his toe a little
mīṇṭu
and afterwards.
cayampeṟa nāmam īntār cāykkāṭu mēviṉār
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu gave him a name (Iśvaraṉ) to be victorious.

puyaṅkamaiñ ñāṉkum pattum
it will mean respectively twenty shoulders and ten heads;
kam head]]

{1:110}__1+
{$} maruntu avaṉ
Civaṉ is the nectar.
vāṉavarkkum tāṉavarkkum peruntakai
he is the noble-minded god to the celestials and the acurar.
piṟaviṉoṭu iṟavum āṉāṉ
He is both birth and death
[[piṟavu-piṟappu;
viḻukkuṭippiṟavu aritu,
vēḻappiṟavum viḻai takkatuveṉ (peruṅkati, 3-6-115, 5-3-86);
piṟavu āḻik karaikaṇṭārē (villipāratam Kāṇṇaṉ tūtuccarukam, 1)
iṟavu-iṟappu.]]
aruntavamuṉivarōṭu āl niḻaṟkīḻ iruntavaṉ vaḷanakar iṭaimarutē
iṭimarutu is the fertile city of Civaṉ who sat under the shade of the banyan tree along with the sages of severe penance.
{1:110}__2+
{$} tōṟṟavaṉ
Civaṉ granted birth by his grace.
kēṭavaṉ
he destroyed everything.
tuṇaimulaiyāḷ kūṟṟavaṉ
has a half of a lady of breasts which can be compared to each other.
kolpulittōl acaitta nīṟṟavaṉ
he smears himself with the holy ash and ties a skin of a tiger capable of killing. (kolpuli: this epithet denotes its nature when it was alive).
niṟaipuṉal nīr caṭaimēl ēṟṟavaṉ vaḷanakar iṭaimarutē
iṭimarutu is the fertile city of Civaṉ who received on his long caṭi the floods of the Kaṅki.
{1:110}__3+
{$} paṭaiuṭai maḻuviṉaṉ
Civaṉ has a weapon of a battle-axe.
pālveṇṇīṟṟaṉ
smears himself with the holy ash as white as milk.
naṭai navil ēṟṟiṉāṉ
has a bull that practises walking.
ñālam ellām uṭai talai iṭu palikoṇṭu uḻalvāṉ
wanders throughout the world receiving alms placed in a broken skull.
iṭaimarutu iṉitu uṟai emiṟai
he is our god who dwells happily in iṭimarutu.
{1:110}__4+
{$} paṇaimulai umai orupaṅkaṉ
Civaṉ who has on his half Umi of big breasts.
oṉṉār tuṇai matiḷ mūṉṟaiyum cuṭaril mūḻkakkaṇai turantu
having shot an arrow to make the three forts which were helpful to perpet ate all atrocities and belonging to enemies, to be engulfed by flames.
aṭutiṟal kālaṟ ceṟṟa iṇai ili vaḷanakar iṭaimarutē
iṭimarutu is the fertile city of Civaṉ to who no one can be compared, and who destroyed the god of death who has the power to take away the lives.
{1:110}__5+
{$} poḻilavaṉ; puyalavaṉ; puyal iyakkum toḻilavaṉ
Civaṉ is the garden; is the cloud; is the one who does the work of cusing them to move.
tuyaravaṉ
he is sufferings.
tuyar akaṟṟum kaḻalavaṉ
has feet which remove the sufferings.
kariuri pōr/ttu ukanta eḻilavaṉ vaḷanakar iṭaimarutē
iṭimarutu is the fertile city of the beautiful god who is eminent by covering himself with the skin of an elephant.
{1:110}__6+
{$} niṟaiyavaṉ
Civaṉ is the moral firmness.
puṉaloṭu matiyam vaitta poṟaiyavaṉ
has the load of crescent and water (of the Kaṅki)
pukaḻ avaṉ
is fame itself.
pukaḻaniṉṟa maṟaiyavaṉ
is the person who revealed the vetams to be prised by all.
maṟikaṭal nañcai uṇṭa iṟaiyavaṉ vaḷanakar iṭaimarutē
iṭimarutu is the fertile city of the master who consumed the poison that rose in the ocean of rolling waves.
{1:110}__7+
{$} naṉivaḷarmatiyoṭu nākam vaitta paṉimalarkkoṉṟai ampaṭarcaṭaiyaṉ
Civaṉ has a spreading caṭi wearing cool koṉṟi flowers, on whih are placed a cobra along with a crescent much increasing in size.
muṉivaroṭu amararkaḷ muṟai vaṇaṅka iṉitu uṟai vaḷanakar iṭaimarutē
iṭimarutu is the fertile city where he dwells happily to be worshipped by sages and the immortals when their turn comes.
{1:110}__8+
{$} tarukkiṉa arakkaṉa tāḷum tōḷum nerittavaṉ
Civaṉ pressed the feet and shoulders of the proud, arakkaṉ, (arakkaṉ=arakkaṉa +a; a is the plural case termination as it is followed by tāḷ and tōḷ)
neṭuṅkai mā matakari aṉṟu urittavaṉ
flayed the big rutting elephant having a long trunk, in the distant past.
oṉṉalar puraṅkaḷ mūṉṟum erittavaṉ vaḷanakar iṭaimarutē
iṭimarutu is the fertile city of Civaṉ who burnt the three cities of the enemies.
{1:110}__9+
{$} periyavaṉ
Civaṉ has greatness.
peṇṇiṉoṭu āṇum āṉāṉ
he has both female and male halves.
variaravu aṇai maṟikaṭal tuyiṉṟa kariyavaṉ alaravaṉ kāṇpu ākiya eriyavaṉ
he look the form of fire which could not be seen by Piramaṉ seated in the (lotus) flower, and the black Māl who sleeps on the bed of a serpent having lines, in the ocean of rolling waves.
{1:110}__10+
{$} cintai il (1) camaṇoṭu tērarcoṉṉa puntiil uraiyavai poruḷ koḷātē
Do not take into your minds the senseless words as doctrines which are told by tēvar (buddhists) and toughtless camaṇar; (people of this world!)
antaṇar ottiṉoṭu aravam ōvā entaitaṉ vaḷanakar iṭaimarutē
iṭimarutu is the fertile city of our father where the sound of devotees prising him and the reciting of the Vētam-s by brahmins, never cease.
[[Variant reading: (1) amaṇoṭu]]
{1:110}__11+
{$} ilai mali poḻil iṭaimarutu iṟaiyai
on the god who is in iṭimarutu which has gardens which has leaves in abundance.
nalammiku ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa palam mikutamiḻ ivai pattum vallār
those who are able to recite these ten tamiḻ verses which confer benefit on them and composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ of exceedingly good qualities.
ulaku uṟupukaḻiṉoṭu ōṅkuvarē
they will rise high in the esteem of the people attining fame in this world.

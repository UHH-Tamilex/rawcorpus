{5:66}__1+
{$}
ōtam ār kaṭaliṉ viṭam uṇṭavar
Civaṉ drank the poison that rose in the ocean of tides.
pūta nāyakaṉ
the master of pūtam-s
[[pūtam may also mean living beings.]]
poṟkayilaikku iṟai
the Lord of beautiful Kayili.
mātu or pākattaṉ
has a lady on one half.
valañcuḻi īcaṉaip pātam ēttap paṟaiyum nam pāvamē
our sins will decrease gradually if we prise the feet of the god in Valañcuḻi.
{5:66}__2+
{$}
kayilai nātaṉ
the Lord in Kayili.
kaṟuttavar muppuram eyilkaḷ tī eḻa velvala vittakaṉ
the able god who can gin victory by setting ablaze the walls of fortification and the three cities of enemies.
[[When we consider the alliteration the reading should be vēvalavittakaṉ which when split into, will be ēvala vittakaṉ the able person who can discharge an arrow.]]
mayilkaḷ ālum valañcuḻi īcaṉaip payil kilār cilar pāvit toḻumparē
some sinful base persons will not practise uttering the name of the Lord in valañcuḻi where peacocks dance out of joy.
[[toḻumpar base persons;
toṇṭu pūṇṭu amutam uṇṉāt toḻumpar cōṟu ukakku māṟē (tirumāli, 5)]]
{5:66}__3+
{$}
iḷaiya kālam emmāṉai aṭaikilāttuḷaiyilāc cevit toṇṭarkāḷ
devotees who did not reach our Lord in young age and who are deaf to good advice!
num uṭal vaḷaiyum kālam
when your bodies become bent low due to old age.
valañcuḻi īcaṉaik kaḷai kaṇākak karuti uymmiṉē
save yourselves thinking of the Lord in valañcuḻi as your support.
{5:66}__4+
{$}
naṟai koḷ pūm puṉal koṇṭu eḻu māṇikkāy
to protect the bachelor (mārkaṇṭēyaṉ) who started to worship god with aromatic water.
kuṟaivu ilāk koṭuṅ kūṟṟu utaittiṭṭavaṉ
Civaṉ who definitely kicked the god of death who is never decreasing in his cruelty.
maṟaikoḷ nāvaṉ
recites the vētam with his tongue.
valañcuḻi mēviya iṟaivaṉai iṉi eṉṟu (kol) kāṇpatē
when am I to see Civaṉ dwelling in valañcuḻī, in this birth (kol expletive)
{5:66}__5+
{$}
viṇṭavar puram mūṉṟum eri koḷat tiṇ tiṟal cilaiyāl eri ceytavaṉ
Civaṉ burnt the three cities of the enemies to be consumed by fire by a very strong bow.
vaṇṭu paṇ muralum taṇ valañcuḻi aṇṭaṉukku aṭimait tiṟattu āvaṉē
I would become a bonded slave in the matter of doing service to Civaṉ in cool valañcuḻī where the bees hum like melody-types.
{5:66}__6+
{$}
paṭam koḷ pām poṭu pāl matiyam caṭai aṭaṅka vāḻavallāṉ umpar tampirāṉ
the master of the celestials, and the god who controlled and made the cobra with a hood and white crescent live side by side.
maṭantai pākaṉ
who has a lady as a half.
valañcuḻiyāṉ
and who dwells in valañcuḻi.
aṭi aṭaintavarkku aṭimait tiṟattu āvaṉē
I shall willingly become a bonded slave to those devotees who have approached the feet of god.
{5:66}__7+
{$}
nāk koṇṭu paravum aṭiyār viṉai pōkkavalla puricaṭaip puṇṇiyaṉ
Civaṉ the embodiment of all virtues having a twisted caṭi who is capable of removing the Karmams of devotees who can prise him with their power of speech.
māk koḷ cōlai valañcuḻi īcaṉ taṉ ēkkoḷap puram mūṉṟu eriyāṉavē
the three cities become engulfed by fire when Civaṉ in valañcuḻi which has mango-groves, fixed his arrow in the bow.
{5:66}__8+
{$}
tēṭuvār, piramaṉ tirumālavar, āṭu pātam avarum aṟikilār
Piramaṉ and tirumāl search his rised foot during dance; even they do not know them (feet).
māṭa vīti valañcuḻi vāṇaṉait tēṭuvāṉ uṟukiṉṟatu eṉ cintaiyē
my mind is trying to search the god who dwells valañcuḻi which has streets with mansions. (When the great gods, Piramaṉ and Māl are not able to know his feet what a pity my mind is trying to search for him!)
{5:66}__9+
{$}
kaṇ paṉikkum
tears roll down her eyes.
kai kūppum
she joins her hands in worship.
kaṇ mūṉṟuṭai naṇpaṉukku eṉai nāṉ koṭuppēṉ eṉum
she says I shall offer myself to the friend who has three eyes.
vaṇ poṉṉit teṉ valañcuḻi mēviya paṇpaṉ ip poṉaic ceyta paricu itē
this is the nature that the god who is the embodiment of all good qualities and who is on the southern bank of the river poṉṉi of fertility, has done to my talivi who is as valuable to me as gold. (These are the words of the friend).

ilaṅkai vēntaṉ irupatu tōḷ iṟa, nalamkoḷ pātattu oru viral ūṉṟiṉāṉ, malaṅkupāy vayal cūḻnta valañcuḻi valam koḷvār aṭi
the feet of those devotees who go round from left to right, valañcuḻi, which has fields in which the fish, true eel, leaps, belonging to Civaṉ who pressed one toe of his foot which does immense good, to crush the twenty shoulders of the King of ilanki (Irāvaṇaṉ).
eṉtalai mēl (ē)
are on my head

entai tiṇtōḷkaḷ
the strong shoulders of our father.
pavaḷat taṭavaraipōlum
resemble the big mountin of coral.
attōḷmicaiyē pavaḷakkuḻait taḻaittāl okkum palcaṭai
the many caṭi falling on the shoulders resemble coral which puts forth tender sprouts.
accaṭaimēl pavaḷak koḻuntu aṉṉa paimmukanākam
the cobra with a hood like the tender leaf of the coral is on the caṭi.
annākattoṭum pavaḷakkaṇ vālamaticūṭum paṉimalarē
the cool flowers worn by him are the white crescent which resembles the flowers of the coral and that cobra.

muruku ār naṟumalar iṇṭai taḻuvi vaṇṭē muralum (1)peruku āṟuaṭaicaṭaik kaṟṟaiyiṉāy
Civaṉ who has a collection of caṭi which has been approached by the swollen river and on which the bees hum swarming on the circlet of faultless flowers full of fragrance.
piṇimēyntirunta irukāṟkurampai itu nāṉuṭaiyatu; itu pirintāl
body of two pillars (two legs) which is covered with diseases is mine; when I leave this body.
eṉakku uṉ tiruvaṭikkīḻ ōr talaimaṟaivē taruvāy
grant me a hiding place under your holy feet.
[[Variant reading: (1) perukāraṭai]]

{$}
mūvā uruvattumukkaṇ mutalva
the first cuse of all things who has three eyes and a form which does not age!
mikku ūr iṭumpaikāvāy' eṉa
crying, protect us from the suffering which is very much on the increase and spreading
kaṭai tūṅkum maṇiyaik kaiyāl amarar nāvāy acaitta oli olimāṟiyatillai
the sound produced by the clapper of the bell which was hanging at the entrance, when the immortals rang it with their hands, did not stop its sound.
appāl tī vāy erintu poṭiyāyk kaḻinta, tiripuramē
the cities disappeared having been reduced to ash by being burnt by the fire at that instant;
[[cf. ((13) āruyirttiruviruttam 1);
The celestials complined loudly to Civaṉ by ringing the bell at the entrance of Kayili; it is called ārāccimani; a bell which used to be hung at the palace-gate of a King so that any subject who sought justice might ring it;
āṭum kaṭaimaṇi nā acaiyāmal akilam ellām nīṭum kuṭaiyil taritta pirāṉ eṉṟu nittam navam, pāṭum kavipperumāḷ oṭṭakkūttaṉ patāmpuyattaic cūṭuṅkulōttuṅka cōḻaṉeṉṟē eṉṉaic colluvarē (an old verse); in this verse upto tarittapirāṉ was sung by oṭṭakkūttar; the remining part was sung by Kulōttuṅka cōḻaṉ. This verse is found in tamiḻ nāvalar cariti under the heading oṭṭakkūttaṉpāti cōḻaṉpāti pāṭiyatu with a variant reading in the 2nd line pirāṉ nittam nittam nalam;
taṉ uyirk kaṉṟuvīyat taḷarnta āttariyātāki, muṉneruppu uyirttu vimmi mukattiṉil kaṇṇīr vāra, maṉ uyirkākkum ceṅkōl maṉuviṉ poṟkōyil vāyil, poṉ aṇi maṇiyaic ceṉṟu kōṭṭiṉāl puṭaittataṉṟē (periya purāṇam, tirunakaracciṟappu, 27)]]

vantu amarar muṉṉāḷ muntic ceḻumalar iṭṭu muṭitāḻttu aṭi vaṇaṅkum nantikku muntuṟa āṭceykilāviṭṭa nalneñcamē
my good natured mind which was incapable of doing service first of all to Civaṉ whose feet the immortals came to this world in olden times and laying down fertile flowers having precedence over human beings and bowed their heads before his feet.

what is the use of grieving now, when the sins that bound us, which were committed in precious births, after joining us in this birth.

vaṭṭac caṭaikkaṟṟai alampac ciṟitu alarnta nanti vaṭṭattoṭu koṉṟai vaḷāviya nampaṉaiyē cinti
Visit Civaṉ on whose collection of circular caṭi koṉṟi flowers are mingled with nantivaṭṭam flowers which slightly blossom as the Kaṅki wabbles;
[[(canti):
cantittum mīṉanōkki talaivaṉai mūṉṟupōtum vantittum (parañcōti tiruviliyātal, tirunakaracciṟappu, 106)]]
antivaṭṭattu iḷaṅkaṇṇiyiṉ āṟu amar ceñcaṭaiyāṉ punti vaṭṭattiṭaippukku niṉṟāṉaiyum poy eṉpaṉō
will I say, Civaṉ who entered into the area of my mind and remined there having a young chaplet that shines in the evening and a red caṭi on which the river stays?
[[The commentator of the Tarumapura edition is of the opinion that this verse has slight variations such as

taṉ mattakattu ōr iḷampiṟai cūṭiyacaṅkaraṉē
cankaraṉ (the dispenser of goodthings) who wears a young crescent on his head.
uṉmattaka malar cūṭi ulakam toḻac cuṭalaip pal mattakam koṇṭu palkaṭaitōṟum palitirivāṉ
wanders for receiving alms, in many entrances of houses holding a skull with teeth found in the cremation ground, to be worshipped by the people of the world, wearing on his head datura flowers.
eṉ mattakattē iravum pakalum pirivu ariyāṉ
he hardly leaves my head night and day.

vāṉ iraikkum iraippā
Civaṉ who is in the form of sound which reaches the heaven!
paṭutalai ēntukaiyā
who holds a lifeless skull in the hand!
maṟaitēṭum entāy
our father who is searched by the vētams.
eṅkaḷ uttamaṉē
our god who is possessed of all attributes!
uraippār uraippaṉavē ceyti (āl)
you do what you are requested to do by people.
araippāl uṭuppaṉa kōvaṇac ciṉṉaṅkaḷ
what you dress in your waist are pieces of loin-cloth stitched together.

tuṟakkappaṭāta uṭalaittuṟantu
leaving the body which is very difficult to be completely freed from attachments.
ventūtuvarōṭu iṟappaṉ
I shall go beyong this world along with the cruel messengers of the god of death.
iṟantāl iru vicumpu ēṟuvaṉ
if I go beyond I shall ascend the extensive heaven.
ēṟivantu piṟappaṉ
after ascending I shall be born in this world coming down.
piṟantāl
If I am born as a human being.
piṟai aṇi vārcaṭaippiññakaṉpēr maṟappaṉ kolō eṉṟu eṉ uḷḷam kiṭantu maṟukiṭumē
my mind will whirl lying prostrate whether I shall forget the name of Civaṉ the destroyer, who adorns his long caṭi with a crescent.

neñcē

kal neṭuṅkālam vetumpi
the mountins become heated for a long time.
karuṅkaṭal nīr curuṅki
the water in the black ocean becoming less in quantity.

pañcam uṇṭu eṉṟu eṉṉoṭum cūḷaṟum

in this earth which is the refuge for all living beings, there is a big mountin of gold (Civaṉ) with three eyes which do not wink.

nāṉmukaṉ mēṟceṉṟu mēlum aṟintilēṉ
the Piramaṉ of four faces could not know the head even though he went into the sky taking the form of a swan.
mālum kīḻ iṭantu (kīḻum) aṟintilaṉ
Māl also did not know the feet, too even though he digged the earth and went down, taking the form of a pig.
māl uṟṟatē vaḻipāṭuceyyum pālaṉ micaic ceṉṟu pācam viciṟi maṟinta cintaik kālaṉ aṟintāṉ aṟitaṟku ariyāṉ kaḻal aṭiyē
Kālaṉ (the god of death) who had a perverted mind knew the feet of Civaṉ who wears kaḻal, and who could not be known, hurled the noose on the boy

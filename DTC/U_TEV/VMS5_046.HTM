{5:46}__1+
{$}
tuṉṉak kēvāṇac cuṇṇa veṇṇīṟu aṇi; poṉ nakkaṉṉa caṭaip pukalūrarō
Civaṉ in Pukalūr who has a caṭi which is gold which has bloomed like a flower, and who smears fine powder of white sacred ash and dresses in a stitched loin-cloth!
miṉ nakkaṉṉa veṇ tiṅkaḷaip pāmpuṭaṉ eṉṉukkō uṭaṉ vaittiṭṭiruppatē
with what purpose did you keep along with a cobra a white crescent which is like a glittering lightning?
{5:46}__2+
{$}
iraikkum pāmpum
(placing) a hissing cobra,
eṟi taru tiṅkaḷum
a crescent that is swallowed by it
nuraikkum kaṅkaiyum nuṇṇiya ceñcaṭai
and a foaming Kaṅki on the minute red caṭi.
puraippu ilāta poḻiṟ pukalūrai uraikkumā colli oḷ vaḷai cōrumē
my dughter is slipping off her bangles describing the Lord in Pukalūr which has defectless gardens, as he is described in works on him.
[[puraippu defect;]]
[[PP: puraippu ilāp paramparaṉē (tiruvāymoḻi; 4-3-9);]]
[[uraikkumā colli can be interpreted in this way also; asking others to describe him;]]
[[This verse is akapporuḷ.]]
{5:46}__3+
{$}
ūcalām aravu alkul eṉ cōr kuḻal
my dughter with tresses of hir that slip down, who has a waist like a dancing cobra.
ēcalām paḻi tantu eḻil koṇṭaṉar
took away her beauty giving in exchange slander with which ladies reproach her.
ōmakaḷē! colāy
oh my dughter, tell me
muṟaiyō eṉṟu pūcam nām iṭutum pukalūrarkkē
we shall make a loud uproar to the Lord in pukalūr saying, is it just on your part?
{5:46}__4+
{$}
miṉṉiṉ nēriṭaiyāḷ umai paṅkaṉai
Civaṉ who has Umi who has a waist as flexible as the lightning, as a half.
taṉṉai nēr ilāta talaivaṉai
the chief who has nothing to be compared to him.
puṉṉaik kāṉal poḻil pukalūraṉai eṉṉuḷ āka vaittu iṉpuṟṟiruppaṉē
I shall feel happy by placing the Lord in pukalūr which has sea-shore gardens of mast-wood trees, in my heart.
{5:46}__5+
{$}
viṇṇiṉ ār mati cūṭiya vēntaṉai eṇṇi
fixing the thoughts on the King who adorned his head with a crescent that shines in the sky.
nāmaṅkaḷ ōti
uttering his names.
eḻuttu añcum kaṇṇiṉāl
of we consider his mantiram consisting of five letters
kaḻalkāṇpu iṭam ētu eṉil
if one asks which is the place where we can a vision of his feet
puṇṇiyaṉ pukalūrum eṉ neñcumē
they are pukalūr belonging to Civaṉ who is the embodiment all good actions, and my heart.
{5:46}__6+
{$}
aṇṭavāṇar amutu uṇa nañcu uṇṭu
consuming the poison so that the inhabitants of heaven may taste the nectar.
paṇṭu nāṉ maṟai ōtiya pāṭalaṉ
Civaṉ who chanted the four vetams with music, long ago.
toṇṭarākit toḻutu matippavar puṇṭarīkattu uḷār pukalūrarē
Civaṉ in pukalūr dwells in the lotus of the hearts of those who esteem him becoming his devotees, and worship him.
{5:46}__7+
{$}
tattuvam talai kaṇṭu aṟavār ilai
there is no one who has found out the true nature of tattuvam, thirty-six in number.
tattuvam talai kaṇṭavar kaṇṭilar
those who have completely investigated all the tattuvams have not seen god who is above tattuvams.
tattuvam talai 1. niṉṟavarkku allatu
except for those who are interested in the investigation of tattuvams.
taṇ pukalūraṉ tattuvaṉ alaṉ
Civaṉ in cool pukalūr is not the reality for others.
[[Variant reading: 1. talaikaṇṭavarkku]]

{$}
peruṅkai ākip piḷiṟi varuvatu ōr karuṅkai yāṉaik kaḷiṟṟu uri pōrttavar
Civaṉ covered himself with the skin of a male elephant which has a trunk with which it can kill and which came roaring having a big trunk.
[[arumpaṉait taṭakkai aparakāttiram vāy vāl eyiṟu aintiṉum kolva (cīvakacintamani, 2154);
koṉṟu vāḻtoḻiliṉum vaṉpaṇit toḻiliṉum, kaṉṟiya toḻiṟkai karuṅkai eṉappaṭum (tivākaram)]]
varuṅkaiyāṉai matakaḷiṟu añciṉaip poruṅkaiyāṉai (kaṇṭīr) pukalūrar
is like the elephant which does not satiate if we look at it very often; is the elephant with a big trunk which fights with the five senses which are like frenzied elephants.
[[The five senses are always compared to elephants in Tamiḻ literature;
uraṉ eṉṉum tōṭṭiyāṉ ōr aintum kāppāṉ (Kuṟal, 24)]]
{5:46}__9+

poṉ otta niṟattāṉum porukaṭal taṉ otta niṟattāṉum aṟikilāp puṉṉait tātu poḻiṟ pukalūrarai eṉ attā eṉa eṉ iṭar tīrum
my sufferings will come to an end if I say about the Lord in Pukalūr which has gardens of mast-wood trees with abundant pollen, who could not be known by Piramaṉ who has the colour of gold and Māl who is blue like the ocean whose waves dash aginst the shore.

mattaṉāy matiyātu malaitaṉai ettiṉāṉ tiraḷ tōḷ muṭi pattu iṟa ottiṉāṉ viralāl
Civaṉ gently pressed with his toe to break the heads and globular shoulders of Irāvaṇaṉ who, being puffed up with pride, tried to throw away the mountin by uprooting it, without regarding it was the abode of Civaṉ.
oruṅku ēttalum pottiṉāṉ pukalūrait toḻumiṉ
(People of this world!) worship with joined hands Pukalūr belonging to Civaṉ who protected him

{$} kuṟai aṇi (1) kullai mullai aḷaintu kuḷir mātavimēl ciṟai aṇivaṇṭukaḷ cēr tirunākēccarattaraṉē
Civaṉ in Tirunākeccaram where the beautiful winged bees reach the common delight of the woods, having wallowed in the indispensable sacred basil, and arabian jasmine!
piṟai aṇivāḷ nutalāḷ umaiyāḷavaḷ pēḻkaṇikka
Umiyāḷ who has a bright forehead like the crescent, to stare in fear.
niṟai aṇineñcu aṉuṅka
the strong mind to be in distress
nīlamāl viṭam uṇṭatueṉṉē
what is the reason for consuming the big poison of blue colour?
[[pēḻkaṇittal is used in several senses; it is used in the sense of being afrid of; it is also used in the sense of closing one's eyes as in sleep;]]
[[PP: tirumaṭattil orupāl aṇaintupēḻkaṇittu maruvum uṇarvil tuyil koṇṭār (periya purāṇam, tirunāvukkaracar, 275);]]
[[PP: miṉkaṉṟum eyiṟṟukkōṇmāvēṅkaiyeṉṟu iṇaiyavēyum, piṉceṉṟu kātalkūrap pēḻkaṇittu iraṅkukiṉṟa (Kamparāmāyaṇam, marāmarappaṭalam, 12);]]
[[it is used also in the sense of repentance:]]
[[PP: piṟpāl niṉṟu pēḻkaṇittalpeṟutaṟku ariyaṉ perumāṉē
(tiruvācakam, 45.7)]]
[[Variant reading: (1) kollai]]
{7:99}__2+
{$} kuruntu ayalē kuravam araviṉ eyiṟu ēṟṟu arumpa
when the bottle flower puts forth buds like the teeth of the cobra, and which grows near the wild lime tree.
cerunticempoṉ malarum tirunākēccarattāṉē
Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram where the tree carunti blossoms flowers like gold!
aruntava māmuṉivarkku aruḷ ākiōr ālataṉkīḻiruntu
sitting under the banyan tree and bestowing grace upon the great sages of severe penance
aṟamē puritaṟku iyalpu ākiyatu eṉṉai (kol, ām)
what is the reason for consenting to give religious instruction to them
[[kol, ām; expletives.]]
{7:99}__3+
{$} kōlam malarkkuvaḷai kaḻunīrvayalcūḻkiṭaṅkil cēloṭuvāḷaikaḷ pāy tirunākēccarattaraṉē
Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram where the carnatic carp and the scabbard fish jump in the moat surrounded by fields in which beautiful blue nelumbo flowers blossom along with red indian water lily!
pālaṉatu āruyir mēl pariyātu (1) pakaittu eḻunta, kālaṉai vīṭuvittuk karuttu ākkiyatu eṉṉai (kol, ām)
what is the reason for your idea of being partial towards the young boy whose life the god of death wanted to take away without pity and with enmity, to make him (the god of death) die?
[[Variant reading: (1)pakaittu]]
{7:99}__4+
{$} muṉṟil iḷaṅkaḻukiṉ mutuyāṉaimatu aḷaintu, teṉṟalpukuntu ulavum tirunākēccarattaraṉē
Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram where the balmy southern breeze blows combining in it the honey in the old spathe of the young arecapalms which grow in the courtyards of houses!
kuṉṟamalaikkumari koṭiēr iṭaiyāḷ veruva
when the dughter of imayam which is surrounded by many hills, who has a waist as slander as the creeper, to become frightened.
veṉṟimatakariyiṉ uripōrttumeṉṉai (kol,ām)
what is the reason for playing the victorious rutting elephant and covering yourself with its skin?
[[veṉṟi: this epithet denotes its previous state;]]
[[PP: veṉṟikkaḷiṟṟaiviritāravaṉ veṉṟavāṟum (cīvakacintāmaṇi, 14);]]
[[PP: muṉpu veṉṟiyaiyuṭaiya kaḷiṟu (Do. nacciṉārkkiṉiyar).]]
{7:99}__5+
{$} varai tarumāmaṇiyum varaiccantu akilōṭum unti
pushing the big precious stones found in the mountin and sandal-wood and eagle-word growing in that mountin.
tirai porutaṇpaḻaṉattirunākēccarattaraṉē
Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram which has cool fields where waves dash aginst the ridges!
araivirikōvaṇattōṭu aravu ārttu orunāṉmaṟainūl urai peruka (v) uraittu aṉṟu ukantu aruḷ ceytatu eṉṉē
what is the reason for expounding in detil the meaning of the four vētams, pleased with those who were eager to learn them, in the distant past, having tied a cobra with a broad loin cloth in the waist?
{7:99}__6+
{$} ceṅkayal pāykaḻaṉit tirunākēccarattaraṉē
Civaṉ in tirunākeccaram where there are fields in which red carp jumps!
taṅkiyamātavattiṉ taḻalvēḷviyiṉiṉṟu eḻunta ciṅkamum nīḷpuliyum ceḻumāl kariyōṭu alaṟap poṅkiya pōrpurintu piḷantu īruri pōrttatu eṉṉē
what is the reason for covering yourself with the skins, wet with fresh blood, by splitting and having fought angrily with the fat and big elephant, of tiger with a long body and lion, all of which rose from the sacrificial fire by the aid of the permanent great penance of the sages of tārukāvaṉam?

{$} viritaru mallikaiyum malarccaṇpakamum aḷaintu tiritaruvaṇṭu paṇcey tirunākēccarattaraṉē
Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram where the bees wandering after wallowing in the blossoming jasmine and champak flowers hum like melody-types!
viriara nāṇatuāka (1) māmēruvilatuāka, ariyaṉamuppuraṅkaḷ avai āraḻal ūṭṭal eṉṉē
what is the reason for setting on fire which could not be easily extinguished, the three cities, which could not be easily vanquished, by converting the big mēru as the bow and the cobra (vācuki) with lines on it as the bow-string?
[[Variant reading: (1) varimēru]]
{7:99}__9+
{$} paṅkaya māmalarmēl matuuṇṭu paṇ vaṇṭu aṟaiyac ceṅkayal niṉṟu ukaḷum tirunākēccarattaraṉē
Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram where red carp fish frisks when the bees having drunk the honey in the lotus flower hum like melody-types in a high pitch!
aṅku iyal yōkutaṉṉai aḻippāṉceṉṟu aṇaintu
Having gone there and being near Civaṉ in order to destroy the yōkam Civaṉ was practising in Kayili.
[[The place where Civan practised yōkam is very well known; it is hinted by the word aṅku, a demonstrative pronoun denoting place.]]
mikappoṅkiya pūṅkaṇaivēḷpoṭiyāka viḻittal eṉṉē
what is the reason for opening your frontal eye to reduce to ash to vēḷ (cupid) who has flowers for his arrows and who was severely angry?
{7:99}__10+
{$} toṇṭu iraittu vaṇaṅkit toḻilpūṇṭu aṭiyār paravum teḷtirait taṇcūḻ tirunākēccarattaṉē
Civaṉ in nākēccaram surrounded by cool fields of clear waves where the devotees take upon themselves the duty of performing service to you making a big noise, bow to you and prise you!
kuṇṭaraik kūṟaiyiṉṟittiriyum camaṇ, cākkiyappēy miṇṭarai viravu (1) ākkiyatu eṉṉai (kol, ām)
what is the reason to make the base camaṇ who wander naked and the cākkiyar, the ignorant persons who have devilish propensities to stick to their pet doctrines which they have perceived!
[[Variant reading : (1) ākiyatu]]
{7:99}__11+
{$} koṅku aṇaivaṇṭu araṟṟa
when the bees that reach the pollen hum in a high pitch.
kuyilum mayilum (1) teṅkamaḻ pūmpoḻil cūḻ tirunākēccarattaraṉai
on Civaṉ in tirunākēccaram which is surrounded by flower gardens where the smell of honey spreads and where indian cuckoos and peacocks sing and dance respectively
[[tēṅkamaḻ has become teṅkamaḻ to suit rhyme.]]
vaṅkam malikaṭalcūḻ vayal (2) nāval ārūraṉcoṉṉa paṅkam ilpāṭal vallār avartam viṉaipaṟṟu aṟumē
the Karmam of those who can sing the songs which are without defect composed by ārūraṉ of nāval which is surrounded by fields like the sea on which many ships sil, will lose their grip on them.
[[Variant reading: (1) teṅkaṇai (2) nāvalaūraṉ]]
[[ Fields are compared to the sea,]]
[[PP: vaṇtōṭṭup piṇaṅku katirkkaḻaṉināppaṇ ēmuṟṟu, uṇaṅkukalaṉ āḻiyiṉ tōṉṟum ōr eyil maṉṉaṉ oru maṭamakaḷē (puram, 338-9-12).]]

vāṉavar tāṉavar vaikal malar koṇarntu iṭṭu iṟaiñci
worshipping dily Civaṉ, by placing flowers which the celestials and people of this world who are famous by their munificence, with flowers brought by them.
[[tāṉavar is derived from tāṉam (donation)]]
tāṉavar māl piramaṉ aṟiyāta takaimaiyiṉāṉ
Civaṉ who has the greatness of not being known to Tāṉavar, Māl and Piramaṉ.
[[tāṉavar: this word means the sons of Taṉu, one of the wives of Kācapa.]]
āṉavaṉ
one who has a bull
ātipurāṇaṉ
the very old primordial one.
aṉṟu ōṭiya paṉṟi eyta kāṉavaṉaikkaṇṭiyūyar aṇṭa vāṇar toḻukiṉṟa tē
what a wonder the inhabitants of heaven worship in Kaṇtiyūr, the hunter who spot an arrow at the pig which ran to kill Aruccuṉaṉ in the distant past!

vāṉa matiyamum vāḷ aravum puṉalōṭu caṭait tāṉam (atu) eṉa vaittu uḻalvāṉ
(Civaṉ) wanders, placing at their place in his caṭi the moon that is in the `sky', the cruel cobra and the water of Kaṅki.
taḻal pōl uruvaṉ
has a form as bright as fire.
kāṉa maṟi oṉṟu kai uṭaiyāṉ
has on his hand a deer that lives in the forest
[[kāṉamaṟi: the underlined word is a generic epithet, as the deer that Civaṉ holds in his hand was born in the sacrificial fire of the sages of Tārukāvaṉam.]]
kaṇṭiyūr irunta ūṉam il vētam uṭaiyaṉai nām aṭiuḷkuvatē
we esteem the feet of the god who has Vētam-s without any defect, and who is in Kaṇṭiyūr.

paṇṭu aṅku aṟuttatu ōr kai uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ cut off the hand of the Fire god in the distant past (at the sacrifice of Takkaṉ);
[[PP: veyyavaṉ aṅki viḻuṅkattiraṭṭiya, kaiyait taṟittāṉ eṉṟu untīpaṟa, kalaṅkiṟṟu vēḷvi eṉṟu untīpaṟa
(Tiruvācakam, tiruvuntiyār,7)]]
paṭaittāṉ talaiyai aṅku aṟuttatum uṇṭu
there is a story of his having cut off the head of the creator
ūroṭu nāṭu avaitāṉ aṟiyum
village and countries know these.
kaṇṭam kaṟutta miṭaṟuṭaiyāṉ
[he] has a black neck!
[[kaṇṭam and miṭaṟu have the same meaning.;
miṭaṟu may be a scribal error for miṭal]]
kaṇṭiyūr irunta toṇṭarpirāṉaik kaṇṭīr aṇṭa vāṇar toḻukiṉṟatē
the residents of heaven worship the master of devotees in Kaṇtiyūr
[[kaṇṭīr expletive]]

muṭiyiṉ muṟṟātatu oṉṟu illai
there is nothing that cannot be accomplished if one tries.
ellām uṭaṉ tāṉ uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has everything within himself.
koṭiyum uṟṟa(v) viṭai ēṟi
riding on a bull which is drawn on the flag also.
ōr kūṟṟu orupāl uṭaiyāṉ
has Umi on one half.
kaṭiyaviṉai nōy muṟṟak kaḷaivāṉ
will weed out completely the irresistible karmams, and diseases.
kaṇṭiyūr iruntāṉ aṭiyum uṟṟār toṇṭar
his devotees reached the feet of Civaṉ who is in kaṇṭiyūr
illai (kaṇṭīr) aṇṭa vāṉavarē
the celestials dwelling in heaven do not have that good fortune (kaṇṭīr as in previous verse).

paṟṟi ōryāṉai uritta pirāṉ
the master who flayed on elephant catching hold of it.
pavaḷattiraḷ pōl muṟṟum aṇintatu ōr nīṟu uṭaiyāṉ
has holy ash over the whole of the body which is red like coral;
[[pavaḷam pōl mēṉiyiṟ pāl veṇṇīṟum (appar, kōyil, (2) 4); perhaps the reading pavaḷattiraḷ mēl became pavaḷattiraḷ pōl due to scribal error]]
muṉṉamē koṭutta kaltaṅku uṭaiyavaṉ tāṉ aṟiyāṉ
has a dress soaked in red ochre which was given to him in ages gone by; even he does not know when he got it.
kaṇṭiyūr irunta kuṟṟam il vētam uṭaiyāṉai ām aṇṭar kūṟuvatē
the celestial beings talk about Civaṉ who revealed the defectless vētams and who is in kaṇṭiyur
[[If we take it as yām aṇṭar we and the celestials is another meaning; In the first meaning ām is expletive]]

pōrppaṉai yāṉai uritta pirāṉ
the master who flayed an elehant which has a trunk of the palmyra tree, and which is used in warfare;
[[paṉaikkai mummuta vēḻam urittavaṉ (appar, kōyil, tirukkuṟuntoki, 1)]]
poṟivāy aravam cērppatu vāṉattu irai kaṭal cūḻ ulakam itaṉaik kāppatu kāraṇamākak koṇṭāṉ
he tied the cobra which has spots in order to protect the world which is surrounded by the ocean whose roaring noise reaches the sky.
kaṇṭiyūr irunta kūrppu uṭai oḷvāḷ maḻuvaṉai yām aṇṭar kūṟuvatē
we and tevar speak about the god who is in kaṇṭiyūr and who has a sharp and very bright battle-axe.

aṭṭatu kālaṉai
Civaṉ killed the god of death.
āyntatu vētam āṟu aṅkam aṉṟu
he made research into vētams and six ankams in the distant past.
cuṭṭatu kāmaṉaikkaṇ ataṉālē toṭarntu eriya
burnt kāmaṉ (cupid) with his frontal eye to be burning continuously.
kaṭṭu avai mūṉṟum eritta pirāṉ
the master who burnt the three forts that were built.
kaṇṭiyūr irunta kuṭṭam muṉ vētappaṭaiyaṉaiyām aṇṭarkūṟuvatē
we and the tevar speak about Civaṉ in kaṇṭiyūr who has a weapon of vētam which is as vast as the ocean
[[kuṭṭam deep ocean;
kaṭaṟkuṭṭam pōḻvar kalavar (nāṉmaṇikkaṭiki, 18)]]

aṭṭum oli nīr aṇi matiyum malar āṉa ellām iṭṭup potiyum caṭaimuṭiyāṉ
Civaṉ who has a caṭi coiled into a crown into which are placed and concealed many flowers, beautiful crescent, and flowing and roasing water.
iṇṭai mālai aṅkaik kaṭṭum aravu (atu) tāṉ uṭaiyāṉ
has a cobra which is wound round the beautiful hand like the garland of circlet which is worn on the head.
kaṇṭiyūr irunta koṭṭum paṟaiyuṭaik kūttaṉai yām aṇṭar kūṟuvatē
we and tevar speak about the dancer who has a drum beaten to measure time, and who is in kaṇṭiyūr.

tīviṉai māyntaṉa
All evil karmams have vanished.
maṅkiṉa nōykaḷ
diseases have become diminished.
maṟukiviḻat tōyntaṉa pāvam
sins have lessened to become bewildered
namaic ceṟukkakillā
they do not have the power to afflict us.
anaṅkaic ceṟṟuk kāynta pirāṉ
the master destroyed cupid by being angry with him. (anaṅku=anaṅkaṉ this is not used in its derivative meaning, as cupid became formless after he was burnt by the frontal eye of Civaṉ)
[[peruntiṟattu anaṅkaṉai anaṅkamā viḻittatum (Campantār, Vaṭakurankaṭutuṟi, 9)]]
kaṇṭiyūr empirāṉ
our master in kaṇṭiyūr.
aṅkam āṟiṉaiyum āynta pirāṉallaṉō aṭiyēṉai āṭ koṇṭavaṉē
is Civaṉ who accepted me as his servant, not the master who researched the six aṅkams?

maṇṭi malaiyai eṭuttu mattu ākki
using as the churning staff removing the mountin having collected themselves together.
avvācukiyait taṇṭi amar kaṭainta kaṭal viṭam kaṇṭaruḷi
seeing the poison that rose in the ocean which was churned by the immortals by attaching that Vācuki by winding it round the churning staff.
nañcu uṇṭa pirāṉ
the master who consumed the poison.
oḷitta pirāṉ
the master who concealed it
kaṇṭiyūr aṇṭavāṉavaṉ añci ōṭi naṇṇak kaṇṭapirāṉ alalṉō
is he not the master who is in Kaṇṭiyur and who saw to it that that the poison ran out of fear and reach him as its refuge?

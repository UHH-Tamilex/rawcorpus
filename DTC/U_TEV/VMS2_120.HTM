{2:120}__1+
{$} veṇṇīṟu cāntam eṉappūci
besmearing the white sacred ash like sandal-wood paste
veḷḷam caṭai vaittavar
Civaṉ who kept the floods of the Kanki on the caṭi.
kāntaḷ ārum viral ēḻaiyoṭu āṭiya kāraṇam
the reason for dancing along with the lady whose fingers are like the petals of the red variety of malabar glory lily.
āyntu koṇṭu āṅku aṟiya(n) niṟaintār avar
he pervades everywhere which can be known only by intuitive knowledge
ārkolō vēntaṉ mūkkīc carattu aṭikaḷ ceykiṉṟatu ōr meymmaiyē
who is capable of knowing the true purpose of the actions done by the god in mūkkiccaram which was built by a king.
[[Perhaps vēntaṉ means Kōcceṅkaṭ Cōḻa Nāyaṉār who built seventy temples to Civaṉ.]]
{2:120}__2+
{$} veṇtalai ōrkalaṉāppali tērntu
collecting alms having the white skull as the begging bowl
viricaṭaik 1. koṇṭal ārum puṉal cērttu
having become united with the water which was received in the loosened cati
[[koṇṭal-koḷḷutal a rare usage;
vāṉ kāval koṇṭāṉ vaḻi niṉṟu vaikalum tāṉ kāval koṇṭal takum (puṟapporuḷ veṇpāmāli, 179)]]
umaiyāḷoṭum kūṭṭamā
and being united with umayāl
viṇṭavartam matileyta piṉ
after having discharged an arrow on the forts of the enemies
vēṉil vēḷ ventu eḻak kaṇṭavar
He cused the vēḷ who has spring as his season, to be well-burnt
mūkkīc carattu em aṭikaḷ ceykaṉmamē
these are the acts done by our god in mūkkīccaram
[[Variant reading: 1. koṇṭavarum]]
{2:120}__3+
{$} maruvalār tam matil eytatuvum
discharging an arrow on the forts of the enemies
māl matalaiyai uruvil āra(v) eri yūṭṭiyatum ulaku uṇṭatu (āl)
and setting fire to the body of Kāmaṉ, have been known to the world (ārav eri - va is augmentation for metre)
ceruvil ārum puli ceṅkayal 1. āṇaiyiṉāṉ ceyta poru il mūkkīc carattu em aṭikaḷ ceyyum pūcalē
are the battles that our god in mūkkiccaram which has no comparison and built by the King who had exercise of sovereign power and who had the banners of tiger and red carp fish which are held high in battles
[[Variant reading: 1. āṉaiyiṉāṉ]]
{2:120}__4+
{$} aṉṉam aṉṉa(n) naṭaic cāyalāḷōṭu aḻaku eytavē
to appear beautiful with a lady of tender nature and the git of a swan
miṉṉai aṉṉa caṭaik kaṅkaiyāḷ mēviya kāraṇam
the reason for Kaṅkiyāl to stay in the caṭi which is like lightning
teṉṉaṉ kōḻi eḻil vañciyum ōṅku ceṅkōliṉāṉ maṉṉaṉmūkkīc carattu aṭikaḷ ceykiṉṟatōr māyamē
is a wonderful act done by the god in mūkkīccaram built by the King whose impartial rule was supreme in the beautiful vañci kōḻi, and who ruled over the pāṇtiyan country also
[[vañci - the capital of cāra nāṭu: kōḻi : uṟaiyūr the capital cōḻanatu; the origin of the name kōḻi is mentioned in detil in cilappatīkāram, 10-247-248, aṭiyārkkunallār`s commentary]]
{2:120}__5+
{$} viṭam muṉ ār av aḻal vāyatu pāmpu araivīkkiyē
having tied an angry cobra having poison in it, in the waist
naṭam muṉ ār av aḻal āṭuvar pēyoṭu naḷḷiruḷ
will dance in the dense darkness in company with the pēys, in the midst of fire
vaṭamaṉ nīṭu pukaḻp pūḻiyaṉ teṉṉavaṉ kōḻi maṉ aṭal maṉ mūkkīc carattu aṭikaḷ ceykiṉṟatu ōr accamē
it is a dreadful act the god in mūkkīccararam which was built by the strong king who is the Lord of Kōḻi, Pāṇtiyaṉ and Cēraṉ whose fame lasted for a time in the North (pūḻiyaṉceraṉ; vaṭamaṉ - vaṭakkumaṉ)
{2:120}__6+
{$} ventu nīṟu meyyil pūcuvar; vīṅku iruḷ āṭuvar
Civaṉ will besmears all over his body well-burnt sacred ash; will dance in the dense darkness
vantu eṉ ār avvaḷai koḷvatum iṅku ōr māyam ām
It is a deceptive act to take away my beautiful and rare bangles coming to my place
am taṇ māmaṉataṉ nēriyaṉ cempiyaṉ ākkiya mūkkīccarattu aṭikaḷ entai ceykiṉṟatu ōr ētamē
what our father and god who dwells in mūkkiccaram built by beautiful and kind coḻaṉ who rules over nēri an destroys the pride of his enemies, does is a faulty act.
{2:120}__7+
{$} araiyil ārum kalai illavaṉ
Civaṉ has no dress that is worn on the waist
āṇoṭu peṇṇumāy urai il ār avvaḻal āṭuvār oṉṟu alar
Being both male and female, dances in the fire which is full of fame and has many forms
kāṇmiṉō
you please understand this (people of this world!)
viravalārtam matil mūṉṟu uṭaṉ vevvaḻal ākkiṉāṉ
destroyed by the hot fire the three forts of the enemies simultaneously
araiyaṉ mūkkic carattu aṭikaḷ ceykiṉṟatu ōr accamē
is a dreadful act which is committed by the god in mūkkīccaram built by Kāccenkatcōḻaṉ
{2:120}__8+
{$} īrkkum nīrc ceñcaṭaikku ēṟṟatum kūṟṟai utaittatum
receiving the water that drags every thing in the red caṭi and kicking the god of death
kūrkkum nal mūvalaivēl valaṉ ēntiya koḷkaiyum
and the idea of holding on the ride side a sharp and good trident of three blades
ārkkum vāyāṉ arakkaṉ urattai neritta(v) aṭal mūrkkaṉ mūkkīc carattu aṭikaḷ ceyyā niṉṟa moympu (atē)
are the strong acts of the extremely angry and strong god who cruched the chest of the arakkaṉ who roared loudly opening his mouth, and who dwells in mūkkīccaram
{2:120}__9+
{$} nīruḷ ārum malar mēl uṟaivāṉoṭu mālumāy
Māl in company with Piramaṉ who dwells on a flower that grows in the water nīruḷ ārum malar generic epithet)
cīruḷ ārum kaḻal tēṭa meyttīttiraḷ āyiṉāṉ
Civaṉ became truly a mass of fire, to search the famous feet (and head)
cīriṉāl ilaṅku oḷir teṉṉavaṉ, cempiyaṉ, villavaṉ cērum mūkkīc carattu aṭikaḷ ceykiṉṟatu or cemmaiyē
the uprightness that is done by thegod in mūkkiccaram where the pāṇtiyaṉ who is preeminent by his fame, coḻaṉ and ceraṉ gathered for worship

cempiyaṉ colaṉ; believed to be the who descendents of cipi, a mythologyical king;
puḷ uṟu puṉkaṇ tīrtta veḷvēl, ciṉam keḻutāṉaic cempiyaṉ maruka- (puṟam 37-5-6);

{$} veṇ pulāl mārpu iṭutukilar
buddhist who cover their chests of white flesh, with a cloth
veṟṟarai uḻalpavar
and camaṇars who wander naked
uṇpiṉālē uraippār moḻi ūṉam (atu) ākkiṉāṉ
(Civaṉ) cused the words of those people who talk after eating, defective
oṇ pulāl vēl mika vallavaṉ ōṅku eḻiṟ kiḷḷi cērpaṇpiṉ mūkkīc carattu aṭikaḷa ceykiṉṟatu ōr paccaiyē
that is the rudeness committed by the god in mūkkīccaram which was reached by the great and exceedingly beautiful kiḷḷi who is skilled in handling the bright vēl to which flesh is sticking, to worship god (Kiḷḷi-cōḻaṉ)

{$} mallai ār mummuṭi maṉṉar mūkkīc carattu aṭikaḷaic celvarāka niṉaiyum paṭi cērttiya centamiḻ, nallarāy vāḻpavar kāḻiyuḷ ñāṉacampantaṉa
the chaste tamiḻ verses composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ, of a native Kaḻi where good people reside, which were offered to esteem as the Lord the god in mūkkiccaram which was worshipped by the three kings who wore crowns and who were very strong;

those who can recite (the verses) are definitely able to rule over heaven also.

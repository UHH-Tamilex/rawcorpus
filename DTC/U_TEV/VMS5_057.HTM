cennel ār vayal cūḻ tiruk kōḷili maṉṉaṉē
the King in Tirukkōḷili surrounded by field in which red coloured superior variety of paddy grows!
uṉai muṉṉamē niṉaiyātu oḻintēṉ
I did not at all think of you early in my life.
nāṉ uṉacēvaṭi iṉṉam ēttilēṉ
I did not prise your lotus red feet even now.
[[PP: iṉṉam:
iṉṉam pālikkumō ippiṟaviyē
(Nāvukkaracar Kōyil, (1) 1]]
aṭiyēṉai maṟaval(ē)
Do not forget me who is your slave.
{5:57}__2+
{$}
viṇṇuḷār toḻutu ēttum viḷakkiṉai
the light that is prised and worshipped with joined hands by the celestials in heaven.
maṇṇuḷār viṉai tīrkkum maruntiṉai
the cure that removes the acts of the people of this world which are like diseases.
paṇ uḷār payilum tiruk kōḷili aṇṇalār aṭiyē toḻutu uymmiṉ(ē)
(People of this world!) save yourselves by worshipping with joined hands the feet of god in Kōḷili where people of musical knowledge are in great numbers.
{5:57}__3+
{$}
1. nāḷum nammuṭai(ya) nāḷkaḷ aṟikilōm
we are incapable of knowing how long we shall live, everyday.
āḷum nōykaḷ ōr aimpatōṭu āṟu eṭṭum ēḻai maippaṭṭu iruntu nīr naiyātu(ē)
without yourselves languishing in ignorance when the ninety-eight diseases hold you in their grip
kōḷili araṉ pātamē kūṟum(ē)
prise the feet of araṉ (Civaṉ), one in Kōḷili.
[[Variant reading: 1. nāmum]]
{5:57}__4+
{$}
viḻaviṉ ōcai oli aṟāt taṇ poḻil paḻakiṉār viṉai tīrkkum paḻam pati
the old city which removes the sins of those who have become familiar and which has cool gardens and where the bustle of festivals and music do not cease.
aḻal kaiyāṉ amarum tiruk kōḷilik kuḻakaṉār tiruppātamē kūṟum(ē)
(People of this world!) prise only the holy feet of the youth in tirukkōḷili, who holds fire in his hand.
[[taṇ poḻil kōḷili, paḻampati ākiya kōḷili is the syntactical link;
aḻal kaiyāṉ, kuḻakaṉār both refer to Civaṉ]]
{5:57}__5+
{$}
mūlam ākiya mūvarkkum mūrttiyai
the god with a visible form who is the origin of the trinity Piramaṉ, Māl and uruttiraṉ
kālaṉākiya kālaṟkum kālaṉai
who dealt death to the god of death himself who takes away the lives of the beings at the end of the span of their lives.
kōlam ām poḻil cūḻ tiruk kōḷilic cūlapāṇi taṉ pātam toḻumiṉ(ē)
worship with joined hands the feet of Civaṉ who holds in his hand a trident and who dwells in tirukkōḷili surrounded by beautiful gardens.
{5:57}__6+
{$}
kāṟṟaṉai
Civaṉ who is in the form of air.
kaṭal nañcu amutu uṇṭa veṇ nīṟṟaṉai
who smears himself with white sacred ash and who consumed the poison that appeared in the ocean, as he would do nectar.
nimir puṉcaṭai aṇṇalai
the god who has an erect and ruddy caṭi.
āṟṟaṉai
who bears on his head the river Kaṅki.
amarum tiruk kōḷili eṟṟaṉār aṭiyē toḻutu ēttum(ē)
(People of this world!) prise and worship with joined hand only the feet of the god who has a bull and who dwells in Kōḷili.
{5:57}__7+
{$}
vētam āya viṇkaḷ talaivaṉai
the chief of the celesitals in heaven, who is himself the vetams.
ōti maṉṉuyir ēttum oruvaṉai
the unequalled god whom the imperishable souls prise uttering his names.
kōti vaṇṭu aṟaiyum tiruk kōḷili vēta nātaṉ pātam virumpum(ē)
(People of this world!) desire the feet of the Lord of the vētams in Kōḷili where in the flowers the bees penetrate for honey and hum noisily.
{5:57}__8+
{$}
nītiyāl toḻuvārkaḷ talaivaṉai
the chief of those who worship him with joined hands in the right way.
vātaiyāṉa viṭukkum maṇiyiṉai
the gem that gives relief from afflications
[[talaivaṉ, maṇi, vētanāyakaṉ pātam is the syntactical link.]]
kōti vaṇṭu aṟaiyum tiruk kōḷili vēta nāyakaṉ pātam virumpum(ē)
(People of this world!) cherish with love the feet of the chief of the vētams who is in Kōḷili where the bees hum with great noise penetrating into the flowers for honey.
[[Note : The last two lines are the same as in the 7th verse)]]

mālum nāṉmukaṉālum aṟivu oṇāp 1. pāliṉ meṉ moḻiyāḷ oru paṅkaṉai
Civaṉ who has as a half a lady whose words are as sweet as milk and who could not be seen by Māl and Piramaṉ of four faces.
kōlam ām poḻil cūḻ tiruk kōḷili nīlakaṇṭaṉai nittal niṉaimiṉ(ē)
worship dily fixing your thoughts on Civaṉ who has a blue neck and who is in Kōḷili surrounded by beautiful gardens (People of this world!).
[[aṟivoṇāp paṅkaṉai, nīlakaṇṭaṉai niṉaimiṉ is the syntactical link.]]
[[Variant reading: 1. pālu meṉ moḻi]]

arakkaṉ āya ilaṅkaiyar maṉṉaṉai nerukki am muṭipattu iṟuttāṉ
Civaṉ crushed the beautiful ten heads of the arakkaṉ, the King of the people of ilaṅki, squeezing him.
avaṟku 1. irakkam ākiyavaṉ tirukkōḷili aruttiyāy aṭiyē toḻutu uymmiṉ (ē)
(People of this world!) save yourselves by worshipping his feet themselves with avidity in Kōḷili which is the residence of the god who later took compassion on him. (and granted boons)
[[Variant reading: 1. irakkamāyavaṉ).]]

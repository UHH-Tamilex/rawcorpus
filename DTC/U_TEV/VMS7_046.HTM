{7:46}__1+
{$} kaṭalnākaik kārōṇam mēvi iruntīrē
Civaṉ who dwelt with desire in Nākikkārōṇam on the sea-shore!
pattu ūrpukkuirantu uṇṭu
entring into many villages and eating begging alms
[[pattu does not mean exactly ten but is used in the sense of many.]]
pala patikam pāṭi
singing many garlands of songs.
pāvaiyaraik kīṟi pēci
speaking lies to girls who are as beautiful as a portrit.
paṭiṟu āṭit tirivīr
you wander indulging in mischief.
cettārtam elumpu aṇintu
having adorned yourself with the bones of gods who are dead.
cē ēṟittirivīr
wander riding upon a bull.
celvattai maṟaittuvaittīr
you hid your wealth.
eṉakku orunāḷ iraṅkīr
you do not take pity on me even on a single day
[[and say I have nothing to give you]]
muttu āram
a neck-lace of pearls
ilaṅkimiḷir maṇivayirak kōvai avai pūṇat tantaruḷi
giving me a string of rubies, and diamonds which are very much glittering.
meykku iṉitā nāṟum kattūri kamaḻcāntam paṇittaruḷa vēṇṭum
you must bestow on me sandal-paste mixed with sweet-smelling musk, to smear on my body.

{$} kaṭalnākaik kārōṇam mēvi iruntīrē
see 1st verse.
vēmpiṉoṭu tīṅkarumpu viravi eṉaittīṟṟi
having made me enjoy the mixture of the bitter margose leaves and sweet sugar-cane juice
[[margosa stands for worldly pleasures and sugar-cane juice for the pleasure derived from God's grace.]]
virutti nāṉ umaivēṇṭa
when I requested you for a means of livelihood.
turuttipukku aṅku iruntīr
you entered into Turutti and stayed there without coming out of it.
pāmpiṉoṭu paṭarcaṭaikaḷ avaikāṭṭi
showing the cobras and the spreading cati
veruṭṭippakaṭṭanāṉ oṭṭuvaṉō
shall I brook if you frighten and allure me?
(1) palakālum uḻaṉṟēṉ
I wandered following you many times
cōmpiṉoṭu ceṅkaḻunīr taṇkiṭaṅkil tikaḻum tiruvārūrpukku irunta tīvaṇṇarnīrē
you yourself who has the colour of fire and entered into tiruvārūr where in the cool moats arrow-head aquatic plant and purple indian water-lily are prominent, and stayed there.
kāmpiṉoṭu nēttiraṅkaḷ paṇittaruḷa vēṇṭum
you must give me varieties of silk known as Kāmpu and nēttiram.

(1) pūṇpatu ōr iḷa āmai

poruviṭai oṉṟu ēṟi

pollāta vēṭam koṇṭu
assuming an ugly form.
ellārum kāṇa
to be seen by all.
pāṇpēci
speaking flattering words;

paṭutalaiyil palikoḷkaitavirīr

pāmpiṉoṭupaṭarcaṭaimēl mativaitta paṇpīr

vīṇpēci(2) maṭavārkai veḷvaḷaikaḷ koṇṭāl
if you snatch the white bangles from the hands of ladies by speaking unnecessary words.

viṭṭatu ōrcaṭaitāḻa
the caṭi which is loosened to hang low.
vīṇaiviṭaṅkāka
the vīṇi to appear beautiful and conspicuous.
vītiviṭai ēṟuvīr
you mount and ride on a bull in the streets.

vaṭṭavārkuḻal maṭavārtammai mayalceytal mātavamō mātimaiyō
is it fitness or an act of penance to cuse the ladies who have long tresses of hir which they dress as a circle, to be infatuated with love?
vāṭṭam ellām tīrakkaṭṭi emakku īvatutāṉ eppōtu
when are you going to give me bars of gold to remove all my distress?

{7:46}__5+
{$} kaṭal nākaik kārōṇam mēvi iruntīrē
you are seated with desire in nākikkārōṇam on the sea-shore.
miṇṭu āṭittiritantu
wandering by indulging in vin acts.
veṟuppaṉavē ceytu
committing only detestable acts.
viṉaikkēṭu palapēci vēṇṭiyavā tirivīr
you wander according to your whim speaking words which are useless.
toṇṭu āṭittirivēṉaittoḻumpu talaikku ēṟṟum cuntaraṉē
beautiful god who does not bestow your grace, having accepted as your protege me who is wandering doing humble services to you.
kantam mutal āṭai āparaṇam paṇṭārattē eṉakkup paṇittaruḷa vēṇṭum
you must order from your treasury, dress, sandal-paste and ornaments.
paṇṭutāṉ piramāṇam oṉṟu uṇṭē
is there any evidence for anybody having seen you before?
nummaikkaṇṭārkkum kāṇpu aritāyk kaṉalāki nimirntīr
you rose high as a column of fire difficult to be seen by goda (Māl and Piramaṉ) who had seen you on other occasions (ēṟṟum is an adverbial participle; the syntactical link is toḻumputalaikku ēṟṟum, tirivīr.)
[[Variant reading: (1) uṇṭō]]
{7:46}__6+
{$} ilavu itaḻvāy umaiyōṭu erutu ēṟi
riding on a bull with Umi who has lips like the flowers of the red-flowers of the silk-cotton tree.
pūtam icaipāṭa
the pūtam to sing.
iṭupiccaikku ēccu uccam pōtu
in the noon when sacrificial fire is mintined, to receive the alms given.
pala akampukku uḻitarvīr
you wander entering into many houses.
paṭṭōṭu cāntam paṇittaruḷātirukkiṉṟa paricu eṉṉa paṭiṟō
what a cruelty is the manner by which you keep quiet and do not order silk and sandal-paste!
aṉṟu amarar vēṇṭa
when the immortals requested you in the distant past.
ulavutiraikkaṭal nañcai uṇṭaruḷic ceytatu umakku irukka oṇṇātiṭavē
as you had unbounded compassion which made you not to turn a deaf ear to their request, you consumed the poison which rose in the ocean which is always moving.
kalavam mayiliyalavarkaḷ naṭam āṭum celvakkaṭal nākaik kārōṇam mēvi iruntīrē
you were in wealthy nākikkārōṇam on the sea-shore, where ladies who are as tender as the peacock with a big til, dance.

{$} kaṭal nākaikkārōṇam mēvi iruntīrē
[[see 1st verse.]]
tūcuuṭaiya akal alkul tūmoḻiyāḷ uṭaltolaiyāta kālattu ōrcoṟpāṭāy vantu
coming as on mutual under-standing when you tried to remove the love-quarrel which did not end, of the lady speaking pure words and a broad waist on which a valuable cloth is worn.
tēcuuṭaiya ilaṅkaiyarkōṉ varai eṭukka aṭarttu
pressing down the King of the inhabitants of Ilaṅki, whose body was lustrous when he lifted the mountin.
tīppiyakītampāṭa
when he sang divine music
tēroṭu vāḷ koṭuttīr
you gave him a chariot and a sword.
nēcam uṭai aṭiyavarkaḷ varuntāmai arunta
the devotees who have love towards you to eat their food without suffering from hunger, during scarcity.
niṟaimaṟaiyōr uṟaivīḻimiḻalaitaṉil nittam kācuaruḷicceytīr
you granted dily one coin in vīḻimiḻali where brahmins full of vēdic lore live.
ivai eṉakku(um) aruḷavēṇṭum
you must grant me (also) like that a coin as subsistance allowance for a day.

see 1st verse.
māṟṟamēl oṉṟu uraiyīr vāḷā nīriruntīr
you do not give any reply to my request you keep silent.
vāḻvippaṉ', eṉa āṇṭīr
you voluntarily admitted me as your slave saying I shall help you to live.
umakku vaḻi aṭiyēṉ
I am a hereditary slave to you
āṟṟavēl tiru uṭaiyīr
you have plenty of wealth.
nalkūrntīrallīr
you have not become poor.
aṇiārūr pukappeyta arunitiyam ataṉil
in the heap of rare wealth which you have accumulated in beautiful ārūr.
tōṟṟam mikumukkūṟṟil orukūṟu vēṇṭum
I must get one third of the three portions, which I think I must get.
tārīrēl oru poḻutum aṭi eṭukkal oṭṭēṉ
I will not allow you to go beyond out at any time, if you do not give me; I shall catch hold of your legs.
kāṟṟu aṉaiya kaṭum parimā ēṟuvutu vēṇṭum
I want a galloping horse whose speed is like the wind; to ride upon.

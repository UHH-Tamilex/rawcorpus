{$} kalikkacci aṉēkataṅkāpatam
Aṉēkataṅkāpatam in Kacci of bustle.
tēṉ ney purintu uḻal ceñcaṭai emperumāṉatu iṭam
is the place of our god who has a red caṭi and wanders desiring the honey.
[[tēṉ is also known by the word tēṉey]]
[[PP: tēṉeyyoṭu kiḻaṅku māṟiyōr (porunarāṟṟuppaṭi, 214).]]
tikaḻ (1) aiṅkaṇai akkōṉai erittu eri āṭiiṭam
the place of Civaṉ who dances in fire, burning that chief who has eminent five arrows.
kulavāṉatu iṭam
the place of Civaṉ of superior nature.
kuṟaiyā maṟaiyām māṉai (2) iṭattu ōr kaiyaṉ iṭam
the place of the god who holds in his left hand a deer which has spots not decreasing in numbers;
[[maṟai - puḷḷi: spot]]
[[PP: maṟai ēṟṟiṉ mēliruntu āṭittuṟai ampi
(Kalittoki, 103-38)]]
matam māṟupaṭap poḻiyum malaipōl (3) āṉai uritta pirāṉatu iṭam
and the place of the master who flayed an elephant which had the appearance of a hill and which was inimical and from which fluid of must flowed.
[[Variant reading: (1) aiṅkaṇaiyakōṉai (2) iṭattor kaiyaṉiṭam (3) yāṉai]]

kūṟunaṭaik kuḻikaṭ pakuvāyaṉa pēy ukantu āṭaniṉṟu ōriṭa
when the old jackals houls without ceasing and pēys which have large open mouths and sunken eyes and short in statu with a quick paces, and dance out of joy, (kūṟunaṭai-kuṟunaṭai the first letter is lengthened to suit rhyme).
vēṟupaṭak kuṭakattilai ampalavāṇaṉ niṉṟu āṭal virumpum iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who dwells in ampalam in tilli in the west desires dancing standing in a superior and different way
[[Pērūr near Coimbatore is known as western Citamparam]]
ēṟuviṭaik koṭi emperumāṉ, imaiyōr perumāṉ umaiyāḷ kaṇavaṉ, āṟu caṭaikku uṭai appaṉ iṭam
and the place of the father who has a river on his caṭi, the husband of Umiyāl, the god of the celestials who do not wink an our Lord who has a distinguished flay of a bull.

koṭikaḷiṭaik kuyil kūvum iṭam
the place where indian cuckoos cry in the creepers.
mayil ālum iṭam
the place where peacocks dance.
ceṭikoḷ viṉaippakai tīrum iṭam
the place where the inimical acts which are like the bush of luxuriant growth are removed.
maḻuvāḷ uṭaiya, piṟaittuṇṭam muṭi, kaṭikoḷ puṉalcaṭai koṇṭa, nutalkaṟaikkaṇṭaṉ iṭam
the place of the warrior who has a black neck, a battle-axe and a crescent worn as a chaplet on the forehead and a water of brightness borns on the caṭi.
(1) tiru ākum iṭam
the place where wealth flourishes.
tirumārpakalattu aṭikaḷ iṭam
the place of the god who wears many ornamanets on the beautiful chest.
[[mārpakalam should be split as mārpa+kalam and not mārpu+akalam if it is split in the latter way it would be redundant; if these words are transposed as akalam mārpu then it will mean the broad chest.]]
aḻal vaṇṇaṉ iṭam
and the place of the god who has the colour of fire.

koṅku nuḻaittaṉa vaṇṭu aṟai koṉṟaiyum kaṅkaiyum tiṅkaḷum cūṭucaṭai, maṅkul, nuḻai malaimaṅkaiyai naṅkaiyaip paṅkiṉil taṅka (1) uvantu aruḷ cey
Civaṉ who felt joy in placing in his half a distinguished lady of the dughter of the mountin; and has a caṭi in which he bears koṉṟi flowers which are like gold made into thin plates on which bees hum loudly, Kaṅki and a crescent.
caṅku kuḻaiccevi koṇṭu aruvittiraḷ (1) pāy aviyāt taḻal pōl uṭaittam aṅkai maḻuttikaḻkaiyaṉ iṭam
the place of the god in whose palms the red hot iron is shining brightly without being extinguished in spite of the fact that the accumulation of white rays flow like streams from the ear-ring made of conch.

paitta paṭattalai āṭu aravam payilkiṉṟaiṭam payilap pukuvār
those people who enter into this world which never leave the head of āticēṭaṉ who can dance spreading his head which consists of hoods.
cattam oṭu neṟivaitta iṭam
is the place where they control the mind and establish it in a religious course.
tikaḻkiṉṟa iṭam
is the place which to their mind is superior.
tiruvāṉ aṭikkē vaitta maṉattavar pattar maṉam koḷa vaitta iṭam
the place in which the minds of devotees were dedicated to be occupied by God by people who placed their minds at the feet if Civaṉ, the Lord of wealth.
maḻuvāḷ uṭaiya attaṉ iṭam
the place of the master who has a weapon of a battle-axe.
aḻal vaṇṇaṉ iṭam
and the place of the god who has the colour of fire.

is the place which destroys the acute sufferings on my relatives by the servants of the god of death who has a club as his weapon.

the place where those who deditate on Civaṉ in this birth with a body, cast their bodies.
karu (1) nañcai nukarnta kaṇṭam uṭaip pirāṉatu iṭam

kaṭal ēḻu kaṭanta aṇṭam uṭaip perumāṉatu iṭam
and the place of the god who has not only this world firt by the seven oceans but also the whole of the universe.

kaṭṭu mayakkam aṟuttavar kaitoḻutu ēttum iṭam
is the place worshipped by those who severed the confounded state of being bound by attachments, with joined hands and prised by them.
viṭai ūrti iṭam
the place of the god who has a bull as his vehicle.
(1) aṭṭa puyaṅkap pirāṉatu iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who wears eight cobra as his ornaments (the eight cobras are (1) vācuki (2) aṉantaṉ (3) takkaṉ (4) caṅkapīlaṉ (5) Kuḷikaṉ (6) patumaṉ (7) makāpatūmaṉ (8) Kārkkōṭakaṉ.)
katirōṉ oḷiyāl viṭṭa (2) iṭam
in the garden into which the sun's rays do not enter.
kuyil pēṭai taṉ cēvaloṭu āṭum iṭam orumātavimōṭu maṭṭu mayaṅki aviḻnta malar maṇam puṇarum iṭam
and the place where the female of the Indian cuckoo sports with its male and the unequalled flower of the common delight of the woods combines with fragrance, blossoming with over flowing honey.

caṅkaiyavar puṇartaṟku ariyāḷ
Civaṉ who is difficult to be united by people who are always in doubt.

the dispenser of good things who dances standing in the cremation ground to be witnessed by the lady whose teeth are like the buds of arabian jasmine and who has great fame which never leaves her.
(2) emkaiyavaṉ

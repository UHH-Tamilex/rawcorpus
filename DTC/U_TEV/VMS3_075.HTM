{3:75}__1+
{$} imaiyōr, emtamatu cintaipiriyāta perumāṉ' eṉa iṟaiñci vantu tuticeyya
the celestials who do not wink, worship saying He is the god who do not leave our minds, and come to this world and prise him.
vaḷar (1) tūpamoṭu tīpam malivāymaiyataṉāy, antiamarcantipala arccaṉaikaḷ ceyya
and perform many arccaṉis in the evenings and mornings according to special rituals with lights and increasing incense.
amarkiṉṟa aḻakaṉ
the handsome god who desires them
[[Variant reading: ((1) tīpamoṭu tūpamali]]
cantammalikuntaḷa nalamātiṉoṭu mēvupati caṇpai nakarē
is the city of caṇpi, the place where he resides with the good Umi having beautiful tresses of hir.
{3:75}__2+
{$} aṅkam, virituttiaravu, āmai viravu āramamar mārpil aḻakaṉ
the handsome god with a chest on which there is a garland of bones, cobra with spreading spots on its hood, sheel of the tortoise all joining together.
paṅkayamukattu arivaiyōṭu piriyātu payilkiṉṟa pati (tāṉ)
the city from which he resides without parting with Umi whose face is like the lotus flower:
poṅku paravaittiraikoṇarntu pavaḷattiraḷ polinta ayalē, puricaṅku ippi taraḷat tiraḷ piṟaṅku oḷikoḷ caṇpai nakarē
is the city of caṇpi where by the side of the heaps of coral brought by the waves of the raging sea, the heaps of pearls, brought forth from conches spiralling clock-wise and sheel-cysters, shine in contrast to each other. (polintavayalē in the fields where they shine)
{3:75}__3+
{$} pōḻum matitāḻum natipoṅku aravu taṅku puri puṉcaṭaiyiṉaṉ
the god with a golden twisted caṭi on which stay the crescent which looks like being cut, a river that flows downwards, and an angry cobra.
yāḻiṉmoḻi māḻaiviḻi ēḻai iḷamātiṉoṭu iruntapati(tāṉ)
the city in which he resides with young Umi who has no individual actions of her own, who has eyes like the tender mango cut into two, and words as sweet as the music of yāḻ.
vāḻai, vaḷar ñāḻal, makiḻ, maṉṉupu(ṉ)ṉai tuṉṉupoḻilmāṭu
in the part where the plintin trees, thriving fetil cassia, (ñāḻal pulinakakkoṉṟai) pointed-leafed and ape-flowered trees (makiḻ), and the long-standing mast-wood trees are very close to each other.
maṭalār tāḻai mukiḻvēḻam mikutantameṉa untu takucaṇpainakarē
is the grand city of canpi where the fragrant screw-pines with long leaves put forth buds like the highly valuable tusks of elephants.
{3:75}__4+
{$} muḻavukoṭṭa
when the muḻavu is played on a high pitch.
iṭṭa aṭi vaṭṭaṇaikaḷ kaṭṭa
the paces that were kept to measure of time to show gesture with both hands.
naṭṭam āṭikulavum (pati)
the city where the dancing god is rejoicing.
kulavum paṭṭanutal kaṭṭumalarmaṭṭumali pāvaiyoṭu mēvupati
and the city where he resides with a lady who has the rich fragrance of flowers and a shining plate of gold tied on the forehead.
vaṭṭamati taṭṭumali poḻiluḷ tamatu vāy�mu vaḻuvātamoḻiyār caṭṭakalai(1) eṭṭu maruvu eṭṭum vaḷartattaipayil caṇpai nakarē
is the city of caṇpi where the growing parrots practise the entire sixty-four arts learnt by the people whose words do not swerve from truth, in the prosperous parks where the full moon is obstructed in its path by their hights.
[[Variant reading: (1)eṭṭu marumeṭṭum]]
{3:75}__5+
{$} paṇ aṅku eḻuvupāṭaliṉoṭu āṭal piriyāta paramēṭṭi pakavaṉ
the supreme being in the exalted place, one who has the six attributs of wealth etc, who never ceases from dancing and singing for which melodies are produced (pakam - the six attributs aicuvariyam, vīriyam, pukaḻ, tiru, ñāṉam, virākkiyam; one who possesses all these is known as pakavaṉ).
aṇaṅku keḻuvupākam uṭai(ya) ākam uṭai(ya) aṉparperumāṉatu iṭam ām
the place of the great god of the devotees, who has a person on one half of which there is a lady.
[[aṇaṅkukeḻuvu have become aṇaṅkukeḻuvu; cf. eṭutta naṟaviṉ kulaiyalaṅkāntaḷ (Kalittoki, 40-12); taḻaṅkural muraciṟ cāṟṟi (cīvakacintāmani 378) and their commentaries.]]
iṇaṅku eḻuvi āṭukoṭi māṭam atil nīṭuvirai ār puṟavu elām
in the small jungles of excessive fragrance and in the storeys of mansions where the fluttering flags which have been set up, look similar.
taṇ am keḻuvi ēṭu alarkoḷ tāmaraiyil aṉṉam vaḷar caṇpai nakarē
is the city of caṇpi where the swans are thriving in the lotus of floded petals which are very cool.
{3:75}__6+
{$} pālaṉ uyirmēl aṇavu kālaṉ uyir pāṟa utaiceyta paramaṉ
the supreme god who kicked to destroy the life of Kālaṉ (God of death) who approached pāḻaṉ (mārkaṇteyaṉ) to take away his life.
ālum mayilpōl iyali āyiḻaitaṉōṭum amarvu eytum iṭamām
the place where he resides with a lady wearing choice ornaments and has a tender nature like the dancing peacock.
cāli vayal kōlam malicēl ukaḷa nīlam vaḷarcaṇpainakarē
is the city of caṇpi where blue nelambo flowers grow in the having red variety of paddy fields where the carnatic carp jumps over.
ēlam malicōlai iṉa vaṇṭu malar (1) kiṇṭi naṟavu uṇṭu icaiceya
when the swarm of bees penetrate into flowers and drink the honey, in the garden where cardamom is abundanly growing.
[[Variant reading: (1) keṇṭi]]
{3:75}__7+
{$} viṇpoy ataṉāl maḻai viḻātu oḻiyiṉum
even though drops of rin do not fall as the clouds fail to pour.
viḷaivutāṉmika uṭai(ya) maṇ poy ataṉāl vaḷamilātu oḻiyiṉum
even though there is no yield in the lands due to loss of fertility which yield at other times abundant produce.
tamatu vaṇmai vaḻuvār, uṇpa karavār
(residents of Kāḻi) will not swerve from their bounty and will not conceal edibles.
ulakiṉ ūḻi palatōṟum nilaiyāṉa pati (tāṉ) caṇpai nakar īcaṉ aṭitāḻum aṭiyār tamatu taṉmaiyatuvē
it is the nature of devotees who fall at the feet of the god in the city of campi which has not been destroyed and has been permanent in this world in each one of the many aeons.
{3:75}__8+
{$} varaikkulamakaṭku oru maṟukkam varuvitta matiil valiyuṭai, arakkaṉatu urakkaracirattuṟa aṭarttu aruḷ purinta aḻakaṉ irukkaiyatu
the seat of the beautiful god who granted his grace after pressing down with his leg on the chest, hands and heads of the arakkaṉ who was strong but foolish to make the noble dughter of the mountin bewildered.
arukkaṉ mutalāṉa imaiyōr kuḻuēḻviḻaviṉil, tarukkulam nerukkum malitaṇpoḻilkaḷ koṇṭal aṉa caṇpai nakarē
is the city of caṇpi where the prosperous and cool gardens which are like clouds, afflict the garden of Kaṟpakam in the heaven, and where during the festival conducted for seven days the celestials beginning from the sun, gather together.
[[We know from maturikkañci that a festival was conducted for seven days; kaḻunīrkoṇṭa eḻunāḷ anti, āṭutuvaṉṟu viḻāviṉ nāṭu ār/ttaṉṟē (427-428); it is called pavunam; the seven day festival is mentioned by appar in his tēvāram on Kuṟukki vīraṭṭam patikam (2)]]
{3:75}__9+
{$} nīlavarai pōla nikaḻ kēḻal uru nīḷpaṟavai nēr uruvam ām; mālum malarāṉum aṟiyāmaivaḷar tī uruvam āṉa varataṉ
the god granting boons, who assumed the form of a growing fire not to be known by Māl and Piramaṉ seated in the lotus flower who respectively changed themselves into a perfect form of a pig resembling a blue mountin and the form of a long bird (swan).
cēlum iṉa vēlum aṉa kaṇṇiyoṭu naṇṇupati
the city where he stays with a lady whose eyes are like the carnatic carp and a group og spears.
cūḻ puṟavu elām cāli mali cōlaikuyil puḷḷiṉoṭukiḷḷaipayil caṇpai nakarē
is the city of caṇpi where paddy is abundant about the place, and where in the gardens cuckoo, parrots and other birds are living.
{3:75}__10+
{$} pōtiyarkaḷ
the buddhists who worship the pipal tree.
piṇṭiyarkaḷ
jins who revere the acoka tree.
pōtu vaḻuvātavakai uṇṭu
after eating, never missing any meal in any part of the day.
pala poy ōti avar koṇṭuceyvatu oṉṟum ilai
after uttering several false statements they have nothing good which they take upon themselves to do.
naṉṟu atu uṇarvīr
people who know the good path for you!
āti emai āḷuṭaiya arivaiyoṭu pirivili amarntapatitāṉ, cātimaṇi teṇtirai koṇarntu vayal puka ēṟi caṇpai nakar uraimiṉ
prise the city of caṇpi where the clear waves bring high quality pearls and throw them to enter into the fields.
{3:75}__11+
{$} vāriṉ malikoṅkai umai naṅkaiyoṭu caṅkaraṉ makiḻntu amarum ūr
on the place where caṅkaraṉ resides gladly with Umi, a lady of distinction who has breasts which swell by wearing bodice.
cāriṉ mural(1) teṇkaṭal vicumpuṟa muḻaṅku olikoḷ caṇpai nakarmēl
and the city of caṇpi which has the roaring sound of the sea on all sides, to reach the sky.
pāril malikiṉṟa pukaḻniṉṟa tamiḻ ñāṉacampantaṉ uraicey
composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ possessing knowledge of Tamiḻ and a fame which is permanent and on the increase in this world.
cīriṉ mali centamiḻkaḷ ceppumavar cērvar neṟiyē civalōkam
Those who are able to recite the chaste Tamil verses of increasing fame will attin in due course the world of Civaṉ.
[[Variant reading: (1) teṉkaṭal]]

{2:7}__1+
{$} vaṉṉikoṉṟai matamattam erukkoṭu kūviḷam poṉ iyaṉṟacaṭaiyil polivitta purāṇaṉār
the ancient god, Civaṉ who wore in his caṭi which is like gold leaves of indian mesquit, koṉṟi flowers, datura flowers, yarkam, and bel leaves to appear brilliantly.
teṉṉa eṉṟu varivaṇṭu icai cey tiruvāñciyam eṉṉai (1) āḷ uṭaiyāṉ iṭamāka ukantatē
Tiruvāñciyam where the bees with lines on them hum like teṉṉa' is the place that Civaṉ who admitted me into his grace, desired as his abode.
[[Variant reading: (1) āḷvu]]
{2:7}__2+
{$} kālakālar
Civaṉ is himself the god who killed the god of death.
karikāṉiṭai mānaṭam āṭuvar
will perform the great dance in the charred cremation-ground.
mēlar
is superior to all things, all living beings and all tēvars.
vēlaiviṭam uṇṭu iruḷkiṉṟamiṭaṟṟiṉar
has a neck that has become black by consuming the poison that rose in the ocean.
mālai kōlamati māṭam maṉṉum tiruvāñciyam ñālam vantu paṇiyappolikōyil nayantatē
the temple which is with splendour to be worshipped by the people of this world, in tiruvañciyam where in the storeys the beautiful moon stays in the evenings, is desired by him.
{2:7}__3+
{$} mēvil oṉṟar
if one worships Civaṉ with desire he will be identical with him. (Civaṉ is the sole thing.)
virivuṟṟa iraṇṭiṉar
has extensive spiritual wisdom and ritumls and ceremonies prescribed in the ākamams (Civaṉ is cakti and Civam).
[[iraṇṭu may mean also the supreme Being as the one, indivisible the attributeless; the aspect of god which cuses the five functions which are ciruṭṭi, titi caṅkāram, tirāpavam, aṉukkirakam.]]
mūṉṟumāy
being the energy of will, energy which provides the souls with gross and subtle bodies and with experience-planes according to their Karmam, the energy of wisdom which has the virtue of liberating the souls from the bondage of Karmam and establishing them in bliss. (It may also mean the three lights ie. the sun, the moon and the fire.)
mūṉṟum āy nāviṉ nālar
chants with his tongue the four vētams that investigate the three things ie. pati, pacu, pācan.
uṭal añciṉar
has the holy five forms of pari, āti, icci, ñāṉam and Kiriyi of the five energies. (It may also mean the pañcappiramamantiram bodies) (has his body bathed in the five produte of the cow).
āṟar
has the six attributes (has a river on his head)
ēḻ ōcaiyar
is the seven notes of the diatonic scale.
tēvil eṭṭar
has eight divine forms (this means aṭṭamūrttam of Civaṉ)
tiruvāñciyam mēviya celvaṉār
the god who dwells in tiruvāñciyam.
tam aṭiyārkaṭkup pāvam tīrppar; paḻipōkkuvar
will remove the sins of his devotees and obliterate reproach.
[[This verse contins many subtle truths according to the philosophy of civa cittāntam.]]
{2:7}__4+
{$} cūlam ēntivaḷarkaiyiṉar
Civaṉ has a long hand in which he holds the trident.
meycuvaṇṭu ākavē cāla nalla poṭippūcuvar
will smear profusely with good sacred ash fit for his body.
māmaṟai pēcuvar
will chant the great vētam.
cīlammēvum pukaḻāl perukum tiruvāñciyam
tiruvāñciyam whose name spreads everywhere by its fame got by good conduct.
ālam uṇṭa aṭikaḷ(ḷ) iṭamāka ukantatē
is the place which the god who consumed the poison, desired as his abode.
[[ḷ within brackets is augmentation for rhythm.]]
{2:7}__5+
{$} kai ilaṅkumaṟi ēntuvar
Civaṉ holds in his hand a glittering young deer.
(1) kāntaḷ am melviral taiyal pākam uṭaiyār
has as a half a young lady whose beautiful, soft fingers are like the petals of the red malabar glory lily.
aṭaiyār puram ceṟṟavar
destroyed the cities of the enemies.
ceyyamēṉik karumiṭaṟṟār
has a black neck in the red body.
tiruvāñciyattu aiyarpātam aṭaivārkku aṭaiyā arunōykaḷ
incurable diseases will not approach those who approach the feet of the father in tiruvāñciyam.
[[Variant reading: (1)kāntaḷa]]
{2:7}__6+
{$} aravampūṇpar
Civaṉ adorns cobras as his ornaments.
aṇiyum cilampu ārkka akam toṟum iravil nalla palipēṇuvar
begs with desire good alms in the night in every house, his cilampu worn as an adornment, to make a sound.
nāṇilar
does not feel ashamed to do so.
nāmamē paravuvār viṉai tīrkka niṉṟār
is determined to remove the Karmams of those who prise his names only.
tiruvāñciyam maruvi ēttamaṭamātoṭum niṉṟa emmaintar
our strong god who stood along with a young lady, to prise tiruvāñciyam reaching it.
{2:7}__7+
{$} (1) viṇṇilāṉapiṟai cūṭuvar tāḻntuviḷaṅkavē
Civaṉ will wear the crescent which is shining in the sky, descending low;
[[PP_EXT: piṟainutal vaṇṇam ākiṉṟu
(Puṟam, 1-9)]]
kaṇṇiṉāl anaṅkaṉ uṭalam poṭi ākkiṉār
reduced to ashes the body of Anaṅkaṉ by his frontal eye.
paṇṇil āṉaicaipāṭalmalkum tiruvāñciyattu aṇṇalār tam aṭipōṟṟavallārkku allal illaiyē
there will be no sufferings for those who have knowledge of vētam and ākamam to prise the feet of the god in Tiruvāñciyam where singing of songs which have the seven notes is always increasing (Anaṅkaṉ is not used in its derivative sense).
[[Variant reading: (1) viṇṇilvāṉ]]
{2:7}__8+
{$} māṭamnīṭukoṭimaṉṉiyateṉ ilaṅkaikkumaṉ
the King of ilaṅki in the south where the tall flags are permanently fixed in the storeys.
vāṭaūṭa varaiyāl aṭarttu aṉṟu aruḷceytavar
Civaṉ bestowed his grace in the past after having pressed him by the mountin to make him become weak and show resentment.
vēṭavēṭar
has the form of a hunter.
tiruvāñciyam mēviya vēntaraippāṭa nīṭumaṉattārviṉai paṟṟu aṟuppārkaḷ
those who have thriving minds to prise the King who dwells in tiruvāñciyam will cut at the root of Karmams and attachments of two kinds (attachment to the body and attachment to possessions and relations).
{2:7}__9+
{$} neṭiyamāloṭu ayaṉ ētta niṉṟārtiruvāñciyattu aṭikaḷpātam aṭaintār aṭiyārkaṭku aṭiyār
the devotees of those devotees who have approached the feet of the Civaṉ in tiruvāñciyam who remined to be prised by the tall Māl and Ayaṉ.
ceṭikoḷnōyiṉ aṭaiyār
will not get distressing diseases.
ceṟu tīviṉai tiṟampār
will not swerve from right conduct into evil acts which will destroy them.
kaṭiya kūṟṟamum kaṇṭu akalum
the dreadful god of death too will go away from them on seeing them.
pukaltāṉvarum
higher stage of existence (Civakaṭi) will come to them of its own accord.
{2:7}__10+
{$} piṇṭam uṇṭu tirivār
amaṇar who wander eating balls of cooked food.
piriyum tuvar āṭaiyar
and buddhists who wear a loose robe soaked in myrtle-dye.
miṇṭarmiṇṭumoḻimeyala
the harsh words of those two ignorant persons are not true.
poy ilai
there is no falsehood in my statement.
emiṟai
our Lord.
vaṇṭu keṇṭimarum poḻilcūḻtiruvāñciyattu aṇṭavāṇaṉ
and who dwells in all the words and who is in tiruvāñciyam surrounded by gardens where bees work work into the flowers and stay there.
aṭikai toḻuvārkku illai allalē
there will be no sufferings for those who worship with joined hands his feet.
{2:7}__11+
{$} teṉṟal tuṉṟu poḻil ceṉṟu aṇaiyum tiruvāñciyattu eṉṟum niṉṟa iṟaiyāṉai uṇarntu
realising Civaṉ who dwelt always in tiruvāñciyam where the southern balmy breeze blows and reaches the dense gardens.
aṭiēttalāl
by prising his feet.
naṉṟu kāḻimaṟai ñāṉacampantaṉa centamiḻ oṉṟum uḷḷam uṭaiyār uyarvāṉam aṭaivār
those who have set their minds on the verses of refined Tamiḻ composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ of Kāḻi which has many good things, will reach the highest world which is bliss.

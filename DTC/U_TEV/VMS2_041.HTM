{2:41}__1+
{$} kaṟṟārum kēṭṭārum
those who study about Civaṉ and hear about him.
maṇpukār
will not come to the earth for being born agin.
vāṉpukuvar
will attin eternal bliss
[[vāṉ here stands for eternal bliss.]]
maṉam iḷaiyār
will not get mental fatigue.
paciyālum kaṇ pukār
their eyes will not shrink into the socket even though they are hungry
(They will not know what is hunger)
piṇi aṟiyār
will not know diseases.
[[kaṟṟārum kēṭṭārum is like a lamp placed between two places: iṭainilait tīvakam]]
viṇpukār eṉa vēṇṭā
we need not say that those two people will not enter the celestial world (as it is a low position)
veṇmāṭa neṭuvītit taṇ pukārc cāykkāṭṭu em talaivaṉtāṉ cārntārē
they have definitely reached the feet of our Lord who is in Cāykkāṭu, a suburb of cool Pukār with long streets of white storeys.
{2:41}__2+
{$} kāṭē pōy maṟaintuṟaital purintāṉum
the Lord who desired to go into the forest and live hiding himself.
pūmpukārc cāykkāṭē patiyāka uṭaiyāṉum
he has Cāykkāṭu in the beautiful Pukār as his place;
viṭaiyāṉum
one who has a bull.
vāykkāṭu mutumaramē iṭamāka vantu aṭainta pēykku āṭal purintāṉum
and one who danced to the accompaniment of the songs of the pēy which reached the old banyan tree as its refuge, which is in an entensive forest.
periyōrkaḷ perumāṉē
is the King of the great people.
{2:41}__3+
{$} naṉṉeñcē
My good mind!
nī nāḷum niṉai (kaṇṭāy)
Think of him dily.
cānāḷum vāḻnāḷum yār aṟivār
who can know with certinty the day when we will die and the years we will live?
cāykkāṭṭu em perumāṟkē
to worship our Lord only who is in Cāykkāṭu
pū nāḷum talaicumappa
the head to carry dily load of flowers.
pukaḻ nāmam cevikēṭpa
the ears to hear his fame and names.
nā nāḷum naviṉṟu ētta
the tongue to prise him dily, practising the aforesid things; (if you can do those).
nalviṉaiyē peṟalām
you can get good actions of virtue:
[[nalviṉai-puṇṇiyam]]
{2:41}__4+
{$} poṉiyaṉṟa taṭṭalartta pūñcerunti kōṅkuamaruntāḻpoḻilvāy
in the garden which is bent down where the common cōng [[kōṅku]] and bright Cerunti whose buds have been made to blossom like a salver made of gold.
moṭṭalartta taṭantāḻai muruku uyirkkum kāvirippūmpaṭṭiṉattuc cāykkāṭṭu em paramēṭṭi pātamē
only the feet of the Lord who is the supreme being in Cāykkāṭu, a suburb of Kāvirippūmpaṭṭiṉam where the curved fragrant screw-pine whose buds have been made to unfold, spreads its fragrance.
kaṭṭu alartta malar tūvik kai toḻumiṉ
People of this world! worship with your hands scattering flowers whose fastening of the petals have been loosened.
[[pātamē kaitoḻumiṉ is the syntactical order; this is called in grammar as viṟpūṭṭupporuḷ kōḷ]]
{2:41}__5+
{$} kōṅkaṉṉa kuvimulaiyāḷ koḻumpaṉaittōḷ koṭiiṭaiyaip pāṅku eṉṉa vaittu ukantāṉ
the Lord who gladly placed on his half a lady who has conical breasts like the bud of common cōng [[kōṅku]] and plump shoulders like bamboos and a waist like a slender creeper.
paṭarcaṭaimēl pāl matiyantāṅkiṉāṉ
he had borne on his spreading matted locks of hir a crescent as white as milk.
pūmpukārc cāykkāṭṭāṉ tāḷ niḻaṟkīḻōṅkiṉār
those who are exalted under the protection of the feet of the Lord in Cāykkāṭu in beautiful Pukār.
ōṅkiṉār eṉa ulakam uraikkum
the world will say that they are the really exalted people.
{2:41}__6+
{$} cāntu ākanīṟu aṇintāṉ
one who smeared himself with ashes as sandal-paste.
cāykkāṭṭāṉ
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu.
kāmaṉai muṉtīntu ākam erikoḷuvac ceṟṟu ukantāṉ
one who was pleased to make the fire consume the body of Kāmaṉ to be burnt, and destroyed him.
tirumuṭimēl ōyntāra maticūṭi
having worn on his head the crescent which came to him decreased in size, to stay there.
oḷitikaḻum malaikaḷ tōḷ tōyntu ākam pākamā uṭaiyāṉum viṭaiyāṉ
the Lord who has a bull, having embraced the shoulders of the dughter of the brilliant mountin, has her as half of his form.
{2:41}__7+
{$} maṅkul tōy maṇimāṭam matitavaḻum neṭuvītic caṅku elām karaiporutu tiraipulampum cāykkāṭṭāṉ
the Lord in Cāykkāṭu where the waves make a noise dashing aginst the shore with all conches, and has long streets on the beautiful storeys of which the moon crawls and on which clouds rest.
koṅku ulāvari vaṇṭu iṉṉicai pāṭummalar koṉṟait toṅkalāṉ aṭiyārkku
to the devotees of the Lord who has a garland of blossomed koṉṟi [[indian laburnam]] on which the bees with lines which wander for honey, hum making a sweet sound.
cuvarkkaṅkaḷ poruḷ ala
the celestial worlds are not places of importance.
[[they will reach the feet of the Lord which are superior to the celestial worlds]]
{2:41}__8+
{$} toṭal ariyatu oru kaṇaiyāl puramūṉṟum eriyuṇṇa
having discharged an arrow which is difficult to be shot. On the three cities to be consumed by fire,
[[toṭal, toṭutal, toṭuttal; in the first sense it is touching]]
paṭa aravattu eḻil āram pūṇṭāṉ
adorned himself with serpents with hoods, as beautiful garlands
[[ārattaḻaiyarāppūṇṭu (Kōviyār, 91)]]
paṇṭu arakkaṉaiyum taṭavaraiyāl tivaraittōḷ ūṉṟiṉāṉ cāykkāṭṭai
Cāykkatu belonging to the Lord who pressed down in olden times the shoulders of Arakkaṉ which were as strong as the big mountins, with the extensive mountin (Kailācam)
iṭavakaiyā aṭaivōm eṉṟu eṇṇuvārkku iṭarilai
there will be no distress for those who think of reaching Cāykkātu as one of the places (desired by the Lord).
{2:41}__9+
{$} vaiyam nīr ēṟṟāṉum
one who received the water when the earth was donated to him (Māl),
[[when anything is given water is poured and then it is given as a sign of having renounced all rights on that property]]
malar uṟaiyum nāṉmukaṉum
the god of four faces who resides in the (lotus) flower.
aiyaṉ māriruvar/kkum aḷappu aritu avaṉ perumai
his greatness cannot be measured by both of them who have protection and creation as their duties
[[āl- expletive]]
taiyalārpaṭṭu ōvāc cāykkāṭṭu emperumāṉait teyvamāp pēṇātār teḷivuṭaimai
the wisdom of those who do not worship as the supreme god our great Lord in Cāykkāṭu where the singing by young girls never ceases.
tēṟōm
we will not believe it to be true wisdom.

having become so miserly as not to afford even a loin cloth of four inches in width which is tied near the thigh.
[[ulōvi: this is derived from ulōpam, a noun, meaning extreme greed; this verb has been used in the following quotations:

oṇṇumeṉṉiṉ aḵtu utavātu ulōviṉārum uyarntārō (Kamparāmāyaṇam, Kārmukappaṭalam, 46; Kitkintākāntam, pampippaṭalam 24)]]
aṟam kāṭṭum camaṇarum
Camaṇar who make a show of aṟam.
cākkiyarum alar tūṟṟum tiṟam kāṭṭal kēḷātē
without paying heed to the means of spreading scandal by the Cākkiyar (Buddhists)
teḷivu uṭaiyīr
you who have clear wisdom,
puṟaṅkāṭṭil āṭalāṉ pūmpukārc cāykkāṭuceṉṟu aṭaimiṉ
go and reach Cāykkāṭu in beautiful Pukār, belonging to the Lord who dances in the cremation ground.

the ladies with soft feet on which they wear anklets, after getting tired playing with the light ball striking it.
[[paintu=pantu; moḻimutaṟpōli;
tārakam putaitta taṇ malar naṟum paintu (peruṅkati 3-8-64)]]
[[nom paintu is not the correct reading]]
am pantum varikkaḻalum aravam cey pūṅkāḻic campantaṉ
Campantaṉ of beautiful Kāḻi where the beautiful balls and molucca beans make sound.
tamiḻ pakarnta cāykkāṭṭup pattiṉaiyum empantam eṉakkaruti ēttuvārkku iṭar keṭum
the sufferings of those who prise the Lord with the ten Tamiḻ verses sung (by him) on Cāykkaṭu thinking it to be their support, will perish.

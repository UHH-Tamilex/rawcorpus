{1:63}__1+
{$} eriyār maḻuvoṉṟu ēnti
holding on one hand a burning red-hot iron.
aṅkai iṭutalaiyē kalaṉā
and having the skull placed in the palm as the vessel.
varivaḷaiyār aiyam vavvāy
you do not snatch away the alms given by ladies wearing bangles which have lines.
mānalam vavvutiyē
you snatch away their great beauty. Why is this?
cariyā nāviṉ vētakītaṉ tāmarai nāṉ mukattaṉ
one who has four faces; who sings the Vētam with his tongue which never slips down.
periyāṉ piramaṉ pēṇi āṇṭa piramapurattāṉē
God who dwells in Piramapuram which was ruled with love by the great Piramaṉ.

{$} (1) peyal ār caṭaikku ōr tiṅkaḷ cūṭi
Having borne a crescent on the matted hir which has clouds.
[[peyal: cloud.]]
[[PP_EXT: peyaṟuḷi mukiḻeṉa
(Kalittoki, 56-18)]]
[[That Civaṉ has on his matted hir clouds is found in the following quotation:]]
[[PP: muṟaiyiṭṭa ceḻiya ṉetir muṟuvalittu añcalai eṉṉāk, kaṟaiyiṭṭu viṇ puranta kantara cuntarak kaṭavuḷ, tuṟaiyiṭṭu varukaṭalaic cuvaṟap pōyp parukumeṉap, piṟaiyiṭṭa tiruccaṭaiyiṟpeyal nāṉkum varaviṭuttāṉ
(Tiruviḷiyāṭal Purāṇam, 18-17)]]
pey palikku eṉṟu ayalē kayal ār taṭaṅkaṇ añcol nallār kaṇtuyil vavvutiyē
having gone begging alms that are placed in the bowl, you snatch the sleep of the ladies who speak sweet words and have large eyes like the carp fish.
iyalāl naṭāvi
Having administered in the proper manner.
intiraṉ āḷ maṇmēl
in the earth in which Intiraṉ ruled in the guise of a bamboo.
[[The name Vēṇupuram owes its origin to this story.]]
viyal ārmuracam ōṅku cemmai vēṇu purattāṉē
O God in spotless Vēṇupuram where the sound of the large drum (muracu) is always heard.
[[Variant reading: (1) piyalār]]

you do not snatch away the offerings of flowers along with the alms given (by those wives of sages, residents of Tārukavaṉam.)

God of targī who dwells in all places of beautiful shrines which have been worshipped by sages who performed penance.

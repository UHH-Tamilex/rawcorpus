{3:104}__1+
{$} viṇkoṇṭa mati cūṭi
wearing on the head the white crescent which has the sky as its place.
nīṭuviri puṉcaṭai tāḻa
the long, loosened and ruddy caṭi to hang low
peṇ koṇṭa mārpil veṇṇīṟu pūci
smearing on the chest which has a lady, the white sacred ash
pēṇār pali tērntu
begging alms without minding it to be a despicable act.
kaṇ koṇṭa cāyaloṭu ērkavarnta kaḷvarkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of the Chief who stole the beauty along with the tenderness that captivates the eye.
paṇ (1) koṇṭa vaṇṭiṉam pāṭi āṭum pariti(n) niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyamam where the swarm of bees which hum like music and dance round the flowers.
[[Note: This shrine is now known as Paritiayappar Koyil]]
[[pariti(n) niyamam: (n) within brackets is augmentation for rhythm; it will apply to all the following nine.]]
[[Variant reading: (1) koṇṭu]]
{3:104}__2+
{$} aravuoli, vil loli, ampiṉoli aṭaṅkār puram mūṉṟum niravavallār
Civaṉ who was able to raze to the level of the ground all the three cities of the enemies with the sound of the bow-string which was a serpent, the sound of the bow which was the mountin, Mēru, and the sound of the arrow which consisted of the air, Māl and fire.
nimir puṉcaṭai mēl nirampāmaticūṭi
wearing a crescent on the erect and ruddy caṭi.
(1) iravu ilpukuntu eṉ eḻilkavarnta iṟaivarkku iṭam(pōlum)
the place of the Lord who entered into my house at night and stole my beauty.
paravavallār viṉai pāḻpaṭukkum pariti(n) niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyamam which itself makes the good and bad acts, of those who are able to prise it, barren.
[[Variant reading: (1) iraviṟ pukuntu]]
{3:104}__3+
{$} vāḷ mukavārkuḻalvāḷ neṭuṅkaṇ vaḷaittōḷ mātu añca
to make the lady who has shoulders like the bamboo, has long-eyes like the sword, long tresses of hir and a bright face, to be frightened.
nīḷ(1)mukam ākiya paiṅkaḷiṟṟiṉ urimēl nikaḻvittu
covering the body with the skin of a black elephant which has a long face.
nāṇ mukam kāṭṭi nalam kavarnta nātarkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of the master who stole my feminine qualities by appearing to be shy before me.
pāṇ muka vaṇṭiṉam pāṭi āṭum pariti(n) niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyanam where the swarms of bees which have music in them hum and wander everywhere.
[[Variant reading:(1) mukaṭu]]
{3:104}__4+
{$} veñcuram cēr viḷaiyāṭal pēṇi
reaching the burning ground which is like the hot desert and desirous of playing there.
viripuṉcaṭai tāḻa
the loosened and ruddy caṭi to fall low.
tuñcu iruḷ mālaiyum (1) naṇpakalum tuṇaiyārpali tērntu
collecting alms having the pūtams as his companions in the dark night when all people are asleep and in the noon.
am curumpu ārkuḻul cōra uḷḷam kavarntārkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of Civaṉ who stole the mind of ladies, their tresses of hir to fall low by loosening.
pañcuram pāṭi vaṇṭu yāḻ muralum pariti(n) niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyamam where the bees sing the melody type pañcuram and hum like the music produced in yāḻ.
[[Variant reading: (1) naṉpakalum]]
{3:104}__5+
{$} nīrpulku puṉcaṭai niṉṟu ilaṅka
to shine on the ruddy caṭi to which water is attached.
neṭuveṇmaticūṭi
wearing a white and long crescent
tārpulku mārpil veṇṇīṟu aṇintu
adorning the chest on which there are garlands with white sacred ash.
talai ār pali tēr vār
collecting alms to fill the skull.
ērpulku cāyal eḻilkavarnta iṟaivarkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of the god who stole the imposing appearance and gentleness which is always on the increase.
pār pulku tol pukaḻāl viḷaṅkum pariti(n) niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyamam which is eminent by the ancient fame that has spread throught the world.
{3:104}__6+
{$} veṅkaṭuṅkāṭṭakattu āṭal pēṇi
desirous of dancing in the hot and fearful cremation-ground.
viripuṉcaṭai tāḻa
the loosened and ruddy caṭi to hang low.
tiṅkaḷ tirumuṭimēl viḷaṅka
and the crescent to shine on his head.
ticai ār pali tēr vār
obtins avilable alms in the several directions.
caṅkoṭu cāyal eḻil kavarnta caivarkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of Civaṉ who is the first civaṉ, and who stole the bangles made of conch, gentleness and beauty.
paiṅkoṭi mullai paṭar puṟavil pariti(n) niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyamam where the creeper of green mulli spreads in the forest tract.
{3:104}__7+
{$} piṟaivaḷar ceñcaṭai piṉ tayaṅka
the red caṭi on which the crescent is waxing to shine on the back.
periya maḻu ēnti
holding a big battle-axe.
maṟai oli pāṭi
singing the vētams with their proper accents
veṇṇīṟupūci
smearing himself with white sacred ash,
maṉaikaḷ palitērvār
obtins alms in the house,
iṟaivaṉai cōra eḻil kavarnta iṟaivarkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of the master who stole the beauty to cuse the bangles worn on the wrist to slip off.
paṟaioli caṅku oliyāl viḷaṅkum pariti(n) niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyanam which is eminent by the sound of the drums and conches.
{3:104}__8+
{$} ācu aṭaivāṉavar tāṉavarōṭu aṭiyār amarntu ētta
the devotees prise with desire along with acurar and the celestials who approach Civaṉ as their support.
mācu aṭaiyāta veṇṇīṟu pūci
smearing himself with the white sacred ash which has not the slightest blackness.
maṉaikaḷ pali tērvār
will obtin alms in the houses.
kācu aṭai mēkalai cōra uḷḷam (1) kavarntārkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of the god who stole the mind, to cuse the girdle worn on the waist set with gems, to slip off.
pācaṭait tāmarai vaiku poykaip pariti(n) niyamam (ē)
is Paritiniyanam where in the natural tanks the lotus flowers with green leaves are staying.
[[Variant reading: (1) kavarntār]]
{3:104}__9+
{$} nāṭiṉar kāṇkilar nāṉmukaṉum tirumāl nayantu ēttak kūṭalar āṭalarāki
not being avilable to Piramaṉ of four faces and Tirumāl who could not see Civaṉ though they searched for him, and playing with them when they prised imploring Civaṉ.
nāḷum kuḻakar palitērvār
obtining alms dily assuming the form of a youth.
ēṭu alar cōra ēḻil kavarnta iṟaivarkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of Civaṉ who stole the beauty of cuse the flowers with petals worn on the tresses of hir to fall down.
pāṭalar āṭalar āy vaṇaṅkum pariti(n) niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyanam where devotees pay homage to Civaṉ singing and dancing out of ecstasy.
{3:104}__10+
{$} kal vaḷar āṭaiyar kaiyil uṇṇum kaḻukkaḷ iḻukku āṉa colvaḷamāka niṉaikka (1) vēṇṭā
(Devotees of Civaṉ!) Let you not think that the wicked words of the buddhists who dress themselves in the robe dyed in red ochre and amaṇar who hold in their hands bowl from which food is taken and devoured and who are like stake for impaling criminals, are having any substance.

cuṭu nīṟu atu āṭi
bathing in well-burnt sacred ash
nal vaḷaicōra nalam kavarnta nātarkku iṭam (pōlum)
the place of the master who stole beauty to cuse the good bangles to slip off from the hand.
pal vaḷar mullai am kollai vēlip paritin niyamam(ē)
is Paritiniyaman which is surrounded by the forest tract where the arabian jasmine which blossoms like teeth, is growing.
{3:104}__11+
{$} pai aravam viri kāntaḷ vimmu pariti(n) niyamattut taiyal ōr pākam amarntavaṉai
on Civaṉ who desired on one half a lady, and who dwells in Paritiniyamam where malabar glory lily is thriving which blossoms like the hood of the cobra.
tamiḻ ñāṉa campantaṉ poyili mālai puṉainta pattum paravip pukaḻntu ētta
if one prises singing Civaṉ's glory in the second person, with the help of the garland of tamiḻ verses which do not contin any falsehood and adorned by Ñāṉacampantaṉ who had deep knowledge of tamiḻ.
piṟappu aṟuttal aiyuṟavu illai
there is not the slightest doubt that they root out being born agin.
avalam aṭaiyā (ē)
suffering will not go near them.

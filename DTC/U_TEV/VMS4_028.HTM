{4:28}__1+
{$} muṉpu elām (1) iḷaiyakālam mūrttiyai niṉaiyātu ōṭi
hankering after worldly pleasures the former days when I was young, without the slightest thought of Civaṉ who has a beautiful form.
kaṇkaṇa irumi
coughing making a sound Kankaṇa (Kankana is onametopoeia)
nāḷum karuttu aḻintu
without the thought of Civaṉ till the youthful age came to an end.
aruttam iṉṟi
without any aim.
piṉpakal uṇaṅkal aṭṭum pētaimār (2) pōṉṟēṉ
I was like the foolish women who spread grain to dry in the afternoon when there is no sunshine.
uḷḷam aṉpaṉāy vāḻamāṭṭēṉ
I am incapable of cherishing you with a loving mind.
atikai vīraṭṭaṉīrē
the Lord in Atikai Vīraṭṭam!
[[vīraṭṭaṉīr is the nominative of address of vīraṭṭaṉār]]
[[Variant reading: (1) iṉaiyakālam (2) pōṉṟeṉ uḷḷam]
{4:28}__2+
{$} atikai vīraṭṭaṉīrē
[[see 1st verse]]
kaṟaipperuṅkaṇṭattāṉē
Civaṉ who has a big black spot in the neck!
kāykatirnamaṉai añci
being afraid of the god of death who is like the scorching sun.
niṟaipperuṅkaṭalum kaṇṭēṉ
I hid myself in the big ocean full of water.
nīḷvarai ucci kaṇṭēṉ
I ascended the peak of the tall mountain (thinking foolishly that I can escape death)
[[kīḻatu nīrakampukiṉum mēlatu, vicumpiṉ piṭarttalai ēṟiṉum puṭaiyatu, nēmimālvaraikku appuṟampukiṉum, kōḷ vāyttuk koṭṭum kūṟṟattu, mīḷik koṭu nāvilakkutal aritē (Puṟattiraṭṭu, 402 (āciriyamālai).]]
piṟaipperuñceṉṉiyāṉē
Civaṉ who wears a crescent on the great head!
piññakā
destroyer of all things!
ivai aṉaittum aṟuppatu ōr upāyam kāṇēṉ
I do not see any means of completely destroying these things such as the fear of death and birth and others.
{4:28}__3+
{$} atikai vīraṭṭaṉē
Civaṉ in atikai vīraṭṭam!
nātaṉār eṉṉa nāḷum naṭuṅkiṉar āki
devotees quaking with fear daily to call themselves as masters.
taṅkaḷ ētaṅkaḷ aṟiya māṭṭār iṇai aṭi toḻutōm eṉpar
they will not know they are committing the wrong of thinking themselves as masters but will say we worshipped with joined hands his two feet!
ātaṉ nāṉ
I who am an ignorant person.
avaṉ eṉṟu eḷki
despiring you and thinking that I am yourself.
niṉpatam paravātu uykkum paḻaviṉai paricilēṉē
I have the present of bad acts done in previous births as I am wasting my days without praising your feet.
{4:28}__4+
{$} cuṭalai cēr cuṇṇameyyar
Civaṉ has a body on which he smears the powder which is in the cremation-ground.
curumpu uṇa virinta koṉṟaip paṭalaicēr alaṅkal mārpar
wears on his chest a garland in which green leaves mixed with koṉṟai flowers which blossom for the bees to drink the honey.
[[paṭalai: leaves;]]
[[PP: paṭalaikkaṇṇip parēreṟuḻttiṇitōḷ (perumpāṇāṟṟuppaṭai, 60; neṭunalvāṭai, 31)]]
paḻaṉam cēr kaḻaṉit teṅkiṉ maṭalai nīr kiḻiya ōṭi ataṉiṭai maṇikaḷ cintum keṭila vīraṭṭammēya kiḷarcaṭai muṭiyaṉārē
is the Lord who has a shining caṭai coiled into a crown, and who is in Ketila vīraṭṭam where the water in Keṭilam flows to tear the flat leaf of cocoanuts which grow by the side of paddy fields adjacent to tanks and scatters gems into it.
{4:28}__5+
{$} mantiram uḷḷatāka
when there is the mantaram.
maṟikaṭal eḻum neyyā
all the seven ocean which roll up and down being the ghee.
intiraṉ vēḷvittīyil eḻuntatu ōr (1) koḻuntiṉ vaṇṇam cintiram āka nōkki
seeing the poison which was like the flame with red colour which rose in the sacrificial fire of intiraṉ.
teruṭṭuvārteruṭṭavantu
after people who could convince him about his superiority came and convinced him.
(2) kantiram muralum cōlaik kāṉal am keṭilattār (ē)
Civaṉ is in Keṭilam which has good odour by the gardens in which the clouds thunder.
[[kantaram cloud, this has been change kantiram to suit rhyme; kāṉalaṅkāṉal; āl, am are augments used in combination of nouns and case-endings.)
[[variant readings: (1) koṭiyiṉ vaṇṇam (2) kantaram muraṉum]]

{$} maiññalam aṉaiya kaṇṇāḷ paṅkaṉ māmalaiyai ōṭi meyñarampu utiram pilka vicaitaṇintu arakkaṉ vīḻntu
the arakkaṉ ran and lifted the big mountain belonging to Civaṉ who has a lady whose eyes are as beautiful as the blue nelumbo flower, and fell down and his haste abated, and when blood flowed from the sinews of the body.
(1) kaiññarampu eḻuvik koṇṭu
producing music from the nerves of his hand.
kātalāl iṉitu coṉṉa kiññaram kēṭṭu ukantār keṭila vīraṭṭaṉār (ē)
Civaṉ in Ketiḷa vīraṭṭam, was pleased to listen to the music by the instrument Kiññaram which was played with devotion pleasing to the ear.

cempoṉ cey maṇi māṭat tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our Lord in Tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in Tiruvārūr which has beautiful storeys made of pure gold.
em panta valviṉai nōy tīrttiṭṭāṉ (kāṇ)
has done away with our irresistible acts that bind us and diseases.
ēḻ kaṭalum ēḻ ulakum āyiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is the seven oceans and the seven worlds;
[[PP: ēḻulakum āṉāṉ taṉṉai, ēḻkaṭalum ēḻ malaiyum āṉāṉ taṉṉai
(Nāvukkaracar, Tiruvārur (17) 3.]]
vampu untu koṉṟai am tār mālaiyāṉ (kāṇ)
wears a garland on the chest made of koṉṟi flowers which spread their fragrace.
vaḷarmati cēr kaṇṇiyaṉ (kāṇ)
wears a chaplet a crescent that is thriving.
vāṉōr vēṇṭa
when the celestials requested him.
ampu oṉṟāl mū eyilum eri ceytāṉ (kāṇ)
burnt all three forts with a single arrow.
(1) aṉal āṭi
dancing in the fire.
āṉ añcum āṭiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
bathed in the five products of the cow.
[[Variant reading: (1) aṉalāṭi kāṇ]]

vāṉavarkaḷ tuti ceytu ēttum tikku elām niṟainta pukaḻt tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our Lord in Tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in Tiruvārūr which gets its fame from celestials who prise him and pray to him, which fills all the four quarters.
akku ulām araiyiṉaṉ (kāṇ)
has in his waist chank-beads which are moving.
aṭiyārkku eṉṟum āramutāy aṇṇikkum aiyāṟṟāṉ (kāṇ)
is the Lord in Aiyāṟu who is as sweet as the rare nectar, to his devotees.
kokku ulām pīliyoṭu koṉṟai mālai, kuḷir matiyum kūr aravum nīrum ceṉṉit tokku ulām caṭaiyiṉaṉ (kāṇ)
has a caṭi in his head where the flower resembling the feather of a crane, moving peacocks feather, garland of koṉṟi, cool crescent, excessively cruel cobra and water are moving, united among themselves.
toṇṭar cellum tū neṟi (kāṇ)
is the spotless path on which the devotees tread.

cīr ēṟu maṇi māṭat tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our Lord in Tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in Tiruvārūr which has beautiful storeys increasing in paramount importance.
nīr ēṟu caṭaimuṭi em nimalaṉ (tāṉ, kāṇ)
is our spotless god on whose caṭi coiled into a crown there is abundant water.
neṟṟi mēl oṟṟaikkaṇ niṟaivittāṉ (kāṇ)
cused the forehead to be filled with a single eye;
[[cf. malai maṭantai viḷaiyāṭi vaḷai āṭu karattāl makiḻntu avaḷ kaṇ putaittalumē valliruḷāy ellā, ulakuṭaṉ tāṉ mūṭa iruḷ ōṭum vakai neṟṟi oṟṟaikkaṇ paṭaittu ukanta uttamaṉ (cuntarar, tirukkalayanallūr, 4)]]
vār ēṟu vaṉa mulaiyāḷ pākattāṉ (kāṇ)
has Umi, who has beautiful breasts on which a bodice is worn rising high.
vaḷarmati cēr caṭaiyāṉ (kāṇ) mātēvaṉ (kāṇ)
has on his caṭi a thriving crescent.
[[mati is used in the sense of piṟai; irukōṭṭu orumati eḻil peṟa milaicciṉai (nakkīratēva nāyaṉār tiruveḻukuṟṟirukki, 5) has the name of makātevaṉ (mahādevaṉ)]]
kār ēṟu mukil aṉaiya kaṇṭattāṉ (kāṇ)
has a neck which is like a very dark cloud;
[[koṇṭalum nīlamum purai tirumiṭaṟar (campantar, tirukkaḻumalam, (2) 2);
kār eṉṉa ārum kaṟai miṭaṟṟu ampalavaṉ;
puliyūr araṉ miṭaṟṟiṉ māṇpatu aṉṟē paṉimukilē; karumāl viṭaiyuṭaiyōṉ kaṇṭam pōṟ koṇṭal: ciṟṟam palattāṉ miṭaṟṟiṉ muṟpaṭu nīḷmukil (Kōviyar, 56, 323, 326, 347)]]

tēṉ ēṟu malarc cōlait tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ,ē)
our Lord in tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in tiruvārūr which has flower-gardens with honey in abundance.
kāṉ ēṟu kaḷiṟṟurivaip pōrvaiyāṉ (kāṇ)
covers himself with a skin flayed from an elephant which is eminent in the forest.
[[kāṉ ēṟu kaḷiṟu : the underlined words are generic epithets]]
kaṟpakam (kāṇ)
is the divine Kaṟpakam tree, granting all that devotees wish for.
kālaṉai aṉṟu utai ceytāṉ (kāṇ)
kicked the god of death, long ago.
ūṉ ēṟum 1. uṭai talaiyil pali koḷvāṉ kāṇ
receives alms in a broken skull to which flesh is sticking very much.
[[muṭaināṟum puṉ kalattil ūṇ alā ūṇāṉai (navukkaracar, tiruppuṉkūrum tiruniturum, 6)]]
uttamaṉ (kāṇ)
is the supreme god.
oṟṟiyūr mēviṉāṉ (kāṇ)
dwells with desire in oṟṟiyūr
āṉ ēṟu atu oṉṟu ēṟum aṇṇal (tāṉ, kāṇ)
the chief who rides on a bull that belongs to the bovine species.
ātittaṉ pal iṟutta āti (tāṉ, kāṇ)
the primordial god who broke the teeth of the sun-god.
[[Variant reading: 1. pakutalaiyil ]]

naṟappaṭu pūmalar tūpam tīpam nalla naṟuñcāntam koṇṭu ētti nāḷum vāṉōr, ciṟappōṭu pūcikkum tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our Lord in tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in tiruvārūr where the celestial beings celebrate festivals and perform dily worship bringing with them beautiful flowers with honey, incense, lamps, good and fragrant sandal-paste, and prise him;
[[ciṟappu annual festival,
ciṟappoṭu pūcaṉai cellātu vāṉam, vaṟakku mēl vāṉōrkkum īṇṭu (Kuṟal, 18)]]
piṟappoṭu iṟappu eṉṟum illātāṉ kāṇ
has no birth and death at any time.
peṇ uruvōṭu āṇ uruvam āyiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is the female form and male form combined in himself and the beings in the world.
maṟappaṭum eṉ cintai maruḷ nīkkiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
removed the confusion of my mind which was cught in sinful ways (this refers to his being a jin in his early life)
vāṉavarum aṟiyāta neṟi tantāṉ (kāṇ)
showed afterwards the way which even the celestials could not know.

imaiyōr ēttum ceṅkamala vayal puṭai cūḻ tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our Lord in mūḷaṭṭaṉam in tiruvārūr surrounded on all sides by fields in which red lotus grows, and which is prised by celestials who do not wink.
caṅkaraṉ (kāṇ)
does good to people
cakkaram māṟku aruḷ ceytāṉ (kāṇ)
granted a discus to Māl.
taruṇēntu cēkaraṉ
has on his head a young crescent.
[[taruṇēntu cēkaraṉē eṉum (centaṉār tiruvicippā, tiruvāvaṭutuṟi, 4)]]
talaivaṉ (tāṉ, kāṇ)
is the Lord.
am kamalattu ayaṉ ciraṅkaḷ aintil oṉṟai aṟuttavaṉ (kāṇ)
cut off one of the five head of Ayaṉ who is seated in a beautiful louts flower.
aṇi poḻil cūḻ aiyāṟṟāṉ (kāṇ)
is in aiyāṟu surrounded by beautifying gardens.
eṅkaḷ perumāṉ (kāṇ)
is our god, Civaṉ
eṉ iṭarkaḷ pōka aruḷ ceyyum iṟaivaṉ (kāṇ)
is the master who grants his grace, in order that all my sufferings may take leave of me.
(imaiyōr ēttum celvaṉ is the syntactical link)

cōlai cūḻnta, 1. teṉṟal ār maṇam kamaḻum, tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our god in tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in tiruvārūr where the abundant fragrance of the southern balmy breeze spreads everywhere, and which is surrounded by gardens.
naṉṟu aruḷi
granting good things.
tītu akaṟṟum nampirāṉ (kāṇ)
is our master who removes all evils.
nāṉ maṟaiyōṭu āṟu aṅkam āyiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is himself the four vētams and six aṅkams.
miṉ tikaḻum cōtiyāṉ (kāṇ)
is the brilliance in the lightning.
āti (tāṉ, kāṇ)
is the source of all things.
veḷḷēṟu niṉṟu ulavu koṭiyiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
has a flag in which a white bull is always prominent.
tuṉṟu 2. poḻil kacci ēkampaṉ tāṉ, (kāṇ)
is in ēkampam in Kacci which has dense gardens.
cōṟṟuttuṟaiyāṉ (kāṇ)
is in Cōṟṟuttuṟi.
[[Variant reading: 1. teṉṟalāṉ 2. pukaḻkkacci]]

1. cennalatta vayal puṭai cūḻ tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our Lord in tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in tiruvārūr which has fields of red soil and fertility, surrounding it on all sides.
poṉ nalatta naṟuṅkoṉṟaic caṭaiyiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
has adorned his caṭi with fragrant koṉṟi flowers which are as beautiful as gold.
pukalūrum pūvaṇamum poruntiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
dwells in pukalūr and pūvaṇam (pūvaṇam is a shrine near maturi)
miṉ nalatta nuṇ iṭaiyāḷ pākattāṉ (kāṇ)
has a lady who has a minute waist as flexible as the lightning on one half.
[[miṉṉum paṇiyum purai maruṅkuṟ peruntōḷi (Kōviyār, 5)]]
vētiyāṉ (kāṇ)
chants the vētams, (is the brahmin among gods).
veṇpuri nūl mārpiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
wears on his chest a sacred white thread of (three) strands.
koṉ nalatta mūvilai vēl ēntiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
held in his hand a trident of three blades which strikes fear into the minds of enemies and at the same time does good.
kōlam mānīṟu aṇinta mēṉiyāṉ (kāṇ)
has a body on which he smears the great sacred ash adding beauty to it.
[[Variant reading: 1. ceṉṉalatta]]

teṇtirai nīr vayal puṭai cūḻ tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our Lord in tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in tiruvārūr surrounded by fields full of water which have clear waves.
viṇṭavar tam puram mūṉṟum eri ceytāṉ (kāṇ)
burnt all the three cities of the enemies.
vēlaiviṭam uṇṭu iruṇṭa kaṇṭattāṉ (kāṇ)
has a neck that became black by consuming the poison that rose in the ocean.
maṇṭalattil oḷi vaḷara viḷaṅkiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
shone in the sun and moon so that their brilliance might increase
[[If maṇṭalattil is split into maṇ + talattil the meaning will be in this earth.]]
vāymūrum maṟaikkāṭum maruviṉāṉ (kāṇ)
is in vāimūr and maṟikkāṭu (maṟikkāṭu is the translation of vātāraṇiyam)
puṇṭarikak kaṇṇāṉum pūviṉ mēlaip puttēḷum kāṇpu ariya purāṇaṉ (tāṉ, kāṇ)
is the god as the ancient, who could not be seen by Māl who has eyes like lotus and the god, Piramaṉ who is seated on a lotus flower.
[[mēlai : ai augment used in combination, as of nouns and case endings.]]

tiruvārūril tirumūlaṭṭāṉattu em celvaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
our Lord in tirumūlaṭṭāṉam in tiruvārūr.
ceruvaḷarum ceṅkaṇ māl ēṟṟiṉāṉ (kāṇ)
has a big bull which has red eyes and rejoices in fighting
[[ceruvaḷarum : is a generic epithet;
Māl may also mean tirumāl]]
teṉ āṉaik kāvāṉ (kāṇ)
dwells in beautiful āṉikkā (āṉikkā - a shrine near tiruccirāppaḷḷi)
tīyil vīḻa maruvalar tam puram mūṉṟum eri ceytāṉ
brunt all the three cities of the enemies to be consumed by fire.
vañcakar pāl aṇukāta maintaṉ (tāṉ, kāṇ)
is the strong youth who does not go near deceitful persons.
aruvaraiyai eṭuttavaṉ taṉ ciraṅkaḷ pattum ainnāṉku tōḷum nerintu alaṟa aṉṟu tiruviralāl aṭarttavaṉ (kāṇ)
pressed with his sacred toe, all the ten heads and twenty shoulders of the arakkaṉ who lifted the mountin, Kaili which could not be removed from its place, were crushed, in the distant past, to make him roar.

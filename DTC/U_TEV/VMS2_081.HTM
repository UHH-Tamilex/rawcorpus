{2:81}__1+
{$} pūtattiṉ paṭaiyiṉīr
you have an army of pūtam.
pūṅkoṉṟait tāriṉīr
you wear on your chest a garland of beautiful koṉṟi flowers.
ōtattiṉ oliyōṭu
along with the sound of the sea
umpar vāṉavar pukuntu
the major deities who are Ayaṉ and tirumāl, and other celestials, having entered into your temple.
vētattiṉ icai pāṭi
chanting the vētam with music.
virai malarkaḷ corintu ēttum pātat tīr
you have feet which are prised by them after pouring forth fragrant flowers.
vēṇupuram patiyākak koṇṭīrē
you reside in vēṇupuram as your place.
(umpar the principal deities; ie = Piramaṉ, tirumāl; vāṉavar intiraṉ and his subjects)
{2:81}__2+
{$} cuṭukāṭu mēviṉīr
you are fond of residing in the cremation ground
tuṉṉam pey kōvaṇam tōl uṭai āṭai atu koṇṭīr
you wear a loin cloth made of many pieces stitched together, and have a dress and an upper cloth made of skin
[[puliyataḷ kōvaṇaṅkaḷ uṭaiyāṭaiyāka uṭaiyāṉ (Ñāṉacampantar, koccivayam (1) 7)]]
[[Variant reading: uṭaiyāyavatu.]]
umaiyāḷai orupākam aṭaiyāḷam atu koṇṭīr
you had Umayal on one half as a mark of identification
aṅkaiyiṉil paracu eṉum paṭai āḷvīr
you keep for use a weapon called paracu (battle-axe) in your palm.
vēṇupuram patiyākak koṇṭīrē
you had as your place vēṇupuram.
{2:81}__3+
{$} kaṅkai cēr caṭai muṭiyīr
you have done into a crown your matted locks of hir on which there is kaṅki.
kālaṉaimuṉ ceṟṟu ukantīr
you became great by destroying kālaṉ in the distant past. ((ukattal-uyartal); ukattal - uvattal: ukantīr you delighted).
tiṅkaḷōṭu iḷa aravum tikaḻ ceṉṉivaittu ukantīr
you were glad to place on your shining head a young cobra along with the crescent moon.
maṅkai ōr kūṟu uṭaiyīr
you have a young lady as a half
maṟaiyōrkaḷ niṟaintu ēttap paṅkayam cēr vēṇupuram patiyākak koṇṭīr
-you had as your place vēṇupuram where lotus flowers are in abundance, to be prised by scholars who have learnt maṟi, being crowded everywhere.
{2:81}__4+
{$} nīr koṇṭa caṭaimuṭimēl nīḷ matiyam pāmpiṉoṭum ēr koṇṭa koṉṟaiyiṉōṭu eḻil mattam ilaṅka
the long crescent moon, the cobra, the beautiful koṉṟi flowers, and beautiful dhātura flower to glitter on the matted locks of hir done into the form of a crown on which there is water (of Kaṅki).
cīr koṇṭa māḷikai mēl cēyiḻaiyār vāḻttu uraippak kār koṇṭa vēṇupuram patiyākakkalantīrē
you resided in vēṇupuram on which there are clouds, when the ladies wearing red gold ornaments were singing songs of benediction, standing on the wealthy mansions.
{2:81}__5+
{$} ālai cērtaṇ kaḻaṉi naṟavu uṇṭu
having drunk the honey in the cool fields where sugar-cane grows.
cōlai cēr vaṇṭu iṉaṅkaḷ aḻakāka icaipāṭa
the swarms of bees which reach the garden begin to hum like music, melodiously.
tū moḻiyār
women who speak truthful words.
kālaiyē pukuntu iṟaiñcik kai toḻa
to worship you with their hands bowing to you, entering your temple in the morning itself.
meym mātiṉoṭum pālai yāḻ vēṇupuram patiyākak koṇṭīrē
you hadas your abode vēṇupuram where pāliyaḻ is played, along with the lady who is in your form.
(ālai mills where sugar-cane is crushed and made into jaggery)
{2:81}__6+
{$} maṇi malku mālvarai mēl mātiṉoṭum makiḻntu iruntīr
you were seated gladly with a lady on a big hillock of increasing beauty. (tōṇiyappar who bestowed his grace on campantar is seated on a boat with Umi on an artificial hillock of masonry structure. People in cirkaḻi call it `mali' and above that, facing south is the temple of caṭṭinātar).
tuṇi malku kōvaṇttīr
you wear a loin cloth stitched together with several pieces of cloth.
cuṭukāṭṭil āṭṭu ukantīr
you rejoiced in dancing in the cremation ground.
paṇi malku maṟaiyōrkaḷ parintu iṟaiñca
the brahmins who are always doing services to you to worship you with devotion!
vēṇupurattu aṇi malku kōyilē kōyilāka amarntīrē
you desired the temple of increasing beauty in vēṇupuram as the good temple.
{2:81}__7+
{$} nīlam cēr miṭaṟṟiṉīr
you have a neck has the blue colour of the poison.
nīṇṭa ceñcaṭaiyiṉīr
you have long and red matted locks of hir.
kōlam cēr viṭaiyiṉīr
you have a beautiful bull.
koṭuṅkālaṉ taṉaic ceṟṟīr
you destroyed the cruel god of death.
ālam cēr kaḻaṉi aḻaku ār vēṇupuram amarum kōlam cēr kōyilē kōyilākak koṇṭīr
you resided in the beautiful temple situated in vēṇupuram where the fields filled with water are beautiful to look at, as the good temple. (ālam can be taken as the lengthening of the first letter; then it will be alam plough).
{2:81}__8+
{$} irai maṇṭic caṅku ēṟum kaṭal cūḻ teṉ ilaṅkaiyar kōṉ viraimaṇṭu muṭi neriya varaitaṉṉil viral vaittīr
you placed your toe in the mountin to crush the heads which had excessive fragrance, of the king of southern ilaṅki which is surrounded by the sea where the conches come to the shore moving swiftly for their food. (irai can be taken as a verb; then the meaning will be, with the sound to become big)
[[variant reaidng : tirai maṇṭic caṅkēṟum]]
karaimaṇṭip pērōtam kalantu eṟṟum kaṭal kaviṉ ār virai maṇṭu vēṇupuramē amarntu mikkīrē
you increased in greatness having resided in vēṇupuram of excessive fragrance which is beautiful by the sea where the big waves moving swiftly combine and strike aginst the shore. (kaviṉ ārvēṇupuram, viraimaṇṭu vēṇupuram)
[[Variant reading: perukōtam kalantēṟum; vēṇupumeyyamarntu]]
{2:81}__9+
{$} tī ōmpum maṟaivāṇarkku ātiyāmticai mukaṉ mālōṅkip pōy iḻintārum pōṟṟu ariya tiruvaṭiyīr
you have beautiful feet which could not be worshipped by Māl who went downwards and Piramaṉ (ticai mukaṉ having four faces for each of the four directions and who is in originator of the brahmins who mintin the three fires, and who flew high into the sky.)
pāy ōṅku marakkalaṅkaḷ paṭutiraiyāl mottuṇṭu cēy ōṅku vēṇupuram ceḻum patiyāt tikaḻntīrē
you were eminent having vēṇupuram as a fertile place where the ships with tall sils are tossed by roaring waves and are to be seen from a long distance (In the hymns of tēvāram and nālāyira tivya pirapantam brahmins are compared to Piramaṉ;
[[antaṇar talaimaik kuṇam kūṟuṅkāl piramaṉoṭu kūṟiyum, aracarai māyaṉoṭu kūṟiyum, vaṇikarai nitiyiṉ iḻaṉoṭu kūṟiyum, vēḷāṇ māntarai varuṇaṉoṭu kūṟiyum talaimaikkuṇac col nikaḻttap paṭum; avai ellām avaravar ceyyuḷuṭkaṇṭu koḷka (tolkāppiyam, marapiyal, 75, pērāciriyar);
maṟaiyōr.... aṉṟu ulakam paṭaittavaṉēyaṉaiyavarkaḷ (periya tirumoḻi 3-10-2);
paṅkayattu ayaṉavaṉ aṉaiya, tiṭamoḻi maṟaiyōr (Do. 4-3-3).]]
{2:81}__10+
{$} nilai ārnta uṇṭiyiṉar
The jins who have their food standign
[[Variant reading vuṇṭiyaṉa]]
neṭuṅkuṇṭar
and who are very low in character.
cākkiyarkaḷ
buddhists.
pulai āṉār aṟavuraiyaip pōṟṟātu
without paying heed to the religious instruction of both these base people.
uṉ poṉṉaṭiyē nilaiyākap pēṇi
desiring with stead fastness your golden feet only (pēṇi has been lengthened into metre pēṇī to suit metre)
nī caraṇ eṉṟār tamai eṉṟum vilaiyāka āṭkoṇṭu vēṇupuram virumpiṉaiyē
you desired vēṇupuram admitting people who say always you are my our refuge to your grace as if you having bought them by a sale deed as slaves.
[[It was the custom among jins to eat their food, standing;
vīṅkiyatōḷkaḷum tāḷkaḷumāy niṉṟu veṟṟaraiyē, mūṅkaikaḷ pōl uṇṇum mūṭar, kaiyiliṭu cōṟu niṉṟuṇṇum kātal amaṇarai (appar, tiruvārūr tiruviruttam (1) 6, 9)]]

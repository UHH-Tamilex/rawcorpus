[[Note: This shrine is now known as Tirukkavaḷi]]

{1:62}__1+
{$} (1)aṭal neñcē
my strong mind!
(2) nāḷ āya pōkāmē
our life being wasted without any useful purpose.
nañcu aṇiyum kaṇṭaṉukkē (3) āḷ āya aṉpu ceyvōm
we shall be affectionate towards Civaṉ only who adorns his neck with the poison, by becoming his slave.
araṉ nāmam kēḷāy
Listen to the names of Araṉ.
nam kiḷaikiḷaikkum kēṭu paṭāt tiṟam aruḷi
granting the state of not being ruined, to our relation and their relations.
kōḷ āyanīkkumavaṉ kōḷili em perumāṉ (ē)
the god who removes evils, is our Lord in Kōḷili.
[[(Variant readings: (1) maṭaneñcē (2) nāḷāṉa (3) āḷāṉa, aṉpui āḷākaaṉpu)]]
{1:62}__2+
{$}
aḻaku āmai, aṇi kēḻaṟ kompu āṭu aravattu ārtta tōṭu aravattu orukātaṉ
Civaṉ wears in one ear as ear-ring a cobra, has tied in the string of a dancing cobra a beautiful tortoise-shell and a beautiful hog's tusk, as ornaments.
tuṇaimalar naṟcēvaṭikkē pāṭu aravattu icai payiṉṟu paṇintu eḻuvār tam maṉattil kōṭaravam tīrkkumavaṉ
the god who removes distress in the minds of the devotees who bow and wake up from sleep practising music loudly and sing at the feet of the defectless twin lotus red feet of Civaṉ.
kōḷili em perumāṉ (ē)
[[see 1st verse]]
{1:62}__3+
{$} naṉṟu naku nāḷ malarāl nal irukku mantiram koṇṭu oṉṟi vaḻipāṭu ceyaluṟṟavaṉtaṉ ōṅku uyirmēl kaṉṟivarukālaṉ uyirkōkakkaṇṭu
destroying the life of Kālaṉ (the god of death) who with rage came upon the precious life of Mārkkaṇṭēyaṉ who worshipped with concentration Civan with good blossomed fresh flower and mantiram of the good Vētam-s.
avaṉukku aṉṟu aḷittāṉ
bestowed on him the boon of ever being sixteen years of age, on that day.
koṉṟai malar poṉ tikaḻum kōḷili em perumāṉ (ē)
is our Lord in Kōḷili on whose form the koṉṟi flowers glitter like gold.
[[It would make better sense if the reading were koṇṭavaṉukku' in the 3rd line]]
{1:62}__4+
{$} vanta maṇalāl iliṅkam maṇṇiyiṉ kaṇ pālāṭṭum cintai ceyvōṉ taṉ karumam tērntu
knowing the action of one who was concentraling his mind upon bathing the iliṅkam with milk in the river maṇṇi which was improvised by the sand brought by it.
citaippāṉ varum tantaitaṉaic cāṭutalum
when he punished the father who came with the intention of destroying his action.
caṇṭīcaṉ eṉṟu aruḷi
granting him the position of Caṇṭīcaṉ.
kontu aṇavu malar koṭuttāṉ kōḷili em perumāṉ (ē)
our Lord in Kōḷili gave him the (koṉṟi) flower in bunches as a mark of distinction.
{1:62}__5+
{$} vañcamaṉattu añcu aṭakki
having subdued the five senses in the deceitful mind.
vaikalum
dily.
naṟpūcaṉaiyāl
by the good act of pūcaṉi (Pūjā)
nañcu amutu ceytaruḷum nampi eṉavē niṉaiyum pañcavaril pārttaṉukkup pācupatam īntu ukantāṉ
Civaṉ became great by granting to pārttaṉ among the five brothers who was always meditating on him as the supreme being who consumed the poison as nectar.
koñcu kiḷi mañcu aṇavu kōḷili emperumāṉ (ē)
is our Lord in Kōḷili where the parrots which speak softly fly near the clouds.
{1:62}__6+
{$} (1) tāviyavaṉ uṭaṉ iruntum kāṇāta taṟparaṉai
the supreme being whom Māl who measured the world could not know though he was with Civaṉ as his half.
āvitaṉil añcu oṭukki amkaṇaṉ eṉṟu ātarikkum nāiyalcīr naminanti aṭikaḷukku nalkumavaṉ
who grants his grace to Naminanti Aṭikaḷ whose prise is on all mouths and who cherishes with love Civaṉ as having benign looks, subduing the five senses in the soul.
kō iyalum pū eḻukōl kōḷili emperumāṉ(ē)
is our Lord in Kōḷili where in the gardens the flowers which are considered superior appear in the twigs. (the meaning of this line is not clear)
[[Variant reading: (1) kāviyavaṉ]]
{1:62}__7+
{$} kalnavilum māl varaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has a big mountin, Kayili, bearing boulders.
kārtikaḻum māmiṭaṟṟāṉ
has a black neck which shines like the sable cloud.
col navilum māmaṟaiyāṉ
has the great vētam whose words are uttered loudly.
(1) tōttiram cey vāyiṉ uḷāṉ
is on the lips of those who prise him.
miṉ navilum ceñcaṭaiyāṉ
has a red caṭi which glitters like the lightning.
veṇpoṭiyāṉ
smears his person with white sacred ash.
aṅkaiyiṉil koṉ navilum cūlattāṉ
holds a trident in his hand, which strikes fear in the minds of his enemies.
kōḷili emperumāṉ (ē)
is our Lord in Kōḷili.
[[Variant reading: (1) tōttiram cēr, tōttiram col]]
{1:62}__8+
{$} antarattil tēr ūrum arakkaṉmalai aṉṟu eṭuppa
when the arakkaṉ who was driving his chariot in the sky lifted the mountin in that distant past.
cuntarattaṉ tiruviralāl ūṉṟa avaṉ uṭal nerintu mantiratta maṟaipāṭa
when he sang the vētam which contins mantiram as his body was crushed when Civaṉ fixed firmly his beautiful toe.
vāḷ avaṉukku īntāṉum
the god who granted him a sword named cantirakācam.
kontu aratta maticceṉṉikkōḷili emperumāṉ (ē)
is our Lord in Kōḷili who wears on his red head a crescent and bunches of flowers.
{1:62}__9+
{$} nāṇam uṭai vētiyaṉum nāraṇaṉum naṇṇa oṇṇāttāṇu
Civaṉ who is like a firm pillar and who could not be known by Vētiyaṉ (Piraman) who had no sense of shame and Nāraṇaṉ.
eṉai āḷ uṭaiyāṉ
possesses me as his slave.
taṉ aṭiyārkku aṉpu (1) uṭaimaipāṇaṉ icai pattimaiyāl pāṭutalum parintu aḷittāṉ
gave with affection many gifts when pāṇaṉ sang music with devotion as he had love towards the devotees of Civaṉ
[[This pāṇaṉ is mentioned by name as Pattiraṉ.]]
kōṇal iḷampiṟaicceṉṉikkōḷili emperumāṉ (ē)
is our Lord in Kōḷili who bears on his head a curved young crescent.
[[Variant reading: (1) uṭamaip]]
{1:62}__10+
{$} taṭukku amarum camaṇaroṭu tarkka cāttirattavar col iṭukkaṇ varum moḻikēḷātu īcaṉaiyē ēttumiṉkaḷ aṇṇal kaḻal
People of this world! without paying heed to the words of the buddhists who are well-versed in the science of Logic and camaṇar who wear mats desiring them, which will give you distress, prise the eminent feet of Civaṉ only.
naṭukkam ilā amar ulakam naṇṇalum ām koṭukkakilā varam koṭukkum kōḷili emperumāṉ (ē)
you can reach the world of the immortal which has no distress; our Lord in Kōḷili will grant boons which other deities are incapable of giving.
{1:62}__11+
{$} nampaṉai; nal aṭiyārkaḷ nām uṭai(ya) māṭu eṉṟu irukkum kompu aṉaiyāḷ pākaṉ eḻil kōḷili emperumāṉai
on our Lord who is in beautiful Kōḷili, who has as a half a lady who is like a tender bough, and whom the good devotees esteem as their wealth and who has the name of Nampaṉ.
vampu amarum taṇkāḻic campantaṉ vaṇtamiḻ koṇṭu iṉpu amaravallārkaḷ eytuvarkaḷ īcaṉai (ē)
those who are able to enjoy happiness with the reputed Tamiḻ verses of Campantaṉ of cool Kāḻi where there is abundant fragrance, will ultimately reach Civaṉ.

{2:121}__1+
{$} muṉṉam niṉṟa muṭakkāl muyaṟku aruḷceytu nīḷ puṉṉai niṉṟu kamaḻ pātirip puliyūr uḷāṉ
Civaṉ who is in Pātirippuliyūr where the tall mast-wood tree spreads its fragrance without ceasing, having bestowed his grace to a hare with a crippled leg which remined there, long ago
[[A sage by name Mankanar was cursed to become a hare with crippled leg and he was freed from that curse in this shrine]]
taṉṉai vaṇaṅkum taṉait tavamillikaḷ piṉṉai niṉṟa piṇi yākkaiyaip peṟuvārkaḷ
those who have not so much of good fortune as to worship Civaṉ in this shrine will obtin a body here to all diseases which never leave them, in the next birth.
{2:121}__2+
{$} koḷḷi nakka pakuvāya pēykaḷ kuḻaintu āṭavē
pēys which have a wide open mouths in which fire in shining, to dance, their minds becoming tender.
muḷ ilavam mutukāṭṭu uṟaiyum mutalvaṉ(ṉ) iṭam
the place of the chief who dwells in the cremation ground where red-flowered silk cotton trees grow.
puḷ iṉaṅkaḷ payilum pātirippuliyūrtaṉai uḷḷa
to meditate on pātirippuliyūr where flocks of birds are always living.
nammēl viṉai āyiṉa oḻiyum(kaḷē)
the acts, good and bad which are with us will cease. (oḻiyuṅkaḷ: kaḷ is a superfluous plural suffix)

{2:121}__3+
{$} maruḷ il nallār vaḻipāṭu ceyyum maḻuvāḷar
Civaṉ who has a battle-axe whom the good people without bewiderment of mind worship.
mēl poruḷ il nallār payil pātirippuliyūr uḷāṉ
who is in patirippuliyūr where good people who have no superior to them are crowded.
(1) veruḷiṉ māṉiṉ piṇai nōkkal ceytu veṟi ceytapiṉ
fixing his gaze on Umi who is like the female deer with startled looks; and striking fear in her.
aruḷi
bestowing his grace.
ākattiṭai vaittatuvum aḻakākavē
placing her in his body is also to add beauty.
[[Variant reading: (1) veruḷimāṉiṉ]]
{2:121}__4+
{$} pētiṉālum pukaiyālum uyttē aṭiyārkaḷ tām pōtiṉālē vaḻipāṭu ceyya
the devotees carrying and directing flowers and incense, to worship at the proper times.
puliyūrtaṉuḷ ātiṉālum avalam(m) illāta aṭikaḷ
the deity who is without fault by anything in puliyur; (1) (ātiṉālum-yātiṉālum)
[[uṟavu ātum ilaṉ eṉṟu (periya tirumoḻi, 5-5-8); ātum illai maṟṟavaṉil eṉṟu atuvē tuṇintu (tiruvimoḻi, 9-1-11)]]
maṟai ōti nāḷum iṭumpiccai ēṟṟu (uṇṭu) uṇappālatē
chanting the vētams dily is it proper for him to eat receiving alms that is given? (uṇṭu seems to be superfluous)

{2:121}__5+
{$} ākam nallār amutu ākka uṇṭāṉ aḻal
Umi who is in his body changed the poison into nectar and so Civaṉ consumed it; (aḻal: poison)
[[aḻalvāy nākattu āreyiṟu aḻuntiṉar (cilappatikāram, 5-124); aḻalum viṭattaiyuṭaiya araviṉ vāyil kūrtta eyiṟu aḻuntak kaṭiyuṇṭārum (do. aṭiyārkkunallār); vārcaṭaiyōṉ aruntiya nañcu amutu ākkiya ampikai (apirāmi antāti, 5); caṭāṭaviyār uṇṭakāḷaviṭattai amutu ākkum civāṉantamē (maturāpuri ampiki māli, 26).]]
aintalai nākam nallār parava(n) nayantu (aṅku) arai ār/ttavaṉ
who tied a cobra of five-hoods desiring it, to be prised by good people.
pōkanallār payilum pātirippuliyūrtaṉuḷ
in pātirippuliyūr where good people enjoying all pleasures of life are crowded.
pākam nallāḷoṭu niṉṟa em paramēṭṭiyē
is the god in the most exalted place who remins there with lady as his half.

{2:121}__7+
{$} aravappaṭum koṅku vaṇṭu aṟai kuḷirkāṉal vāy
in the sea-shore garden where the bees which rub the pollen in the flowers hum noisily.
caṅku aravap paṟaiyiṉ(ṉ) oli avai cārntu eḻa
the sound of the conches rises accompanied by the sound of the drum which can produce a loud sound.
poṅku aravam(m) uyar pātirippuliyūrtaṉuḷ
in pātirippuliyūr where sounds which are increasing in tempo are always loud.
(aṅku) aravam(m) araiyil(l) acaittāṉai aṭaimiṉ (ē)
(people of this world) approach Civaṉ who tied a cobra in his waist.
{2:121}__8+
{$} vīkkam eḻum(m) ilaṅkaikku iṟaivilaṅkal(l) iṭai ūkkam iḻantu alaṟa(v) viral iṟaiyūṉṟiṉāṉ
Civaṉ fixed his toe for a short while to make the King of ilanki who had rising pride, to lose his zeal and roar under the mountin.
pūkkamaḻum puṉal pātirippuliyūrtaṉai nōkka
to have a vision of partirippuliyūr which has water in which flowers spread their fragrance.
melintu aṇukāviṉai(n) naṇukuṅkaḷē
our acts will become wead and will not approach us and will become thin.
{2:121}__9+
{$} aṉṉamtāvum aṇi ārpoḻil maṇiārpuṉṉaipoṉ am tātu cori pātirippuliyūrtaṉuḷ
in Pātirippuliyūr where the beautiful mast-wood trees in the beautiful garden in which the swans leap, shed their pollen.
muṉṉam tāvi aṭimūṉṟu aḷantavaṉ nāṉmukaṉ
Māl who measured long ago with this feet the three worlds and nāṉmukan.
taṉṉam tāḷ uṟṟu uṇarātatu or tavanītiyē (uḷḷatu)
the god, Civaṉ who is the right conduct of penance, remins, whose head and feet could not be known even a little, though both of them made efforts.

(people of this world prise only patirippuliyūr of the chief who desires the white sacred ash, without listening to the words which were spoken out of jealousy by the big tērar (buddhists) and the low amaṇar who observe religious austerities wandering in the streets with bodies stripped off their clothes (puliyūrai ēttumiṉ is the syntactical link).

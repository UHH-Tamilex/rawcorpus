{2:42}__1+
{$} akku irunta āramum āṭu aravum āmaiyum tokku irunta mārpiṉāṉ
Civaṉ has on his chest a garland of Uruttirākkam, a dancing cobra, a shell of tortoise, combining together
[[akku may also mean bone;
akku irunta āram: a garland of bones]]
tōl uṭaiyāṉ
((He)) dresses himself in a skin;
veṇṇīṟṟāṉ
((He)) smears himself with white holy ash;
pukkirunta tol kōyil
the ancient temple into which he entered and stayed.
poy ilā meyn neṟikkē takkiruntār ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in Ākkūr where worthy people who are fit for the true path which is without falsehood, live.
{2:42}__2+
{$} nīr āra vār caṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has a long caṭi which is full of water;
nīṟu uṭaiyāṉ; ēṟu uṭaiyāṉ
((He)) smears holy ash; (He)) has a bull;
kār ār pūṅkoṉṟaiyiṉāṉ kātalitta tol kōyil
the ancient temple which he who wears beautiful koṉṟi flowers that blossom profusely in winter, loved.
kūr āral vāy niṟaiyak koṇṭu
having a mouthful of āral found in plenty;
[[āral: a kind of small fresh-water fish;]]
[[PP: nuṇ āral, paru varāl, kurūuk keṭiṟṟa, kuṇṭu akaḻi
(Puṟam, 18-9-10)]]
ayalē kōṭṭakattil tārā malku ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in Ākkūr where herons are increasing in the adjoining deep tanks.
[[tārā: a kind of crane;]]
[[PP: pakarat tārā aṉṉam pakaṉṟil paṇintu ētta
(Campantar, Caṇpinakar, 3);]]
[[PP: tārā ārum vayal cūḻnta cāḷakkirāmattu aṭikaḷai
(Periya Tirumoḻi, 1-5-10)]]
{2:42}__3+
{$} vāḷ ār kaṇ, centuvarvāy, māmālaiyāṉ taṉ maṭantai tōḷ ākam pākamāp pulkiṉāṉ
Civaṉ embraced the body which has beautiful shoulders, of the dughter of the mountin, who has lips like red coral and eyes like sword.
tol kōyil
his ancient temple
vēḷāḷar eṉṟavarkaḷ vaḷḷaṉmaiyāl mikku irukkum tāḷāḷar ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in ākkur where [there are] people of enterprise who are eminent and are renowned as vēḷāḷar
{2:42}__4+
{$} koṅku cēr taṇkoṉṟai mālaiyiṉāṉ
Civaṉ puts on a garland of cool koṉṟi which has honey and fragrance.
kūṟṟu aṭarap poṅkiṉāṉ
flew into a rage when the god of death came to snatch the life of mārkaṇṭēyaṉ
poṅku oḷicēr veṇṇīṟṟāṉ pūṅkōyil
the beautiful temple of Civaṉ who smears white sacred ash of increasing brightness.
aṅkam āṟōṭu arumaṟaikaḷ ai vēḷvi taṅkiṟār ākkūrival tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in ākkūr where brahmins who have learnt the abstruse vētams and six aṅkams perform dily the five sacrifices
[[The five sacrifices are 1) piramayākam, 2) tevayākam, 3) pūtayākam 4) pitiryākam and māṉtayākam.]]
{2:42}__5+
{$} āṭu aravam vīkkiṉāṉ
Civaṉ tied a dancing cobra
vīntu aḻintār veṇtalai eṉpu ākkiṉāṉ pala kalaṅkaḷ (ātarittu)
made many ornaments out of the bones and white skulls of those who are dead and gone (wishing for them)
ātarittup peṇ pākam ākkiṉāṉ tol kōyil
the ancient temple of Civaṉ who made one half as a lady wishing for her.
[[atarittu can be added along with ākkiṉāṉ also.]]
āmpal am pūm poykai puṭai tākkiṉār ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in ākkūr where people dug out a tank which has beautiful water-lily flowers, both white and red.
{2:42}__6+
{$} paṇ oḷicēr nāṉ maṟaiyāṉ
Civaṉ revealed the four vētams which have their accents and light of knowledge
pāṭaliṉōṭu āṭaliṉāṉ
sings and dances
kaṇ oḷi cēr neṟṟiyiṉāṉ
has a forehead that is bright by the frontal eye.
kātalitta tol kōyil
the ancient temple that he loved.
viṇ oḷi cēr māmatiyam tīṇṭiyakkāl veṇmāṭam taṇ oḷi cēr ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in ākkūr when the white storeys get cool light when the big and bright moon touches them.
{2:42}__7+
{$} vīṅkiṉār mummatilum vil varaiyāl ventu aviya vāṅkiṉār
Civaṉ bent by stringing the bow which was the mountin, to be consumed by fire and to be destroyed, the three forts of the acurar puffed with pride.
vāṉavarkaḷ vantu iṟaiñcum tol kōyil
his ancient temple where the celestials worship him coming to the earth.
pāṅkiṉ ār nāṉ maṟaiyōṭu āṟu aṅkam palakalaikaḷ tāṅkiṉār ākkūṟil tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in ākkūr where brahmins who retin many arts, six aṅkams in addition to the four vētams which are full of good things, in their memory.
{2:42}__8+
{$} kal neṭiya kuṉṟu eṭuttāṉ tōḷ aṭarak kāl ūṉṟi
pressing down the legs to crush the shoulders of īrāvaṇaṉ who lifted the high mountin having stones.
iṉṉaruḷāl āṭkoṇṭa emperumāṉ tol kōyil
the ancient temple of our god who later admitted him as his protege by his benign grace.
poṉ aṭikkē nāḷ tōṟum pūvōṭu nīr cumakkum taṉ aṭiyār ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in ākkūr where his devotees, who carry dily flowers and water to the golden feet, live;
[[PP: poṉ aṭiyē paravi nāḷum pūvoṭu nīr cumakkum niṉṉaṭiyār (campantar, neṭuṅkaḷam, 3);]]
[[PP: pūvoṭu nīr cumantu ēttip pukuvār (appar, tiruviyaṟu, paṇ-Kāntāram, 1)]]
{2:42}__9+
{$} naṉmaiyāṉ nāraṇaṉum nāṉ mukaṉum kāṇpu ariya toṉmaiyāṉ
the primordial god who could not be found out by Nārayaṇaṉ who has many good things and Piramaṉ of four faces.
tōṟṟam kēṭu illātāṉ tol kōyil
the ancient temple of Civaṉ who has no birth and death.
iṉmaiyāl ceṉṟu irantārkku illai eṉṉātu īntu uvakkum taṉmaiyār ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in ākkūr where [there are] people who have the nature of feeling joy in giving to those who went to them soliciting aid, without saying we have nothing to give.
{2:42}__10+
{$} camaṇ tērar nā maruvu puṉmai naviṟṟa
when the camanar and tēvar speak the vile words to which their tongues are accustomed.
pū maruvu koṉṟaiyiṉāṉ pukku amarum tol kōyil
the ancient temple into which Civaṉ who adorns himself with shining koṉṟi flowers, entered and stayed.
cēl maruvu paiṅkayattuc ceṅkaḻunīr paiṅkuvaḷai (tām) maruvum ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭamē
is the self-existing temple in ākkūr where in the tanks of greenish water red indian water-lily, and fertile blue nelumbo flowers are growing.
{2:42}__11+
{$} āṭal amarntāṉai, ākkūril tāṉ tōṉṟi māṭam amarntāṉai
on Civaṉ who rejoiced in dancing and dwelt happily in the self-existing temple at ākkūr.
māṭam cēr taṇ kāḻi nāṭaṟku ariyacīr ñāṉacampantaṉ col pāṭal ivai vallārkku illai ām pāvamē
there will be no sins with those who are able to recite the verses composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ who has fame that could not be measured, and [is] a native of Kāḻi which has many storeyed buildings.

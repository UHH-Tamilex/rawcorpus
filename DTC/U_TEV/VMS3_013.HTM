[[The decade contins in every verse the destruction of the three cities]]

{3:13}__1+
{$} miṉ aṉa eyiṟu uṭai viravalōrkaḷ tam, tuṉṉiya puram
the three cities of the enemies who had teeth shining like lightning, which were close to each other
cuḷinta toṉmaiyar
the primordial god who destroyed them by getting angry.
puṉṉai am poḻil aṇi pūntarāy nakar, aṉṉam aṉṉa (n) naṭai arivai paṅkarē
is the one who has as his half a lady having a git resembling that of the swan, in the city of Pūntarāy which is beautiful by parks of mast wood trees.
[[arivai -- here does not denote stage in the life of a woman, but simply means a woman; it generally denotes a woman between the ages of 20 and 25]]
{3:13}__2+
{$} mūtu aṇi muppurattu eṇṇilōrkaḷai, vētu aṇi carattiṉāl vīṭṭiṉār avar
the god who destroyed countless acurar in the three cities of old decorations, with a hot arrow
[[It is mentioned that the god of fire was at the tip of the arrow;
kuṉṟavārcilai, nāṇ arā, arivāḷi, kūreri kāṟṟiṉ mummatil veṉṟavāṟu eṅṅaṉē (Cuntarar, Tiruvāmāttūr, 1);
tiripuram eriyat tēvar vēṇṭa, erimukap pērampu ēval kēṭpa (Cilappatikāram, 6-40-41)]]
pōtu aṇi poḻil amar pūntarāy nakart, tātu aṇi kuḻal umai talaivar (kāṇmiṉ)
is the husband of Umi who has tresses of hir adorned with pollen, and resides in the city of Pūntarāy where there are parks made beautiful by flowers
[[kāṇmiṉ: In this verse and in verses nos 3,4,5,7,8 and 10 are expletives]]
{3:13}__3+
{$} tarukkiya tiripurattavarkaḷ tām ukap, perukkiyacilaitaṉaip piṭitta peṟṟiyar
god who had the nature of holding a bow to increase the prosperity of the Tēvar, and the proud inhabitants of the three cities to meet with destruction
[[perukkiya is used as an adverbial participle (ceyyiya eṉṉum vāypāṭu)]]
poru(k) kaṭal puṭaitaru pūntarāy nakark, karukkiya kuḻal umai kaṇavar (kāṇmiṉē)
is the husband of Umi who has black tresses of hir and resides in the city of Pūntarāy which has on its side the sea which dashes aginst the shore
[[porukaṭal has become porukkaṭal and karukiya has become karukkiya to suit the rhyme]]
{3:13}__4+
{$} nākamum varaiyumē nāṇum villumā
Having the serpent and the mountin as bowstring and bow respectively.
mākam ār puraṅkaḷai maṟitta māṇpiṉar
the great god who prevented the three cities to fly as they wished in the sky, and destroyed them
pūkam ār poḻil aṇi pūntarāy nakarp, pāku amarmoḻi umai paṅkar (kāṇmiṉ)
is the one who has Umi who speaks words as sweet as syrup of sugar, residing in the city of Pūntarāy which is adorned by parks of areca-palms
{3:13}__5+
{$} (1) veḷḷeyiṟu uṭaiya avviralārkaḷ ūr, oḷḷeri ūṭṭiya oruvaṉār
the god without an equal who set bright fire to the cities of those enemies who had white teeth
[[the whiteness of the teeth appear prominent in contrast to the black bodies of the acurar; that is why this epithet has been given to them]]
oḷir puḷ aṇi puṟaviṉil pūntarāy nakark, kaḷ aṇikuḻal umaikaṇavar (kāṇmiṉ)
is the husband of Umi who has tresses with honey, residing in the city of Pūntarāy which has jungle made beautiful by birds.
[[Variant reading: (1)veḷḷaiyēṟuṭaiyavar viravalārkaḷ]]
{3:13}__6+
{$} tuṅku iyal tāṉavar tōṟṟam mā nakar, aṅkiyil vīḻtara āynta ampiṉar
He who chose an arrow with which he made the grand and big cities of the tāṉavar who had formerly had the honour of being the devotees of Civaṉ,
[[In puranams it is sid that the acurar of the three cities who were stunch devotees of Civaṉ were later made to hate Civaṉ and become his enemies by the guiles of Nāratar]]
[[tuṅku-tuṅkam: this epithet has been given, thinking of their former greatness and Campantar's compassion for such people to meet with complete destruction from the same god who had granted them many boons]]
poṅkiya kaṭal aṇi pūntarāy nakar, amkayal a(ṉ)ṉa ka(ṇ)ṇi arivai paṅkarē
is the god who has as his half a lady who has eyes as beautiful as the carp fish, residing in the city of Pūntarāy which is made beautiful by the foaming and raging sea.
{3:13}__7+
{$} aṇṭarkaḷ uyntiṭa avuṇar māytarak, kaṇṭavar
the god who saw to it to save the tēvar and to destroy the acurar
kaṭalviṭam uṇṭa kaṇṭaṉār
Who drank the poison that rose from the sea and stationed it in his neck.
puṇṭarīka(v) vayal pūntarāynakar, vaṇṭu amar kuḻali taṉ maṇāḷar (kāṇmiṉ)
is the husband of the lady who has tresses of hir on which bees settle, residing in the city of Pūntarāy which has fields where lotus is growing (as weeds)
{3:13}__8+
{$} māciṉa arakkaṉai varaiyiṉ vāṭṭiya kaṇṭaṉār
the warrior who made the arakkaṉ of great sins to suffer, under the mountin (Kailācam)
kāyciṉa eyilkaḷaik kaṟutta kaṇṭaṉār
the warrior who destroys the fortress of burning anger.
pūcurar politaru pūntarāy nakark, kācaicey kuḻal umai kaṉavar (kāṇmiṉ)
is the husband of Umi who has tresses of hir resembling flowers of iron-wood tree, residing in the city of Pūntarāy where brahmins are properous.
[[kācai ceykuḻalāḷ: kācai cēr kuḻalāḷ (Campantar, Pūntarāy (2) 8)
{3:13}__9+
{$} tām mukam ākkiya acurar tampati, vēmmukam ākkiya vikirtar
the one who is different from the world and who made the cities of the acurar which were as important as the face, to be engulfed by fire.
kaṇṇaṉumpūmakaṉ (um) aṟikilāp pūntarāy nakark kōmakaḷ eḻilpeṟum arivai kūṟarē
is the Lord who has as one portion a beautiful lady and the ruler of the city of Pūntarāy, who could not be found by Kaṇṇaṉ (Māl) and Piramaṉ seated on a (lotus) flower
[[The conjuction um should be added to pūmakaḷ also]]
{3:13}__10+
{$} muttara acurarkaḷ moytta muppuram, attakum aḻaliṭai vīṭṭiṉār
the god destroyed the three cities which was crowded round by the three classes of acurar, to be engulfed by that great fire.
amaṇ, puttarum aṟivu oṇāp pūntarāy nakark, kottu aṇikuḻal umai kūṟar (kāṇmiṉ)
is the god who has as his half Umi who adorns on her tresses of hir bunches of flowers, resides in the city of Pūntarāy and who could not be comprehended by the amaṇar and puttar (buddhist)
[[As the acurar had forts made of gold, silver and iron they are called 'muttara acurar;
oṉṉār, irumpuṟu māmatil, poṉiñci, veḷḷippuricai aṉṟu ōr, turumpuṟac ceṟṟa koṟṟattu empirāṉ (Kōviyār, 167)]]
[[um should be added to amaṇ]]
{3:13}__11+
{$} puram ericeytavar
the god who burnt the three cities
pūntarāy nakarp, param, mali kuḻal umai naṅkai paṅkarai
on him who has Umi, the lady of distinction who has the weight of tresses of hir, and residing in the city of Pūntarāy
[[param -- pāram: weight;
cf.aḷakapāram: aḷakapāram micai acaiya (Kalinkattupparaṇi 53)]]
paraviya pantaṉ meyp pāṭal vallavar
those capable of reciting the truthful verses composed by Pantaṉ prising the god.
cirammali civakati cērtal tiṇṇamē
it is very definite that they will attin salvation which is as eminent as the head is to the body
[[Even in the 11th verse the incident of destruction of the three cities is mentioned]]

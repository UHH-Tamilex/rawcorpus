kaṉakam māvayiram (1) muntu māmaṇik kayilai kaṇṭum
after seeing the Kayili which has gold, big diamonds, and other eminent, and big gems.
uṉakaṉāy
being a mean person
[[This is a corruption of muṉakaṉ; the consonant in the first letter is dropped]]
[[PP: muṉṟōṉ aruḷ muṉṉum uṉṉā viṉaiyiṉ muṉakar (Kōviyār, 217)]]
arakkaṉ ōṭi eṭuttalum
when the arakkaṉ lifted the mountin running towards it.
aṉakaṉāy niṉṟa īcaṉ ūṉṟalum alaṟi vīḻntāṉ
when Civaṉ who was without sin fixed his toe firmly fell down roaring.
maṉakaṉāy ūṉṟiṉāṉēl
if he has fixed his toe with the intention of killing him.
maṟittum nōkku illai aṉṟē
one could not see him agin alive.
[[Variant reading: 1. vayiramuttu]]

katittavaṉ kaṇ civantu kayilai naṉ malaiyai ōṭi atirttu avaṉ eṭuttiṭalum arivai tāṉ añca
when the lady, Umi was frightened as the roaring arakkaṉ being angry with the mountin and thereby his eyes becoming red, ran and lifted the beautiful Kayilimali.
īcaṉ netittavaṉ ūṉṟiyiṭṭa nilai aḻintu alaṟi vīḻntāṉ
Civaṉ who has the wealth of penance lost his state by having fixed his toe, fell down roaring.
matittu iṟai ūṉṟiṉāṉēl
if the god had fixed tightly.
maṟittum nōkku illai aṉṟē
see 1st verse.

neṭu varaiyē pōla vīḻntāṉ
fell down like a tall mountin.
maṟittu iṟai ūṉṟiṉāṉēl

kariyattāṉ kaṇ civantu
Irāvaṇaṉ with red eyes was scorched.
kayilai naṉ malaiyaippaṟṟi iriyattāṉ eṭuttiṭa(l)lum
as he lifted the beautiful Kayilimali catching hold of it, the beings living there to flee in several directions.

if he had fixed firmly to cuse death to him.

arakkaṉ kaṟṟaṉaṉ kayilai taṉṉaik kāṇṭalum ōṭi
the arakkaṉ, running towards Kayili belonging to Civaṉ, who learnt the vētams with the proper intentions, as soon as he saw it.
ceṟṟavaṉ eṭuttavāṟē
by the manner by which he lifted it with enmity.
cēyiḻai añca
as Umi wearing beautiful ornaments feared
īcaṉ uṟṟu iṟai ūṉṟā muṉṉam uṇarvu aḻivakaiyāl vīḻntāṉ
fell down as his consciousness was destroyed even before Civaṉ fixed his toe firmly.
maṟṟu iṟai ūṉṟiṉāṉēl
if the Lord had fixed his toe very firmly.
maṟittum nōkku illai aṉṟē

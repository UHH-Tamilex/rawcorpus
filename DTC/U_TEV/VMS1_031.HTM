{1:31}__1+
{$} viḻunīr maḻuvāṭpaṭai aṇṇal
the god who has an excellent water of the Kaṅki and the weapon of a battle-axe.
viḷaṅkum kaḻunīr kuvaḷai(m) malarak kayalpāyumkoḻunīr vayalcūḻnta kuraṅkaṇiṉ muṭṭam toḻunīrmaiyar
those who have the nature of worshipping with hands joined Kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭam surrounded by fields having fertile water in which the carp leaps to make the blue nelumbo and shining blue indian water lily, to blossom
[[kuvaḷaimalara the underlined letter is augmentation for rhythm.]]
tītu uṟu tuṉpam ilarē
will not have sufferings which are the result of single deeds.
{1:31}__2+
{$} viṭai cēr koṭi aṇṇal
the god who has a flag on which is drawn the from of a bull.
viḷaṅkum māṭak kaṭaicēr karumeṉ (1) kuḷattu ōṅkiya kāṭṭil
in the forest which is eminent by a tank of black and soft water which is adjoining the back entrance of the shining mansions.
kuṭaiār puṉal malku kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭam uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ who has Kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭam which has increasing water in which people dive and plunge, as his shrine.
eṉai āḷ uṭai(ya) entai pirāṉē
is our father and master who admitted me as his slave.
[[Variant reading: (1) kuḻaṟoṅkiya kāṭṭil]]
{1:31}__3+
{$} cūlappaṭaiyāṉ viṭaiyāṉ cuṭunīṟṟāṉ
Civaṉ has a trident, a bull and smears well-burnt sacred ash.
kālaṉtaṉṉai āruyir vavviya kālaṉ
took away the precious life of the Kālaṉ (god of death) by kicking him with his leg.
kōlappoḻil cūḻnta kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭattu ēlam kamaḻ puṉcaṭai entai pirāṉē
is our father and master who has a caṭi from which the urgent for perfuming the hir spreads and who dwells in Kuraṅkuṇil muṭṭam, surrounded by beautiful gardens.
{1:31}__4+
{$} vāṭā virikoṉṟai valattu orukātil tōṭuār kuḻaiyāṉ
Civaṉ wears blossomed koṉṟi flowers which never fade, and a womens' ear-ring on the left ear and on the right a mens' ear-ring.
nalapālaṉam nōkki
having in his mind protecting me well.
kāṭātaṉa ceyta kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭam āṭāvaruvār (avar) aṉpu uṭaiyārē
the god who cames dancing in Kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭam who does acts which others are incapable of doing, has love towards me.
{1:31}__5+
{$} iṟaiyār vaḷaiyāḷai ōrpākattu aṭakki
having contined on one half a lady who wears bangles on her forearm.
kaṟaiyār miṭaṟṟāṉ
Civaṉ has a neck in which there is poison.
karikīṟiya kaiyāṉ
tore and elephant with his hands (flaying an elephant was play for Civaṉ is the idea)
kuṟaiyār maticūṭi
wears a crescent.
kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭattu uṟaivāṉ emai āḷ uṭai(ya) oṇcuṭarāṉē
is the bright light that dwells in Kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭam, has us as his slaves.
{1:31}__6+
{$} kalavam mayil kāmuṟu peṭaiyoṭu āṭik kulavum poḻil cuḻnta kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭam nilavum perumāṉ aṭinittal niṉaintē
meditating dily on the feet of the Lord who is permanent is Kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭam surrounded by gardens where the male peacocks of long tils dance with their females and rejoice.
palavum payaṉ uḷḷaṉa paṟṟum oḻintōm
we left off attachments towards many things which are useful, which are difficult to leave off.
[[niṉaintu ... paṟṟum taḻintōm is the syntactical link; this is known in Tamiḻ grammar as pūṭṭuviṟ poruḷkōḷ (pūṭṭuviṟ poruḷkōḷ); a method of construing a verses by taking together the first and last words of the verse.]]
{1:31}__7+
{$} māṭu ār malarkkoṉṟai vār caṭai vaittu
wearing the koṉṟi flowers which are yellow like gold, in the growing caṭi.
tōṭu ār kuḻaitāṉ oru (1) kātil ilaṅku
a women's ear-ring and a fitting mens' ear-ring to shine in each ear.
kūṭār matileytu
shooting an arrow on the forts of the enemies.
kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭattu āṭuār aravam(m) arai ārttu amarvāṉē
Civaṉ will stay with desire in Kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭam tying a dancing cobra as a waist-cord in the waist.
[[Variant reading: (1) kātiṉilaṅka]]
{1:31}__8+
{$} mai ārniṟam mēṉi arakkartam kōṉai uyyā vakaiyāl aṭarttu
pressing down the King of the arakkar, whose body was as black as the dark cloud, so that he could not escape.
iṉṉaruḷ ceyta koy ār malar cūṭitaṉ kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭam kaiyāl toḻuvār viṉai kāṇṭal aritē
the acts of those who worship with hands joined Kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭam of civan who wears flowers that are plucked and afterwards granted his benign grace to Irāvaṇaṉ will not be visible, (will vanish)
{1:31}__9+
{$} veṟiār malart tāmaraiyāṉoṭu mālum
māḷ, and Piramaṉ who is seated in a fragrant lotus flower.
aṟiyātu acaintu ētta ōr ār aḻalākum kuṟiyāl nimirntāṉ taṉ kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭam neṟiyāl toḻuvār
those who worshiup in the proper manner the shrine Kuraṅkaṇil muṭṭam of Civaṉ who stood high in the form of an unbearable column of fire, to be prised by both of them who could not know him, and feeling exhused.
viṉainiṟka (1) killavē
the acts of those people will not have the ability to ramin with them.
[[Variant reading: (1) niṟkilāvē]]
{1:31}__10+
{$} kaḻuvār vaḻuvāccamaṇ
the camaṇar who never in their religious observances and do not clean their bodies by bath.
tuvar āṭai kalantu mey pōrkkum cākkiyar vākku avai koḷḷēl
(People of this world) Do not accept as truth the words of the cākkiyar who cover their bodies with a cloth soaked in red ochre.
kuḻumiṉ caṭai aṇṇal kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭattu eḻil veṇpiṟaiyāṉ aṭicērvatu iyalpē
it is proper behavious to approach the feet of Civaṉ who wears a beautiful crescent and who is in Kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭam belonging to the god who has a caṭi resembling a collection of lightning.
{1:31}__11+
{$} kolār maḻu ēnti kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭam
on Kuraṅku aṇil muṭṭam of Civaṉ who holds a battle-axe capable of killing.
kal ār matil kāḻiyuḷ ñāṉacampantaṉ col ār tamiḻmālai cevikku iṉitāka vallārkku
to those who can recite, pleasing to ear, the garland of tamiḻ verses contining beautiful words by ñāṉacampantaṉ of Kāḻi which has a wall of fortification built with stones.
piṟavā vakai vīṭu eḷituām
salvation which is the cuse of not being born agin, is easy.

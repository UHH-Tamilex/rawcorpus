{3:68}__1+
{$} vāḷavari kōḷapuli kīḷatu uri tāḷiṉ micai nāḷum makiḻvar
Civaṉ rejoices always as the skin of the tiger which has bright stripes and is capable of killing, touches his feet.
āḷum avar
who admits people as his protege
vēḷ anakar
is without sin and is cherished with love by all.
pōḷ ayil kōḷ kaḷiṟu āḷi
who subdued the elephant which is capable of killing and which can split the opposing beasts with its tusks.
varavil tōḷ amarar
is the god who carries on his shoulders a superior bow.
matar kūḷi tāḷam eḻu mīḷi
is the chief who dances to the measure of time kept by the proud pēys.
miḷar tūḷi
smears himself with shining sacred ash.
kāḷamukil mūḷum iruḷ kīḷa
to rend the darkness cused by the sable clouds.
vaḷar poṉ viri tāḷa kayilāyamalaiyē
is his Kayilāyamali which emits rays of silver in its foot portion.
{3:68}__2+
{$} puṟṟu aravu paṟṟiyakai
Civaṉ's hand is holding a cobra that lives in the anthill.
neṟṟiyatu maṟṟu oru kaṇ
the other eye is on the forehead.
oṟṟai viṭaiyaṉ
has a single bull.
ceṟṟu eyil
he destroyed the forts.
uḷḷatu umai
Umi is united in his body without separation.
aṟṟavarkaḷ nal tuṇaivaṉ
is the good companion of those who have given up all attachments.
(1) uṟṟa nakar (tāṉ)
the place where such a great god dwells.
cuṟṟum maṇi peṟṟatu oḷi ceṟṟamoṭu kuṟṟam ilatu eṟṟu eṉa viṉāy
questioning how this mountin is without fault though it has destroyed the brightness of the gems found all round.
kaṟṟavarkaḷ col tokaiyiṉ muṟṟum oḷi peṟṟa kayilāyamalaiyē
the Kayilāyamali is the place which has the prise sung by learned people, and surrounded the world by its brilliance.
[[Variant reading: (1) tuṇaivaṉiṟṟaṉakar tāṉ (2) eṟṟaṉaviṉāy]]

{$} ciṅka arai maṅkaiyarkaḷ
celestial damsels who have a small waist like the waist of the lion.
taṅkaḷaṉa ceṅkainiṟai koṅku malar tūya
scatter fragrant flowers which they have in full in their red hands.
eṅkaḷ viṉai caṅkai avai iṅku akala aṅkamoḻi eṅkum uḷavāy
the words are heard everywhere which are a part of their prayer Let our acts and doubts leave us
tiṅkaḷ iruḷ noṅka
as the crescent swallows the darkness.
(1) oḷi viṅki (2) miḷir toṅkaloṭu taṅka
to stay with the shining garland as its brightness increases.
ayalē kaṅkaiyoṭu poṅku caṭai eṅkaḷ iṟai taṅku kayilāyamalaiyē
the Kayilāyamali is the place where our Lord who has a bright caṭi which expands having on its side Kaṅki

{3:68}__4+
{$} muṭiya caṭai piṭiyatu oru vaṭiya maḻu uṭaiyār
Civaṉ has a caṭi on his head, a sharp battle-axe which can be held in the fist.
veṭiya viṉai koṭiyar keṭa
the wicked people, acurar, who do detestable acts, to perish.
ceṭiyuṭaiya talaiyil iṭu cilpali noṭiya makiḻ aṭikaḷ iṭam
the place of the god who feels joy in speaking a few words for the sake of placing a small quantity of alms in the skull issuing stench.
koṭiya kural uṭaiya viṭai kaṭiya (1) tuṭiyiṉoṭum iṭiyiṉ atira
when the bull having harsh bellowing sound to roar like thunder along with the calves of elephants of quick git.
kaṭiya kural neṭiya mukil maṭiya atar aṭikoḷ kayilāyamalaiyē ām
is the Kayilayamali where as the thunder of the long clouds is drowned, goes on its way at the foot of the hill.

{$} kuṭaṅkaiyil nuṭaṅku eri toṭarntu eḻa
the fire held in the palm rises and shines in a wavy manner without extinguishing.
viṭam kiḷar paṭam koḷ aravam maṭaṅku oḷi paṭarntiṭa
the light of the cobras which have hoods and increasing poison makes the other lights diminish by their brilliance.
naṭam taru pōyt taṭaṅkaṭal toṭarntu uṭaṉ nuṭaṅkuva
the cool clouds going to the big ocean and drawing water from it dance on the mountins.
iṭam koḷa miṭainta kuralāl
by the noise of the thunder which occupies all places.
kaṭuṅkaliṉ
in the rugged boulders.
muṭaṅku aḷai nuṭaṅku aravu oṭuṅku kayilāyamalaiyē
the Kayilāyamali is the place where the curved cobras lie concealing themselves for saftey, that cobras are afrid of the harsh sound of the thunder. of the clouds is a literary conventions;

{$} ētam ila pūtamoṭu kōtai tuṇai ātimutal
the primordial god who is the companion for the lady who is as soft as a garland and the pūtams which have no faults.
vēta vikirtaṉ
one who is spoken in the Vētam as different from the world.
kītamoṭu nīti pala ōti
who revealed songs and many moral conducts.
maṟavātu payil nātaṉ nakartāṉ
the place where the master always stays without forgetting.
tātu poti pōtu viṭa ūtu ciṟai mītu tuḷi kūtal naliya
though the chillness cused by the drops of honey on the wings of the bees which hum to unfold the petals which contin within them pollen, distresses them.
kātal miku cōti kaḷir mātuvayil kōtu kayilāyamalaiyē
is the Kayilāyamali where cuckoos of beauty which love to stay in those gardens, and which have brilliance, pick the flowers found there.

amarar eṉṟum iṟaivaṉ taṉ aṭi ceṉṟu paṇi kiṉṟa nakar (tāṉ)
the city where the immortals bow before the feet of Civaṉ always.
tuṉṟu malar poṉtikaḻ ceṅkoṉṟai virai teṉṟaloṭu ceṉṟu kamaḻa
the fragrance of the koṉṟi flowers which glitter like gold and blossom in bunches, goes along with the southern balmy breeze and speards.
kaṉṟu paṭi tuṉṟu kaḷiṟu eṉṟu ivai muṉ niṉṟa kayilāyamalaiyē
is the Kayilāyamali where the calf, female and the male elephant and other beasts stands in the front portion of the mountin.

{$} maruppiṭai neṟāppu eḻu tarukkoṭu ceruc ceyta parutta kaḷiṟiṉ
like the big elephant which fought with pride so that sparks of fire flew from the tusks.
(1) poruppiṭai irukkaiyai arakkaṉ uṇarātu
arakkaṉ without regarding that it is the seat of Civaṉ in the mountin.
orukku uṭaṉ oruttiyai verukkuṟa veruṭṭalum
as soon as he frightened the unequalled Umi to be seized with fear,
nerukkeṉa nirutta viralāl karuttila oruttaṉai eruttu iṟa teritta kayilāyamalaiyē
the Kayilāyamali is the place of Civaṉ who crushed to break the neck of that arakkaṉ who had no sense, by the toe which practises dances to produce the sound `nerukku'

{$} pariya tirai periya puṉal (1) variyapuli uriyatu uṭai paricai uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ has the nature of a dress of the skin of a tiger which has stripes, and water with big waves from which fire is produced.
variyavaṉai ariya kaṇi uruviṉoṭu puriviṉavar pirivu ilnakar (tāṉ)
the city which Civaṉ who is attached to the form of a lady who wears bangles with lines and eyes with lines in the white portion, and never leaves her.
periya eri uruvam (atu) teriya uru (2) parivutarum arumai ataṉāl kariyavaṉum ariyamaṟai puriyavaṉum maruvu kayilāyamalaiyē
is the Kayilayamali where the black Māl and Piramaṉ who learnt the abstruse vetams, began to search for the form of a big fire, approach often; as that form had the rare nature of cusing only suffering, to forgive them.
[[Variant reading: (1) valiyapuli (2) urupuritaru]]

the Lord who admitted Caṇṭi seeing his services, who had the greatness of being worshipped by the celestials, as his protege

{$} am taṇ varai vanta puṉal tanta tirai cantaṉamoṭu akilum unti
pushing eagle-wood also with the sandal-wood which was brought in the waves which descended from the beautiful and cool mountin.
kantam malar kontiṉoṭu mantipala cintu kayilāyamalaimēl
on Kayilāyamali where many female monkeys scatter fragrant bushes of flowers.
entai aṭi vantu aṇuku cantamoṭu centamiḻiṉ icainta pukalip pantaṉ urai cintai ceya vantaviṉai naintu paralōkam eḷitē
the superior world is easy of attinment if one meditates on the verses done by pantaṉ of pukali which is assoicated with refined tamiḻ, having rhythmic movement, reaching the feet of our father, as the acts which approached us to inflict sufferings become dwindled

urittiṭṭār āṉaiyiṉ tōl utira āṟu oḻuki ōṭa
Civaṉ flayed an elephant's skin, the blood to run and flow as a river.
virittiṭṭār umaiyāḷ añci viral vitirttu
he unfolded it to make Umayāḷ seized with fear and her fingers quiver.
(1) alakkalaṇ nōkki
seeing her distress when Civan flayed and covered himself with the skin.
tarittiṭṭār ciṟitu pōtu
wore it for a little while.
tarikkilarāki
appeared unable to wear it for a long time.
tāmum cirittiṭṭār eyiṟu tōṉṟat tiruppayaṟṟūraṉār
Civaṉ in Tiruppayaṟṟūr also lughed showing his teeth.
[[Variant reading: (1) alakkal, arakkaṉōkki; I think the former to be a better reading;]]
[[This shrine is now known by the name of Tiruppayaṟṟaṅkuti.]]

uvantiṭṭu aṅku umai ōr pākam vaittavar
Civaṉ placed Umi on one half rejoicing in that act.
ūḻi ūḻi
in every aeon
pavantiṭṭa paramaṉār tām
the superior god who appeared
[[pavantanātar (nāvukkaracar, tiruppuṟampayam, 4); pavantam the meaning for this word is given as pretence in the Tamil Lexicon; it is current in Telugu also.]]
(1) malaiccilai nākam ēṟṟi
fastening the serpent as a string to the bow of a mountin.
kavarntiṭṭa puraṅkaḷ mūṉṟum kaṉal eriyākac cīṟi
being angry with the three cities which went and plundered, to be consumed by burning fire.
civantiṭṭa kaṇṇar pōlum tiruppayaṟṟūraṉār
the Lord in Tiruppayaṟṟūr has eyes which showed anger.
[[Variant reading: (1) malaicilai]]

naṅkaḷukku aruḷatu eṉṟu nāṉ maṟai ōtuvārkaḷ taṅkaḷukku aruḷum eṅkaḷ tattuvaṉ
Civaṉ, our ultimate reality, who grants his grace to those who chant the four vetams with the firmness of mind that we shall receive his grace;
taḻalaṉ
has the colour of fire.
taṉṉai eṅkaḷukku aruḷ cey eṉṉa niṉṟavaṉ
stood as the cuse, for all the living beings to request Please grant us your grace
nākam añcum tiṅkaḷukku aruḷicceytār tiruppayaṟṟūraṉār
Civaṉ in Tiruppayaṟṟūr bestowed his grace on the crescent which is afrid of the cobra.

pārttaṉukku aruḷum vaittār
Civaṉ granted his grace to Pārttaṉ.
pāmpu arai āṭa vaittār
placed a cobra in the waist to dance, spreading its hood.
cāttaṉai makaṉā vaittār
placed Cāttaṉ in the relationship of son to him.
cāmuṇṭi cāmavētam kūttoṭum pāṭavaittār
he danced with Cāmuṇṭi (Kāḷi) and chanted the Cāmavētam.
kōḷ arā matiyam nalla tīrttamum caṭaiyil vaittār tiruppayaṟṟūraṉār
Civaṉ in Tiruppayaṟṟūr placed on his caṭi a holy water (of Kanki), a crescent and a cobra capable of killing.

mūvakai mūvarpōlum
Civaṉ is male, female and hermaphrodite and has three forms
[[i.e. four aruvams, one paruvārūram, and four uruvams:
aruvam (aruvam): formlessness;
aruvuruvam (aruvuruvam): form which can be sid to be no form, as the civalinkam;
uruvam (uruvam): visible form.]]
muṟṟum ām neṟṟikkaṇṇar
has a frontal eye and who is all the objects (the eye of wisdom)
(1) nāvakai nāvar (pōlum)
has the tongue from which the inarticulate sounds of cūkkumi, picanti, mattimi, and vikari have their origin.
nāṉ maṟai ñāṉam ellām āvakai āvar (pōlum)
is the origin of the four vētams and the wisdom contined in them.
ātirai nāḷar (pōlum)
has Ātiri as his favourite star.
tēvarkaḷ tēvar (pōlum) tiruppayaṟṟūraṉār
the Lord in Tiruppayaṟṟūr is the supreme god above all other tēvars.
[[Variant reading: (1) nālvakai nālvar]]

āvi āy, aviyum āki
being the smoke of the sacrifical fire and the offerings made to gods in the sacrificial fire.
arukkam āy
being minute
perukkam āki
being big
(1) pāviyar pāvam tīrkkum paramaṉ āy

pulaṉkaḷaip pōka nīkki
destroying the five senses of the body.
puntiyai oruṅkavaittu
focussing the mind
(1) ilaṅkaḷaip pōka niṉṟu
staying having gone beyond the six cakram dynamic tattvik centres which are nerve plexuses in the body.
(2)iraṇṭaiyum nīkki oṉṟāy
removing the idea of ego and Civaṉ and becoming one with Civaṉ
malaṅkaḷai māṟṟavallār maṉattiṉuḷ pōkam āki
being the joy in the minds of those who can remove the impurities of the soul
ciṉaṅkaḷaik kaḷaivar pōlum tiruppayaṟṟūraṉār
the Lord in Tiruppayaṟṟūr will weed out the sufferings of the birth which is the result of anger

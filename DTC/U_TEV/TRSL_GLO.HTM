On the difference to be made between a gloss and a translation

(JLC)

For those who might not be familiar with the concept, I shall here advocate that there is place in the indological technical literature for something which I will term here as gloss (without any pejorative connotation implied!) which is to be distinguished from what we call a translation.

A gloss is a kind of text which cannot stand by itself but is always accompanying another kind of text ---termed here as the original text--- so as to make it more easily accessible to its readers.

A translation is usually a kind of text which is to meant to substitute itself to an original text.

The reader of a gloss wants to read the original text, but finds it too difficult.

The reader of a translation does not want to read the original text.

A gloss and a translation will not be used by the same kinds of people.

Many indian commentaries are glosses, but this kind of literature seems to have been frowned upon in modern european fields of scholarship. The reasons for that phenomenon might be interesting to explore, but I will content myself here with proposing the interested reader (i.e. a reader who knows some Tamil but not enough in order to enjoy reading the Tēvāram) to go through the above given texts, trying to read alternately the Tamil and the English.

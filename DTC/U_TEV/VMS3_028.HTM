{3:28}__1+
{$} kālai ār vaṇṭiṉam (1) kiṇṭiya kār uṟum cōlai ār paiṅkiḷi coṟporuḷ payila
the parrots which live in the gardens on which clouds settle and in which the swarms of bees which hum the tune of marutam, penetrate into the flowers, practise words and their meanings
[[kālai here stands for the tune sung in the morning]]
vēlai ār viṭam aṇi vētiyaṉ virumpu iṭam
the place which the brahmin among gods, Civaṉ, who adorns his neck with the poison that rose in the ocean.
mālai ār mati tavaḻ māmaḻapāṭiyē
is the great Maḻapāṭi on whose mansions the moon which rises in the evening seems to crawl.
[[Variant reading: (1) koṇṭiya]]
{3:28}__2+
{$} kaṟai aṇi miṭaṟu uṭaik kaṇṇutal
Civaṉ who has a neck beautified by the poison.
naṇṇiya piṟai aṇi ceñcaṭaip piññakaṉ
Civaṉ who wears a feather of a peacock, on the red caṭi and adorns a crescent which sought refuge in him.
tuṟai aṇi kuruku iṉam tūmalar tutaiyavē
the flock of swans whic make the ghat beautiful live crowded in spotless (lotus) flowers.
maṟai aṇi nāviṉāṉ
and whose tongue is made beautiful by chanting the vētams.
pēṇum ūr māmaḻapāṭiyē
is the great maḻapāṭi desired by him.
{3:28}__3+
{$} antaṇar vēḷviyum arumaṟaittuḻaṉiyum centamiḻkkītamum cīriṉāl vaḷartara
when the sacrifice of the brahmins, the sound of the vētams and the musical compositions in refined Tamiḻ prosper respectively with their proper procedure, eminence and measure of time.
pantu aṇai mel viralāḷoṭum payilvu iṭam
the place where Civaṉ stays always with a lady in whose soft fingers there is a ball (to play with)
mantam vantu ulavu cīr māmaḻapāṭiyē
is the great maḻapāṭi which has the excellence of the balmy breeze coming from the south and moving about
[[mantam: mantam untu poḻil māmaḻapāṭiyuḷ (Ñāṉacampantar, maḻapāṭi (3) 11)]]
{3:28}__4+
{$} attiyiṉ uritaṉai aḻakuṟap pōrttavaṉ
Civaṉ covered himself with the skin of an elephant to add beauty to it.
muttiyāy
Being himself the liberation from all bondage.
mūvariṉ mutalvaṉāy niṉṟavaṉ
who stood as the chief apart from the three deities (Piramaṉ, Māl, and uruttiraṉ) who perform the functions of creation, protection and destruction.
pattiyāl pāṭiṭa
when devotees sing out of devotion
parintu avarkku aruḷ ceyum attaṉār uṟaiviṭam
the abode of the father who grants his grace to them taking compassion on them.
aṇi maḻapāṭiyē
is the beautiful maḻapāṭi.
{3:28}__5+
{$} kaṅkai ār caṭaiyiṭaik katirmati aṇintavaṉ
Civaṉ who adorns on the caṭi in which there is Kaṅki, a crescent spreading rays.
veṅkaṇ vāḷ aravuṭai vētiyaṉ
the brahmin who has a bright and cruel cobra.
tītu ilāc ceṅkayaṟkaṇ umaiyāḷoṭum cērvu iṭam
the place where he dwells along with Umayāḷ who has eyes resembling red carp fish and nullifies the sins of those who worship her, by her benign looks.
maṅkaimār naṭam payil māmaḻapāṭiyē
is the great maḻapāṭi where young girls practise dance.
{3:28}__6+
{$} pālaṉā�r āruyir pāṅkiṉāl uṇavarum kālaṉār uyir cekak kāliṉāl cāṭiṉāṉ
Civaṉ who trampled under his foot to destroy the life of Kālaṉ who came to do his duty of taking away the precious life of the young boy (markaṇṭēyaṉ)
[[pālaṉār ; ār is an honorified suffix; kālaṉār ār is added to decry him]]
cēliṉ ār kaṇṇiṉāḷ taṉṉoṭum cērvu iṭam
the place where he dwells with a lady whose eyes are like the carnatic carp.
māliṉār vaḻipaṭum māmaḻapāṭiyē
is the great maḻapāṭi where Māl worshipped Civaṉ.
{3:28}__7+
{$} viṇṇil ār imaiyavar meym makiḻntu ēttavē, eṇṇilār muppuram eri uṇa nakai ceytār
Civaṉ lughed at the three cities of the enemies, as the tēvar who do not wink and who dwell in heaven, to prise rejoicing sincerely
kaṇṇiṉāl kāmaṉaik kaṉal eḻak kāynta em aṇṇalār uṟaiviṭam aṇi maḻapāṭiyē
the beautiful maḻapāti is the abode of our chief who destroyed by the fire issuing from the frontal eye, Kāmaṉ.
{3:28}__8+
{$} karattiṉāl kayilaiyai eṭutta kār arakkaṉa, cirattiṉai ūṉṟalum
when Civaṉ pressed his toe to crush the heads of arakkaṉ who lifted Kayili with his twenty hands. (arakkaṉa = arakkaṉ + a! a is case termination denoting plural of the following word)
civaṉ aṭi caraṇ eṉā irattiṉāl kain narampu eṭuttu icai pāṭalum
as soon as he sang music by taking the guts from his hands, with a request which was almost begging, thinking the feet of Civaṉ is our refuge.
varattiṉāṉ maruvu iṭam māmaḻapāṭiyē
the great maḻapāṭi is the accustomed place of Civaṉ who granted boons to that arakkaṉ.
{3:28}__9+
{$} ēṭu ulām malar micai ayaṉ eḻil mālum āy nāṭiṉārkku ariyacīr nātaṉār uṟaivu iṭam
the abode of the master whose greatness was difficult to be known to Ayaṉ who is seated on a lotus flower of many petals and beautiful Māl who took respectively the form of a swan and a pig, and searched for the head and feet.
pāṭu elām peṇṇaiyiṉ paḻam viḻap paimpoḻil māṭu elām malku cīr māmaḻapāṭiyē
is the great maḻapāṭi of importance where wealth of all kinds increase, and which has verdant gardens and where on all sides the fruits of the palmyra tree fall after maturing.
{3:28}__10+
{$} uṟipiṭitta ūttai vāyccamaṇoṭu cākkiyar neṟipiṭitta aṟivu ilā nīcar col koḷḷaṉmiṉ
(People of this world!) Do not accept as truth the words of the low people who have not sense enough to catch hold of the right path, of the cākkiyar, and camaṇar who have filthy teeth and hold a net-work of ropes for placing water-pots suspended from the hand;
[[PP: uṟi muṭitta kuṇṭikai tam kaiyil tūkki ūttaivāyc camaṇarkku ōr kuṇṭākkaṉāy,
ūṉ ikantu ūṇ uṟikkaiyar kuṇṭar pollā ūttai vāyccamaṇar
(Appar, atiki vīraṭṭāṉam, tantakam (1) 8; tirumaḻapāṭi (1) 4);]]
[[SANDHI: uṟipiṭitta ūttai = uṟipaṭittūttai;
neṟipaṭitta aṟivilā = neṟipaṭittaṟivilā]]
poṟipiṭitta araviṉam pūṇ eṉak koṇṭu, māṉ maṟi piṭittāṉ iṭam māmaḻapāṭiyē
the great Maḻapāṭi is the place of Civaṉ who holds in his hand a young deer, having as ornaments many cobras having spots in their hoods.
[[SANDHI: poṟi piṭitta araviṉam -- poṟi piṭittaraviṉam]]
{3:28}__11+
{$} ñālattu ār ātirai nāḷiṉāṉ nāḷ toṟum cīlattāl mēviya tirumaḻapāṭiyai
on Maḻapāṭi where Civaṉ who has as his star Ātiri; which is famous throughout the world, stays always with desire due to his easy accessibility.
ñālattāl mikka cīr ñāṉacampantaṉ col kōlattāl pāṭuvār
those who can sing the songs composed by Ñāṉacampantaṉ whose eminence is well-known in this world, with the civite appurtenances (holy ash, sacred beads and purity of the body)
kuṟṟam aṟṟārkaḷ
are without blemish.

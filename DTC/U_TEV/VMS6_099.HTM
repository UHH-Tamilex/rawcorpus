{$} (1) puṇṇiyā
Civaṉ who has the forms of all virtuous acts!
pūmpukalūrmēviya puṇṇiyaṉē
Civaṉ in beautiful Pumpukalūr who is the embodiment of all virtuous acts!
eṇṇukēṉ
I who possess the power of thinking.
eṉcolli eṇṇukēṉ
what shall I think of desiring anything?
emperumāṉtiruvaṭiyē eṇṇiṉ allāl
what shall I think about except the feet of our Lord!
maṟṟu ōrkaṇ ilēṉ
I have no other thing to be seen.
maṟṟu ōrkaḷaikaṇ illēṉ, kaḻal aṭiyē kaitoḻutu kāṇiṉ allāl
except the desire of seeing your feet wearing kaḻal, with joined hands, I have no other support.
oṇuḷē oṉpatu vācal vaittāy
you placed nine apertures in the body in which I dwell
[[the nine apertures are: two eyes, two nostrils, two ears, anus and meatus urinarius]]
[[oṇ is the corruption of oṉṟu]]
okka aṭaikkum pōtu uṇaramāṭṭēṉ
I am incapable of thinking and seeing you when all the nine close simultaneously.
uṉ (2) aṭikkē pōtukiṉṟēṉ
I am coming to take shelter under your feet.
[[Please accept me now itself is the suggested sense; the ē in aṭikkē
is used in the sense of excluding the wordly life from the lord's feet]]
[[variant readings: (1)puṇṇiyaṉē (2) aṭiyē]]

{$} tiruppukalūrmēviya tēvatēvē
the god above the ordinary tevars, who is in Tiruppukalūr!
caṅkai oṉṟu iṉṟiyēi tēvar vēṇṭac camuttirattiṉ nañcu uṇṭu cāvāmūvācciṅkamē
Civaṉ who is as strong as the lion and who does not die or age even after consuming the poison that rose in the ocean, when the tēvar requested you to consume it, without any other thought except saving the celestials from death.
aṅkamē pūṇṭāy
you adorned yourself with bones.
aṉal āṭiṉāy
you danced in fire.
ātiraiyāy
you have Ātirai as your favourite star.
āl niḻalāy
you sat under the shade of the banyan tree (to impart spiritual wisdom and clear the doubts of the four sages).
āṉēṟuūrntāy
you rode on a bull which belongs to the bovine species.
paṅkam oṉṟu illāta paṭarcaṭaiyiṉāy
you have a spreading caṭai which has no defect.
pāmpōṭu tiṅkaḷ pakaitīrttu āṇṭāy
you admitted into your grace a cobra and a crescent removing their mutual enmity.
uṉ aṭikkēpōtukiṉṟēṉ
see 1st verse.

see 1st verse.
pai aravak kaccaiyāy
you who wear as a girdle a cobra which has a hood.
pāl veṇṇīṟṟāy
you smear your body with sacred assh as white as milk.
paḷikkumkakkuḻaiyiṉāy
you wear in your ear an ear-ring made of conch which is as white as crystal quartz
paṇ ār iṉ col maiviravu kaṇṇāḷaip pākam koṇṭāy
you had on one half a lady whose eyes are coated with collyrium and who speaks words as sweet as the melody-type which has all the seven scales.
māṉ maṟikai ēntiṉāy
you held in your hand a young deer.
vañcakkaḷvar aivaraiyum eṉmēl taravu aṟuttāy
you gave full freedom for the deceitful robbers, the five senses, to play havoc with me (taravu aṟuttal)
[[iruvaraiyum mūvaraiyum eṉmēl ēvi illātataravu aṟuttāykku illēṉ Tiruppukalūr-10 of the decades; (aintu is spoken of as aivar out of anger.]]
avar vēṇṭum kāriyam iṅku āvatuillai
there is no mischief for them to do according to their-wish.
poy uraiyātu uṉ aṭikkē pōtukiṉṟēṉ
I am entering your feet without uttering falsehood.

teruḷātār mūeyilum tīyil vēvac cilaivaḷaittuc ceṅkaṇaiyāl ceṟṟa tēva
the god who destroyed with an arrow, soaked in blood, bending a mountain into a bow, the three forts of the acurar who had no clarity of mind, to be consumed by fire. (ceṅkaṇai: cemmai is a generic epithet; it may also mean a straight arrow.)
maruḷātār tam maṉattil vāṭṭam tīrppāy
you will remove the troubles in the minds of those who are not confused.
maruntāyp piṇi tīrppāy
you will cure diseases, being the peoper medicine.
vāṉōrkku eṉṟum aruḷ āki
assuming the form of grace to the celestials.
ātiyāy
being the cause of all things.
vētam āki
being the vētams.
alarmēlāṉ nīrmēlāṉ āyntum kāṇāpporuḷ āvāy
you are the thing which Piramaṉ seated on a flower and Māl who sleeps on the ocean, could not find in spite of their search.

pār ēṟpaṭutalaiyil palikoḷvāṉē

paṇṭu (1) aṉaṅkaṟ kāyntāṉē

kār ēṟumukil aṉaiya kaṇṭattāṉē

karuṅkaik kaḷiṟṟu urivai kataṟap pōrtta pōr ēṟē
the lion which can give battle and covered himself with the skin of a black elephant of long trunk, to make it roar.

oruvaṉaiyum allātu uḷḷam uṇarātu
my mind will not know except you who is unequalled.
uṇarccittaṭumāṟṟattu uḷḷē niṉṟa iruvaraiyum mūvaraiyum eṉmēl ēvi
having ordered on me the five sense which are unsteady in my inner feeling which are visible to the eye.

illāta taravu aṟuttāykku
to you who gave full freedom to these.
illēṉ
I have no fullness of love.
ēlakkaruvaraicūḻ kāṉalilaṅkai vēntaṉ kaṭuntēr mītu ōṭāmaik kālāṟceṟṟa poruvaraiyāy
Civaṉ who has a mountain that fought with which you controlled with your leg and prevented the fast chariot from flying above the mountain, Kayilai, which belonged to the King of Ilaṅkai which has a sea-shore garden surrounded by black mountains in which cardamom was grown.

{2:19}__1+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
Civaṉ who is permanent in Nellikkā.
aṟattāl uyir kāval amarntaruḷi
being pleased to protect living beings to establish righteousnese.
maṟattāl matil mūṉṟu uṭaṉ māṇpu aḻitta tiṟattāl
by the way he destroyed the three forts simultaneously as they were treading the unrighteous path.
terivu eytiya tī veṇ tiṅkaḷ niṟattāṉ
has the colour of the white moon in his body which is as brilliant as the fire (the colour of the white sacred ash is compared to the white colour of the moon.
[[terivu brilliance]]
{2:19}__2+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
[see 1st verse.]
pati (tāṉ) iṭukāṭu
his dwelling place is the burial-ground.
paiṅkoṉṟai toṅkal
the fresh koṉṟi flowers are his garlands.
mati (tāṉ, atu) cūṭiya maintaṉum (tāṉ)
he is also the youth who wears a crescent.
viti (tāṉ)
is the destiny.
viṉai (tāṉ)
is the acts.
viḻuppam payakkum neti (tāṉ)
is the treasure that brings one eminence.
{2:19}__3+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
see 1st verse.
nalam (tāṉ) avaṉ
is the goodness (auspiciousness)
nāṉamukamtaṉ talaiyaik kalam (tāṉ, atu) koṇṭa (1) kapāliyum (tāṉ)
is also the god known by the name of Kapāli who had as his begging bowl the skull of Piramaṉ of four faces.
[[kapāli is used simply used in the sense of Civaṉ]]
pulam (tāṉ)
is the spiritual knowledge.
pukaḻāl eri viṇ pukaḻum nilam (tāṉ)
is the object of sense prised by the celestials in the heaven which is eminent, by his fame.
[[Variant reading: (1) kavāliyum]]
{2:19}__4+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
see 1st verse.
talai (tāṉ, atu) ēntiya tam aṭikaḷ
god who held in his hand a skull.
kalai (tāṉ) tiri kāṭu iṭam nāṭu iṭam ām
his country is the forest where the deer wanders.
matil mūṉṟu uṭaiya malai (tāṉ) eṭuttāṉ
took a bow to make the three forts to sustin defeat.
nilai (tāṉ)
is the permanent thing in this world where all other things are transient.
{2:19}__5+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
see 1st verse.
tavam (tāṉ)
is the penance.
kati tāṉ
is the higher state of existence as a fruit of that penance.
mativār caṭaimēl uvantāṉ
felt joy in wearing a crescent on the long caṭi.
cuṟa vēntaṉ uru aḻiyac civantāṉ (ākac) ceyac ceytu ceṟuttu
was angry to destroy the body of Maṉmataṉ (cupid) who had on his flag the form of a shark.
ulakil nivantāṉ
became supreme in this world.
{2:19}__6+
{$} nellikāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
see 1st verse.
veṟi ār malark koṉṟai amtār virumpi
desirous of wearing a garland made of fragrant koṉṟi flowers,
maṟiyār
holds a young deer (in the hand)
malai maṅkai makiḻntavaṉ (tāṉ)
felt happy in being united with the dughter of the mountin.
(1) kuṟiyāl kuṟikoṇṭavar pōyk kuṟukum neṟiyāṉ
is the path by which those who meditate upon him by the initiation of the preceptor, reach him fixing him as their goal.
[[Variant reading: (1) kuṟiyār]]
{2:19}__7+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
see 1st verse.
piṟai (tāṉ) caṭai cērttiya entai pemmāṉ
our father and god who placed a crescent on his caṭi.
iṟai (tāṉ)
is the chief.
iṟavāk kayilai malaiyāṉ
is in the mountin Kayili which has no destruction.
maṟai (tāṉ)
is himself the vētams.
puṉal oṇ mati malku ceṉṉi niṟai (tāṉ)
has filled his head with water and brilliant crescent which are flourishing.
{2:19}__8+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
see 1st verse.
maṟaittāṉ, piṇimātu oru pākam taṉṉai
concealed on one half a lady having united her with him
[[PP_EXT: peṇ uru tiṟaṉ ākiṉṟu avvuruttaṉṉuḷ aṭakkic karakkiṉum karakkum
(Puṟam 1, 7-8)]]
arakkaṉ mikaiyai varaiyāl miṟaittāṉ
made the pride of the arakkaṉ suffer by the mountin.
caṭaimēl kuḷir kōl vaḷaiyaik kuṟaittāṉ
contined on his caṭi the cool lady of Kaṅki who wears bangles of fine workmanship,
[[As Kaṅki is feminine gender in sanskrit, the attributes of a lady are given to her.]]
niṟaittāṉ
filled his devotees with good and auspicious things.
{2:19}__9+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
see 1st verse.
taḻal tāmaraiyāṉ vaiyam tāyavaṉum
(to) Piramaṉ who is in the lotus which is red like fire and Māl who measured the earth.
kaḻal (tāṉ) muṭi kāṇiya nāṇ oḷirum aḻaltāṉ
was a fire emitting rays, to them who were ashamed (as they could not find out the feet and head).
aṭiyārkku aruḷāyp payakkum niḻal (tāṉ)
is the refuge to the devotees to whom he grants everything, being the embodiment of grace itself.
{2:19}__10+
{$} nellikkāvuḷ nilāyavaṉ (ē)
see 1st verse
kaṉattu ār tirai māṇṭu aḻal kāṉṟa nañcai eṉ attā eṉa vāṅki atu uṇṭa kaṇṭaṉ
has a neck in which the poison stays as the celestials prayed, my father, consume the poison which rose increasing in severity like fire which was spat by the ocean from which the clouds draw water.
maṉattāl camaṇ cākkiyar māṇpu aḻiya niṉaittāṉ
took it into his mind to destroy the greatness of the camaṇar and cākkiyar.
{2:19}__11+
{$} pukar ētum ilāta puttēḷ ulakiṉ nikarām nellikkāvuḷ nilāvaṉai
our Civaṉ who is permanent on Nellikkā which is equal to the heaven of the celestials which is without the slightest blemish.
(1) nakarānala ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa pakarvār (avar) pāvam ilātavarē
those who are able to recite the verses composed by Ñāṉacampantaṉ of undying good qualities are definitely without sins.
[[Variant reading: (1) nakarāṉala, nakarār nala, nakarā nala ñāṉacampantaṉa col.)

Tiruttāḷaccati: Agreement of time measure in singing and dancing

{1:126}__1+
{$} pantattāl vantu eppāl payiṉṟu niṉṟa umpar
the tēvar who reside everywhere having come, being bound by āṇavamalam
appālē cērvāy ēṉōr
devotees who meditate upon the feet of the god in regions beyond tamiḻnāṭu
1. kāṉ payil muṉivarkaḷ kaṇamum
and the groups of sages who live in forests
cintittē vantippa
to meditate and worship
cilampiṉ maṅkai taṉṉoṭum cērvār
god who is united within the dughter of the mountin.
nāḷ nāḷ nīḷ kayilait tikaḻtaru paricu atu elām
all the eminence of sitting majestically dily in the high Kayili
cantittē intap pārcaṉaṅkaḷ niṉṟu tam kaṇāl tāmē kāṇā vāḻvu ārattakavu ceytavaṉ iṭam
the place of the god who was so merciful, that the people of this world may visit and see with their physical eyes and enjoy happiness and prosperity in an ample measure.
kantattāl eṇ tikkum kamaḻntu ilaṅku cantaṉakkāṭu ār pū ār cīrmēvum kaḻumala vaḷa nakarē
is the fertile city of Kaḻumalam which is eminent due to flowers in the forest of sandal-wood tree by which it is famous as its fragrance spreads in all the eight directions

{$} piccaikkē iccittu
Having desired receiving alms
picaintu aṇinta veṇpoṭip pīṭuār nīṭuār māṭu ārum piṟai nutal arivaiyoṭum
with the lady who has a forehead like the crescent and who is by the side with eminence and fame, having besmeared his person with white sacred ash mixing it with water.
uccattāl nacci(ppōl) toṭarntu aṭarnta veṅkaṇ ēṟu ūrāūr ām nīḷvīti
in the long streets in the villages, riding on a bull of fierce looks which followed him and could attack people, desiring the noon as the proper time for that act.
oli cey icai vaccattāl
as he was singing songs
naccuccēr vaṭamkoḷ koṅkai maṅkai mārvārā nērēmāl ārum vacivalavaṉatu iṭam
the place of the god who can fascinate the ladies (wives of the sages) who wore on their breasts garlands of pearls which are lovable, to come in his presence and be infatuated with love.
kaccattāl mecci
Being fond of, in very large numbers
pūkkalantu ilaṅku vaṇṭuiṉam
the swarm of shining bees which are in the flowers.
kār ār kār ār nīḷ cōlaik kaḻumala vaḷa nakarē
is the fertile city of Kaḻumalam which has long gardens where black clouds settle.

{$} tiṅkaṭkē tumpaikkē tikaḻntu ilaṅkum mattaiyiṉ cērē cērē nīrākac ceṟitaru cura natiyōṭu
along with the river in heaven which is in the form of water which is united with the crescent, white dead nettle flowers, and very much shining dhatura flowers
[[mattai ūmattai; the first letter is dropped]]
aṅkaic cērvu iṉṟikkē
without keeping in the palm
aṭaintu uṭainta veṇṭakalaippālē
near the broken white skull, which reached the head of the god.
mēlē māl ēyappaṭarvuṟum avaṉ iṟakum
and the feather of the acuraṉ in the form of a crane who flew high as he was deluded in mind.
poṅkap pēr nañcaiccēr puyaṅkamaṅkaḷ koṉṟaiyiṉ pōtu ār tārē mēvip(1) puritaru caṭaiyaṉ iṭam
the place of the god with twisted matted hir on which are worn garlands of the flowers of the koṉṟi (indian laburnam) and cobras which have great poison in abundance.
kaṅkaikku ēyum poṟpu ār kalantu vanta poṉṉiyiṉ kālēvārā mēlē pāy kaḻuvaḷa nakar
is the fertile city of Kaḻumalam where the channels branching from Poṉṉi come and flow (into the fields) which mixes with several things in its course and has sacredness like the Kaṇki.

{1:126}__4+
{$} aṇṭattāl eṇtikkum amaintu aṭaṅkum maṇtalattu āṟē
marching through this world having the eight directions contined in it.
vēṟē vāṉ āḷvār avar avar iṭam (atu) elām maṇṭippōy
Rushing forward in those places where the rulers of the eight directions rule which are different from the world.
veṉṟippōr malaintu alainta umparum māṟu ēlātārtām mēvum vali mikupuram eriya
the strong cities in which those people who could not be opposed even by the celestials who were wearied after giving battle desirous of victory, were living to be on fire.
muṇṭattē ventiṭṭē muṭintu iṭinta iñcicūḻ mūvāmūtūr mūtūrā muṉivu ceytavaṉ iṭam
the place of the god who by his anger, made those cities always new to become old cities, had them destroyed by the frontal eye, to become cities surrounded by walls of enclosure which were in ruins.
kaṇṭiṭṭē ceñcoṟ cēr kaviṉ ciṟanta mantirakkālē ōvātār mēvum kaḻumala vaḷa nakarē
is the fertile city of Kaḻumalam, where people who ceaselessly repeat the mantiram which is beautiful by words which are explicit in their meanings, having realised their efficacy.

{$} tikkil tēvu aṟṟu aṟṟē tikaḻntu (1) ilaṅku maṇṭalac cīṟuār vīṭu ār pōr ār tārakaṉ uṭal avaṉ etirē pukkiṭṭē veṭṭiṭṭē
Having cut the body of Tārakaṉ who was lonely and angry and always fond of battle in the world where the rulers of the eight directions are eminent in their places with their properties, going directly in front of him.
pukaintu eḻunta caṇṭattīp pōlē
like the fast spreading fire which rose after issuing smoke.
pū, nīr, tī, kāl, mīppuṇartarum uyirkaḷ tiṟam,
in the different kinds of living beings which are the combinations of he earth, water fire, air, and space.
cokkattē nirttattē toṭarnta maṅkai ceṅkatattōṭu ēyāmē mālōkattuyar kaḷaipavaṉatu iṭam
the place of the god who completely weeded out the sufferings of those beings so that they might not be affected by the fiery anger of the lady (Kāḷi) who followed him and was defeated in the dance called `cokkam!'
kaikkap (2) pēryukkattē kaṉaṉṟu miṇṭu taṇṭalaikkāṭē ōṭā ūrēcēr kaḻumalavaḷanakarē
is the fertile city of Kaḻumalam where though all things are burnt in the fire at the end of the aeon to be detested by all, the cool gardens are thronging escaping destruction, and do not leave that place

{$} ceṟṟiṭṭē veṟṟiccēr tikaḻnta tumpi moympu uṟum cērē vārā
having come to embrance the shoulders of the male elephant (Civaṉ) which was victorious, having destroyed the enemies and being therefore eminent.
nīḷkōtait teri iḻaipiṭiyatu āy
Umi wearing choice ornaments and a long garland having assumed the form of a female elephant.
oṟṟaic cēr muṟṟal kompu uṭait taṭakkai, mukkaṇ, mikku ōvātē pāy mātāṉattu uṟu pukarmuka iṟaiyaip peṟṟiṭṭē
having given birth to the god (Vināyakar) who has spots in the face, a big trunk, a single tusk of mature growth, and must which is flowing very much unceasingly and abundantly.
(maṟṟu) ippār peruttu mikka tukkamum pērānōy tām ēyāmaip pirivu ceytavaṉ iṭam
the place of the god (Civaṉ) who removed from this world acute sufferings and diseases which never leave it.
kaṟṟiṭṭē eṭṭu eṭṭuk kalaittuṟaik karaic celak kāṇātār cērā meykkaḻumala vaḷanakar
is the fertile city of Kaḻumalam which always exists, and which is not approached by people who have not mastered the arts of sixty-four branches, having learnt them.

{$} pattippēr vittiṭṭu
Having sown the big seed of devotion (bakthi)
paranta aimpulaṉkaḷ vāyppālē pōkāmēkāvā
and having guarded the five senses from enjoying external objects.
pakai aṟum vakai niṉaiyā
having focussed their thought to destroy the six inner enemies called emotions
[[āṟupakai: kāmam, kurōtam, lōpam, mōkam, matam, māṟcariyam]]
muttikkē ikkattē muṭikkum mukkuṇaṅkaḷ vāy mūṭā ūṭā
having closed the gates of the three fundamental qualities and showing resentment towards them, as they are obstacles to attinment of bliss.
nāl antak karaṇamum oru neṟiyāyc cittikkē uyttiṭṭu
directed the four inner seats of thought, feeling and volition focussing them on meditation.
tikaḻnta meyp poruḷ cērvār tāmē tāṉākac ceyyumavaṉ iṭam
the place of the god who transformed those who fix their thoughts on the ultimate reality (god), like himself.
caṭṭaṅkaḷ kattiṭṭōr kalantu ilaṅku naṟporuḷ kālē ōvātārmēvum kaḻumalavaḷanakar
is the fertile city of Kaḻumalam where people who have mastered the six aṇkams of the vētam, ceaselessly meditate upon the shining feet of the god which pervades everywhere;

imparkku ētam ceytiṭṭu iruntu araṉ payiṉṟa veṟpu
in addition to giving trouble to the people of this world, when he lifted up the mountin, the permanent abode of Araṉ.
ērār pūnēr ōr pātattu eḻil viral avaṇ niṟuviṭṭu
fixing firmly a beautiful toe of one foot which is as soft as a beautiful flower.
am poṟpūṇ veṉṟittōḷ aḻintu vantaṉam ceytāṟku
to ((Irāvaṇaṉ)) who worshipped god after the strength of his former victorious shoulders wearing beautiful ornaments, was destroyed.
ār ār kūr vāḷ vāṇāḷ aṉṟu aruḷ puripavaṉatu iṭam
the place of god who granted him on that day long life and a sword of many sharp points.
kampattu ār tumpittiṇ kavuḷ corinta mummatak kārār cēṟu ār māvītik kaḻumala vaḷa nakarē
is the city of fertile Kaḻumalam which has big streets where the mud is changed into black mire by the three musts flowing from the temples of the strong elephant which is tied to a pillar.

{$} māltāṉ paṉṟik kōlam koṇṭu ippaṭittaṭam payiṉṟu iṭappāṉ ām
Māl, digged into the earth ceaselessly assuming the form of pig.
ampuccēr uyarnta paṅkayattavaṉōtāṉ ōtāṉ mēyappaṟavaiyiṉ uruvukoḷa
the Piramaṉ seated in the superior lotus which grows in water, assuming the form of the bird which was his mount, though he was well versed in the Vētams.
oṉṟiṭṭu uruviṉatu aṭimuṭiyum aḵtu uṇarātu ceṉṟiṭṭē vantippa
when both worshipped god being unable to find the feet and head of the form that appeared before them, though united in their efforts.
tirukkaḷam koḷ paiṅkaṇiṉ tēcāl vēṟuōr ākāram terivu ceytavaṉatu iṭam
the place of the god who took another form, with lustre, tirukkaḷam koḷ paiṅkaṇiṉ

kālēvārā mēlē pāy kaḻumalavaḷanakar

{$} taṭṭu iṭṭē
holding the eating-plates in the hand.
muṭṭikkaittaṭukku iṭukki niṉṟu uṇāttāmē pēṇātē nāḷum camaṇoṭum uḻalpavarum
those who wander with the doctrine of jinism, fix the seat made of palmyra leaves in the first of the hand and eat their food standing, as if they do not mind anything as important.
(1) iṭṭattāl attam tāṉ itu aṉṟu atu eṉṟu niṉṟavarkku ēyāmē vāy ētuc col puttarum
and buddhists who according to their caprice give the reason without any appropriateness for those who question, This is not the truth, that is the truth'
ilaimali marutam pūppuṭṭattē aṭṭiṭṭup putaikkum meykkoḷ puttarum
and who cover their bodies with robes soaked in myrtle dye and smear the paste of the flowers of myrobalan tree on their buttocks.
pōlvārtām ōrāmē pōyp (2) puṇarvu ceytavaṉatu iṭam
the place of the god who concealed his nature by his craftiness so that people like them might not know it.
kāl veṭṭik kaṭṭittīṅkarumpu tanta paimpulaṉ kālē vārā mēlē pāy kaḻumala vaḷanakarē
is the fertile city of Kaḻumalam where the stems of sweet sugar-canes which yield jaggery and from which the sweet juice flows through channels and overflows the banks.

{$} kañcattēṉ uṇṭiṭṭē kaḷittu vaṇṭu caṇpakakkāṉētēṉē pōr ārum kaḻumala nakar iṟaiyai
on the Lord who dwells in Kaḻumalam where the bees which have drunk the honey in the lotus flowers become intoxicated and fight with another kind of bee which lives in the jungle of champak trees
tēṉ nēr ār tamiḻ virakaṉa moḻikaḷ
the verses by Ñāṉacampantaṉ who is proficent in Tamiḻ which is as sweet as honey.
eñcat tēyvu iṉṟikkē
without any deficiency.
imaittu icaittu amaitta koṇṭu
with the help of those verses which were done to the accompaniment of music, in the twinkling of the eye.
ēḻē ēḻē nālē mūṉṟu iyalicai iyalpā, vañcattu ēyvu iṉṟikkē maṉam koḷap payiṉṟuvōr, tañcaic cār caṇpaik kōṉ camaitta naṟ kalaittuṟaitāmē pōlvār
those who have so mastered the good branches of arts which were revealed by the Lord of Caṇpi who is the refuge of all, that they are the personification of those arts, repeat them with sincerity without frud in the twenty one notes of music of their own desire.
mārpē cērvāḷ vāṉōrcīr matinutal maṭavaralē
the goddess of wealth who has a forehead like the crescent and excellence of the tēvar will stay in the chest of those people.

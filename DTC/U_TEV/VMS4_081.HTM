{4:81}__1+
{$} karunaṭṭa kaṇṭaṉai
one who has fixed firmly the dark colour in his neck.
aṇṭattalaivaṉai
the chief of the world.
kaṟpakattai
who can be compared to the Kaṟpakam tree.
cerunaṭṭa mummatil eyya vallāṉai
one who is capable of discharging an arrow on the three forts which were inimical.
centī muḻaṅkat tirunaṭṭam āṭiyai
who danced when the red fire made a roaring noise.
tillaikku iṟaiyai
the Lord of Tillai.
ciṟṟampalattup perunaṭṭamāṭiyai
the great dancer in Ciṟṟampalam
vāṉavar kōṉ eṉṟu vāḻttuvaṉ
I shall praise him as Lord of the inhabitants of the celestial world.
{4:81}__2+
{$} aṭiyavaṟkāk kaṉṟiya kālaṉaik kālāl kaṭintāṉ
one who destroyed Kālaṉ who is an adept in taking away the lives of living beings, in order to save his devotee, kicking him with his leg.
tillaiyuḷ ciṟṟampalattu naṭṭam ceṉṟu toḻumiṉkaḷ
please go to tillai and worship the dance performed in ciṟṟampalam in tillai.
eṉṟu vantāy' eṉṉum emperumāṉ taṉ tirukkuṟippu oṉṟiyiruntu niṉaimiṉkaḷ
Your meditate on the significant looks of our Lord which seem to ask, when did you come?'
um tamakku ūṉam illai
there will be no fault on your part.
{4:81}__3+
{$} kal maṉavīr
stone hearted people!
kaḻiyum karuttē collik kāṇpatu eṉṉē
what is the use of repeating the ideas that have passed, without any good?
naṉ maṉavar navil tillaiyuḷ ciṟṟampalattu naṭṭam (āṭi)
having danced in ciṟṟampalam in tillai which is praised by people of good mind.
poṉ malaiyil veḷḷikkuṉṟatupōlap polintilaṅki
Havng shone like a hill of silver set upon a golden mountain.
eṉ maṉamē oṉṟippukkaṉaṉ
he entered into my mind to become one with it.
pōnta cuvaṭu illaiyē
there is not even the trace of his having entered into my mind.
{4:81}__4+
{$} kuṉitta puruvamum
the curved eye-brows
kovvaic cevvāyiṟ kumiṇ cirippum
the gentle smile in the red lips which are like the fruits of the common creeper on the hedges. (kumiṇ is a rare word; perhaps it is a variant form of kumiḻ)
paṉittacaṭaiyum
the cool matted locks
pavaḷam mēṉiyil pāl veṇṇīṟum
the white ashes resembling milk smeared on the form which is red as coral.
iṉittam uṭaiyai eṭutta poṟpātamum kāṇappeṟṟāl
if one is fortunate to see the beautiful and sweet foot raised in dance, (iṉittam
a word coined from iṉimai)
inta māṉilattē maṉittappiṟaviyum vēṇṭuvatē
Being born in this wide world as a human being is also desirable. (maṉitappiṟavi has been changed into maṉittappiṟavi due to poetic licence, to be in keeping with yetukai; people in search of god despise human birth as useless, that is why um has been added to piṟavi; This verse is quoted by saivaites)
{4:81}__5+
{$} pattar uḷḷīr
you who are devotees!
ītu ōr piṟavi nantamakku vāyttatu
we who are fortunate in getting this human birth which is very difficult to get must regard it as very valuable.
pārttaṟkup pācupatam aruḷ ceytavaṉ
one who granted the weapon of pacupatam to pārttaṉ(Arjuṉaṉ)
kōttu aṉṟu muppuram tī vaḷaittāṉ
He made the fire consume the three cities on that day long ago, by fixing the arrow in the bow.
tillai ampalattuk kūttaṉukku āṭpaṭṭiruppatu aṉṟō nam kūḻaimaiyē
Is it not our humble duty to be a servant of the dancer in ampalam in tillai.
{4:81}__6+
{$} pūttaṉa poṟcaṭai poṉpōl miḷira
the beautiful flourishing matted locks to glitter like gold.
purikaṇaṅkaḷ
the desiring groups of devotees.
ārttaṉa
roared vociferously.
koṭṭi arittaṉa palkuṟaḷ pūtakaṇam
many dwarf pūtakaṇam to beat the drum Koṭṭi intermittently
[[Koṭṭi: a percussion instrument]]
tētteṉa eṉṟu icai vaṇṭukaḷ pāṭu ciṟṟampalattuk kūttaṉiṉ
better than the dancer in ciṟṟampalam where bees hum tunes onomatopoetically like tēttena
kūttuvallār uḷarō eṉtaṉ kōlvaḷaikkē
who is capable of strange incidents in the matter of my daughter wearing collection of bangles. (This is in the form of akapporul; This is the statement of the foster-mother)
{4:81}__7+
{$} muṭikoṇṭa mattamum
the datura flower worm on the head.
mukkaṇṇiṉ nōkkum
the (cool) looks of the three eyes, gentle smile.
muṟuvalippum
gentle smile.
tuṭikoṇṭakaiyum
the hand that holds tuṭi
[[EXPL: tuṭi: a small drum having two sides, but tapering in the middle, and which is beaten on both sides.]]
tutainta veṇṇīṟum
white ashes in which the form was steeped
curikuḻalāḷ paṭi koṇṭa pākamum
the fit half of the lady with curly hairs.
pāypulit tōlum
the skin of the pouncing tiger
[[pāy: an epithet fit to be given when it was alive.]]
[[PP: naṉi mural vaḷai muṭi aḻipu iḻipu (paripātal, 2-40);
muraṟal uyiruḷ vaḻiyaṭaiyāy vantu (Do, commentary).]]
tillaiyampalak kūttaṉ kuraikaḻal
the jingling anklets of the dancer in ampalam in tillai.
eṉ pāvi neñcil kuṭikoṇṭavā
what a wonder it is that they occupied my cruel mind with the above mentioned things!
{4:81}__8+
{$} aṇi tillaic ciṟṟampalattu araṉē
araṉ who is in the ciṟṟampalam of beautiful tillai!
paṭaikkalamāka uṉ nāmattu eḻuttu añcum eṉ nāvil koṇṭēṉ
I was chanting with my tongue as a weapon the five letters that form your name (eḻuttu añcu pañcāksharam)
iṭaikkalam allēṉ
I am not one who came in the middle.
eḻupiṟappum uṉakku āṭceykiṉṟēṉ
I have been your servant all the seven births.
tuṭaikkiṉum pōkēṉ
I will not go leaving you even if you dismiss me
toḻutu vaṇaṅkit tūnīṟu aṇintu uṉaṭaikkalam kaṇṭāy
I am seeking refuge in you, having worshipped and bowed you having smeared myself with pure ashes.
{4:81}__9+
{$} poṉ ottamēṉi veṇṇīṟu aṇintu
Having adorned with white sacred ashes the form which glitters like gold.
puricaṭaikaḷmiṉ ottu ilaṅka
the twisted matted locks to shine like lightning.
palitērntu uḻalum viṭaṅka vēṭac ciṉṉattiṉāl malitillaiyuḷ
in tillai which is abundant with the marks of his beauteous form when he wanders begging alms.
ciṟṟampalattu eṉ attaṉ naṭṭam āṭal kaṇṭu
on seeing the dance of my father in ciṟṟampalam
iiru nilam iṉpuṟṟatu
the people of this wide world enjoyed pleasure. (āl expletive; nilam here stands for people inhabiting the earth).
{4:81}__10+
{$} takkaṉ taṉ vēḷviyil cantiraṉaiccāṭa eṭuttatu
The foot was raised to trample the moon in the sacrifice to takkaṉ (takkaṉ).
kālaṉai vīṭa eṭuttatu
was raised to destroy Kālaṉ (kālaṉ the god of death).
nāraṇaṉ nāṉ mukaṉum tēṭa eṭuttatu
was raised to be searched by nāraṇaṉ and nāṉmukaṉ (Vishnu and Brahma)
tillaiyuḷ ciṟṟampalattu naṭṭam āṭa eṭuttiṭṭa pātam aṉṟō
is not that foot which was raised to dance in ciṟṟampalam in tillai.
nammai āṭkoṇṭatu
that accepted us as his protege?

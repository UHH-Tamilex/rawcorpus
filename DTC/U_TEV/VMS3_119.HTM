puḷḷittōl āṭai
the dress of Civaṉ is the skin of a spotted deer.
[[puḷḷi uḻai māṉiṉ tōlāṉ kaṇṭāy (appar, tirumaṟikkāṭu, 4)]]
pūṇpatu nākam
his ornaments are cobras.
pūcu cāntam poṭi nīṟu
the sandal paste that he smears is fine powder of holy ash.
koḷḷit tī viḷakku
the fire in the firebrand is the light
kūvikaḷ kūṭṭam
pēys are the audience that witness the dance.
kāḷiyaik kuṇañcey kūttu uṭaiyōṉ
danced to decrease the fury of Kaḷi.
aḷḷal kār āmai akku vāṉ matiyam ēykka
when the interior of the black tortoise living in mire appears like the moon in the sky.
muḷ tāḻaikaḷ āṉai veḷḷaik kompu īṉum viripoḻil vīḻimiḻalaiyāṉ eṉa viṉai keṭum
the Karmams will perish if one say the Lord in Vīḻimiḻali where in the extensive gardens the fragrant screw-pines with thorns blossom like the elephants tusks;

icaintavāṟu aṭiyār iṭutuval
the flowers offered in worship by devotees according to their capacity.
[[PP: ceruppaṭi āvaṉa viruppuṟu tuvalē
(tirukkaṇṇappa tēvar tirumaṟam (1) 102]]
vāṉōr iḻuku cantaṉattu iḷaṅkamalap pacum poṉ vācikai mēl parappuvāy
you will spread them above the garland worn on the head made of pure gold, just blossomed lotus flowers and sandal-wood paste smeared over your body by the celestials.
patti ceyyātavar pakkal karappāy
you will conceal yourself from those who are not pious towards you.
acumpu (1) pāy kaḻaṉi alar kayal mutalōṭu aṭuttu arintu eṭuttavāṉ cummai
the bundle of ears of corn which have been cut off along with carp fish, and flowers in the fields where cozing water flows.
vicumpu tūrppaṉa pōl vimmiya vīḻimiḻalaiyāṉ eṉviṉai keṭum
the Karmams will perish if one says Civaṉ in Vīḻimiḻi where they increase in height as if they cover and hide the sky.
[[Variant reading: (1) vāy]]

āṟu aṅkaṉ

nīṟṟaṉ

nāṉ maṟaiyaṉ

nīlam ār miṭaṟṟaṉ

neṟṟikkaṇ oruttaṉ

is the inner soul for all living beings.
ilaṉ
at the same time he is out of them.
kēṭu ili
who knows not death.
umai kōṉ
the husband of Umi.
tiruttamāy

nāḷum nīr āṭum poykai; ciṟiyavar aṟiviṉil mikka viruttarai aṭi vīḻntu iṭam pukum vīḻi miḻalaiyāṉ eṉa
if one says the god in Vīḻimiḻi where young people fall at the feet of old people of ripe wisdom and go to their places, and has natural tanks in which people bathe dily.

tāṅka arum kālam
at the time of the great deluge when everything is absorbed into Civaṉ.
tavira vantu iruvar tammoṭum kūṭiṉār aṅkam pāṅkiṉāl tarittu
having carried by his good nature the skeleton of the two, Māl and Piramaṉ when they joined him as their functions came to an end.
paṇṭu pōl ellām paṇṇiya kaṇṇutaṟparamar
the supreme god with a frontal eye, who created everything as before.
tēm koḷ pūṅkamuku, teṅku, iḷaṅkoṭi, mā, ceṇpakam, vaṇpalā iluppai, vēṅkai pū makiḻāl
by the beautiful areca-palm which has sweetness, cocoanut trees, tender creepers, mango-trees, chanpak trees, fertile jack trees, south indian mahua trees, east indian kino trees and pointed-learned ape-flower trees with profuse flowers.
veyil pukā vīḻimiḻalaiyāṉ eṉa
if one says the god in Vīḻimiḻi where in the gardens sunlight does not enter.
viṉai keṭum
his Karmams will perish

kūcum mā mayāṉam kōyil
the temple is the great cremation ground which people abhor from fear;
[[kōyil cuṭukāṭu(tiruvācakam tiruccāḻal, 3)]]
vāyiṟkaṇ kuṭavāyiṟṟaṉa cila pūtam
there are some pot-bellied pūtam-s at the entrance.
pūcummā cāntam pūti
holy ash is the sandal paste which Civaṉ smears.
mel lōti pāti
has as a half a lady with soft tresse of hir.
naṟ poṅku aravu araiyāṉ
wears a furious cobra in the waist.
vācam ām puṉṉai, mauval, ceṅkaḻunīr malar aṇaintu eḻunta vāṉ teṉṟal vīcum mām poḻil
in the mango-groves where the superior southern balmy breeze blows, having come into contact with the flowers purple water-lily, jasmine and mast-wood tree which is full of fragrance.
tēṉ tuvalai cēr vīḻimiḻalaiyāṉ eṉa
if one says Civaṉ in Vīḻimiḻali where drops of honey are gathered.
viṉai keṭum
his Karmams will perish.

pāti ōr mātar
Civaṉ has a lady on one half.
mālum ōr pākar
has Māl also on one side.
paṅkayattu ayaṉum ōr pālar
has Ayaṉ also who is seated in the lotus flower on one side.
[[This description is about the ēkapātattirimūrti]]
āti āy naṭu āy, antam āy niṉṟa aṭikaḷār
the god who is the origin, the middle and the end of all things.
amararkaṭku amarar
the god above the immortals.
pōtu cēr ceṉṉip purūravāp paṇi cey pūcurar
the brahmins to whom pururavā who adorned his head with flowers, performed service.
pūmakaṉ aṉaiya vētiyar vētattu oli aṟā vīḻimiḻalaiyāṉ eṉa viṉai keṭum
the Karmams will perish if one says Civaṉ in Vīḻimiḻali where the sound produced by the chanting of the vētams by brahmins who can be compared to Piramaṉ, never ceases.
[[pūmakaṉ Piramaṉ seated in the lotus flower.]]

taṉ tavam periya calantaraṉ uṭalam taṭinta cakkaram aruḷ eṉṟu
praying, grant me the discus which cut the body of Calantaraṉ who was great by his penance.
aṉṟu ari vaḻipaṭṭu iḻicciya vimāṉattu iṟaiyavaṉ
the Lord who dwells under the vimāṉam which was lowered from heaven by Ari (Māl) who worshipped Civaṉ here in days gone by.
piṟai aṇi caṭaiyaṉ
who adorns his caṭi with the crescent.
niṉṟa nāḷ kālai
in the early morning which was very long
irunta nāḷ mālai
and in the evening of the day which stayed for a long time.
kiṭanta maṇ mēl varu kaliyai veṉṟa vētiyarkaḷ viḻā aṟā vīḻimiḻalaiyāṉ eṉa
if one says the god in Vīḻimiḻali where festivals never cease and which has brahmins who have conquered the evils of the Kali age in this earth.
viṉai keṭum
his Karmams will perish.

kaṭutta vāḷ arakkaṉ kayilai aṉṟu eṭutta karam uram ciram aṭuttatu ōr viralāl nerintu alaṟa
when the angry and cruel arakkaṉ roared when his hands which lifted the mountin, Kayili, in the distant past, his chest and head were crushed by a toe that was pressed lightly.
añcu eḻuttu uraikka
when he chanted the mantiram of five letters.
[[paṇṭai irāvaṇaṉ pāṭi uyntaṉa... añcu eḻuttumē (campantar, pañcākkarattiruppatikam, 8)]]
taṭam miku neṭu vāḷ aruḷiṉaṉ
granted him a big and long sword.
paṭitta nāṉ maṟai kēṭṭirunta paiṅkiḷikaḷ pataṅkaḷai ōtap pāṭu irunta viṭaikkulam payiṟṟum viripoḻil vīḻimiḻalaiyāṉ eṉa
if one says god in Vīḻimiḻali which has extensive gardens where the herd of bulls which were near, practise the words which the green parrots chant having listened to the four vetams which were recited by brahmins.
viṉaikeṭum
his Karmams will perish.

aḷaviṭal uṟṟa ayaṉ neṭumālum aṇṭam maṇ keṇṭiyum kāṇā muḷai eri āya mūrttiyai
Civaṉ who has a body of rising fire which could not be measured by Ayaṉ and tall Māl who wanted to measure it by flying into space and digging into the earth.
tīrttam mukkaṇ em mutalvaṉai
our origin of all things who is pure and has three eyes.
muttai
who is as precious as the pearl.
taḷai aviḻ kamalattaviciṉ mēl aṉṉam taṉ iḷam peṭaiyoṭum pulki
the male swan that is on the seat of the lotus which has blossomed, embraces its young female swan.
viṉaik katirk kavari vīca vīṟṟirukkum miḻalaiyāṉ eṉa
if one says Civaṉ in miḻali where it sits majestically when the ripe corns of paddy fan it like the yak til fan.
viṉai keṭum
his Karmams will perish.

kaiyil kañcip pōtu uṭaiyar kōcārak kalatikaḷ kaṭṭurai viṭṭu
leaving aside the fabricated words of the wicked men who have only perception of the range of the five senses, and who have gruel in their hands at meal time.
añci, tē iriya eḻunta nañcu ataṉai uṇṭu
having consumed the poison that rose to make the celestials flee out of fear.
amararkku amutu aruḷi
and giving the nectar to the immortals.
iñcikkē katalikkaṉi viḻa, kamukiṉ kulaiyoṭum paḻam viḻa
when the fruits of the areca-palm fall with its clusters cuse the plintin fruits fall on the fortified wall,
teṅkiṉ miñcukkē mañcu cēr poḻil vīḻimiḻalaiyāṉ eṉaviṉai keṭum
the Karmams of those who say Civaṉ in Vīḻimiḻali which has gardens where at the top of cocoanut trees clouds rest, will perish.

vēntar vantu (1) iṟaiñca vētiyar vīḻimiḻalaiyuḷ viṇ iḻi vimāṉattu ēynta taṉ tēviyōṭu uṟaikiṉṟa īcaṉai em perumāṉai
on our Lord and Civaṉ who dwells with his Tēvi who is suited to him, in the vimāṉam which decended from heaven, in Vīḻimiḻali where brahmins are crowded, to which kings come from their countries to pay their respects.
tōynta nīrt tōṇipurattu uṟai maṟaiyōṉ
the brahmin who dwells in Tōṇipuram which has holy tanks in which people bathe
tūmoḻi ñāṉacampantaṉ vāynta pāmālai vāy navil vārai vāṉavar vaḻipaṭuvār
the celestials will worship those who recite the garland of suitable verses composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ of holy words.

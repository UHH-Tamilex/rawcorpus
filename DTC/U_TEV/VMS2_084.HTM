[[There is a tradition that Campantar transformed Naṉippaḷḷi which was formerly an arid desert tract into a maritime tract; this is mentioned in Tīrukkaḷiṟṟuppaṭiyār, verse 12:
nātaṉ naṉi paḷḷi cūḻnakar kāṉakam ākki aḵtē pōtiṉ malivayal ākkiya kōṉ
(āḷutiya piḷḷiyār tiruvantāti 7);
ñālattiṉar aṟiya maṉṉum naṉipaḷḷiyatu, pālai taṉai neytal ākkiyum
(āḷuṭiya pilliyār tiruvulāmāli 75);
this is not mentioned in periya purāṇam.]]

[[It is famous as the birth place of the mother of Ñāṉacampantar (Periya Purāṇam, Tiruñaṉacampantar purāṇam, verse 109)]]

{2:84}__1+
{$} kāraikaḷ, kūkai, mullai, kaḷa, vākai īkai, paṭartoṭari, kaḷḷi, kaviṉi
Kārikaḷ, kūki, mulli, kaḷa, vāki, īki, spreading toṭari and kaḷḷi growing beautifully and luxuriantly.
cūraikaḷ pampu vimmu cuṭukāṭu amarnta civaṉmēya cōlai nakartāṉ
the city with gardens which Civaṉ who loved the cremation ground where cūrikal which grow crowded are full, desired to dwell.
[[kārai: a low shrub with sharp axillary spines met with in scrubby jungles and common waste places.]]
[[kaḷa-(kaḷā): a low spreading spiny evergreen shrub.]]
[[vākai: sirissa tree]]
[[īkai (īṅkai): tiger-stopper]]
[[paṭar toṭari: spreading ashy babool]]
[[cūrai: oblique leaved jujube]]
[[kūkai: east indian arrow-root]]
[[mullai: wild jasmine]]
tēraikaḷ āraicāya mitikoḷḷa
the frogs trample on āri to make it bend.
[[ārai: an aquatic crytogamus plant used for greens]]
vāḷai kuti koḷḷa
the scabbard fish leap
vaḷḷai tuvaḷa
the creeping hindweed becomes flexible
nāraikaḷ āral vāri
when the cranes catch in a sweep a kind of fish called āri
[[āral: brownish or greenish sand-eel]]
vayal mēti vaikum naṉipaḷḷi (pōlum) namarkāḷ
People belonging to our religion! is Naṉipaḷḷi where the buffaloes wallow in the fields.
{2:84}__2+
{$} namarkāḷ
people of our religion!
caṭaiyiṭaipukku oṭuṅkiyuḷ taṅkuveḷḷam vaḷar tiṅkaḷ kaṇṇi ayalē iṭai iṭai vaittatu okkum malar tottu mālai iṟaivaṉ iṭam koḷpatitāṉ
the place occupied by Civaṉ who wears a garland of bunches of flowers which appears to be tucked in by the side of the chaplet of a crescent which appears to grow in the flood that is being restrined, entering into the cati and stays there.
maṭaiyiṭai vāḷaipāya mukiḻvāy nerintu maṇam nāṟum nīlam malarum, naṭaiyuṭai aṉṉam vaikupuṉal ampaṭappai naṉi paḷḷi (pōlum)
is Naṉipalli which has gardens fed by water where swans eminent by their git stay and where the blue nelumbo flowers spread their fragrance as the ends of buds are crushed when the scabbard fish leaps in the small sluries of the fields
[[(malarum naṉipaḷḷi, vaiku paṭappai naṉi paḷḷi is the syntactical link]]

{$} namarkāḷ
People of our religion!
peṟumalarkoṇṭu toṇṭar vaḻipāṭu ceyya oḻipāṭu ilāta perumāṉ
the god whom the devotees worship with flowers that they can get and which never ceases.
kaṟumalar kaṇṭam āka viṭa muṇṭa kāḷaiiṭam āya nakar, kātalnakar
the place to which the youth who drank the poison to make the neck appear like a black flower; is warmly attached.
veṟumalar toṭṭu viṭṭa vicaipōṉa kompiṉ viṭupōtu alarnta viraicūḻ naṟumalar alli pulli oli vaṇṭu uṟaṅku naṉipaḷḷi (pōlum)
is Naṉipaḷḷi where bees desirous of honey touch the empty flowers in the branches which hang low they as they leave them and sleep humming embracing the inner petals of the good flowers which were not touched by them attracted by the fragrance, their humming now having stopped.

shining with the brilliant garland of skulls by the side of the caṭi where the cool Kaṅki stays.

is Naṉipaḷḷi where the white cranes stay in the evening in the cool gardens.

wearing a women's ear-ring in one ear
oru kātu ilaṅku curi caṅku niṉṟu puraḷa
and an ear-ring made of spiralling conch which is shining and slipping in the other ear.

the place to which our father who dances in the fire standing in the cremation ground, is very much attached

those who want to attin bliss immediately go as it is the rule, and sprinkle on their heads fragrant water

mēkamoṭu ōṭutiṅkaḷ malarā aṇintu
having adorned himself with the crescent that moves quickly in the sky along with the cloud;
[[Civaṉ has clouds also on his caṭi; vaṉtiṟal varuṇaṉ viṭṭamāriyai vilakka īcaṉ, miṉtikaḻ caṭaiyiṉiṉṟu nīkkiya mēkam nāṉkum (Parañcoti muṉivar, Tiruviḷiyātaṟpurāṇam, 19-26)]]
malaiyāṉ maṭantai maṇipoṉ ākam ōr pākam āka aṉal āṭum entai perumāṉ amarnta nakar (tāṉ)
the city where our father and god, Civaṉ, who dances in the fire having as a half the body of the dughter of the mountin glittering like beautiful gold.
ūkamoṭu āṭumanti ukaḷum cilampa akil unti oṇpoṉ iṭaṟi
striking aginst bright gold and pushing eagle-wood found in the mountin where the black monkey plays with its female.
nākamoṭu āram vāru puṉal vantu alaikkum naṉi paḷḷi (pōlum)
is Naṉipaḷḷi where the water (of the Kaviri) which gathers the curapuṉṉi tree and sandalwood tree, dashes aginst the banks.

koṭukoṭṭivīṇai murala
when the instrument koṭukoṭṭi and viṇi are played.
takaimali taṇṭu cūlam aṉal umiḻum nākam, vakai mali vaṉṉi koṉṟai matamattam vaitta perumāṉ ukanta nakar (tāṉ)
the city that Civaṉ who placed on his person datura flowers, koṉṟi, different kinds of vaṉṉi leaves (indian mesquit) a cobra spitting fire, a trident and an exceedingly beautiful club.
pukaimali kantamālaipuṉaivārkaḷ pūcal, paṇivārkaḷ pāṭal peruki
loud uproar of those who adorn Civaṉ with fragrant garlands to which is added incense; and the songs of those who pay obeisance, increase in volume.
[[cf. tukiṟkum mālai mutaliyavaṟṟiṟkum eṭutta aḻakiya iṉiya akiṟ pukaiyum (Cilappatikāram, 13-122-126, Aṭiyārkkunallār)]]
nakaimalimuttu ilaṅku maṇalcūḻ kiṭakkai naṉipaḷḷi (pōlum)
is Naṉipaḷḷi which has long stretches of sand which shine like the brilliant pearl.

valam miku vāḷaṉ, vēlaṉ, vaḷaivāḷ eyiṟṟu matiyā arakkaṉvaliyōṭu, ulammiku tōḷkaḷ olka viralāl aṭartta perumāṉ ukanta nakar(tāṉ)
the city desired by Civaṉ who pressed with his toe the strength and the shoulders which were stronger than a round stone, of the Arakkaṉ who has bright curved teeth, a sword bringing victory and vēl, and who did not esteem Civaṉ, to become injured.
nilamiku kīḻum mēlum nikar ātum illai eṉa niṉṟa nīti ataṉai nalam miku toṇṭarnāḷum aṭiparaval ceyyum naṉi paḷḷi (pōlum)
is Naṉipaḷḷi where the devotees who have good qualities worship his feet prising all the time the nature of Civaṉ who is without anything equal to him in the lower worlds and the upper worlds

niṟa uru oṉṟu tōṉṟi eri oṉṟi niṉṟatu oru nīrmai cīrmai niṉaiyār
without thinking the unequalled beauty and its greatness where a bright body appeared before them in the form of a column of fire.
aṟa uruvēta nāvaṉ ayaṉōṭu
along with Ayaṉ who chants the Vētams which are the embodiment of right conduct,
mālum aṟiyāta aṇṇal nakar (tāṉ)

puṟavirimullai mauval kuḷir piṇṭi puṉṉai puṉaikoṉṟai tuṉṟu potuḷi
the jasmine which blossoms in the sylvan tract, the jasmine, the cool asōka tree, mast-wood tree, beautiful koṉṟi which grow crowded and are prosperous.
naṟaviri pōtu tātu putuvācam nāṟu naṉipaḷḷi (pōlum)
is Naṉipaḷḷi where the buds which unfold the honey and the pollen, spread new fragrance.

{$} kaṭal varai ōtam malku kaḻikāṉal pāṉal kamaḻ kāḻi yeṉṟu karutappaṭu, poruḷ āṟum nālum uḷatāka vaittapatiyāṉa ñāṉamuṉivaṉ
the sage endowed with Civañāṉam who is a native of the place Kaḻi where the six ankams and four Vētams always exist and where the blue nelumbo flowers spread their fragrance in the seaside gardens near the backwaters where the waves rage from the sea like mountins.
attarpiyal mēl iruntu iṉṉicaiyāl uraitta paṉuval
with the help of the musical compositions which were composed by sitting on the shoulders of his father
iṭupaṟai oṉṟa naṭu iruḷ āṭum entai naṉi paḷḷi uḷka viṉaikeṭutal āṇai namatē
it is our authority for the karmams to perish if one thinks of Naṉipaḷḷi of our father who dances at midnight when the drums measure time.
[[āṇai namatē (Campantar, kōlaṟu patikam, ll; tiruvētikuṭi, ll; tirukkaḻumalam-paṇ-puṟanīrmi, 11); muttippakava mutalvaṉ tiruvaṭiyai, attikkum pattar etir āṇai namatu eṉṉavalāṉ (Aḷuṭiya piḷḷiyār tiruttoki, 45); transforming Naṉipalli into a maritime tract is mentioned in the following lines also: tāḻum caraṇaccataṅkaip paruvattē, pālaiyum neytalum pāṭavalāṉ (do, ll-16-17)]]

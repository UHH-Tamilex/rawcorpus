{2:40}__1+
{$} empirāṉ(um)
our chief.
eṉakku amutam āvāṉum
and the Lord who is sweet like nectar to me.
taṉ aṭaintār tampirāṉ āvāṉum
and the benefactor of those who approach him.
taḻal ēntukaiyāṉum
and one who holds fire in his hand.
kampamā kari uritta kāpāli
and the Lord having a skull who flayed the big elephant tied to a post (kampamā
is a generic epithet as the elephant that Civaṉ flayed was born in the sacrificial fire of the sages of tārukāvaṉam)
kaṟaikkaṇṭaṉ
and who has a black neck.
vampu ulām poḻiṟ piramapurattu uṟaiyum vāṉavaṉē
is the god who resides in piramapuram which has gardens from which fragrance spreads everywhere.
{2:40}__2+
{$} tām eṉṟum maṉam taḷarāt takutiyiṉarāy
those who are always fit and firm in their minds.
ulakattukku ām eṉṟu caraṇpukuntār tamaikkākkum karuṇaiyiṉāṉ
the Lord protects by his grace those who take refuge in god, thinking he is the only refuge for the world.
ōm eṉṟu maṟaipayilvār piramapurattu uṟaikiṉṟa, kāmaṉ taṉ uṭal eriyak kaṉal cērnta kaṇṇāṉē
is Lord who has an eye of fire which made the body of Kāmaṉ to burn, and who resides in piramapuram which has people who repeat many times maṟai beginning it with ōm.
{2:40}__3+
{$} nalneñcē! uṉai irantēṉ
my good Mind! I implored you.
uyvataṉai vēṇṭutiyēl
if you wish deliverance from the cycle of birth and death!
nam perumāṉ tiruvaṭiyē uṉṉam ceytiru
always be thinking only of the feet of our Lord.
vāyatuvē
my tongue!
aṉṉam cēr piramapurattu āramutai eppōtum am cīrpaṉ
always praise the greatness of the god who is like the rare nectar who resides in piramapuram where swans flock together.
kaṇṇē
my eyes!
parintiṭappār
look at his form to make him bestow his grace on you.
{2:40}__4+
{$} maṉamē
my mind!
cā nāḷ iṉṟi
without the day when our life will come to its end.
caṅkai taṉait tavirppikkum kōṉ āḷum tiruvaṭikkē ettaṉaiyum koḻumalar tūvu
scatter any amount of fertile flowers at the feet of our Lord which admit us into his grace, and who removes our doubt whether our end will come or not.
nā
my tongue!
tēṉ āḷum poḻiṟ piramapurattu uṟaiyum tīvaṇaṉai nāḷum nal niyamam ceytu avaṉcīr naviṉṟu ēttu
praise ceaselessly the fame, after finishing your good routine daily, of the god whose colour is like fire and who resides piramapuram having gardens with plenty of honey.
{2:40}__5+
{$} kaṇ nutalāṉ
the Lord has an eye on the forehead.
veṇṇīṟṟāṉ
has shamppod himself with white ash.
kamaḻ caṭaiyāṉ
has fragrant matted locks of hair. (As they there are many flowers, the epithet kamaḻ is given to caṭai)
viṭai ēṟi
one who is seated on a bull.
peṇ itam ām uruvattāṉ
Has a form in which a lady is agreeable (comfortably placed)
piññakaṉ
He is the destroyer (piññakaṉ some scholars think that this word is a corruption of the word piñcakaṉ, which means the Lord who adorns himself with a peacock's feather in his head)
pēr pala uṭaiyāṉ
he has countless names.
viṇ nutalāt tōṉṟiya cīrp piramapuram toḻavirumpi eṇṇutal ām celvattai iyalpāka aṟintōm
we learnt naturally the greatness of the wealth which comes to one by meditating always with a desire to worship piramapuram which has a fame that it is uppermost in the minds of the gods of heaven. (viṇ-viṇulakattavar)
{2:40}__6+
{$} erutu ēṟi
Having sat upon a bull.
koṅku ēyum malarccōlaik kuḷir piramapurattu uṟaiyum caṅkē ottu oḷirmēṉiccaṅkaraṉ taṉ taṉmaikaḷē
The different natures of cankaraṉ who has a white form completely resembling the conch and who resides in piramapuram, which is cool by the gardens of fragrant flowers (koṅku honey) (For the subject taṉmaikaḷ the predicate is aruḷpuriyum emperumāṉākiya caṅkaraṉ).
eṅkēṉum yātākip piṟantiṭiṉum
wherever and in whatever kind of lower animals his devotees may be born.
taṉ aṭiyārkku iṅkē eṉṟu aruḷpuriyum emperumāṉ
our Lord who bestows his grace upon them, I am here to help you in this birth.
{2:40}__7+
{$} cilai (atu) veñcilaiyākat tiripuramūṉṟu ericeyta ilai nuṉai vēl taṭakkaiyaṉ
the Lord has in his big hand a sharp weapon of three blades and he burnt the three wandering cities having converted the mountain into a cruel bow (cilai-malai, vil; tiripuram is viṉaittokai as it is followed by mūṉṟu)
[[variant readings: cilaiyatuvē; vilainuṉi. ēntiḻaiyāḷ oru kūṟaṉ
has as a half a lady beautifully decked with jewels.]]
alaipuṉal cūḻ piramapurattu arumaṇiyai aṭipaṇintāl
If one bows to the feet of the god who is like a rare jewel, and who is in piramapuram surrounded by moving water.
nilai uṭaiya peruñcelvam nīṭuulakil peṟalām
one can obtain in the superior world (the abode of Civaṉ) the great and permanent wealth of enjoying the Lord always.
{2:40}__8+
{$} eritta mayir vāḷarakkaṉ veṟpu eṭukka
when the cruel arakkaṉ (Irāvaṇaṉ) of hairs as red as fire lifted the mountain.
tōḷoṭu tāḷ nerittaruḷum civamūr/tti
the embodiment of all auspicious things who crushed his shoulders and legs.
nīṟu aṇintamēṉiyiṉāṉ
one who has shampooed sacred ash on his body.
urittavarit tōl uṭaiyāṉ
Has a dress made of the tiger'sskin which he flayed.
uṟai piramapurattaṉṉait taritta maṉam eppōtum peṟuvār (tām) takkōrē
worthy persons will have the mind which always remember piramapuram, his residence.
{2:40}__9+
{$} kariyāṉum nāṉmukaṉum kāṇāmaik kaṉal uruvāy ariyāṉ ām paramēṭṭi
the god who is in the highest place which has no superior place to that and who was difficult to be known as he assumed a form of fire, by the god who is black and the god with four faces.
aravam cēr akalattāṉ
He has on his chest a cobra.
teriyātāṉ iruntu uṟaiyum tikaḻ piramapuram cēra uriyār
those who have the right to meditate on the eminent piramapuram where the Lord could not be known by people however sharp their intelligence may be (except by his grace).
ēḻ ulakum uṭaṉ āḷa uriyār
have the right to rule over all the seven worlds at the same time. (ēḻulaku upper worlds which are seven in number! or it may mean the seven islands mentioned in purāṇams)
{2:40}__10+
{$} uṭai ilār cīvarattār taṉ perumai uṇarvu ariyāṉ
His greatness could not be comprehended by the jains who are without dress (naked) and Buddhist monks who wear a yellow coloured dress. (The name of the dress of the Buddhist monk is known as cīvaram; vide: Maṇimēkalai, 5-61, Dr.V.S.Ayyar's notes)
muṭaiyil ār veṇtalaikkai mūrttiyām tiruvuruvaṉ
He has several forms of mūrti (ñāṉamūrti, mantiramurti and so on.) who holds in his hand a white skull which is full of the stench of flesh.
takkār, peṭaiyilār vaṇṭu āṭum poḻil piramapurattu uṟaiyum caṭaiyil ār veṇpiṟaiyāṉ tāḷ paṇivār
the worthy persons will bow to the feet of the Lord who has a white crescent moon on his matted locks of hair and who dwells in piramapuram which has gardens where bees both male and female are always wandering (from flower to flower).
{2:40}__11+
{$} taṉ aṭaintārkku iṉpaṅkaḷ taruvāṉai
on the Lord who grants worldly happiness and final bliss to those who come to him.
tattuvaṉai
and the god who is the ultimate reality.
kaṉ aṭainta matilpiramapurattu uṟaiyum kāvalaṉai
and the king who dwells in piramapuram which has a wall of endosure of fine workmanship. (kaṉ aṭainta matil wall of endosure built of stones; kaṉ-kal)
muṉ aṭaintāṉ campantaṉ moḻipattum ivai vallār
those who are able to recite these ten verses composed by campantaṉ who reached god in the previous birth. (muṉ previous birth, young age, presence of god, in the presence of his father and others.; campantaṉ a part of the full name ñāṉacampantaṉ)
poṉ aṭaintār
will get wealth.
pōkaṅkaḷ pala aṭaintār
will get many enjoyments.
puṇṇiyar
are people of moral and religious virtues done in previous births (The past tense is used to denote certainty).

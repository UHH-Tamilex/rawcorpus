{$} pulaṉ aintum poṟi kalaṅki neṟi mayaṅki
as the five senses get confused and bewildered in their sense-organs and go out of order and get mixed up.
aṟivu aḻintiṭṭu
and as one loses conciousness.
aimēl unti
and as the phlegm is pushed up the throat
(1) alamanta pōtāka
at that time when one's mind is reeling
añcēl' eṉṟu aruḷceyvāṉ amarumkōyil
the temple where Civaṉ dwells with desire, saying Do not be afrid', and bestows his grace.
valam vanta maṭavārkaḷ naṭam āṭa
when the ladies who circumambulated the shrine dance.
muḻavu atira
when the muḻavu is played in a high pitch to measure time as an accompaniment.
maḻai eṉṟu añci
being afrid that it is the sound of thunder.
cilamanti-alamantu maram ēṟi mukilpārkkum tiruvaiyāṟē
is Tiruviyāṟu where some female monkeys which are perturbed climb up the trees and look at the clouds.
[[Variant reading: (1) alamvanta]]

{$} viṭal ēṟupaṭa nākam araikku acaittu
Having tied in the waist a cobra with hood which lives in fissures of the earth,
[[viṭal-viṭar: a fissure in the earth]]
veṟpu araiyaṉ pāvaiyōṭum aṭal ēṟu oṉṟu atuēṟi
and riding on a strong bull with the dughter of the King of mountins.
amcolīr! pali' eṉṉum aṭikaḷ kōyil
the temple of Civaṉ who says, Ladies who speak beautiful words! give me alms'
kaṭal ēṟit tiraimōti
when the sea rises and dashes by its waves.
kāviriyiṉuṭaṉvantu kaṅkulvaiki
coming along the river, Kaviri and staying during night.
tiṭal ēṟi
climbing on the ait.
curi cuṅkam ceḻumutatu aṅku īṉṟu alaikkum tiruvaiyāṟē
is Tiruviyāṟu where the mature pearls which come out of the spiralling conches are driven hither and thither.

{$} kaṅkāḷar
Civaṉ who adorns with the complete skeleton of gods who die.
kailāyamalaiyāḷar
who is seated in the mountin Kayilāyam
kāṉappērāḷar
who is in Kaṉappēr
[[This ancient shrine was the capital of Vēṅki Mārpaṉ, a petty chieftin and is now known as Kāliyār Koyil.]]
maṅkai maṅkāḷar
who has a young lady as his half.
tiricūlappaṭaiyāḷar
who has the weapon of a trident.
viṭaiyāḷarpayilum kōyil
the temple where he who has a bull is always there.
koṅku āḷ appoḻil nuḻaintu
Having crept through a narrow passage in that famous garden which is rich in honey.
kūr vāyāl iṟaku ulartti
Having pecked adjusted and dried its feather with the sharp beak.
kūtal nīkki
and being free from chillness.
ceṅkāl nal veṇkuruku paiṅkāṉal iraitērum tiruvaiyāṟē
is Tiruviyāṟu where in the green gardens the white and beautiful crane of red legs is in search of its prey.

{$} nīrōṭu kūviḷamum nilāmatiyum veḷḷerukkum niṟainta koṉṟaittārōṭu taṇ karantai caṭaikku aṇinta tattuvaṉār taṅkum kōyil
the temple where Civaṉ the ultimate reality who wears on his caṭi cool basil garland of koṉṟi flowers which are profuse, white yarcum flowers, bel and a crescent along with water (of Kaṅki), stays.
kār ōṭi vicumpu aḷantu kaṭi nāṟum poḻil aṇainta kamaḻ tārvītit tēr ōṭum araṅku ēṟic cēyiḻaiyār naṭam payilum tiruvaiyāṟē
is Tiruviyāṟu where ladies wearing beautiful ornaments, ascend the dancing stage and practise dance where in the streets the garlands spread the fragrance, and gardens which spread fragrance after reaching the region of clouds and seem to measure the sky, where clouds are running.

{$} vēntu āki
Being the real King.
viṇṇavarkkum maṇṇavarkkum neṟikāṭṭum vikirtaṉ āki
Being the god who is different from the world, and who shows the right path to the people of this earth as well as to the inhabitants of paradise.
pūntāma naṟuṅkoṉṟai caṭaikku aṇinta puṇṇiyaṉār naṇṇum kōyil
the temple where the embodiment of all virtues, Civaṉ, who adorns his caṭi with the brilliant koṉṟi garlands, dwells.
kāntāram icai amaittuk kārikaiyār paṇpāṭakkaviṉ ārvītit tēm tām eṉṟu araṅkēṟic cēyiḻaiyār naṭam āṭum tiruvaiyāṟē
is Tiruviyāṟu where some ladies arrange the note of Kāntāram and sing music while some other ladies wearing beautiful ornaments ascend the stage and dance producing the sound tēm tām' in the beautiful streets (tēm tām is onomatopoeia)
{1:130}__7+
{$} niṉṟu ulām neṭu vicumpil nerukkivarumpuram mūṉṟum nīḷvāy ampuceṉṟu ulām paṭi toṭṭa cilaiyāḷi malaiyāḷi cērum kōyil
the temple where Civaṉ who lives in a mountin and who used a bow from which he discharged an arrow with a long edge on the three cities which oppressed people, in the extensive sky where they were staying and wandering.
kuṉṟu elām kuyil kūvak koḻum piracam malarpāyntu vācam malku teṉṟalār aṭivaruṭac ceḻuṅkarumpu kaṇvaḷarum tiruvaiyāṟē
is Tiruviyāṟu where the fertile sugar-cane increases in height by one joint when the balmy breeze of the south which has increasing fragrance, as the rich honey flows into the flowers, when the indian cuckoos sing in all hills, gently rubs it;
[[cf.kōṅkarumpu curapuṉṉai, kuravu ār cōlaik kuḻāvari vaṇṭu icai pāṭum pāṭalkēṭṭut, tīṅkarumpu kaṇvarum kaḻaṉi cūḻnta tirukkōvalūr (periya tirumoḻi, 2-10-4) teṉṟalār: ār is an honoric suffix as kuṉṟu is clubbed with teṉṟal it should mean potiyil, the place from which the balmy south breeze starts or it may mean artifical mounds.]]

{$} añcātē malai eṭutta arakkarkōṉ talaikaḷpattum mañcuāṭutōḷ neriya aṭarttu
having pressed down to break the beautiful shoulders and ten heads of the King of arakkar who lifted the mountin (Kayili) without fear.
avaṉukku aruḷpurintamaintar kōyil
the temple of the strong god who afterwards granted his grace to him.
iñcāyal iḷanteṅkiṉ paḻam vīḻa
as the mature cocoanut of the young cocoa-nut tree which gives a pleasant shadow, falls from it.
iḷamēti irintu aṅku ōṭi

ceñcālik katir uḻakki

{1:130}__9+
{$} mēl ōṭi vicumpu aṇavi viyal nilattai mika akaḻntu mikku nāṭum mālōṭu nāṉmukaṉum aṟiyātavakai niṉṟāṉ maṉṉumkōyil
the temple where Civaṉ who could not be known by Piramaṉ of four faces, and Māl when they searched for Civaṉ intensively by digging the extensive earth and running upwards to approach the sky, stays permanently.
[[mēl ōṭivicumpaṇavi is the action of Piramaṉ;
viyal nilattai mika akaḻtal is the action of Māl; this is a reversed sequence, a syntactical arrangement of a series of predicates in an order which is the reverse of that in which their respective nominatives stand.]]
kōl ōṭak kōl vaḷaiyār kūttāṭak kuvimulaiyār mukattiṉiṉṟu cēl ōṭac cilaiyāṭac cēyiḻaiyār naṭam āṭum tiruvaiyāṟē
is Tiruviyāṟu where women wearing beautiful ornaments are dancing, when from the faces of young girls of conical breasts eyes like the carp, lock in many directions and the eyebrows are bent, and when ladies wearing a collection of bangles dance to the instructions of the instructor who holds a stick in his hand to direct them.

{$} toṇṭīr
Devotees!
kuṇṭuāṭu kuṟṟuṭukkaic camaṇaroṭu cākkiyarum kuṇam oṉṟu illāmiṇṭāṭu miṇṭar urai kēḷātē mēvi āḷāmiṉ
Become willingly the proteges of Civaṉ by not heeding the words of the hard-hearted ignorant persons who do not have good qualities ie. the camaṇar who are low and dress with a small cloth, and cākkiyar (buddhists)
eṇtōḷar mukkaṇṇar emīcar iṟaivar iṉitu amarum kōyil
the temple where Civaṉ of eight shoulders, three eyes and our Lord and master dwells happily.
ceṇṭāṭu puṉaṟ poṉṉic ceḻumaṇikaḷ vantu alaikkum tiruvaiyāṟē
is Tiruviyāṟu where the river Poṉṉi of good water plays like a ball of flowers the big precious stones and tosses them in its waves.
{1:130}__11+
{$} aṉṉam malipoḻil puṭaicūḻ aiyāṟṟuemperumāṉai
On our Lord in Aiyāṟu which is surrounded on all sides by gardens abounding in swans.
amtaṇkāḻi maṉṉiyacīr maṟaināvaṉ vaḷar ñāṉacampantaṉ maruvupāṭal ivai pattum
with all these ten songs which were composed by Ñāṉacampantaṉ of increasing fame who chants with his tongue Vētam-s which have greatness, and who lives permanently in beautiful and cool Kāḻi.
iṉṉicaiyāl icaiyumkāl īcaṉaṭi ēttuvārkaḷ
those who prise the feet of Civaṉ when ever they can do it with sweet music.
taṉ icaiyōṭu amar ulakil tavaneṟiceṉṟu tāḻātu eytuvar
will reach without delay entering into the famous world of the immortals by the means of penance which they have performed in this world
[[aṉṟu, ē: both are expletives.]]
[[Prising the Lord with the songs is itself equal to penance.]]

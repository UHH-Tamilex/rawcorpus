maṟaikkāṭṭu uṟaiyum maṇāḷaṉ (tāṉ, ē)
the bridegroom who dwells in Maṟaikkāṭu.
tūṇṭu cuṭar aṉaiya cōti (kaṇṭāy)
is like the lamp which shines brightly when trimmed.
tol amarar cūḷāmaṇi (tāṉ, kaṇṭāy)
is the priceless gem that is worn on the heads of the immortals of antiquity.
kāṇṭaṟku ariya kaṭavuḷ (kaṇṭāy)
is the god who is not easy to be seen by people who do not think of him.
karutuvārkku āṟṟa eḷiyāṉ (kaṇṭāy)
is very easy to obtain a vision of him by those who think of him.
(1) vēṇṭuvār vēṇṭuvatē īvāṉ (kaṇṭāy)
grants all that devotees desire;
[[vēṇṭuvatē : singular number denoting the entire genus or class;]]
[[īvāṉ denotes the superiority of god; Anything given by a superior person to an inferior person is denoted by the word ītal]]
meyn neṟi (kaṇṭāy)
is himself the true path.
viratam ellām māṇṭa maṉattār maṉattāṉ (kaṇṭāy)
is in the minds of those who are worthy of receiving his grace by holy practices.
[[All the kaṇṭāy, tāṉ, ē are expletives in this as well as the following verse]]
[[Note on maṇāḷaṉ : Akattiyar obtained a vision of Civaṉ who married Pārvati in the Himalayas, as was promised by Civaṉ.]]
[[Variant reading: (1) vēṇṭiṉār vēṇṭiyatē, vēṇṭuvār vēṇṭiyatē]]

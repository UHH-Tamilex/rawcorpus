{1:56}__1+
{$} kār ār koṉṟai kalanta muṭiyiṉar
Civaṉ has on his head koṉṟi flowers which blossom profusely in winter.
pārār nāḷum paraviya pāṟṟuṟaiyār ār āti mutalvar
the chief who is the rare origin of all things and who dwells in pāṟṟuṟi prised always by the people of this world;
cīr ār cintai celacceytār
turned the eminent minds of devotees towards him.
{1:56}__2+
{$} nallārum avar
he is also the good person.
tīyar eṉappaṭum collār
he is also spoken as a wicked person.
naṉmalarcūṭiṉār
though he is like that, he adorned himself with the good flowers (of my words)
pal ārveṇṭalaic celvar
he is the wealthy person who has a white skull having all the teeth intact.
em pāṟṟuṟai ellārum toḻum īcar
is our Lord in pāṟṟuṟi who is worshipped by perfect souls and others.
{1:56}__3+
{$} viṇ ār tiṅkaḷ viḷaṅkum nutaliṉar
Civaṉ has the crescent that moves in the sky on his forehead
[[PP_EXT: piṟainutal vaṇṇam ākiṉṟu
(Puṟam 1-9)]]
eṇṇār vantu eṉ eḻil koṇṭār
took away my beauty coming without any thought.
paṇ ār vaṇṭu iṉam pāṭal cey pāṟṟuṟaiyuḷ nāḷ nāḷum uṟaivar
he dwells always in pāṟṟuṟi where swarms of bees hum like songs having melody-types.
{1:56}__4+
{$} pūvum tiṅkaḷ puṉainta muṭiyiṉar
Civaṉ adorned his head with flowers and the crescent.
ēviṉ allār eyil eytār
shot an arrow on the forts of the enemies.
pāvam tīrpuṉal malkiya pāṟṟuṟaiyō eṉ cintai oruvar
is it pāṟṟuṟi which has holy water that removes the sins, the place of Civaṉ the unequalled one, who dwells in my mind?
{1:56}__5+
{$} pākam peṇṇum uṭaiyavar, pāṟṟuṟai nākam pūṇṭanayavar
Civaṉ who has as a half a lady and who has adorned himself with cobras and who is in pāṟṟuṟi.
mākam tōy maticūṭi makiḻntu
having worn the crescent that is in the sky, and feeling happy.
eṉatu ākam poṉniṟam ākkiṉār
made my body, change into yellow colour (made my body turn sallow).
{1:56}__6+
{$} pōtu poṉtikaḻ koṉṟai puṉaimuṭi nātar
the master who adorns his head with other flowers and koṉṟi which is brilliant like gold.
vantu eṉnalam koṇṭār
came near me and took away my beauty.
pātam toṇṭar paraviya pāṟṟuṟai vētam ōtum vikirtar
is Civaṉ different from the world who chants the vetam and whose feet are prised by devotees in pāṟṟuṟi.
{1:56}__7+
{$} vāṭal veṇṭalai cūṭiṉar
Civaṉ bore on his head a dried white skull.
mālviṭai ceyta kuṟippiṉar
has some motive in having the big bull as his mount.
pāṭal vaṇṭiṉam paṇ ceyum pāṟṟuṟai āṭalnākam acaittār
who tied a dancing cobra and who has pāṟṟuṟi as a shrine, where the swarm of bees which usually hum, sing melody types.
{1:56}__8+
{$} vevva mēṉiyarāy veḷḷai nīṟṟiṉar pavva nañcu aṭai kaṇṭar, empāṟṟuṟai mavval cūṭiya maintar
Civaṉ has a body which is hot; has besmeared himself with white holy ash; has in his neck the poison that rose in the ocean; is the youth who adorned his head with arabian jasmine flowers and who is in pāṟṟuṟi.
evvam ceytueṉ eḻil koṇṭār
took away my beauty giving me distress in return.
{1:56}__9+
{$} ēṉam aṉṉamum āṉavarukku eri āṉavaṇṇattu em aṇṇalār
our god who assumed the form of a pillar of fire to those two who took the forms of a pig and swan (Māl and Piramaṉ)
pāṉal ammalar vimmiya pāṟṟuṟai, vāṉaveṇpiṟai maintar
is the youth who bore on his head the crescent shining in the sky and who is in pāṟṟuṟi where blue nelumbo flowers blossom profusely.
{1:56}__10+
{$} venta nīṟṟiṉar
Civaṉ besmears himself with well burnt holy ash.
vēliṉar nūliṉar
has a trident (of three blades); wears a scared threed.
vantu eṉ naṉṉalam vauviṉār
came to my house and stole my exquisite beauty.
paintaṇ mātavicūḻtaru pāṟṟuṟai maintar ōr maṇāḷar
the youth in pāṟṟuṟi which is surrounded by cool and fertile creeper, the common delight of the woods, is really a bridegroom.
{1:56}__11+
{$}
People of this world!
pattar maṉṉiya pāṟṟuṟaimēviya pattunūṟupeyaraṉai
on Civaṉ who has one thousand names and dwells in pāṟṟuṟi where pious devotees stay permanently.
pattar ñāṉacampantaṉatu iṉtamiḻpattum pāṭipparavum
prise god by singing all the ten verses of sweet Tamiḻ of ñāṉacampantaṉ who is a pious devotee.
[[Note: There is no reference in this decade to Iravaṇaṉ and camaṇar and buddhists. These ten verses have the theme of akapporuḷ]]

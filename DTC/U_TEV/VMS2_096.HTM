{2:96}__1+
{$} poṅku veṇ purivaḷarum poṟpuṭai mārpaṉ, emperumāṉ, ceṅkaṇ āṭu aravu āṭṭum celvaṉ em civaṉ uṟai kōyil
the temple where the god who wears a sacred thread of increasing whiteness in the beautiful chest, our Lord, our Civaṉ, the god who cuse the naturally dancing snake with red eyes to dance; dwells.
paṅkam il pala maṟaikaḷ vallavar pattarkaḷ paravum taṅku veṇtiraik kāṉal taṇvayal kāḻinaṉṉakarē
is the good city of Kāḻi which has cool fields and sea-shore groves white waves come and stay and which is prised by devotees and brahmins who are learned in many defectless vētams.
{2:96}__2+
{$} tēvar tāṉavar parantu tiṇvarai mālkaṭal niṟuvi
The tevar and tāṉavar spreading themselves and having set up as the churning rod the strong mountin, in the big ocean (of milk)
nāvatāl amirtu uṇṇa nayantavar irintiṭakkaṇṭu
on seeing them who were desirous of eating the nectar by whetting their tongues, run helter skelter
āva eṉṟu arunañcamuṇṭavaṉ amartarum mūtūr
the ancient place where the god who drank the rare poison taking pity on them.
kāval ār matil cūḻnta kaṭipoḻil kāḻi naṉṉakarē
is the good city of Kāḻi which is surrounded by a guarded wall of enclosure and which has fragrant parks.
{2:96}__3+
{$} kariyiṉ māmukam uṭaiya kaṇapati tātai
the father of kaṇapati who has the big face of an elephant;
palpūtam, tiriya ilpalikku ēkum ceḻuñcuṭar cērtarum mūtūr
the ancient place where the effulgent light which goes begging to houses, when many pūtams are wandering by its side, dwells.
cariyiṉ muṉkai naṉ mātar catipaṭa mānaṭam āṭi, uriya nāmaṅkaḷ ēttum olipuṉaṟkāḻi naṉṉakarē
is the good city of Kāḻi which has roaring water and where the beautiful women who wear on their forearm a kind of bracelet, dance to the accompaniment of music and time-measure, prise the god with names which are by right his own.
{2:96}__4+
{$} caṅka veṇkuḻaic ceviyaṉ
He wears a white ear-ring made of conch.
taṇmaticūṭiya ceṉṉi aṅkam pūṇeṉa uṭaiya appaṉukku aḻakiya ūr
the beautiful place of the father who has bones as his ornaments and a head that wears a cool crescent.
tuṅka māḷikai uyarnta tokukoṭi vāṉiṭaimiṭaintu vaṅka vāḷmati taṭavum maṇipoḻiṟkāḻi naṉṉakarē
is the good city of Kāḻi which has parks resembling emerald and the group of flags set up high in the tall mansions become close to one another and seem to touch the bright moon as white as silver.(vaṅkam may mean also boat)
[[taḻicciṟai nīriṟ piṟaikkalam cērtarum tāravaṉē (tiruvācakam 151)]]
{2:96}__5+
{$} maṅkai kūṟu amarmeyyaṉ; māṉmaṟi ēntiya kaiyaṉ
the god who has a lady as him half on his person; he holds a young deer in his hand.
eṅkaḷ īcaṉ' eṉṟu eḻuvār iṭarviṉai keṭuppavaṟku ūr
the city of the god who destroys the sins which cuse suffering, of those who wake up from sleep uttering the words our Lord!.
caṅkai iṉṟi nal niyamam tām ceytu takutiyiṉ mikka, kaṅkai (1) nāṭu uyar kīrtti maṟaiyavar kāḻi naṉṉakarē
is the good city of Kāḻi where brahmins whose fame is as white as Kaṅki, and who are superior in good conduct having performed the good dily routines without any doubt live.
[[kaṅkai nāṭu maṟaiyavar this may be interpreted as brahmins who were brought from that part of the country where Kaṅki flowers; kaṅkaināṭu uyarkīrtti brahmins have such a great fame that it has reached the country in which Kaṅki flows]]
[[Variant reading: (1)nāṭuvar; (It is a poetical convention that fame is white in colour)]]
{2:96}__6+
{$} nāṟu kūviḷam mattam nākamum cūṭiya nampaṉ; ēṟum ēṟiya īcaṉ iruntu iṉitu amartarum mūtūr
the ancient place where the god who adorns on his head fragrant bel leaves, dhātura flowers and cobra and who rides on an uncontrollable bull, stays and dwells with happiness (um should be added to kūviḷam and mattam)
nīṟu pūciya uruvar
Has a person on which he smears sacred white ash
neñciṉuḷ vañcam oṉṟu iṉṟit tēṟuvārkaḷ ceṉṟu ēttum
Those who are clear in their minds without deceit go and worship god (as their refuge)
cīr tikaḻkāḻi naṉṉakarē
is the good city of Kāḻi which is eminent by its greatness.
{2:96}__7+
{$} nantitaṉ muḻaviṭaik kāṭṭil naṭam atu āṭiya nātaṉ
the Lord who danced in the cremation ground when Nanti played on the muḻavu
[[Muḻavu here means kuṭamuḻavu;]]
[[PP: kuṭamuḻa nantīcaṉai vācakaṉāk koṇṭār appar, taṉittiruttaṇṭakam (2) 11)]]
[[I think muḻaviṭai should be muḻaviṭa]]
viṭam amarntu
Having drunk the poison relishing it
orukālam aṟam virittu uraittavaṟku ūrām
is the place of the god who, once upon a time, gave out philosophy elaborataly.
iṭamatāmaṟai payilvār iruntavar tiruntiya (1) pōtik kuṭam (atu) ār maṇimāṭam kulāviya kāḻi naṉṉakarē
is the good city of Kāḻi where beautiful storeys in which a pot-like part is placed on the capital of a pillar and where those who learn the extensive vētams, and great sages, play upon the perfect instruments. (pōti; pōtikai; the last letter is dropped.)
[[Variant reading: (1)tiruntiyam pōtiṟ]]
{2:96}__8+
{$} kārkoḷ mēṉi avvarakkaṉtaṉ kaṭuntiṟaliṉaik karuti
that arakkaṉ of black body thinking of his cruel strength (a is called paṇṭu aṟicuṭṭu in Tamiḻ grammar; demonstrative letter which denotes distant past)
ērkoḷ maṅkaiyum añca eḻil varai eṭuttavaṉ neriya
to crush him who removed the high mountin, to frighten even the beautiful lady of Umi (eḻil-eḻucci)
cīrkoḷ pātattu ōr viralāl ceṟutta em civaṉ uṟaikōyil
the temple in which our Civaṉ who shbdued him by a single toe of the famous foot.
tār koḷvaṇṭiṉam cūḻnta taṇvayal kāḻi naṉṉakarē
is the good city of Kāḻi of cool fields around which the bees settled in the flowers go road and round.
{2:96}__9+
{$} mālum māmalarāṉum maruvi niṉṟu ikaliya maṉattāl
Māl and Piramaṉ in the big (lotus) flowers joined together and being inimical in their minds.
pālum kāṇpu aritāya parañcuṭartaṉ pati
the city of the divine supreme light even portion of which could not be seen by them.
cēlum vāḷaiyum kayalum ceṟintu taṉ kiḷaiyoṭumēya
when the carnatic carp, the scabbard fish and the carp join with their group and roam about.
ālum cāli naṟkatirkaḷ aṇivayal kāḻi naṉṉakarē āṭum
is the good city of Kāḻi which has beautiful fields where the ears of corn of fertile paddy dance in the breeze.
{2:96}__10+
{$} cittarōṭu nal amarar ceṟinta naṉmāmalar koṇṭu, muttaṉē! aruḷ eṉṟu muṟaimaicey kāḻi naṉṉakar
the good city of Kāḻi where the mystics possessing supernatural powers and good natured immortals give rent to their grievances worshipping with defeltless, big and abundant flowers, saying, one who is by nature free from bonds! Bestow on us your grace!'
puttar poymiku camaṇar polikaḻal aṭiiṇai kāṇum cittam (maṟṟu) avarkku ilāmaittikaḻnta naṟ ceḻuñcuṭarkku ūr ām
is the place for the effulgent and beneficial light who was splendour that the buddhists and camaṇar of bundle of lies could not have the mind to know the feet which more shining anklets.
[[poymiku should be added before puttar also; it is a figure of speech in which a word used in the middle of a sentence goes to amplify the meanings of words in various parts of the same; it literally means a lamp in the centre illuminating all round; in Tamiḻ it is called iṭainilait tīvakam]]

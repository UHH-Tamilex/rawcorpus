{2:110}__1+
{$} cempoṉ ār vēṅkaiyum (1) ñāḻalum cerunti ceṇpakam āṉaik kompum āramum mātavi curapuṉai kuruntu alar parantu unti
pushing the flowers of wild-lime trees, long-leaved two-sepalled gamboge, common delight of the woods, sandal-wood, tusks of elephants, champak flowers, panicled golden-blossomed pear-tree, fetid cassia, and east-indian kino-tree which has flowers like gold.
am poṉ nērvaru kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai uṟaikiṉṟa empirāṉ
our master who dwells in Māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri which brings with it gold.
imaiyōr toḻu paiṅkaḻal ēttutal ceyvōm
we shall prise the feet wearing armour of gold which are worshipped with joined hands by the celestials who do not wink.
[[Variant reading: (1) nāḻalum]]
{2:110}__2+
{$} viḷavu tēṉoṭu cātiyiṉ palaṅkaḷum vēymaṇi nirantu unti
pushing in a row the pearls from bamboos, fruits of nutmeg tree, honeycombs and wood-apple trees.
aḷavi nīrvaru kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai uṟaivāṉ
Civaṉ who dwells in Māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri in whose floods all the above things come mingled.
attuḷavamālmakaṉ aiṅkaṇaikkāmaṉaic cuṭaviḻittavaṉ
Civaṉ who opened his frontal eye to burn Kāmaṉ who has five arrows and who is the son of that Māl who adorns himself with the leaves of the sacred basil.
neṟṟi aḷakavāḷnutal arivaitaṉ paṅkaṉaiaṉṟi paṟṟu aṟiyōm
we know of no other god except Civaṉ who has as his half a lady with a bright forehead with a few tresses of hir at the top.
[[neṟṟi aḷakam-tilakam ārnta neṟṟiyiṟ paṉiccaiyāṉa aḷakapārattāl (takkayākapparaṇi, 25 commentary)]]
[[The five arrows of Kāmaṉ: aiviraimāṇpakaḻi (paripātal, 10-97)]]
[[māmpū acōkappū tāmaraippū mullaippū tēmpāytarukuvaḷaic cevvippū-mēmpaṭṭa, maintu pūṇmārpa mataṉaṉ kaṇaiyāṉa, aintupū ākum avai (verse quoted in Takkayākapparaṇi commentary, 71)]]
{2:110}__3+
{$} kōṭu tēṉ cori kuṉṟiṭaip pūkamum kūntaliṉ kulaivāri ōṭunīrvaru kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai uṟai nampaṉ
Civaṉ who dwells in Māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri whose water runs rapidly scooping the clusters of the fruits of south indian talipot palm and areca-palms growing in hills on which the peaks pour forth honey.
vāṭiṉār talaiyil palikoḷpavaṉ
receives alms in the skull of Piramaṉ who has deceased.
vāṉavar makiḻntu ēttum kēṭilā maṇiyaittoḻal allatu keḻumutal aṟiyōm
we do not practise any other thing except worshipping the gem without destruction whom the residents of heaven prise with joy.
{2:110}__4+
{$} ilavam (1) ñāḻalum īñcoṭu curapuṉṉai iḷamarutu ilavaṅkam kalavi nīrvaru kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai uṟaikaṇṭaṉ
the warrior who dwells in māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri which brings floods in which red-flowered silk cotton trees, fetid cassia, date palms, long leaved two sepelled camboge, young flowering murdah, and cloves are mingled.
alaikoḷ vārpuṉal ampuli mattamum āṭu aravuṭaṉ vaitta malaiyai vāṉavar koḻuntiṉai allatu vaṇaṅkutal aṟiyōm
we do not know to do obeisance to any other god except the tender leaf in the creaper of the celestials, and the mountin which placed along with the cobra datura flowers, a crescent and flowing water which has waves.
[[(Variant reading: (1) nāḻalum]]
{2:110}__5+
{$} kōṅku ceṇpakam kuruntoṭupātiri kuraviṭaimalar unti ōṅkunīr varukāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai uṟaivāṉai
to Civaṉ who dwells in māṅtuṟi on the northern bank of the Kaviri which brings great floods pushing the flowers of common cung, champak, wild lime tree, yellow fragrant trumpet-flowered trees and bottle-flowers.
pāṅkiṉāl iṭum tūpamum tīpamum pāṭṭu avimalar cērttit tāṅkuvār
those who worship combining flowers, food offerings songs of prises, lamps and burning incense according to rules enjoined in ākamams.
avar nāmaṅkaḷ nāviṉāl talaippaṭum tavattōr
are people who have performed penance and are really persons who always utter with their tongues the names of that Civaṉ.
{2:110}__6+
{$} peruku cantaṉam kār akil pīliyum perumaram (1) nimirntu unti
pushing sandal-wood trees of increasing fragrance black eagle-wood tree, peacock's feathers and other big trees to stand erect.
porutu
and coming dashing aginst the banks.
kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟaip puṉitaṉ emperumāṉai
our pure Lord, who dwells in māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri.
pariviṉāl iruntu
staying with piety.
iraviyum matiyamum pār maṉṉar paṇintu ētta
when the sun and the moon and Kings of this world pay respects to, and prise, Civaṉ.
marutavāṉavar vaḻipaṭum malaraṭi vaṇaṅkutal ceyvōm
we shall adore the lotus feet of Civaṉ which is worshipped by a group of celestials by name marut'
[[Variant reading: (1) nirantu]]
{2:110}__7+
{$} naṟava mallikai mullaiyum mauvalum nāḷmalar avai vāri iṟavil vantu eṟi kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai iṟai
the god who dwells is māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri which washes on the bank in abundance, scooping the flowers, blossomed in the early morning, of jasmine having honey arabian-jasmine and wild jasmine.
aṉṟu aṅku aṟavaṉ ākiya kūṟṟiṉaic cāṭiya antaṇaṉ
the brahmin who trampled under his foot the god of death who perform his duty without fear on favour in the distant past at the same place (where mārkaṅṭēyaṉ was worshipping Civaṉ)
varai (1) villāl niṟaiya vāṅkiyē valittu eyil eytavaṉ niraikaḻal paṇivōm
we shall adore the feet of Civaṉ which wear in a row armours for the feet; who shot an arrow with great effort on the forts with a mountin converted into a bow bending it into a semi-circle.
[[Variant reading: (1) villā]]
{2:110}__8+
{$} mantam ārpoḻil māṅkaṉi mantikaḷ māntiṭa
when the female monkeys eat the mango-fruits in the gardens where the southern balmy breeze stays.
māṇikkam unti nīrvaru kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai uṟaivāṉai nintiyā
disregarding Civaṉ who dwells in māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri which brings floods pushing rubies.
eṭuttu ārttaval arakkaṉai nerittiṭuviralāṉai
who crushed with his toe the strong arakkaṉ (Irāvaṇaṉ) who lifted the mountin and roared.
cintiyā maṉattār avarcērvatu tīneṟiyatutāṉē
those who do not think of him in their minds reach hell as their stage of existence.
{2:110}__9+
{$} nīlamāmaṇi nittilat tottoṭu niṟaimalar nirantu unti, āliyāvaru kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai amarvāṉai mālum nāṉmukaṉum tēṭiyum kāṇkilā malar aṭi iṇai
the two feet resembling lotus, of Civaṉ who dwells in māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri which flows with a roaring sound and pushing flowers in rows, mass of pearls and sapphire in an arranged order, which could not be found even after search by Māl and nāṉmukaṉ (Piramaṉ)
nāḷum kōlam ētti niṉṟu āṭumiṉ pāṭumiṉ
sing their prises, and dance by prising their beauty dily (People of this world!)
kūṟṟuvaṉ naliyāṉ
the god of death will not afflict you.
{2:110}__10+
{$} niṉṟu uṇum camaṇ tērarum nilaiilar
camaṇar who eat their food standing and tēvar (buddhists) have no fired principles.
neṭuṅkaḻai naṟavu ēlam naṉṟu māṅkaṉi kataliyiṉ palaṅkaḷum nāṇaliṉ nurai vāri
taking in a sweep foams and Kans (a coarse grass), plantin fruits, big mango-fruits, fragrant cardamoms and tall bamboos.
oṉṟi nēr varum kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai orukālam aṉṟi uḷ aḻintu eḻum paricu aḻakitu
the nature of the heart melting always thinking a māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri in which the above mentioned produces come stright combing together, is beautiful.
atu avarkku iṭam ām
that ecstatic state of mind is their place.
{2:110}__11+
{$} varaivaḷam kavar kāviri vaṭakarai māntuṟai uṟaivāṉai
on Civaṉ who dwells in Māntuṟi on the northern bank of the Kāviri which robs the products of the mountin.
cirapuram pati uṭaiyavaṉ, kavuṇiyaṉ, ceḻumaṟai niṟaināvaṉ araeṉum paṇivallavaṉ ñāṉacampantaṉ aṉpuṟumālai
the garland of verses composed with devotion by Ñāṉacampantaṉ, whose tongue is fully conversant with excellent Vētam-s, born in Kavuṇiya Kōttiram, and who has as his native place Civapuram and who is capable of uttering the name `Ara'
paraviṭum toḻilvallavar allalum pāvamum ilar
those who are capable of prising (with the aid of those verses) have no sufferings and sins.

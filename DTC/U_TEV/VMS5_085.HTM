maṭṭuvār kuḻalāḷoṭu māl viṭai iṭṭamā ukantu ēṟum iṟaivaṉār
the Lord who rides on a big a bull joyfully desiring it along with his consort maṭṭuvār Kuḻalāḷ
[[maṭṭuvār kuḻalāḷ is the name of the Tēvi in Cirāppaḷḷi temple.]]
kaṭṭu nīttavarkku iṉṉaruḷē ceyyum ciṭṭar (pōlum) cirāppaḷḷic celvarē
the Lord in Cirāppaḷḷi is the god of established line of good conduct by the virtuous, who grants his sweet grace only to those who have renounced all bondage.
{5:85}__2+
{$}
ari ayaṉ talai veṭṭi vaṭṭu āṭiṉār
Civaṉ played as a game-piece cutting the heads of ari (Māl) and Ayaṉ (Piramaṉ)
ari ayaṉ toḻutu ēttum arum poruḷ
the rare object which is worshipped with joined hands and prised by ari and Ayaṉ
periyavaṉ cirāppaḷḷiyaip pēṇuvār, ari ayaṉ toḻa aṅku iruppārkaḷē
those who cherish with love cirāppaḷḷi of the great god, will remin in the world of Civaṉ to be worshipped by ari and Ayaṉ

{$}
ariccu irāppakal aivarāl āṭṭuṇṭu
Being harassed and eaten away night and day by the five senses
[[aivar: aintu is spoken as aivar out of anger]]
curiccu irātu nercē oṉṟu collakkēḷ
(My mind!) Listen to what I say as a good thing, so that you may not be undergoing suffering
tiriccirāppaḷḷi eṉṟalum
as soon as you say `tiriccirāppaḷḷi
tī viṉai nariccirātu naṭakkum naṭakkumē
all our sins will take leave of us definitely without tormenting us, diminished in its severity;
[[narittu has become nariccu; being low;]]
[[PP: narippāy nāyēṉ iruppēṉō
(Tiruvācakam, 386);
[[A kind of small green gram is called narippayaṟu]]

eṉakkē tāyumāyt talai kaṇṇumāy
Being the mother, head and eyes to me
pēyaṉēṉaiyum āṇṭa peruntakai
the noble-minded god who admitted a mad man like me also into his grace
tēyanātaṉ
the chief of many countries
cirāppaḷḷi mēviya nāyaṉār eṉa nam viṉai nācamē
our Karmams will be completely destroyed if we say about the presiding god who dwells in cirāppaḷḷi
[[The presiding deity in civa temples is called nāyaṉār without adding the honorofic prefix of tiru;
(tiru); aḵtu muṟ kūṟiya vakaiyil tirukkōyil, tiruvāyil, tiruvalaku eṉṟavaṟṟiṟku nāyakarākiya nāyaṉārait tiru nāyaṉār eṉṉātatu pōlak koḷka (Kōviyār, 1, perāciriyar's commentary)]]

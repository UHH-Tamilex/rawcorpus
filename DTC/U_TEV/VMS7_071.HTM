yāḻaip paḻittaṉṉa moḻi maṅkai oru paṅkaṉ, pēḻaic caṭai muṭimēl piṟai vaittāṉ iṭam pēṇil
if one desires to worship the shrine of Civaṉ who has on one half a lady whose words ridicule the music produced in yāḻ, and who placed a crescent in his caṭi coiled into a crown which is like a chest;
[[PP_(pēḻaiccaṭai):
pēḻaiccaṭaiyar (cuntarar, tirukkaṭavūrmayāṉam, 9);
pētaivārcaṭai (campantar, tirukkēticcaram, 6)]]
tāḻaip poḻilūṭē ceṉṟu pūḻaittalai nuḻaintu, vāḻaikkaṉi kūḻaikkuraṅku uṇṇum maṟaikkāṭē
it is definitely Maṟikkāṭu where the small monkeys creep through a narrow small door getting through the garden of fragrant screw-pine and enjoy eating plantin fruits.

{$}
cikarattiṭai iḷaveṇpiṟai vaittāṉ iṭam teriyil
if one wants to know the shrine of Civaṉ who placed on his head a young white crescent.
mukarattiṭai muttiṉ (ṉ) oḷi pavaḷattiraḷ (1) ōtam takarattiṭai tāḻaittiraḷ ñāḻaltiraḷ nīḻal makarattoṭu cuṟavam koṇarntu eṟṟum maṟaikkāṭē
it is definitely maṟikkāṭu where the waves which have collections of coral in which are concealed the pearls which are produced in the oysters, bring along with them makaran and shark dash aginst the shore in the shade of the groves of feted cassia and fragrant screw-pine that grow amidst the fragrant wax-flower dog-bane trees.
[[Variant reading: (1) ōtattakarattiṭai]]
{7:71}__3+
{$}
aṅkaṅkaḷum maṟaināṉkuṭaṉ virittāṉ iṭam aṟintōm
we knew the place of Civaṉ who expounded the four vētams along with the six aṅkams.
teṅkaṅkaḷum neṭumpeṇṇaiyum paḻamvīḻ maṇal paṭappai
in the enclosed sandy gardens where the matura fruits of the cocoanut trees and the tall palmyra trees fall down.
caṅkaṅkaḷum ilaṅku ippiyum valampurikaḷum iṭaṟi
throwing on the shore ordinary conches, shining oysters and valampuri (conches spiralling clockwise)
vaṅkaṅkaḷum uyarkūmpoṭu vaṇaṅkum maṟaikkāṭu (ē)
even the inanimate things like the ships pay homage coming to the shore with their masts.
[[teṅkam : teṅku+am]]

narai viraviya mayirtaṉṉoṭu pañcavaṭi mārpaṉ
Civaṉ wears on his chest a sacred thread called pancavaṭi made with grey hirs of tēvar who die.
[[paṇimēliṭṭa pācupatar pañcavaṭi mārpiṉar cuntarar, tirukkaṭavūr mayaṉam, 6]]
urai viraviya uttamaṉ iṭam uṇaral(l) uṟu maṉamē
my mind which tries to know the place of the supreme god who is famous!
kurai viraviya (1) kulaicēkarak koṇṭal talai viṇṭa, varai puraivaṉa tirai porutu iḻintu eṟṟum maṟaikkāṭu (ē)
it is maṟikkāṭu where the mango trees on the top of which clouds seem to crawl, grow on the roaring shore, where the waves which are in size like mountins dash on the shore, break into pieces and descend down
[[cēkaram mangotree;
veyyōṉ ... cēkaramāki niṉṟāṉ (Kantapurāṇam, curapatumaṉ vatarppaṭalam, 473);
Waves are compared to mountins, (cuntarar, tiruvaṇcikkaḷam, 10)]]
[[Variant reading: (1) kulacēkarakkoṇṭal]]

maṉamē
my mind!
toḻutu ēttac caṅkaippaṭa niṉaiyātu eḻu
start without thoughts of any doubt to worship with joined hands, and prise Civaṉ.
kaṅkaiccaṭai muṭi uṭaiyavaṟku iṭam āvatu
the place of Civaṉ who has a caṭi coiled into a crown in which there is Kaṅki.
paravai aṅkaik kaṭal arumāmaṇi untik karaikku ēṟṟa vaṅkattoṭu cuṟavam koṇarntu eṟṟum maṟaikkāṭē
it is definitely maṟikkātu where the expansive ocean with its hands of waves push rare and big precious stones and pile them on the shore and strike aginst it bringing along with them ships and sharks.

aṭal viṭaiyiṉaṉ
Civaṉ has a strong bull.
(1) maḻuvāḷiṉaṉ
has a weapon of a battle-axe
[[vāḷ is used in the sense of a weapon.]]
(1) nal ār aṇi koṉṟaip paṭarum caṭaimuṭi uṭaiyavaṟku iṭam āvatu
the place which is the residence of Civaṉ who has a spreading caṭi coiled into a crown which wears koṉṟi flowers and good and holy mountin ebony flowers.
paravaik kaṭaliṭai iṭaikaḻi arukiṉil kaṭināṟu taṇ kaitai maṭaliṭai iṭai veṇkuruku eḻu maṇinīr maṟaikkāṭē
is definitely maṟikkāṭu which has blue water where in frequent intervals of the expansive ocean and by the side of the backwaters, the white cranes rise and fly from the inter spaces of the petals of the cool fragrant screw-pines which spread their fragrance.
[[Variant reading: (1) maḻuvāḷiṉavarālaṇi koṉṟai]]
{7:71}__7+
{$} muḷai vaḷar iḷamati uṭaiyavaṉ
Civaṉ wears a tender and waxing crescent which rose fresh in the sky.
muṉceyta valviṉaikaḷ kaḷaikaḷaintu eṉai āṉal (l)uṟu kaṇṭaṉ(ṉ) iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who admitted me as his protege after weeding out the irresistible acts done in previous births, like removing out weeds.
cennel vaḷai viḷai vayal kayal pāytaru kuṇa vār maṇaṟ kaṭalvāy vaḷaivaḷaiyoṭu calañcalam koṇarntu eṟṟum maṟaikkāṭē
it is definitely maṟikkāṭu where the carp fish jump in the fields where the variety of superior paddy of yellowish hue is bent due to the weight of the corns and where the eastern sea (bay of bengal) which has extensive sand brings the ordinary spiralling conches with a superior variety of conch by name calaṇcalam and strikes aginst the shore.

{$}
kaṅkaiccalam puri caṭaimuṭi uṭaiyavaṟku iṭam āvatu
the place of Civaṉ who has a caṭi coiled into a crown and twisted, on which the water of Kaṅki stays.
nalam periyaṉa curumpu ārntaṉa kalam periyaṉa cārum kaṭaṟkarai porutu iḻi paravai
the waves of the sea where the big ships voyage carrying good cargoes and fragrant things on which bees swarm and stay, dash aginst the shore and recede.
valampuriyoṭu calañcalam koṇarntu eṟṟum maṟaikkāṭē
is definitely maṟikkāṭu where they bring along with them conch spiralling clockwise and calaṇcalan.
namkōṉiṭam aṟintōm
as we knew maṟikkāṭu, we knew the place of our King.

kuṇṭuāṭiyum camaṇāṭiyum kuṟṟuṭukkaiyartāmum kaṇṭār kaṇṭa kāraṇam(m) avai karutātu kai toḻumiṉ
(People of this world!) people who wear short dresses, being low and preaching doctrine of amaṇ religion, though they have realised some truths with their limited knowledge, you do not mind them as great, but worship with joined hands the shrine of.
eṇtōḷiṉaṉ, mukkaṇṇiṉaṉ, ēḻicaiyiṉaṉ, aṟukāl vaṇṭu āṭu taṇpoḻil cūḻntu eḻu maṇinīr maṟaikkāṭu (ē)
Maṟikkāṭu where blue sea-water rises high, and which is surrounded by cool gardens where the bees of six legs roam about and which belongs to Civaṉ who has eight shoulders, three eyes and who has the seven notes of music.
[[Syntactical link: (1) maṟaikkāṭē toḻumiṉ]]

vār ūr vaṉamulaiyāḷ umai maṟaikkāṭṭai
on maṟikkāṭu which is the abode of Civaṉ who has as a half Umi, who has beautiful breasts which rise above the bodice.
pār ūr pala puṭai (1) cūḻvaḷavayal nāvalarvēntaṉ ārūraṉa tamiḻmālaikaḷ pāṭum(m) aṭittoṇṭar
the votaries who sing the garlands of tamiḻ composed by ārūraṉ, who is the chief of the place, nāvalūr which has fertile fields and is surrounded by many place in this world.
nīr ūrtaru nilaṉōṭu uyar pukaḻ ākuvar (tām, ē)
will attin fame which will exist as long as world surrounded by ocean lasts.

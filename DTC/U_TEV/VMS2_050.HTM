{$} viṭai ēṟum vētiyaṉē
the brahmin among gods who rides on a bull
teṉṟal ār maṇi māṭa māḷikai cūḷikaikku etir nīṇṭa peṇṇaimēl aṉṟil vantu aṇaiyum āmāttūr ammāṉē
the father in Āmāttūr where in the palmyra tree which stands high opposite the open terrace of the mansions which have beautiful storeys which the balmy breeze from the South fills, the bird aṉṟil joins it, returning to its nest.
[[aṉṟil: a bird, whether male or female very familiar in Indian Poetry, as a standard or model of constancy and inseparable love; aṉṟil is always associate with the palmyra tree]]

kuṉṟavārcilai, nāṇ arā, arivāḷi, kūr erikāṟṟiṉ mummatil veṉṟavāṟu eṅṅaṉē
how did you gain victory over the three forts with the aid of the big fire and wind being at the tip of the arrow are being the arrow, the bow-string being the cobra Vācuki and the Mēru being the long bow?

{$} viṇṇavaṉē
O God!
karavu il māmaṇi poṉ koḻittu
bringing to the surface gold and big gems which are not hidden.
cantu kār akil tantu
pushing sandal wood and black coloured eagle wood.
iḻi pampai nīr aruvi vantu alaikkum āmāttūr ammāṉē
our father in āmāttūr where the streams of water of the river pampai which flows there erodes both the banks!
paravi vāṉavar tāṉavar palarum kalaṅkiṭavanta kārviṭam veruva uṇṭu ukanta aruḷ eṉ (kol)
what is the reason of your grace of becoming great by consuming the black poison which appeared to make the many celestials and acurar perplexed who spread themselves in two roaws, when they cried aloud out of fear?
{2:50}__3+
{$} pūṇṭa kēḻal maruppu, arā, virikoṉṟai, vāḷvari, āmai pūṇ eṉa āṇṭanāyakaṉē
the chief who kept in use as ornaments, the shell of a tortoise, a tiger's skin, blossomed koṉṟai flowers, cobras, hog's tusk all of which you have worn as an ornaments!
āmāttūr ammāṉē
the father in āmāttūr!
nīṇṭavār caṭaitāḻa
the very long caṭai to hang low.
nēriḻai pāṭa
Umai who has worn ornaments appropriate to her beauty was singing.
nīṟumeypūci
smearing the sacred ash on your body
māl ayaṉ māṇṭa vār cuṭalai naṭam āṭum māṇpu (atu) eṉ
what is the dignity in dancing in the long cremation ground where Māl and Ayaṉ have died?
{2:50}__4+
{$} pāliṉ nērmoḻi maṅkaimār naṭam āṭi iṉṉicai pāṭa, ālaicūḻ kaḻaṉi nīḷpati āmāttūr ammāṉē
the father in āmāttūr which is a long city surrounded by fields where sugar-canes grow (ālai-karumpu (sugar-cane) ālai may also mean sugar-cane press).
cēliṉ nēr aṉa kaṇṇi, veṇṇakai, māṉviḻittirumātaip pākam vaittu
having placed the beautiful Umai who has startled looks like those of the deer, white teeth and who has eyes resembling the fish, carnatic carp.
ēla mātavam nī muyalkiṉṟa vēṭam (itu) eṉ
what is the form with which you try to perform great penance to be suited to that form?
{2:50}__5+
{$} vaṇṭal ār kaḻaṉik kalantu malarnta tāmarai mātar vāḷ mukam, aṇṭavāṉavartoḻum āmāttūr ammāṉē
the father in āmāttūr where the celestials in heaven come and worship and where the blossomed lotus flowers growing close together in the fields where silt is deposited, resemble the bright faces of ladies!
toṇṭar vantu vaṇaṅki māmalar tūvi niṉkaḻalēttuvār (avar) uṇṭiyāl varutta iraṅkātatu eṉṉai (kolām)
what is the reason for your not taking pity on your devotees who come to you and bow down their heads before you and praise your feet scattering flowers on them, when they suffer from hunger?
{2:50}__6+
{$} cōtiyē, cuṭarē, curumpu amar koṉṟaiyāy
the brilliant light, the sun, god who wears koṉṟai on which there are bees!
tiru niṉṟiyūr uṟai ātiyē, araṉē, āmāttūr ammāṉē
the chief who dwells in tiruṉṟiyūr, araṉ, and the father in āmāttūr!
ōti āraṇam āya nuṇporuḷ aṉṟu nālvarmuṉ kēṭka
when the four sages asked you about the subtle ideals having learnt the vētams, in the distance past, in your presence.
naṉṉeṟi nītiyāl nīḻal uraikkiṉṟa nīrmai(yatu) eṉ
what is the affability for expounding the truth of good path sitting under the banyan tree; in the proper way?
{2:50}__7+
{$} paṅkayam matu uṇṭu vaṇṭu icai pāṭa māmayilāṭa viṇ muḻavu aṅkaiyāl atirkkum āmāttūr ammāṉē
the father in āmāttūr where the bees having drunk the honey in the lotus hum like music and the great peacocks dance, the clouds make a sound like the maḻavu, by their hands (viṇ denotes the cloud in the sky)
[[vicumpiṉ tuḷivīḻiṉ allāl (Kuṟal, 16)]]
maṅkai vāḷnutal māṉ maṉattiṭaivāṭi ūṭa
the lady who has a bright forehead and looks like the deer to be sulky grieving in her mind.
maṇam kamaḻ caṭaik kaṅkaiyāḷ irunta(1) karuttu āvatu eṉṉai (kol ām)
what is idea of Kankaiyāḷ to stay in the fragrant caṭai.
[[Variant reading: (1) karuttāyatu eṉṉai]]
{2:50}__8+
{$} kuṉṟu eṭutta nīcācaraṉ tiraḷ tōḷ irupatum neritara aṉṟu aṭarttu ukantāy! āmāttūr ammāṉē!
the father in āmāttūr who became great by pressing down long ago to crush all the twenty round shoulders of the arakkaṉ who moves at night and who lifted the mountain!
niṉṟu aṭarttiṭum aimpulaṉ nilaiyāta vaṇṇam niṉaittu uḷattiṭai, veṉṟu aṭarttu urupāl maṭamātai virumputal eṉ
what is the idea for desiring a young lady on one half having pressed down suduing the five senses which persist in oppressing and which are un stedy in their nature?
{2:50}__9+
{$} taiyalāḷoṭu piccaikku iccai tayaṅkutōl arai ārtta vēṭam koṇṭu, aiyam ēṟṟu ukantāy; āmāttūr ammāṉē
the father in āmāttūr! you became great by receiving alms having a form in which you tied a shining skin in the waist, desiring begging alms with a young lady.
ceyyatāmaraimēl iruntavaṉōṭu māl aṭi nīḷmuṭitēṭa
Piramaṉ who was seated in a red lotus flower and Māl to go in search of your feet and tall head.
veyya ār aḻalāy nimirkiṉṟa (1) veṟṟimai eṉ
what is the reason for your distinctive greatness in growing tall as a pillar of hot and unbearable fire?
[[Variant reading: (1) vēṟṟumai eṉ]]

{$} muttai veṉṟa muṟuvalāḷ umai paṅkaṉ eṉṟu imaiyōr paraviṭum attaṉē! ariyāy! āmāttūr ammāṉē
the master whom the celestials who do not wink praise the Lord who has as a half Umai whose teeth are superior in beauty to pear;s! rare god! the father in āmāttūr.
puttar puṉ camaṇ ātar poymmoḻi nūlkaḷ piṭittu alartūṟṟa
when the buddhists and low camaṇar who are ignorant persons to slander being firm in their works which contain false words.
niṉ aṭi pattar pēṇa niṉṟa param āya pāṉmai (atu) eṉ
what is the nature of being superior to superior things for the devotees to esteem your feet?

{$} toṭal veṇtalai mālai ārttu mayaṅkiruḷ(ḷ) eri ēnti mānaṭam āṭal mēyatueṉ eṉṟu
questioning the reason for performing the great dance holding the fire in the night of dense darkness, having tied a garland of dried white skulls.
āmāttūr ammāṉai
the father in āmāttūr.
kōṭal nākam arumpu paimpoḻil koccaiyār iṟaiñāṉacampantaṉ pāṭal pattum vallār paralōkam cērvārē
those who are able to recite all the ten verses of ñāṉacampantaṉ, the chief of the inhabitants of Koccai (cikāḻi) which has fertile gardens in which the white glory malabar lily puts foots bud like the hood of the cobra, will reach the superior Civalokam.

(1) aḷḷal am paḻaṉai mēya ālaṅkāṭaṭu aṭikaḷārē
the god in Ālaṅkāṭu adjoining Paḻaṉi full of mire
[[paḻaṉai is the corruption of Paḻiyaṉūr]]
veḷḷa nīrc caṭaiyar (pōlum)
has on his caṭi water which is in the form of floods.
virumpuvārkku eḷiyar pōlum
is easy of approach to those who desire him.
uḷḷuḷē uruki niṉṟu aṅku ukappavarkku aṉpar pōlum
is the friend to those who rejoice, the recesses of their hearts melting.
kaḷḷamē viṉaikaḷ ellām karicu aṟuttiṭuvar (pōlum)
will remove all deceitful acts without the slightest trace of them to remin with the devotees.
[[this word in this and the folowing verses the particle of comparison is used as a mere expletive without any comparison]]
[[Variant reading: 1. aḷḷalār paḻaṉaimēya: aḷḷalār paḻaṉai vēli]]

ālaṅkāṭṭu aṭikaḷārē
see 1st verse.
centaḻal uruvar (pōlum)
has a form resembling red fire.
ciṉaviṭai uṭaiyar (pōlum)
has an angry bull.
venta veṇṇīṟu koṇṭu meykku aṇintiṭuvar (pōlum)
smears on his body the well-burnt white sacred ash.
mantam am poḻil paḻaṉai 1. malkiya vaḷḷal (pōlum)
is the flourishing god of unbounded liberality who is in paḻaṉi which has garden in which there is gentle southern wind.
[[Variant reading: 1. palkiya celvar]]
antam il aṭikaḷ (pōlum)
is the god without death.

kāma vēḷaik kaṉal eḻak kaṇṇiṉāl viḻippar (pōlum)
Civaṉ opened his frontal eye for the fire to rise and consume kāmavēḷ (cupid)
eṇ ilār puraṅkaḷ mūṉṟum eriuṇac carippar (pōlum)
will lugh; for the fire to consume all the three forts of the enemies.
paṇṇiṉ ār muḻavam ōvāp paimpoḻiṟ paḻaṉai mēya aṇṇalār
the god who is the paḻaṉi which has verdant gardens and where muḻavam does not cease from being played to the accompaniment of melody-types.
emmai āḷum ālaṅkāṭṭu aṭikaḷārē
is the god in ālaṅkāṭu who admits us as his slave.

ālaṅkāṭṭu aṭikaḷārē
see 1st verse.
kāṟiṭu viṭattai uṇṭa kaṇṭar eṇ tōḷar (pōlum)
has eight shoulders; has a neck in which there is the poison of irritation in the tongue which he consumed (kāṟutal of irritation in the togue).
tuṟiṭu cuṭalai taṉṉil cuṇṇa veṇṇīṟṟar (pōlum)
smears the fine powder of white sacred ash that is found in the burning groundwhich has shrubbery.
kūṟiṭum uruvar pōlum
has a form which is one half a lady.
kuḷirpoḻil paḻaṉai mēya āṟu iṭu caṭaiyar (pōlum)
has a caṭi in which he places the river, kaṅki and dwells with desire in paḻaṉi which has cool gardens.

tīrttam ām paḻaṉaimēya tiruvālaṅkāṭaṉār
the Lord in ālaṅkāṭu which is adjoining paḻaṉi which has holy waters.
pārttaṉōṭu amarporutu
fighting with pārttaṉ in a battle.
pattimai aḷappar (pōlum)
he will test the intensity of his piety.
kūrtta vāy ampu kōttuk kuṇaṅkaḷai aṟivar (pōlum)
will know the qualities by discharging arrows of sharp ends firing them in the bow.
pērttum ōr āva nāḻi am poruṅ koṭuppar (pōlum)
will grant him after that a quiver with arrows,

vīṭṭiṉār cuṭuveṇṇīṟṟu meykkaṇintiṭuvar (pōlum)
Civaṉ smears on his body burnt white sacred ash of those who died (vīṭṭiṉār - vīṭiṉār the underlined letter is augmentation for rhyme.)
kāṭṭil niṉṟu āṭal pēṇum karuttiṉai uṭaiyar (pōlum)
has the intention of desiring dance standing in the cremation ground.
pāṭṭiṉ ār muḻavam ōvāp paimpoḻiṟ paḻaṉai mēyār
dwells in paḻaṉi which has flourishing gardens and where the muḻavu is played to the accompaniment of vocal music.
āṭṭiṉār aravam taṉṉai ālaṅkāṭṭu aṭikaḷārē
the god in ālaṅkāṭu cused the cobra to dance.

tāḷ uṭaic ceṅkamalat taṭamcoḷ cēvaṭiyar (pōlum)
Civaṉ has red feet which possess greatness and which are like the red-louts with a stem.
nāḷ uṭaik kālaṉ vīḻa utai koṇṭa nampar (pōlum)
is the trustworthy god who kicked kālaṉ who remembers the span of life allotted to every living being, to fall on the earth.
kōḷ uṭaip piṟavi tīrppār
will remove the birth which is full of evils.
kuḷir poḻiṟ paḻaṉai mēya āḷ uṭai(ya) aṇṇal (pōlum) ālaṅkāṭṭu aṭikaḷārē
the deity in ālaṅkāṭu is the chief who admits us as his slave and dwells in paḻaṉi of cool gardens.

kuṟippu uṭai(ya) vēṭam koṇṭu kūṭiṉār umai taṉōṭē
Civaṉ was united with Umi with a form which suggested love
kaṅkaiyāḷaic cūṭiṉār (pōlum)
wore Kankiyāḷ
cuvaṟiṭu caṭaiyār (pōlum)
has a caṭi which absorbed her.
pāṭiṉār cāmavētam
chanted the cāma vētam.
paim poḻil paḻaṉai mēyār, ālaṅkāṭṭu aṭikaḷārē
the god in ālaṅkāṭu and who is in paḻṉi adjoining it which has verdant gardens.
kāḷikāṇa āṭiṉār
danced to be witnessed by kāḷi (This is a local legend. Civaṉ danced with one leg poised upward and defeated kāḷi in the competition of dance).

veṟṟaraic camaṇarōṭu vilai uṭai(ya)k kūṟai pōrkkum oṟṟaraic coṟkaḷ koḷḷār kuṇaṅkaḷai ukappar (pōlum)
Civaṉ will rejoice about the qualities of those who do not esteem the words of the buddhists who act as spys and cover their bodies with a costly robe and the camanar who are naked.
peṟṟamē ukantu aṅku ēṟum perumaiyai uṭaiyar pōlum
has the greatness of riding on a bull, sitting high on it.
ālaṅkāṭṭu aṭikaḷārē-aṟṟaṅkaḷ aṟivar (pōlum)
the god in ālaṅkāṭu will know the sufferings of his devotees and remove them.

iṭaimarutu mēviya iṭaṅkoṇṭār
Civaṉ who occupied as his place Iṭimarutu, wishing it.
āṟu caṭaikku aṇivar
adorns his caṭi with a river.
aṅkait tīyar
has fire in his palm.
(1) aḻakar
is beautiful.
paṭai uṭaiyar
has a battle-axe, trident and other weapons
ampoṉ tōḷ mēl nīṟu taṭavantu
having smeared holy ash on the exceedingly beautiful shoulders.
nittam pali koḷvar
receives alms dily
moytta pūtam kūṟum kuṇam uṭaiyar
has qualities which are prised by the crowded pūtams.
kōvaṇattar
has loin-cloth only as his dress.
kōṭāla vēṭattar
has adorned himself, with a crescent-shaped necklace of pearls;
[[PP: kōlap paṇaikkaccum kūṟai uṭaiyum kuḷir muttiṉ kōṭālamum (periyāḻvār tirumoḻi, 3-3-1.)]]
koḷkai colliṉ
if we say about his nature.
īṟum naṭuvum mutalum āvār
he is the beginning, middle and end of all things.
[[Variant reading: (1) aḻakār]]

iṭai marutu mēvi iṭam koṇṭār
see 1st verse.
maṅkul mati vaippar
will place on his caṭi the crescent which shines in the sky.
vāṉa nāṭar
has the heaven as his place.
maṭamāṉ iṭam uṭaiyar
has a young deer in his left hand
mātarāḷaip paṅkil mika vaippar
places a lady on the half to be superior to himself.
pāl pōl nīṟṟar
smears himself with holy ash as white as milk.
paḷikku vaṭam puṉaivar
adorns himself with a neck-lace of crystal beads.
pāva nācar
destroys the sins of his devotees.
caṅku tirai ukaḷum cāykkāṭu āḷvar
rules cāykkātu where conches frisk in the waves (this shrine is near pūmpukār)
caritai pala uṭaiyar
has many acts to his credit.
taṉmai colliṉ
if we speak about his nature
1. eṅkum pali tirivār
wanders everywhere for alms.
eṉṉuḷ nīṅkār
will not leave me, being within me.
[[Variant reading: 1. eṅkum tiri taruvar]]

{$} vāytta iṭai marutu mēvi iṭam koṇṭār
Civaṉ who occupied iṭimarutu appropriately suited, as his place desiring it.
tēcar
has got brilliance.
tiṟam niṉaivār cintai cērum celvar
is the god who approaches the minds of those who think about his many acts of grace.
tiruvārūr eṉṟum uḷḷār
is always in tiruvārūr
malariṉ kaṇ vācam
is the fragrance in flowers.
māṉ tōl pōrppar
covers himself with the skin of a deer.
maruvum kari uriyar
has the skin of an elephant also as a covering.
vañcak kaḷvar
is a very deceptive thief.
nēcar aṭaintārkku
is a friend to those who have approached him.
aṭaiyātārkku 1. niṭṭuravar
is a hard-hearted person to those who do not approach him.
kaṭṭaṅkar
carries a battle-axe.
niṉaivārkku eṉṟum īcar
is the master always to those who meditate on him.
puṉaṟ poṉṉit tīrttar
has as the holy water the river poṉṉi which is perennial
[[Variant reading: 1. niṭṭuruvar]]

iṭai marutu mēvi iṭam koṇṭār
see 1st verse.
kariya maṉakkaḷvarkkuk karappar
will conceal himself and not bestow his grace on those who have deceptive minds.
uḷḷam karavātē tam niṉaiya kiṟpār pāvam turappar
is able to drive away the sins of those who are capable to think of him without deceptive ideas in their minds.
toṭu kaṭaliṉ nañcam uṇpar
will consume the poison of the ocean which was dug out
[[There is a tradition that the sea now known a Bay of Bengal was dug out by cakarar;
kuṇā atu karai poru toṭu kaṭaṟ kuṇakkum (puṟam 6-3);
kīḻk kaṇṇatu karaiyaip porukiṉṟa cakararāl tōṇṭappaṭṭa cākarattiṉ kiḻakkum (do. old commentary)]]
tūya maṟai moḻiyar
has the words of the vētam which are without blemish.
tīyāl oṭṭi nirappar puram mūṉṟum nīṟu 1. ceyvar
will reduced to ashes the three cities of the senseless acurar by setting fire to them.
[[nirappu poverty of intelligence;
nuṇ uṇar viṉmai vaṟumai (nālaṭiyar, 251)]]
nīḷ caṭaiyar
has a long caṭi
pāy viṭai koṇṭu eṅkum aiyam irappar
will beg alms everywhere, riding on a galloping bull.
emai āḷvar
will admit me as his protege.
eṉṉuḷ nīṅkār
will not leave my mind.
[[Variant reading: 1. cērum]]

evarum pōṟṟa iṭai marutu mēvi iṭam koṇṭār
Civaṉ who occupied as his place iṭimarutu to be prised by all.
koṭi ār iṭapattar
has on his flag the form of a bull.
kūttum āṭi
having performed dance also.
kuḷir koṉṟai mēl vaippar
will place the cool koṉṟi flowers on his caṭi.
kōlam ārnta poṭi ārum mēṉiyar
has a body on which beautiful sacred ash is smeared thickly.
pūtippaiyar
has a bag contining sacred ash.
pulittōlar
has a tiger's skin.
poṅku aravar
has an angry cobra.
pūṇa nūlar
wears sacred thread.
aṭiyār kuṭi āvar
is the near relation to his devotees.
antaṇāḷar ākutiyiṉ mantirattār amarar pōṟṟa
to be prised by the immortals who are invited to receive the oblations offered in the consecrated fire by mantiram by brahmins.
iṭi ār kaḷiṟṟu uriyar
has a skin of an elephant whose roar is like the sound of the thunder.

iṭai marutu mēvi iṭam koṇṭār
see 1st verse.
kā ār caṭaimuṭiyar
has a caṭi coiled into a crown, which is like a garden. (is in catimuti which has gardens is another meaning; catimuti is a shrine)
kārōṇattar
is in Kārōṇam (Kārōṇam may mean any shrine ending in Kārōṇam; Kacci, nāki etc).
kayilāyam maṉṉiṉār
resides permanently in Kayilāyam.
paṉṉum iṉcol pāvār
is in the verses which possess sweet words which lend themselves to research.
poruḷ āḷar
is the meaning of those verses.
vāḷ ār kaṇṇi payilum tiru uruvam pākam mēyār
has a holy body in which a lady whose eyes can be composed to a sword is always there without separation, as a half.
pū ār puṉal aṇavu puṉ kūr vāḻvar
dwells in puṉkūr where there is water abundant with flowers.
puram mūṉṟum oḷḷaḻalākkāyat toṭṭa ē ār cilai malaiyar
has a mountin improvised into a bow in which the arrow which was fixed and discharged to set ablaze the three cities, with bright fire.
eṅkum tāmē
pervades everywhere.

{$} imaiyavarkaḷ pōṟṟa eṉṟum iṭai marutu mēvi iṭam koṇṭār
Civaṉ who occupied always iṭimarutu desiring it, to be prised by the celestials who do not wink.
naṭattiṉ kaṇ purintār
is fond of dancing.
tam il pirintār poḻil ārūr pukku uṟaivar
dwells in ārūr, which has gardens, entering into that city, having separated himself from his original abode.
akal 1. vāya pēyum tāmum piriyār
himself and the pēys with wide-open mouths will not part company.
pēṇu kāṭṭil erintu ār aṉal ukappar
is fond of the fire in the cremation ground, which is burning well, wishing it.
evviṭattum tāmē eṉṟu ēḻil ōcai ēttuvār pāl iruntār
is with those who prise him with the music of seven scales thin King that he is pervading everywhere.

see 1st verse.
viṭṭu ilaṅku mā maḻuvar
has a big battle-axe which shines emitting rays.
vēlai nañcar
consumed the poison of the ocean.
viṭaṅkar
is extremely beautiful.
vari puṉal cūḻ veṇkāṭṭu uḷḷār

maṭṭu ilaṅku tār mālai mārpil nīṟṟar
smears in his chest holy ash, along with the garland worn on it which has much honey.
[[tār- iṉpamālai; mālai-pōrukkuriyatu]]
maḻapāṭiyuḷ uṟaivar; mākāḷattar
will dwell in maḻapāṭi, is in mākāḷam
[[Mākāḷam may mean any shrine ending in mākāḷam such as ampar mākālam, irumpi mākāḷam and uñci mākālam.]]
ciṭṭu ilaṅku val arakkar kōṉai aṉṟu ceḻumuṭiyum tōḷ aiññāṉku aṭarak kālāl iṭṭu
pressing with his leg to crush the twenty shoulders and (ten) strong heads in the distant past, of the strong King of the arakkar who was eminent by his authority.
[[(ciṭṭu = ciṭṭi - ciruṣṭi authority]]
iraṅki (maṟṟu) avaṉukku veṉṟi īntār
gave him victory, taking pity on him.

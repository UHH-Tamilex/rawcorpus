{1:115}__1+
{$} caṅku oḷir muṉkaiyar tam iṭaiyē aṅku iṭum palikoḷum (1) avaṉ
Civaṉ who receives the alms given by the ladies who wear on their wrists brilliant bangles made on conch.
kōpappoṅku aravu āṭalōṉ
performs a dance with a cobra that bursts in anger;
[[this dance is called bhungatrace naṭṉam; its definition with an illustration of natarāja is found on pp. 110-111, slokams, 221-230, in ruravāgama Vol II - published by the French Institute of Indology, 1972]]
puvaṉi ōṅka eṅkumaṉ irāmaṉatīccaram
irāmaṉatīccaram is the place of Civaṉ who pervades everywhere so that all the worlds may become exalted.
[[Variant reading: (1) makaṉ]]
{1:115}__2+
{$} canta nal malar aṇi tāḻcaṭaiyaṉ
Civaṉ has a hanging caṭi which he adorns with beautiful and good flowers.
tanta mattavaṉ tātai(t) tāṉ
he is the father of vināyakaṉ who has a tusk and must. (ō, tāṉ expletives)
antam il pāṭalōṉ aḻakaṉ
is the beautiful god; sings songs which are limitless.
em tavaṉ irāmanatīccaramē
īrāmaṉaticcaram is the place of our ascetic.
{1:115}__3+
{$} taḻaimayil ēṟavaṉ tātai (ō, tāṉ)
Civaṉ is the father of Murukaṉ who rides on a peacock which has a big til.
[[ō, tāṉ: expletives]]
maḻai (1) poḻi caṭaiyavaṉ
has on his caṭi clouds that pour rin;
[[PP: vaṉtiṟal varuṇaṉ viṭṭa māriyai vilakka vīraṉ, miṉtikaḻ caṭaiyiṉiṉṟu nīṅkiyamēkam nāṉkum
(Parañcōti tiruviḷiyātal, 19-26)]]
maṉṉu kātil kuḻai (atu) vilaṅkiya kōla mārpiṉ iḻaiyavaṉ irāmaṉatīccaram
Irāmaṉatīccaram is the shrine of Civaṉ who wears on his beautiful chest a sacred thread which is worn cross, and a mens' earring which stays in the right ear.
[[Variant reading: (1) poti]]
{1:115}__4+
{$} cattiyuḷ āti ōr taiyalpaṅkaṉ
Civaṉ has among his energies a young lady as his āticatti.
mutti(yatu) ākiya mūrttiyōtāṉ
is Civaṉ has a form that grants liberation.
attiya kaiyiṉil aḻaku cūlam vaittavaṉ irāmaṉatīccaram
irāmaṉaticcaram is the shrine of Civaṉ who holds in his hand fire that destroys the world, and a beautiful trident (attīya has been shortened as attiya for yetuki).
{1:115}__5+
{$} tāḻnta kuḻalcaṭai muṭi (ataṉ) mēl tōynta iḷampiṟai tulaṅku ceṉṉip pāynta kaṅkaiyoṭu paṭaaravam ēyntavaṉ
Civaṉ who has on his head a brilliant young crescent which is coming into contact with the caṭi coiled into a crown with curly hirs hanging low, Kaṅki which came swooping down, and a hooded cobra.
irāmaṉatu īccaram
is in the shrine of irāmaṉaticcaram.
{1:115}__6+
{$} carikuḻal ilaṅkiya taiyalkāṇum periyavaṉ
the great god who is looked at with love by the young lady who has shining tresses of hir hanging on the nape.
kāḷi taṉ periyakūttai ariyavaṉ
is very difficult to be understood even by the Kaḷi's dance.
āṭalōṉ
performs the ānanta taṇṭavam.
aṅkai ēntum eriyavaṉ irāmaṉatīccaram
irāmaṉatīccaram is the shrine of Civaṉ who holds fire in his palm.
{1:115}__7+
{$} māṟu ilā mātu orupaṅkaṉ
Civaṉ has on one half a lady who is not different from him. (māṟu ilā may also mean one who has no comparison).
mēṉi nīṟu(atu) āṭalōṉ
bathes his body in sacred ash.
nīḷcaṭaimēl āṟu cūṭuvāṉ aḻakaṉ
is the beautiful god; and wears on his long caṭi a river.
viṭai ēṟavaṉ
rides on a bull.
irāmaṉatīccaram
his shrine is irāmaṉaticcaram.
{1:115}__8+
{$} taṭavarai arakkaṉait talai nerittōṉ
Civaṉ crushed the heads of the arakkaṉ by the big mountin.
paṭa aravu āṭṭiya paṭar caṭaiyōṉ
has a spreading caṭi; cused the cobra with a hood to dance.
naṭam (atu) āṭalāṉ
himself performs dance.
nāṉ maṟaikkum iṭam avaṉ irāmaṉatīccaram
irāmaṉatīccaram is the shrine of Civaṉ who is the origin of the four vētams.
{1:115}__9+
{$} aṉam aṇi ayaṉ aṇi muṭiyum, taṉam aṇi taiyal taṉpākaṉ taṉṉaik kāṇāṉ
Ayaṉ (Piramaṉ) who transformed himself into a swan could not see the beautiful head of Civaṉ who has as his half a lady who has beautiful breasts.
paṉam maṇi (1) varavu ari pātam kāṇāṉ
Ari (Māl) who comes on festivals on a serpent which has precious stones in its hood could not see the feet (paṇa has been changed into paṉa to suit etuki).
iṉam maṇi irāmaṉatīccaram
Irāmaṉatīccaram is the shrine of Civaṉ who is like the group of precious stones.
[[Variant readings: (1) yaravari, vākari)
{1:115}__10+
{$} taṟipōl ām camaṇ cākkiyar col koḷēl
(People of this world!) Do not accept as truth the words of camaṇar and cākkiyar who are like wooden posts.
(1) aṟivu ōrāṉ
Civaṉ could not be known by both the knowledge of the real nature of the soul end knowledge obtined through the senses and the mind.
nāmam aṟintu uraimiṉ
utter his names understanding their significance.
maṟikaiyōṉ taṉ muṭi maṇiār kaṅkai (2) eṟipavaṉ irāmaṉatīccaram
irāmaṉatīccaram is the shrine of Civaṉ who holds in his hand a young deer and on whose head the river Kaṅki with precious stones dashes its waves.
[[Variant readings : (1) aṟivōrārānāmam (2) eṟiyavaṉ]]
{1:115}__11+
{$} tēṉ malarkkoṉṟaiyōṉ ...
Civaṉ wears koṉṟi flowers having honey.
muntamakku ūṉam aṉṟē
No evil will befall you.
[In this verse the other words have been lost]

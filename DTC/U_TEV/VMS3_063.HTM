{3:63}__1+
{$} paiṅkōṭṭu malarppuṉṉaip paṟavaikāḷ
birds which are perching on the mast-wood tree which has flowers in its fertile branches!
payappu ūra
Sallowness to spread all over the body.
caṅkāṭṭam tavirttu
discontinuing intimacy with me
[[payappu-pacappu;]]
[[PP_EXT: payappu eṉmēṉiyatuvē
(Kuṟuntōkai, 219, 1)]]
eṉṉait tavirā nōy tantāṉē
gave me an affliction which never leaves me
[[ē is used to denote pity]]
ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭimēya ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ paṇiceyya
when Ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ who lives in Ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭi is performing all services.
veṅkāṭṭuḷ aṉal ēnti viḷaiyāṭum perumāṉ
Civaṉ who plays in the hot cremation ground holding fire.
[[perumāṉ, tavirttut tavirā nōy tantāṉē is the syntactical link]]
{3:63}__2+
{$} poṉ ām pūṅkaḻik kāṉal puṇartuṇaiyōṭu uṭaṉ vāḻum aṉṉaṅkāḷ
male swans which live with your mates united with you in the sea-shore gardens which have garden pollen shedding from the mast-wood trees!
aṉṟilkāḷ
aṉṟilkāḷ
[[aṉṟil is a bird whether male or female very familiar in Indian poetry, as a standard model for constancy and inseparable love]]
akaṉṟum pōy varuvīrkāḷ
you leave the gardens, go out in search of prey and return to stay here.
kal naviltōḷ ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ kaṇapatīccaram mēya iṉṉamutaṉ iṇaiyaṭikkīḻ
in the presence of the two feet of Civaṉ who is as sweet as the nectar to ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ who has shoulders as strong as the mountain, and who dwells in Kaṇapaticcaram.
eṉatu allal uraiyīrē
will you not inform my sufferings?
{3:63}__3+
{$} kuṭṭattum kuḻikkaraiyum kuḷirpoykaittaṭattu akattum
in the expanse of the cool natural tanks and the banks pits and depressions.
iṭṭattāl iraitērum iruñciṟakiṉ maṭanārāy
young crane with big wings, which searches for its prey with desire!
ciṭṭaṉ cīrcciṟuttoṇṭaṉ ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭimēya vaṭṭavār caṭaiyāṟku eṉ varuttam ceṉṟu uraiyāy
will you not tall about my pain to Civaṉ who has a long and round caṭai and who dwells in ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭi where famous ciṟuttoṅṭaṉ, a wise and virtuous devotee dwells, going to his place?
{3:63}__4+
{$} kāṉ arukum vayal arukum kaḻi arukum kaṭal arukum mīṉiriya
the fish to flee in all directions by the side of the sea-shore garden, by the side of the fields, by the side of the back-water and by the side of the sea.
varupuṉalil iraitēr veṇmaṭanārāy
the young and white common crane which searches for its prey in the flowing water!
tēṉ amartārc ciṟutoṇṭaṉ ceṅkāṭṭāṅkuṭimēya vāṉ amarum caṭaiyārkku eṉ varuttam ceṉṟu uraiyāyē
will you not tell my suffering, going near Civaṉ who has a caṭai comparable to the red sky and who dwells in ceṅkāṭṭāṅkuṭi, the native place of ciṟuttoṅtaṉ who wears a garland which has honey
[[tillai niṉṟōṉ caṭaimēlatu ottuccevvāṉ aṭainta pacuṅkatir veḷḷaic ciṟupiṟaikkē (Kōvaiyār 67); caṭaicekkar vāṉattiṟku uvamai (Do. Pēraciriyar)]]
{3:63}__5+
{$} āral ām cuṟavam ēyntu akaṉkaḻaṉic ciṟaku ularttum pāralvāyc ciṟukurukē! payil tūvi maṭanārāy!
the small crane which is on the look-out for prey drying the wings in the broad fields like the shark which feeds upon the small fish āral, and young crane with dense feathers;
[[pūkkatūum iṉavāḷai, nuṇāral paruvarāl, kuruūkkeṭiṟṟa kuṇṭu akaḻi (puṟam, 18-8-10); pāral-pārttal this word has another form as pārval; nīr illa nīḷ iṭaiya pārval irukkai (puram, 3-18-19); pārval kokkiṉ parivēṭpu añcā (patiṟṟuppattu, 2-28)]]
cīrulām ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭimēya nīrulām caṭaiyārkku eknilaimai ceṉṟu uraiyīrē
will you not tell my condition, going near Civaṉ on whose caṭai water is moving and who dwells with desire in ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭi, the native place ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ whose fame has spread everywhere.
{3:63}__6+
{$} kuḷirpoykaittuṟaikkeṇṭai kavar kurukē
the crane which preys on the Keṇṭai fish available in the ghats of the cool natural tanks, with avidity!
tuṇaipiriyā maṭanārāy
the young crane which is not separated from its mate!
kuṟaikkoṇṭār iṭar tīrttalkaṭaṉ aṉṟē
is it not your duty to remove the afflictions of those who are aggrieved?
kaṟaikkaṇṭaṉ; piṟaicceṉṉik kaṇapatīccaram mēya ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ perumāṉ cīr aruḷ orunāḷ peṟalāmē
is it possible to receive the eminent grace of the Lord who is worshipped by ciṟuttoṇtaṉ, and who is in Kaṇapaticcaram, who has a black neck and a crescent on his head?
{3:63}__7+
{$} karu aṭiya pacuṅkāl veṇkurukē oṇkaḻi nārāy
the white stork which has green legs and black feet! crane which lives in the bright back-water!
oru aṭiyāḷ irantāḷ eṉṟu orunāḷ ceṉṟu uraiyīrē
will you not tell going near, Civaṉ at least on one day the devotees who is a lady besought your grace?
ceruvaṭitōḷ ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭimēya tiruvaṭitaṉ tiruvaruḷē peṟalāmō
is it possible to receive the eminent grace of the deity who dwells with desire in ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭi, whe native place of ciṟuttoṅṭaṉ who has shoulders with good training in wars.
{3:63}__8+
{$} kūr āral iraitērntu kuḷam ulavi vayal vāḻum (1) tārāvē
the duck that lives in the fields after wandering in the tank searching for its prey of āral fish found in plenty!
[[tārā ārum vayal cūḻnta cāḷakkirāmattu aṭikaḷai (periya tirumoḻi, 1-5-10)]]
maṭanārāy
beautiful crane!
tamiyēṟku oṉṟu uraiyīrē
will you not speak one word to me who feels lonely!
cīrāḷaṉ ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭimēya pērāḷaṉ perumāṉ taṉ aruḷ orunāḷ peṟalāmē
I can receive the grave of the Lord who has great fame, and who dwells with desire in ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭi, the native place of ciṟuttoṅṭaṉ who is the father of cirāḷaṉ;
[[nīr ārum caṭaimuṭiyār aruḷiṉāl niṟaitavattup, pērāḷaṉ avartamakkup peruti tirumaṉaiyaṟattiṉ, vērāki viḷaṅku tiru veṇkāṭṭu naṅkai pāla cīrāḷatēvar eṉum tirumaintar avatarittār (periya purāṇam, ciṟuttoṇṭa nāyaṉār purāṇam 17)]]
[[(Variant reading: (1) tārāveṇmaṭanārāy]]
{3:63}__9+
{$} naṟappolipūṅkaḻikkāval navil kurukē
the crane which is always in the sea-shore garden by the side of the backwater in which flowers eminent by their honey, blossom!
ulaku ellām aṟappalitērntu uḻalvārkku eṉ alarkōṭal aḻakiyatē
is it nice on the part Civaṉ who wanders, begging alms given out of charity throughout the world, to take upon himself the reproach which is the result of the slander indulged in by women folk about me?
ciṟappulavaṉ ciṟuttoṇṭaṉ ceṅkāṭṭaṅkuṭimēya piṟappilipēr pitaṟṟi niṉṟu iḻakkō emperunalamē
shall we lose our great beauty by prattling the names of Civaṉ who does not know birth and who dwells in ceṅkāṭṭāṅkuṭi, the native place of ciṟuttoṇtaṉ, the eminent erudite scholar.

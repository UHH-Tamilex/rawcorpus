{4:9}__1+
{$} talaiyē
my head!
talaimālai talaikku aṇintu
having adorned his head with a garland of skulls.
talaiyālē pali tērum talaivaṉai nī vaṇaṅkāy
you bow down to the chief who collects alms in a skull.
talaiyē! nī vaṇaṅkāy
my head, you bow down to Civaṉ
{4:9}__2+
{$} kaṇkāḷ! kāṇmiṉkaḷō
my eyes! having a vision of.
kaṭal nañcu uṇṭa kaṇṭaṉ taṉṉai
Civaṉ who has a neck that consumed the poison that rose in the sea.
eṇtōḷ vīci niṉṟu āṭum pirāṉtaṉaik kaṇkāḷ! kāṇmiṉkaḷō!
my eyes! have a vision of the master who dances standing and swinging the eight shoulders;
[[PP_(eṇtōḷ!):
eṇtōḷ mukkaṇ emmāṉē
(Tiruvācakam, kuḻaitta pattu, 7)]]
{4:9}__3+
{$} cevikāḷ! kēṇmiṉkaḷō, cevikāḷ kēṇmiṉkaḷō
my ears, hear, my ears, hear
civaṉ, emiṟai; cempavaḷa eripōl mēṉippirāṉ tiṟam eppōtum
the different attributes of Civaṉ, our master, who has a body the colour of which is like the red coral and fire.
{4:9}__4+
{$} mūkkē! nī muralāy mutukāru uṟai mukkaṇaṉai
my nose! make a sound about the Lord who has three eyes and dwells in the cremation ground which is the oldest;
[[PP_(mutukāṭu):
ellār puṟaṉum tāṉ kaṇṭu ulakattu,
maṉpataikku ellām tāṉāyt
taṉpuṟam kāṇpōrk kāṇpu aṟiyātē
(Puṟam, 356-7-9)]]
vākkē nōkkiya maṅkai maṇāḷaṉai
the husband of the lady who is all speech.
mūkkē! nī muralāy
see above.
{4:9}__5+
{$} vāyē! vāḻttu (kaṇṭāy) matayāṉai uripōrttup pēya vāḻ kāṭṭakattu āṭum pirāṉtaṉai
my tongue! sing songs of benediction about the master who dances in the cremation-ground where peys live, having covered himself with the skin of an elephant of must fluid.
vāyē vāḻttu (kaṇṭāy)
see above.
{4:9}__6+
{$} neñcē! nī niṉaiyāy nimir puṉcaṭai niṉmalaṉai
my mind! you meditate upon the spotless Civaṉ who has an erect and murky caṭai.
mañcu āṭum malai maṅkai maṇāḷaṉai (neñcē! nī niṉaiyāy)
my mind! meditate on the husband of the daughter of the mountain on which the clouds move as if dancing.
{4:9}__7+
{$} kaikāḷ! kūppittoḻīr, kaṭi māmalar tūvi niṉṟu
my hands! you worship with your hands joined by scattering fragrant big flowers.
(kaikāḷ! kūppittoḻīr) (1) paivāyp pāmpu arai ārtta paramaṉai
the supreme Civaṉ who has tied in his waist a cobra which has a hood in its head.
[[Variant reading: (1)paivāy pāmparai]]
{4:9}__8+
{$} ākkaiyāl payaṉ eṉ?
what is the benefit we get from this body?
araṉkōyil valam vantu
going from left to right round the temple of araṉ.
pūkkaiyāl aṭṭip pōṟṟi eṉṉāta iv ākkaiyāl payaṉ eṉ?
what is the benefit we get from this body when we do not say you protect me' showering flowers with the hands, at his feet.
{4:9}__9+
{$} kālkaḷāl payaṉ eṉ?
what is the use of the legs?
kaṟaikkaṇṭaṉ uṟaikōyil kōlak kōpurakkōkaraṇam cūḻāk kālkaḷāl payaṉ eṉ?
what is the use of the legs when we do not go round the temple Kōkaraṇam of beautiful towers which is the temple where the Lord with a neck which has poison, dwells.
{4:9}__10+
{$} uṟṟār ār uḷarō? uyirkoṇṭupōmpoḻutu kuṟṟālattu uṟai kūttaṉ allāl namakku (uṟṟār ār uḷarō)
are there any other intimate relatives except the dancer who dwells in Kuṟṟālam when the god of death walks away taking our lives.
{4:9}__11+
{$} iṟumāntiruppaṉ kolō īcaṉ palkaṇattu eṇṇappaṭṭuc ciṟumāṉ ēntitaṉ cēvaṭikkīḻc(1)ceṉṟu
having reached the red feet of Civaṉ who holds a young deer, being counted among the hosts of pūtam and pey belonging to Civaṉ, shall I feel exulted?
[[reaching the feet of Civaṉ is itself salvation;]]
[[PP: eyyā nalicaic cevvēṟcēey, cēvaṭi paṭarum cemmal uḷḷamoṭu
(Murukāṟṟuppaṭai, 11 61-62)]]
[[Variant reading: (1) ceṉṟum, ceṉṟaṅku]]
{4:9}__12+
{$} tēṭikkaṇṭu koṇṭēṉ, (tēṭik kaṇṭukoṇṭēṉ) tirumāloṭu nāṉmukaṉum tēṭit tēṭa oṇāttēvaṉai eṉṉuḷē
I searched within myself and realised the god who could not be searched by Tirumāl and Nāṉmukaṉ (Piramaṉ) though they tried their level best to search and find him out. (There is no reference to Irāvaṇaṉ)

maṭa neñcamē
(my) ignorant mind.
paṇ kāṭṭip paṭi āya taṉ pattarkkuk kaṇ kāṭṭi
Having bestowed on the devotees in this world who have learnt about him and worship him producing music, benign looks.
[[Variant reading: paṭiyāyttaṉa]]
kaṇṇil niṉṟa maṇi okkum
the Lord can be compared to the apple of the eye.
peṇ kāṭṭi
having a lady on the left half.
piṟai ceṉṉi vaittāṉ tiruveṇkāṭṭai aṭaintu uy
have yourself redeemed by reaching Tiruveṇkāṭu of the Lord who placed the crescent on his head.
[[In this verse the name of the shrine is placed in the rhyme; the rhymes in the other three lines are arranged to suit this.]]
{5:49}__2+
{$}
neñcē
my mind.
koḷḷi ventaḻal vīci niṉṟu āṭuvar
the Lord dances waving the hot fire in the firebrand.
oḷḷiya kaṇam cūḻ umai paṅkaṉār
has Umi as his half and is surrounded by the bright groups of pūtam.
veḷḷiyaṉ
He appears white as he has smeared the sacred ash.
kariyaṉ
He is black in colour;
[[kari elephant;
One of the five faces of Civaṉ called akōram is black in colour;
It may also mean; He has an elephant whose name in ayirāvaṇam]]
pacu ēṟiya teḷḷiyaṉ tiruveṇkāṭu aṭai
go to tiruveṇkātu belonging to the Lord of clear knowledge who rides on a bull.
[[pacu here means bull; not cow.]]
{5:49}__3+
{$}
ūṉ nōkkum iṉpam vēṇṭi uḻalātē
without roaming about desiring only sensual pleasures partining to the body.
vāṉ nōkkum vaḻi āvatu niṉ miṉ
Be stead-fast in the way which will lead you to eternal bliss; people of this world! (The first two lines are advice to mankind; vāṉ here means eternal bliss)
tāṉ nōkkum taṉ aṭiyavar nāviṉil tēṉ nōkkum tiru veṇkāṭu aṭai; neñcē
My mind! reach tiruveṇkāṭu which is the place desired by the Lord who is as sweet as honey in the tongues of his devotees on whom his benign looks fall (tēṉ is ākupeyar)

maṭa neñcamē
my ignorant mind!
umai añca, paru veṇkōṭṭup paiṅkaṇ vēḻattiṉ uruvam kāṭṭiṉāṉ
the Lord who showed the form of an elephant having big white tusks and tender eyes, to frighten Umi (as the Lord covered himself with the elephant's skin he looked like an elephant).
peru veṇkāṭṭu iṟaivaṉ uṟaiyum iṭam tiru veṇkāṭu aṭaintu uy
redeem yourself by reaching tiruveṇkāṭu, the place where the Lord who is in the big white cremation ground, resides.
[[peruveṇkāṭu the universe after the destruction of the world which is reduced to ashes;
Tiruveṇkāṭu is placed in rhyme.]]

{$}
neñcē
my mind!
paṟṟavaṉ
the Lord is the hold for all living beings; (he is the support of all things).
kaṅkai pāmpu matiyuṭaṉ uṟṟavaṉ caṭaiyāṉ
one who has strong matted locks of hir on which Kaṅki, cobra and the crescent moon are staying.
[[uṟṟavaṉ can be taken as a separate word; the Lord on whose matted locks of hir are Kaṅki, cobra and the crescent; one who has matted locks of hir; the first meaning is better.]]
[[Variant reading: paṟṟavaṉ pāmpu kaṅkai matiyuṭaṉ]]
uyar ñāṉaṅkaḷ kaṟṟavaṉ
one who learnt superior wisdom without being tught.
kayavar puram ōrampāl ceṟṟavaṉ tiruveṇkāṭu aṭai
reach tiruveṇkāṭu of the Lord who destroyed with a single arrow the cities belonging to the base people.

neñcamē
my mind.
umaiyāḷ oru pākamāyk kūṭiṉāṉ
he became one with Umayāḷ as his half.
vēṭaṉāy vicayaṟku aruḷ ceytavaṉ
he gave a weapon, pācupatam, to Vicayaṉ, assuming the guise of a hunter
[[This is an allusion to the story in mahābhāratam]]

god who is always a youth.
civaṉār
he has the name of Civaṉ
cintai mēya veṇ kāṭaṉār aṭiyē aṭai
reach only the feet of the Lord in veṇkāṭu who is always in the minds of people who think about him with love.
[[veṇ kāṭaṉār this word is split and veṇ is retined in the 3rd line and kāṭaṉār is placed in the 4th line in the rhyme; this is called vakiyuḷi (vakaiyuḷi);
scansion in disregard of speech rhythm.]]

neñcamē
my mind!
paṭṭam iṇṭai avai koṭu pattarkaḷ ciṭṭaṉ āti eṉṟu cintai ceyya
the devotees to meditate on the Lord thinking him to be the eminent person and the origin of all things, having with them a plate for the forehead, and a variety of flowers called circlet, for the head.

reach veṇkāṭu belonging to the Lord who has eight forms and who is the light of wisdom and who has a dancing form.

neñcē
my mind!
ēṉa vēṭattiṉāṉum piramaṉum
the one who took the form of a pig and Piramaṉ.
tāṉ avvēṭam tāḻntu aṟikiṉṟilā ñāṉa vēṭaṉ
the Lord who is the embodiment of knowledge whose form of fire could not be found out by both of them though they changed their forms.

mā mati paṟṟu irāvaṇam ceya
in order to make the great mind to be detached.
aiyirāvaṇam uṭaiyāṉ taṉai uḷku miṉ
people of this world! meditate on the Lord who has an elephant called ayirāvaṇam
[[One of the ten constituents of the world of Civaṉ; it is supposed to have two thousand tusks, thousand on each side]]
[[PP: ayirā vaṇam eṟātu āṉēṟu ēṟi amarar nāṭu āḷātē ārūr āṇṭa ayirāvaṇamē
(appar, tiruvārur, taṇṭakam 2-1)]]
irāvaṇaṉ taṉai ūṉṟi aruḷ ceyta irāvaṇaṉ tiruveṇkāṭu aṭaimiṉ
people of this world! go to tiurveṇkāṭu belonging to the Lord black in colour, who pressed down Irāvaṇaṉ first and then granted him boons.
[[The akōra face of Civaṉ is black in colour; moreover as he has Umi on his left half he is named Irāvaṇaṉ (irā+vaṇṇaṉ);
In literature black, blue and green colours are mentioned without much difference.]]

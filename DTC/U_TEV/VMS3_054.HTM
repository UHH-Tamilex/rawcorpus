[[This is the only decade by Campantar which is in two different metres; 1-3 and 4-12]]
{3:54}__1+
{$} antaṇar, vāṉavar, āṉiṉam vāḻka
Let the brahmins, celestials and the herd of cows live long
[[iṉi āvāḻka antaṇar vāḻka eṇpuḻic colluvāṉ oḻinta vilaṅkum oḻinta makkaḷum cāka eṉṉum karuttiṉaṉāyiṉ ivaiyum iṉam ceppuvaṉa aṉṟō eṉpatu (Tolkāppiyam, Collatikāram, Kiḷavi Ākkam, 60, Cēṉāvariyar)]]
taṇpuṉal vīḻka
Let the cool water pour from the clouds.
vēntaṉum ōṅkuka
Let the king become exalted.
tīyatu ellām āḻka
Let all the evils fall down.
araṉ nāmamē cūḻka
Let the name of Civaṉ surround the world.
vaiyakamum tuyar tīrka (ē)
Let the world also be free from sufferings on account of that.
{3:54}__2+
{$} ariya kāṭciyarāy
though Civaṉ is difficult to be seen by those who do not have love.
tamatu aṅkai cēr eriyar, ēṟu ukantu ēṟuvar, kaṇṭamum kariyar
he will appear before sincere devotees in a forms, he will have in his palm fire; will ride with joy on a bull, his neck too is black.
kāṭu uṟai vāḻkkaiyar āyiṉum periyar
he is great though he dwells in the cremation ground.
avar peṟṟi ār aṟivār
who can know his true nature?
{3:54}__3+
{$} venta cāmpal virai eṉap pūciyē
smearing himself with a well-burnt ash as he would smear sandal mixed with perfumes.
tantaiyāroṭu tāy ilar
Civaṉ has no father and mother.
tammaiyē cintiyā eḻuvār viṉai tīrppar (āl)
will remove the sins of those who wake up thinking of him only.
entaiyār avar evvakaiyār kol (ō)
what is the manner of our father!
[[From this verse the second variety of metre begins]]
{3:54}__4+
{$} āti āṭpālavarkku aruḷum vaṇṇamum māṇpum kēṭpāṉpukil aḷavu illai
if anyone questions about the manner in which the first cuse of all things, Civaṉ, grants his grace and [about] his many attributes and greatness; they are innumerable.
kiḷakka vēṇṭā
we need not dilate upon that
entai tāḷ pāl vaṇaṅki
bowing your heads to the feet of our father.
talainiṉṟu
approaching him.
kōṭpālaṉavum viṉaiyum kuṟukāmai takkār ivai kēṭka
Let those who are fit to receive his grace listen to them so that sufferings which cuse pin, and sins which come as the cuse of those.
{3:54}__5+
{$} ētukkaḷālum eṭutta moḻiyālum (1) mikkuc cōtikka vēṇṭā
You need not investigate Civaṉ by statement of reason and by words which are used as illustrations.
eṅkaḷcōti cuṭarviṭṭuḷaṉ
our light divine is brilliant both inside and outside.
mātukkam nīṅkal uṟuvīr maṉam paṟṟi vāḻmiṉ
you live meditating on that light; people who want to be rid of great sufferings!
cātukkaḷ mikkīr
People who are abounding in virtues!
iṟaiyē vantu cārmiṉkaḷ (ē)
approach him at least for a moment!
[[Variant reading: (1) mikac]]
{3:54}__6+
{$} āṭum(m) eṉavum, aruṅkūṟṟam utaittu, vētam pāṭum(m) eṉavum pukaḻ allatu
wise men say that Civaṉ dances, kicks the god of death who is difficult to be warded off and sings the Vētams;
[[Are these acts done by Civaṉ to attin fame or to save souls?]]
nāṭum tiṟattārkkup pukaḻ allatu
if one investigates what is the use of fame to him?
pāvam nīṅkak kēṭum piṟappum(m) aṟukkum(m) eṉakkēṭpīrākil
if you question that he completely roots out the sufferings and births as the sins leave the souls.
aruḷ allatu nāṭṭalāmē
can you establish that there is any other idea except his grace which he bestows without any ulterior motive?
{3:54}__7+
{$} kaṭi cērntapōtu, malarāṉa, kaikkoṇṭu
carrying in his hands fragrant buds and flowers.
nalla paṭi cērnta pāl koṇṭu aṅku āṭṭiṭa
when Civaṉ was bathed with milk which was of good quality.
tātai paṇṭu muṭicērnta kālai aṟa veṭṭiṭa
when Cantēcaṉ severed completely the leg which had joints, of his father, long ago.
mukkaṇ mūrtti aṭicērnta vaṇṇam(m) aṟivār colakkēṭṭum aṉṟē
Have we not heard people with intelligence to say that he reached the feet of Civaṉ who has three eyes
[[cf. araṉ aṭikku aṉpar ceyyum pāvamum aṟam atu ākum; paraṉ aṭikku aṉpu ilātār puṇṇiyam pāvam ākum; varamuṭait takkaṉ ceyta māvēḷvi tīmai āki, narariṉiṟ pālaṉ ceyta pātakam āyttē (Civañāṉacittiyar, Cupakkam, Mutal Atikaraṇam 29)]]
{3:54}__8+
{$} vēta mutalvaṉ mutalāka (1) viḷaṅki vaiyam
as the world is eminent having as its cuse Civaṉ who is the cuse of the vētams.
ulakattavar ētappaṭāmai ēttal ceyya
the people of this world to prise him so that any fault might not occur in their conduct.
pūtamutalvaṉ mutalē mutalāp polinta cūtaṉ oli mālai eṉṟē kalikkōvai collē
the works of Cūtaṉ which proclim Civaṉ all living beings who is the cuse as the supreme god, are the eighteen purāṇam
[[Cūtaṉ was the sage who narrated all the purāṇams to the sages who had gathered in Naimica Vaṉam for a sacrifice]]
[[Variant reading: (1) viḷaṅkivvaiyam]]
{3:54}__9+
{$} pār āḻivaṭṭam pakaiyāl nalintu āṭṭa āṭi
the round world surrounded by the ocean was harassed and put to much suffering and became weak by enemies
pēr āḻiyāṉatu iṭar kaṇṭu
seeing the mental agony of the god who is sleeping on the ocean which was dashing aginst the shore as if engaged in a battle aginst it.
aruḷ ceytal pēṇi
desiring to bestow his grace
nīr āḻiviṭṭu ēṟi neñcu iṭam koṇṭavarkku
to Māl who had Civaṉ in his heart having left the ocean of water and coming to this earth.
pōr āḻi īnta pukaḻum pukaḻuṟṟatu aṉṟē
the famous acts of granting a discus capable of fighting, was prised by all people.
{3:54}__10+
{$} māl āyavaṉum, maṟaivallavaṉ nāṉmukaṉum, pālāyatēvar pakar il amutu ūṭṭal pēṇi
desiring to feed the Tevar who were on their side the nectar which is not sold anywhere Māl and Piramaṉ of four faces who was well-versed in the Vētams
[[āyavaṉ expletive ending of nouns, in the masculine gender singular number in the nominative case]]
kāl āya munnīr kaṭaintārkku aritāy eḻunta ālālam uṇṭu
consuming the ālālam which rose there, whose cruelty was unbearable, by those who churned the ocean which was its source.
amararkku aruḷ ceytatu āmē
that act was of Civaṉ bestowing his grace on the immortals.
{3:54}__11+
{$} aṟṟu aṉṟi
besides that act of grace
am taṇ maturait tokai ākkiṉāṉum
one who founded the Caṅkam in beautiful and cool maturi
(1) teṟṟeṉṟu teyvam teḷiyār
for those people who are not clear about the superiority of Civaṉ to be clear in their minds about that fact.
karaikku ōlai teṇṇīr paṟṟu iṉṟip pāṅku (2) etirviṉ ūravum paṇpu nōkkil
if we think of the fact that the palm leaf in which the verse was written, thrown into the clear water went up-stream safely without being washed away by the water, and reached the bank.
peṟṟu oṉṟu uyarnta perumāṉ perumāṉum aṉṟē
is not the god who held aloft a bull in his flag, a great god?
[[Variant reading: (1) teṟṟeṉṟa (2) etirviṉīravum]]
{3:54}__12+
{$} nallālkaḷ cēr pukali ñāṉacampantaṉ nalla ellārkaḷum paravum īcaṉai ēttu pāṭal
the song with which Ñāṉacampantaṉ of Pukali where good people gather prised the Lord of the universe whom all the good people prise
pallārkaḷum matikkap pācuram coṉṉa pattum
all those which were composed on a sacred poem to be esteemed by all people.
vallārkaḷ
those who are capable of reciting these.
vāṉōr ulaku āḷavum vallar (aṉṟē)
are capable of ruling over the heaven also.
[[Thus ends the Tēvāram of Ñāṉacampantar]]

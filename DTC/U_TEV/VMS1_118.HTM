{1:118}__1+
{$} cuṭumaṇi umiḻnākam cūḻtara araikku acaittāṉ
Civaṉ tied round his waist a cobra that spits a gem which emits rays.
iṭumaṇi eḻil āṉai ēṟalaṉ erutu ēṟi
riding on a bull without riding on a beautiful elephant tied with bells that produce a sound;
[[PP: piṭimata vāraṇam pēṇum turakam niṟkap periya iṭikural veḷḷai eruḻu ēṟum itu eṉṉai kol em iṟaiyē;
(Nāvukkaracar, nākikkārōṇam, tiruviruttam (1) 1)]]
[[PP: ayirāvaṇam ēṟātu āṉ ēṟu ēṟi
Nāvukkaracar, Tiruvārūr (13) 1)]]
viṭam aṇi miṭaṟu uṭaiyāṉ mēviya neṭuṅ kōṭṭup paṭumaṇi viṭucuṭar ār paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise the paruppatam which has lustre given by the big gems that lie in the high peak, where Civaṉ who has a neck beautified by the poison, dwells with desire.
{1:118}__2+
{$} nōy pulku tōl tiraiya
when the skin which is attached to diseases shrinkles.
narai varum nukar uṭampil nīpulku tōṟṟam ellām niṉai
think of all your appearances with which you are attached in this body which enjoys sensual pleasures, when grey hirs appear.
uḷkum maṭa neñcē
my ignorant mind which thinks of ever so many things!
vāy pulku tōttirattāl valam ceytu talaivaṇaṅki
bowing your head, going from left to right; with our mouth full of his prises.
pāypulittōl uṭaiyāṉ paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise the paruppatam of Civaṉ who wears as dress the skin of a tiger that pounces on its prey. (pāypuli the underlined epithet denotes its action when it was alive)
{1:118}__3+
{$} tuṉi uṟu tuyartīrat tōṉṟi
having born to be removed from the sufferings which give you disgust.
ōr nalviṉaiyāl iṉi uṟu payaṉ ātal iraṇṭu uṟamaṉam vaiyēl
(my mind!) do not be double-minded as upi cam get a good benefit by a virtuous act done previously;
iraṇṭuṟa maṉam vaiyēl
verse no.6,
[[ivar tēvar avar tēvar eṉṟu colli iraṇṭāṭṭātu oḻintu īcaṉ tiṟamē pēṇi (nāvukkaracar, Kaṉṟāppūr, taṇṭakam 3)]]
kaṉi uṟu maram ēṟik karumucukkaḻai ukaḷum paṉi uṟu katir matiyāṉ paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise parupatam of Civaṉ who sears a crescent of cool rays, where the langur climbs on the fruit trees and leaps towards the bamboo.
{1:118}__4+
{$} koṅku aṇi naṟuṅkoṉṟait toṅkalaṉ
Civaṉ wears a garland of fragrant koṉṟi which has honey.
kuḷircaṭaiyāṉ
has a cool caṭi.
eṅkaḷ nōy akala niṉṟāṉ eṉa aruḷ īcaṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who is prised as the god who remins to remove our diseases
aiṅkaṇai varicilaiyāṉ anaṅkaṉai aḻaku aḻitta paiṅkaṇ veḷḷēṟu uṭaiyāṉ paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise the paruppatam of Civaṉ who has a white bull of tender eyes and who destroyed the beauty of Anaṅkam who has five arrows and a bow of many bandages
[[Anaṅkaṉ is not used in its derivative sense.]]
{1:118}__5+
{$} tuṟai pala cuṉai (1) mūḻkit tūmalarc cumantu ōṭi maṟai oli vāymoḻiyāl vāṉavar makiḻntu ētta
to be prised with joy by the celestials who bathe in the mountin springs which have many ghats running carrying flawless flowers, and chanting the true words of the Vētam-s according to their accent.
ciṟai oli kiḷi payilum tēṉiṉam oli ōvā
where the noise made by the wings of the parrots and the noise made by the bees are previling always.
paṟaipaṭu viḷaṅku aruvip paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise the Paruppatam of shining streams which make a sound like the sound of the drum,

{1:118}__6+
{$} ēr keḻu maṭaneñcē
my ignorant mind which is very active!
cīrkeḻu ciṟappu ovvāc ceytava neṟi vēṇṭil
if you wish for the path of penance which is never separated from eminence and fame.
iraṇṭu maṉam vaiyēl
do not be double minded (see verse no.3)
kārkeḻu naṟuṅkoṉṟaik kaṭavuḷatu (1) iṭam vakaiyāl, pārkeḻu pukaḻ ōvāp paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise paruppatam whose many kinds of fame in this world never ceases; which is the place of the god who wears fragrant koṉṟi which blossoms profusely in the appropriate season of winter.
[[Variant reading: (1) iṭavakaiyāl]]
{1:118}__7+
{$} puṭai pulku paṭar kamalam pukaiyoṭu viraikamaḻat toṭai pulku naṟumālai tirumuṭimicai ēṟa viṭaipulku koṭi ēnti
holding a flag on which the form of a bull is drawn, the fully-blossomed lotus which has spreading petals incense other fragrant substances and fragrant garlands which are fastened to be pleased on the holy head.
venta veṇṇīṟu aṇivāṉ paṭaipulku maḻuvāḷaṉ paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise paruppatam of Civaṉ who has the weapon of a battle-axe and who smears well-burnt white sacred ash.
{1:118}__8+
{$} niṉaippu eṉum neṭuṅkiṇaṟṟai niṉṟu niṉṟu ayarātē maṉattiṉai valittu oḻintēṉ
I definitely decided in my mind without confusion, standing for a long time near the deep well of thoughts.
avalam vantu (1) aṭaiyāmai
in order that sufferings might not reach me.
(2) kaṉaittu eḻutiraḷ kaṅkai kamaḻ caṭaikkarattāṉ taṉ paṇaittiraḷ pāy aruvip paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise paruppatam which has streams in which collection of fish, by name paṉi leap; belonging to Civaṉ who concealed on his fragrant caṭi Kanki, a collection of water which came with a great noise.
[[Variant readings: (1) vantaṭaiyāmal (2) kaṉatteḻu]]
{1:118}__9+
{$} maruviya valviṉai nōy avalam vantu aṭaiyāmal
in order that sufferings that are the fruits of the irresistible acts to which we are accustomed and become prostrate.
tiruuru amarntāṉum ticaimukam uṭaiyāṉum
Māl who has tiru on his chest and Piramaṉ who has four faces.
iruvarum (1) aṟiyāmai eḻuntatu ōr erinaṭuvē paruvarai uṟa nimirntāṉ paruppatam paravutumē
we shall prise paruppatam of Civaṉ who grew erect like a big mountin in the midst of the fire which rose high so that both of them could not know the head and feet.
[[Variant reading: (1) aṟiyāmal eḻunta]]

{$}
(People of this world!)
caṭam koṇṭa cāttirattār cākkiyar camaṇ kuṇṭar maṭam koṇṭa (1) virumpiyarāy
having the desire of possessing ignorance by the cākkiyar and the low camaṇar who have deceptive religious works.
mayaṅki

ōr pēyt tērppiṉ kuṭam koṇṭu nīrkkuc celvār pōtumiṉ
those who want to draw water from the mirage taking a pot, go,
[[cf. pūttu ārum poykaip puṉal ituvē eṉak karutip pēyttēr mukakkuṟum pētai kuṇam ākātē (tiruvācakam, tiruttoṇōkkam, 1)]]
kuñcarattiṉ paṭam koṇṭa pōrvaiyiṉāṉ paruppatam paravutumē
we shall worship paruppatam of Civaṉ who has covered himself with the cloth of a skin flayed from an elephant.
[[Variant reading: (1) virumpiyarāy]]
{1:118}__11+
{$} veṇ cenel viḷai kaḻaṉi viḻavu oli kaḻumalattāṉ
the native of Kaḻumalam which has the bustle of festivals and fields in which white paddy and red paddy are produced.
paṇ celap pāṭal icai mural paruppatattai
on paruppatam where music is sung along with songs set to the tune of melody types.
naṉ coliṉāl paravum (1) ñāṉacampantaṉ nalla oṇcoliṉ ivai mālai uru eṇat tavam ām
it is equal to penance if one repeats many times these verses which were composed using beautiful and good words by ñāṉacampantaṉ who prises god with good words.

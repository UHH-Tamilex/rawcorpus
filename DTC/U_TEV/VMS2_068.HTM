{2:68}__1+
{$} vāṉ amar tiṅkaḷum nīrum maruviya vārcaṭaiyāṉai
Civaṉ who has combined on his long caṭi a crescent that is the sky, and water
tēṉ amar koṉṟaiyiṉāṉai
who adorns himself with the flowers of koṉṟi having honey
tēvar toḻappaṭuvāṉai
who is worshipped by the tēvar
kāṉ amarum piṇai pulkik kalai payilum kaṭampūril
in Kaṭampūr, where there are stags which embrace their deer which are fond of the forest.
tāṉ amar koḷkaiyiṉāṉait tāḷ toḻa
to worship the feet of the god who acts according to his own ideas.
vīṭu eḷitu ām
it is easy to attin eternal bliss.
{2:68}__2+
{$} araviṉoṭu āmaiyum pūṇṭu am tukil vēṅkai ataḷum viravum tiru muṭi taṉ mēl veṇ tiṅkaḷ cūṭi virumpip paravum taṉikkaṭampūril
in the pearless Kaṭampūr where the god who has borne on his head a white crescent along with the other things and on whose body a beautiful cloth, the skin of a tiger, to worn and having adorned himself with cobras and the shell of a tortoise, stays desirous of that place
paiṅkaṇ veḷḷēṟṟu aṇṇal pātam iravum pakalum paṇiya iṉpam namakku atu ām
if we worship the feet of the chief who has a white bull of greenish eyes, night and day, that itself is happiness to us.
{2:68}__3+
{$} iḷipaṭu 1. iṉ coliṉārkaḷ iruṅ kuḻal mēl icaintu ēṟatteḷipaṭukoḷkai kalanta tīt toḻilār kaṭampūril
in Kaṭampūr where the brahmins of clear doctrines assemble and mintin the fires; the smoke rising from them settles without any difference on the dark tresses of hir of the ladies whose words are as sweet as the iḷi of the seven notes.
oḷitaru veṇ piṟai cūṭi oṇṇutalōṭu uṭaṉākip puliyataḷ āṭai puṉaintāṉ poṟkaḻal nām pōṟṟutum
we shall worship the beautiful feet of the god who adorned himself with the tiger`s skin as a dress; being himself present with Umi of bright forehead, and wearing on his caṭi a shining white crescent
[[(iḷipaṭum iṉ coliṉārkaḷ : iḷipaṭutīñcol iḷamā eyiṟṟi (puṟapporuḷveṇpāmāli, 15)]]
[[Variant reading: 1. meṉ coliṉār]]
{2:68}__4+
{$} paṟaiyoṭu caṅkam iyampa
when the drums are beaten and conches are blown
pal koṭi cēr neṭi māṭam kaṟaiyuṭai vēl neṭuṅkaṇṇār kalai oli cēr kaṭam pūril
in Kaṭampūr where in the tall storeys on which there are fixed many flags, the sound of the mēkali worn on the waist by the ladies who have long eyes like the blood-stined vel, combines together when they dance
(1) maṟai oli kūṭiya pāṭal maruvi niṉṟu āṭal makiḻum piṟaiyuṭai vārcaṭaiyāṉaip pēṇavallār periyōrē
they who can worship with love Civaṉ who has on his long caṭi a crescent and who rejoices dancing combining with the songs which bland with the sound of the vētam, are really greatpeople.
[[Variant reading: (1) maṟaiyoṭu]]
{2:68}__5+
{$} tī viriyak kaḻal ārppa
the cati which is like fire to spread in all directions and the anklets to make sound (tī refers to caṭi which is red like fire)
cē yeri koṇṭu iṭukāṭṭil
in the burial ground holding a red fire
nāviri kūntal naṟ pēykaḷ nakai ceyya naṭṭam naviṉ ṟōṉ
Civaṉ danced when the good pēys were lughing, which had hanging tongues and spreading tresses of hir
naṟ pēykaḷ
is ironical,
[[eḻil cey kūkai (cīvaka cintāmani, 102);
eḻil cey ikaḻccik kuṟippu- (Do. commentary)]]
kā viri koṉṟai kalanta kaṇṇutalāṉ kaṭam pūril
in Kaṭampūr belonging to Civaṉ who adorns himself with koṉṟi flowers blossomed in the parks.
pāviri pāṭal payilvār paḻi poṭu pāvam ilar
those who always sing the verses whose sound travels to a great distance, are without blemish and sin.
{2:68}__6+
{$} taṇ puṉal nīḷ vayal tōṟum tāmaraimēl aṉam vaika
when the swan is seated on the lotus flower is every long field which has cool water
kaṇpuṇar kāvil vaṇṭu ēṟat kaḷ aviḻum kaṭampūril
in Kaṭampūr where honey is released from the parks which attract the eyes as bees sit upon them and hum
peṇpuṉai kūṟu uṭaiyāṉai
Civaṉ who has a portion of his body in which there is a lady
piṉṉu caṭaip perumāṉai
who has an intertwining caṭi
paṇpuṉai pāṭal payilvār
those who prise him often songs made beautiful by melodies
pāvam ilātavar (tāmē)
have so sins
{2:68}__7+
{$} palikeḻu cemmalar cāra
when flawless flowers are got for worship
pāṭaloṭu āṭal aṟāta kali keḻu vīti kalanta kār vayal cūḻ kaṭampūril
in Kaṭampūr surrounded by black fields, in which there are streets of great bustle as songs and dances are never ceasing
olikeḻu kaṅkai karantāṉ
Civaṉ who concealed Kaṅki of greag sound
oṇṇutalāḷ umai kēḷvaṉ
who is the husband of Umi who has a bright forehead
puli ataḷ āṭaiyiṉāṉ taṉ puṉaikaḻal pōṟṟutal poruḷē
prising the feet of the god who dressed himself with a tiger`s skin, is the worthy object of being born as men.
{2:68}__8+
{$} pūmpaṭukil kayal pāya
when the carp jumps in the tanks
puṟaṅkāṭṭil puḷ iriya
and the birds fly in a disorderly manner in the cremation ground
kāmpu aṭu tōḷiyar nāḷum kaṇkavarum kaṭam pūril
in Kaṭampūr where the ladies whose shoulders are superior to the bamboo captivate the eyes.
mēm paṭu tēvi ōr pākam mēvi emmāṉ eṉa vāḻtit, tēmpaṭu māmalar tūvit ticai toḻattiya keṭumē
all evil things will perish, if we worship towards the direction in which Kaḷampūr is situated by scattering flowers having honey, and prising him as our Lord who wished to have as his half a pre-eminent tēvi (The meaning of the first two lines is not as clear as it should be)
{2:68}__9+
{$} tirumaru mārpil avaṉum tikaḻtaru mā mala rōṉum iruvarumāy aṟivu oṇṇā eri uru ākiya īcaṉ
Civaṉ who assumed the form of fire which could not be known by both Māl on whose chest tiru is residing and Piramaṉ who is seated on a bright (lotus) flower
karuvarai kālil aṭartta kaṇṇutalāṉ kaṭampūril
in Kaṭampūr belonging to Civaṉ who crushed with his leg (Irāvaṇaṉ) who was like a black mountin
maruviya pāṭal payilvār vāṉulakam peṟuvarē
Those who often recite the verses which are combined with music will attin heaven

{2:68}__10+
{$} āṭaitavirttu aṟam kāṭṭumavarkaḷum
the jins who preach morality, discarding dress
amtuvar āṭaic cōṭaikaḷ naṉṉeṟi collār
and the followers of buddha who wear robes soaked in myrtle dye and who are persons of poor understanding cannot explin the spiritual path
colliṉum col alakaṇṭīr
even if they explins that it s not true.
vēṭam palapala kāṭṭum vikirtaṉ nam vētam mutalvaṉ
our god who is the origin of vētam and who shows many different forms and who is different forms this world
kāṭu ataṉil naṭam āṭum kaṇṇutalāḷ kaṭampūrē
Kaṭampūr is the shrine of the god, Civaṉ, who dances in the cremation ground
{2:68}__11+
{$} veṇ koṭicēr neṭumāṭam kaṭai navilum kaṭampūril kātalaṉai
on Civaṉ who is very much attached to Kaṭampūr where in the entrances to tall storeys where white flag is fixed.
kaṭaṟ kāḻi naṭai navil ñāṉa campantaṉ naṉmaiyāl ēttiya paṭai navil pāṭal pattum payilvār
Those who are able to recite often all the ten verses composed as aids to attin divine grace and prised auspiciously by ñāṉcampantaṉ who pursued the spiritual path, in Kāḻi proximate to the sea.
(paṭainavil pāṭam
verses which can be used as weapon to destroy evil)
paḻiyoṭupāvam ilar
will have no blemish and sins.

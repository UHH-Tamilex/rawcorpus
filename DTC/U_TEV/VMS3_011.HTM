{3:11}__1+
{$} (1) miṉ iyal ceñcaṭai veṇpiṟaiyaṉ
Civaṉ wears a white crescent on the red caṭi which has the qualities of lightning.
virinūliṉaṉ
wears a loosened sacred thread on his chest.
paṉṉiya nāṉmaṟai pāṭi āṭip pala ūrkaḷ pōy
going to many places dancing and singing the four vētams and repeating them often.
aṉṉam aṉṉa(n) (2) naṭaiyāḷoṭu(m) amarum(m) iṭam
the place where he stays with a lady whose git is comparable to that of the swan.
puṉṉai nalmāmalar poṉ utirkkum puṉa vāyil (ē)
is Puṉavāyil where the good big flowers of the mast-wood trees shed pollen which is golden in colour.
[[Variant reading: (1) miṉṉiya (2) naṭaiyāḷoṭumē]]
{3:11}__2+
{$} vīṇṭavartam puram mūṉṟu erittu
burning the three cities of the enemies.
viṭai ēṟip pōy
going out ruding on a bull.
vaṇṭu amarum kuḻal maṅkaiyoṭum makiḻntāṉ iṭam
the place where Civaṉ stayed with pleasure with a lady of distinction on whose tresses of hir bees settle.
kaṇṭalum ñāḻalum niṉṟu peruṅkaṭal kāṉal vāyap 1. puṇṭarīkam malarp poykai cūḻanta puṉavāyil (ē)
is puṉavāyil which is surrounded by natural tanks where lotus flowers grow, and where in the sea-shore garden near the big ocean fragrant screwpine and fetid cassia stand.
[[Variant reading: 1. puṇṭarikam malar poykai]]
{3:11}__3+
{$} viṭai uṭai(ya) vel koṭi ēntiṉāṉum
Civaṉ who holds a victorious flag of a bull.
viṟal pāriṭam puṭai paṭa āṭiya vēṭattāṉum
who has a form with which he danced, the strong pūtams to surround him.
puṉavāyilil toṭai navil koṉṟai am tāriṉāṉum
who wears a garland of koṉṟi fastened together into a garland, and who is in puṉavāyil.
veṇmaḻuppaṭai valaṉ ēntiya pāl ney āṭum paramaṉ (aṉṟē)
is the supreme god who bathes in milk and ghee and holds in the right hand a white weapon of a battle-axe.
{3:11}__4+
{$} caṅka veṇtōṭu aṇikātiṉāṉum
Civaṉ who wears in his ear a white men's ear-ring made of conch.
caṭaitāḻavē
the cati to hang low.
aṅkai ilaṅku aḻal ēntiṉāṉum
who held in his palm fire which is shining.
aḻakākavē poṅku 1. aravam(m) aṇi mārpiṉāṉum
and who wore on his chest angry cobras, to appear beautiful.
puṉavāyilil paiṅkaṇ veḷ ēṟṟu aṇṇal āki niṉṟa paramēṭṭi (ē)
is the god in the most exalted place and the deity in puṉavāyil having a white bull which has tender eyes.
[[Variant reading: 1. aravum]]
{3:11}__5+
{$} kali paṭu taṇ kaṭal nañcam uṇṭa kaṟaikkaṇṭaṉum
Civaṉ who has a black neck as a result of consuming the poison which rose in teh cool ocean which is always roaring.
puli ataḷ pāmpu araic cuṟṟiṉāṉum
who wound round his waist a tiger's skin and a cobra.
oli taru taṇpuṉalōṭu erukkum matamattamum melitaru veṇpiṟai cūṭip puṉavāyilil niṉṟa viṭai ūrti (ē)
is the god who has a vehicle of a bull and who is permanent in puṉavāyil having worn flowers of yarcum, datura flowers, cool water which is always producing a sound and a waning white crescent.
{3:11}__6+
{$} vār uṟu meṉ mulai maṅkai pāṭa naṭam āṭip pōyk kār uṟu koṉṟai veṇtiṅkaḷāṉum
Civaṉ who wears koṉṟi which blossoms in winter and a crescent, and going dancing when the lady of distinction who has soft breasts on which a bodice is worn, sings.
kaṉal vāyatu ōr pōr uṟu veṇmaḻu ēntiṉāṉum
who held in his hand a white battle-axe fit for fighting which has an edge spitting fire.
cīr uṟu celvam malka, puṉavāyilil irunta civalōkaṉ (ē)
is the Lord of Civalokam who is in puṉavāyil for the eminent wealth to increase.
{3:11}__7+
{$} peruṅkaṭal nañcu amutu uṇṭu ukantu
becoming great by consuming the poison that rose in the big ocean, as nectar.
peruṅkāṭṭiṭait tiruntu iḷameṉmulait tēvi pāṭa naṭam āṭip pōy
having gone dancing in the big cremation ground when the goddess, pārvati, who has immature and soft breasts of perfect form, sings.
poruntalartam puram mūṉṟum eytu
shooting an arrow on all three cities of the enemies.
puṉavāyilil iruntavaṉtaṉ kaḻal ettuvārkaṭku iṭar illai(ē)
there will no sufferings to those who prise the feet of Civaṉ who remins in puṉavāyil.
{3:11}__8+
{$} maṉam miku vēlaṉ
one who had zeal and possessed a weapon like vēl etc.
avvāḷ arakkaṉ vali olkiṭa
the strength of that cruel arakkaṉ to be reduced.
vaṉam miku māl varaiyāl aṭarttāṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who pressed him down by the big mountin which had many forests.
maṉṉiya iṉam miku tol pukaḻ pāṭal āṭal eḻil malkiya, puṉam miku koṉṟai am teṉṟal ārnta puṉavāyil (ē)
is puṉavāyil which is filled with the south breeze which enters into and comes out of, the koṉṟi trees in the sylvan region, and which has flourishing beauty, owing to singing many kinds of ancient fame and dancing (out of joy by devotees); (malkiya puṉavāyil, teṉṟal ārnta puṉavāyil is the syntactical link.)
{3:11}__9+
{$} tiruvaḷar tāmarai mēviṉāṉum tikaḻ pāṟkaṭal karuniṟa vaṇṇaṉum kāṇpu ariya kaṭavuḷ(ḷ) iṭam
the place of the god who could not be seen by Piramaṉ who sits on a lotus of fascinating beauty, and Māl who has black colour and who sleeps on the shining ocean of milk.
naral ari 1. caṅkoṭum ippi unti(n) nalam malkiya porukaṭal veṇtirai vantu eṟiyum puṉavāyil (ē)
is puṉavāyil where the white waves in the sea which has increasing good things, dashe aginst the shore pushing oysters which are spiralling and making a sound and throw them on the shore.
[[Variant reading: 1. caṅkamoṭippi]]
{3:11}__10+
{$} pōti eṉap peyar āyiṉārum
buddhists who derive their name from the wood `pōti'
poṟi il camaṇ cāti uraippaṉa koṇṭu ayarntu taḷarvu eytaṉ miṉ
(Poeple of this world!) do not become depressed in spirit and be weary taking into your mind the preaching of the clan of camanar who has no good fortune.
pōtu aviḻ taṇ poḻil malkum amtaṇ puṉavāyilil vētaṉai nāḷtoṟum ēttu vārmēl vīṭum (ē)
the acts good and bad of those who prise dily Civaṉ who is the meaning of the vētams and who dwells in beautiful and cool puṉavāyil where cool gardens in which buds unfold their petals are flourishing will vanish.
{3:11}__11+
{$} poṉ toṭiyāṉ umai paṅkaṉ mēvum puṉavāyilai
on puṉavayil where Civaṉ who has Umi who wears golden bangles on one half.
kaṟṟavartām toḻutu ētta niṉṟa kaṭaṟkāḻiyāṉ naṟṟamiḻ ñāṉacampantaṉ coṉṉa tamiḻ naṉmaiyāl aṟṟam il pāṭal pattu ētta vallār aruḷ cērvar (ē)
those who are able to prise Civaṉ with the ten verses which are without poverty of ideas and are composed in refined tamiḻ by Ñāṉacampantaṉ who is well-versed in refined Tamiḻ and who is a native of Kāḻi surrounded by the sea and who is prised and worshipped with joined hands by learned people, will receive the grace of god.

[[This shrine is now known as Viruttācalam]]
{1:12}__1+
{$} mattāvarai niṟuvi
having fixed the mountin as the churning staff.
kaṭal kaṭainta(v) viṭam uṇṭa tottu ārmaṇi nīḷmuṭiccuṭarvaṇṇaṇatu iṭam ām
the place of the god who has the colour of the sun and who wears a tall crown in which there is a group of gems set in it and who consumed the poison that rose as a result of churning the ocean
[[kaṭaintavviṭam: the underlined letter is augmentation for rhythm]]
kottu ārmalar kuḷir cantu akil oḷir kuṅkumam koṇṭu muttāṟu vantu aṭivīḻtarumutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm (ē)
we shall reach Mutukuṉṟu where muttaṟu falls at the feet of Civaṉ bringing as tribute bunches of flower, cool sandal wood, eagle wood and bright saffron.
[[iṭam ām mutukuṉṟu is the syntactical link; this applies to the following verses.]]
{1:12}__2+
{$} taḻaiār vaṭaviṭavītaṉil tavamēpuri caivaṉ
the cataciva manifestation of Civaṉ, as one without beginning, who performed penance under the banyan tree which is full of foliage, as takkiṇāmūrṭy.
[[viyavi: the reading found in printed editions, even in the tarumapuram edition, does not make any sense. viṭavi has been lengthened into viṭavī for rhythm; the derivative meaning of the word is a tree with branches.]]
iḻai ār iṭai maṭavāḷoṭum iṉitā uṟaivu iṭam ām
the place where he dwells with pleasure with a lady whose waist is as minute as a single twisted thread.
maḻai vāṉiṭai muḻava(v) eḻil vaḷai vāḷ ukir erikaṇ muḻaivāḷ ari kumiṟum(m) uyar mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm (ē)
we shall reach mutukuṉṟu where the cruel lion which lives in the cave and has beautiful, bright nils and burning eyes, roars aginst the thunder of the cloud in the sky. [[thinking it to be the roaring of the elephant]]
[[muḻava a verb coined from the noun muḻavu]]
{1:12}__3+
{$} viḷaiyātatu ōr paricil varu pacupāca vētaṉai oṇtaḷai āyiṉatavira (v) aruḷ talaiva(ṉ) atu cārpu ām
the place of the master who grants his grace to remove the strong bondages which give sufferings and have been destroyed so that they will have the nature of not bearing any fruit agin.
kaḷai ārtaru katir āyiram uṭaiya(v) avaṉōṭu muḷai mā mati tavaḻum(m) uyar mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm(ē)
we shall reach the great mutukuṉṟu where the great moon just rising seems to crawl along with the sun of thousand rays of brilliance.
{1:12}__4+
{$} curar mātavar toku kiṉṉarar avarō tolaivu illar
the tēvar, great sages and groups of countless Kiṉṉaṟar.
narar āṉa pal muṉivar toḻa iruntāṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who remined to be worshipped by many sages among human beings.
nalam ār (1) aracārvara aṇi poṟkalam (avai) koṇṭu pal nāḷum muracu ār varu maṇam moympu uṭai(ya) mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm (ē)
we shall reach mutukuṉṟu which has the strength of the muracu which is played on occasions of marriages on many days when kings of good qualities visit the shrine with beautiful golden ornaments.
[[Variant reading:(1) aracārāvaṇi]]
{1:12}__5+
{$} aṟai ārkaḻal antaṉtaṉai ayil mūvilai aḻaku ārkaṟai ār neṭuvēliṉmicai ēṟṟāṉ iṭam karutiṉ
if we think of the place of Civaṉ who lifted upon the long trident which has stins and beauty, and three sharp blades, the acuraṉ, antakaṉ who wore a sounding Kaḻal in his leg.
maṟai āyiṉa palacolli oṇ malar cāntu avai koṇṭu
taking in their hands bright flowers and sandal paste, and reciting vētam.
miku muṉivar muṟaiyāl toḻum mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm (ē)
we shall reach mutukuṉṟu which many sages worship by turns.
{1:12}__6+
{$} ē ārcilai eyiṉaṉ(ṉ) uru āki eḻil vicayaṟku, ōvāta iṉ aruḷ ceyta em(m) oruvaṟku iṭam
the place of the unequalled god who granted his good grace which does not cease, to beautiful Vicayaṉ, assuming the form of a hunter who had a bow in which arrows are fixed.
ulakil
in this world.
cāvātavar
those who do not die.
piṟavātavar
and those who are not born agin.
tavamē mika uṭaiyar
have excessive penance only to their credit.
mūvāta pal muṉivar toḻum mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm(ē)
we shall reach mutukuṉṟu which the many sages who do not become old, worship.
{1:12}__7+
{$} taḻal cēr taru tirumēṉiyar
Civaṉ has a body whose colour is like the fire.
cacicērcaṭaimuṭiyar
has a caṭi coiled into a crown on which he places a crescent.
maḻamālviṭai mikaēṟiya maṟaiyōṉ uṟaikōyil
the temple where the brahmin who rides on a young and big bull dwells.
viḻavōṭu oli miku maṅkaiyar takum āṭakacālai muḻavōṭu icai naṭammuṉ ceyum mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm (ē)
we shall reach mutukuṉṟu where the young girls who make a big sound along with festivals perform dance in the fitting golden hall to the accompaniment of muḻavu to measure time.
{1:12}__8+
{$} cetuvāymaikaḷ karuti(v)varai eṭutta tiṟal arakkaṉ katuvāykaḷ pattu alaṟīyiṭak kaṇṭāṉ uṟaikōyil
the temple where Civaṉ who made the strong arakkaṉ who lifted the mountin thinking of evil words, to roar with his ten mouths which were scarred, dwells
[[the longthening of alaṟi into alaṟī is for rhythm.]]
matuvāy ceṅkāntaḷ malar niṟaiyak (1) kuṟaivu illā mutuvēykaḷ muttu utirum poḻil mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm (ē)
we shall reach mutukuṉṟu where the mature bamboos which have no deficiency of any kind, drop pearls to fill the flowers of red species of malabar glory lily which have honey in them.
[[Variant reading: (1) kuṟaiyillā]]
{1:12}__9+
{$} iyal āṭiyapiramaṉ, ari iruvarkku (um) aṟivu ariya ceyal āṭiya tī ār uru āki eḻu celvaṉ
the god who rose as a column of fire which won a victory by its act and who could not be known by both Piramaṉ and ari (Māl) who were self-conceited.
puyal āṭu vaṇpoḻilcūḻ puṉaṟ paṭappait taṭattu arukē muyal ōṭa veṇkayal pāytaru mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm(ē)
we shall reach mutukuṉṟu where by the side of the tanks where water is extensive and surrounded by flourishing gardens on which clouds are moving, white carp fish jumps to make the hares run.
{1:12}__10+
{$} arukaroṭu puttar (avar) aṟiyā araṉ
araṉ who could not be known by arukar (camaṇar) and buddhists.
malaiyāṉ marukaṉ
the son in law of the himālayā mountin.
varum iṭapakkoṭi uṭaiyāṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who has a flag of bull which comes in the front.
malar ār karuku kuḻal maṭavār kaṭikuṟiñci (atu) pāṭi
the girls of the hunters who have tresses of hir of increasing black colour and flowers tucked in, sing the divine melody-type Kuṟiñci.
muruka(ṉ)ṉatu perumai pakar mutukuṉṟu aṭaivōm (ē)
we shall reach Mutukuṉṟu where the greatness of Murukaṉ is narrated by them.
{1:12}__11+
{$} mukilcērtaru mutukuṉṟu uṭaiyāṉai
on Civaṉ who has mutukuṉṟu where clouds gather, as his abode.
miku tolcīrp pukalin nakar maṟaiñāṉa campantaṉ(ṉ) uraiceyta nikar illaṉa tamiḻ mālaikaḷ ivaipattum
all these ten garlands of tamiḻ verses which have no equal composed by ñāṉacampantaṉ who had knowledge of vētam and who is a native of the city of pukali which has ancient fame increasing day by day.
avaiyōṭu pakarum aṭiyavarkaṭku iṭar pāvam (m) aṭaiyā (ē)
sufferings and sins will not reach the devotees who recite these with music.

{3:82}__1+
{$} kompu iriya vaṇṭu ulavu koṉṟai purinūloṭu kulāvi
the flowers of koṉṟi which the bees cuse to leave their branches, come back agin and surround them to shine along with the sacred thread of three strands.
tam pariciṉōṭu cuṭunīṟu taṭavantu
smearing himself with the sacred ash well-burnt, as an indication of his nature.
iṭapam ēṟi
riding on a bull
kam periya cempoṉ neṭu māṭamatil kalvaraivillāka ampu eriya eyta perumāṉ uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr(ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the place where the god, Civaṉ, who discharged an arrow to set fire to the forts which had tall storeys and were made of superior gold, (silver and iron) and which had high workmanship.
[[poṉ includes silver and iron, this is called up a lakkanam in tamiḻ grammar; ie; implying something that has not been made explicit by expressing another thing associated or connected with it]]
{3:82}__2+
{$} īmam ericūḻ cuṭalaivācamutukāṭu naṭam āṭi
dancing in the old cremation ground having it, as his dwelling place where corpses are burnt and funeral-pyres and fire are found everywhere,
ōmaiyaṉa kaḷḷiyaṉa vākaiyaṉa kūkaimural ōcai
and the crying sound of the rock horned owl which lives in the tooth brush tree, spurge and sirissa trees, can be heard.
tūymai uṭai(ya) akkoṭu aravam viravi mikku oḷi tulaṅka
the cobra being combined with the pure chank beads to shed a very brilliant light.
āmaiyoṭu pūṇum aṭikaḷ(ḷ) uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr (ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the dwelling place of the god who adorns himself with the shell of a tortoise.
[[ōṭu shell of the tortoise; ōṭu has been shortened to suit rhythm]]
{3:82}__3+
{$} nīṟuuṭaiya mārpil imavāṉ makaḷōr pākam nilaiceytu
fixing permanently as a half the dughter of Imavāṉ (the himalayas) on his chest which is smeared with sacred ash;
[[vil niṟavāḷ nutal vēl niṟakkaṇ melliyalai mallal taṉ niṟam oṉṟil irutti niṉṟōṉ (Kōviyār, 58)]]
kūṟu uṭaiya vēṭamoṭu kūṭi aḻaku āyatu orukōlum ēṟu uṭaiyarēṉum
through Civaṉ had a body which was beautiful and superior to all others, by combining with the form in which there is the male half.
iṭukāṭu iravil niṉṟu naṭam āṭum āṟu uṭaiya vārcaṭaiyiṉāṉ uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr (ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the abode of Civaṉ who has long caṭi on which there is water, and who dances without ceasing in the burial ground at night.
{3:82}__4+
{$} piṇiyum ilar, kēṭum ilar, tōṟṟam ilar eṉṟu ulakupēṇip paṇiyum aṭiyārkaḷaṉapāvam aṟa iṉṉaruḷ payantu
granting the sweet grace by removing the sins of the devotees who pay homage to Civaṉ with love in this world, thinking that he has no diseases, neither death nor birth.
tuṇiyuṭaiya tōlum uṭai kōvaṇamum nākam (um) uṭal toṅka
the skin and the men's loin cloth which was torn from a big cloth, and the cobra to hang low in his form.
aṇiyum aḻakāka uṭaiyāṉ uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr(ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the abode of Civaṉ who has ornaments beautifying him.
{3:82}__5+
{$} kuḻaliṉ varivaṇṭu mural melliyaṉa poṉ malarkaḷ koṇṭu kaḻaliṉ micai iṇṭai puṉaivār kaṭavuḷ eṉṟu amararkūṭi
the immortals join together and adorn his legs with a circlets of flowers made with soft flowers of this world on which the bees with lines hum like the music of flute, and the golden flowers of the heaven thinking that Civaṉ is the supreme.
toḻalum vaḻipāṭum uṭaiyār
they have their worship with joined hands and adoration.
tuyarum nōyum ilar āvar
they do not know mental sufferings and diseases of the body.
aḻalum maḻu ēntu kaiyiṉāṉ uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr (ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the abode of Civaṉ who holds in his hand a burning red-hot iron.
{3:82}__6+
{$} tuñcal ilar āy amarar toḻutu ētta aruḷ ceytu
bestowing his grace on the immortals who worship with joined hands and prise Civaṉ, without remining lazy.
nañcu uṇṭu miṭaṟu karitāya (nampaṉ)
the neck to become black by consuming the poison.
veḷiṟu āki oru nampaṉ
Civaṉ whose other parts of the body are white in colour.
[[ākiyoru-ākiya oru]]
mañcu uṟa nimirntu
growing tall to touch the sky.
umai naṭuṅka
to make Umi tremble with fear.
matavēḻamañca
the elephant with must to be stricken with fear.
akalattoṭu vaḷāvi uriyāṉ uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr (ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the abode of Civaṉ who covers his back along with the chest with its skin.
{3:82}__7+
{$} kūṭu aravam montai kuḻal yāḻ muḻaviṉōṭum icai ceyya
when monti, flute, yāḻ and muḻavu are played in harmony with one another.
[[montai a musical instrument]]
pīṭar
has the greatness of dancing.
avam ākupaṭar ampuceytu
using a running arrow which lid waste the three cities.
pēr iṭapamōṭum kāṭu aravam ākukaṉal koṇṭu iravil niṉṟu naṭam āṭi
and dancing standing at night in the cremation ground holding fire which makes a tremulous sound when burning.
āṭu aravam ārtta perumāṉ uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr(ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the abode of the god who tied a dancing cobra as his belt.
{3:82}__8+
{$} oruvaraiyum mēl valikoṭēṉ eṉa eḻunta viṟalōṉ
the strong arakkan who rose saying I won't brook anyone being superior in strength to me.
ipperuvaraiyiṉmēl ōrperumāṉum uḷaṉō eṉa vekuṇṭa, karuvaraiyum āḻkaṭalum aṉṉa tiṟal kaikaḷ uṭaiyōṉai aruvaraiyaḷil ūṉṟi aṭarttāṉ
Civaṉ who crushed by pressing firmly by the great mountin and hands like the deep ocean and who flew into a rage saying is there any god on this big mountin?
uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr (ē)
Avaḷivanallūr is his abode.
{3:82}__9+
{$} poṟivariya nākam uyar poṅku aṇai aṇainta pukaḻōṉum
the famous Māl who is sleeping on the swelling superior bed of a serpent which has spots and lines on its body.
veṟivariya vaṇṭu aṟaiya viṇṭa malarmēl viḻumiyōṉum
and the excellent Piramaṉ who is seated on a (lotus) flower, which blossoms as the bees which know the fragrance and have lines on them, hum.
ceṟivu ariya tōṟṟamoṭu āṟṟal mika niṉṟu ciṟitēyum aṟivu ariyaṉ āya perumāṉ uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr (ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the abode of Civaṉ who is difficult to be known even a little, and who stood with great prowess with an appearance difficult to go near.
{3:82}__10+
{$} kaḻi aruku paḷḷi iṭamāka
constructing their hermitage near the backwater.
aṭumīṉkaḷ kavarvārum
camaṇar who catch fish for cooking as food;
[[kaḻikkaraippaṭumīṉ kavarvār amaṇ paḻippāṟai (campantar, tiruvālavāy (4) 10)]]
vaḻi aruku cāraveyil niṉṟu aṭicil uḷki varuvārum
and the buddhists who stand in the scorching sun by the side of the way and come back thinking of their food.
paḻiarukiṉār oḻika
Leave those two who are near reproach.
pāṉmaiyoṭu niṉṟu toḻutu ēttum aḻi aruvitōynta perumāṉ uṟaivatu avaḷivaṇallūr (ē)
Avaḷivaṇallūr is the abode of Civaṉ who bathes in the big stream of tears shed by devotees who, being steadfast in their nature, worship with joined hands and prise him;
[[aḻi aruvitōynta perumāṉ:
pāntaḷ pūṇ āram, parikalam, kāpālam, paṭṭavarttaṉam erutu, aṉpar vārntakaṇ aruvi mañcaṉcālai (tiruvicippā, tiruccāttiyakkuṭi, 2)]]
{3:82}__11+
{$} āṉa moḻi (1) āṉatiṟalōr paravum avaḷivaṇalūr mēl
on Avaḷivaṇallūr which is prised by the strong people who have fame which has substance in it.
pōṉamoḻi naṉmoḻikaḷāya pukaḻ tōṇipura ūraṉ
the native of Tōṇipuram which has fame contined in good words they being words which have spread in all directions.
ñāṉamoḻi mālai (2) palanāṭu pukaḻ ñāṉa campantaṉ
who has the name of Ñāṉacampantaṉ who is prised by the people of many countries; with the garlands of words which contin spiritual wisdom.
tēṉamoḻi mālaipukaḻvār tuyarkaḷ tīyatu ilar (tām, ē)
those who sing the prise of Civaṉ with the aid of the garlands of verses as sweet as honey, will not have any sufferings and evils.
[[Variant reading: (1) āya (2) nala]]

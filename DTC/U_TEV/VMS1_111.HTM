[[Note 1:
This shrine is now known as Kiḻiyūr'. It is a four miles south-east of Tirunaṉṟiyur which is itself two miles north-east of Āṉanta Tāṇtavapuram R.S. South Railway. It is one mile to the north west of Naṉipaḷḷi, the birth-place of the mother of Ñāṉacampantar]]
[[Note 2:
Kaṭimuṭi: This is commonly believed to be the place now known as Kīḻiyūr, which is five miles to the east of āṉanta tāṇṭavapuram rilway station Tanjore Dt. But M.Raghava Ayyar thinks that this decade of verses is on tirucceṉṉampūṇti, a mile and a half east of Kōvilaṭi, Tanjore Dt. The inscriptions which belong to the 9th and 10th centuries mention the god as tirukkaṭimuṭi makātēvār and the shrine as tirukkaṭimuṭi'. There is another shrine by name tiruccaṭimuṭi which is mentioned in Appar tēvāram where both the names are found in one and the same stanza. This Caṭimuṭi is to the north of Kōvilaṭi. Collection of essays p.280-284, 2nd edition, march 1964]]

{$} nīr viṉavutirēl
(People of this world) If you ask me.
aruttaṉai
the God who is in the form of wealth.
aṟavaṉai
who is the embodiment of aṟam.
amutaṉai
who is the nectar
viruttaṉai
who is the eldest one.
pālaṉai
and who is the youngest boy
ulakam ēttum oruttaṉai
the pearless god whom the people of this world prise.
karuttavaṉ
who is in the minds of people who meditate on him.
allatu
in addition to that.
caṭaimuṭivaḷanakar
he is the fertile city of Kaṭimuṭi.
{1:111}__2+
{$} tiraiporu tirumuṭi tiṅkaḷ vimmum, araiporu puli ataḷ aṭikaḷ iṭam
the place of the god who wears a tiger's skin in the waist and has a holy head on which the waves of Kaṅki dash aginst the matted hir, and crescent that is rejoicing.
tiraiyoṭu nurai poru teṇcuṉainīr, karai poruvaḷa nakar kaṭaimuṭiyē
is the city of fertile Kaṭimuṭi where the water in the clear tanks from which waves with foam dash aginst the banks.
{1:111}__3+
{$} āla iḷa matiyiṉoṭu aravu kaṅkai kōla veṇṇīṟṟaṉai
the god who besmears himself with the beautiful and white holy ash, has cobras, Kaṅki along with the rejoicing crescent.
ēla naṉ malaroṭu toḻutu iṟaiñci
to worship joining hands (holding defectless flowers fit for worship (iṟaiñci=iṟaiñca)
virai kamaḻum kālaṉa vaḷanakar kaṭaimuṭiyē
the fertile city of the god whose feet are spreading fragrance, is Kaṭimuṭi (kālaṉa-a; is expletive; it is not the case suffix for the gentive case plural, as it is followed by vaḷanakar which is singular).
{1:111}__4+
{$} koy aṇi naṟumalark koṉṟai amtār, mai aṇi miṭaṟu uṭai maṟaiyavaṉ
the brahmin among the tēvar who has a neck made beautiful by the poison, who wears a garland made of beautiful and fragrant koṉṟi flowers just plucked from the tree.
ūr pai aṇi aravoṭu māṉ maḻuvāḷ kai aṇipavaṉ iṭam kaṭaimuṭiyē
Kaṭimuṭi is the place of the god who adorns on his hands with a deer, a battle axe and a hooded cobra which moves by crawling.
{1:111}__5+
{$} maṟai avaṉ; ulaku avaṉ; māyam avaṉ
He is the vētam which is in the form of sound; is the material world; is the wonderful thing.
piṟaiyavaṉ
one who wears the crescent.
puṉalavaṉ
has water on his head.
aṉalum avaṉ
he is the fire too.
iṟaiyavaṉ eṉa ulaku ēttum kaṇṭam kaṟaiyavaṉ vaḷanakar kaṭai muṭiyē
the fertile city of the god who has a black neck and who is prised by the people of the world as one who dwells inside and outside all living beings, in Kaṭimuṭi.
{1:111}__6+
{$} paṭa aravu ēr alkul palvaḷaik kai maṭa varalāḷai ōr pākam vaittu
having kept on one half a lady who wears many bangles on her hand and has a waist resembling the snake with a hood.
kuṭaticai matiyatu cūṭu ceṉṉik kaṭavuḷtaṉ vaḷanakar kaṭai muṭiyē
the father city of the God who bears on his head the crescent that rises in the west, in Kaṭimuṭi
[[The moon in its crescent form rises in the west;
kuṭamutal tōṉṟiya toṉṟu toḻu piṟaiyiṉ (maturikkāñci, 193)]]
{1:111}__7+
{$} poṭi pulku mārpiṉil puripulku nūl, aṭipulku paiṅkaḻal aṭikaḷ iṭam
the place of the God who wears on his feet golden anklets, and a sacred thread of three strands on his chest besmeared with the holy ash.
koṭi pulku malaroṭu kuḷir cuṉainīr kaṭipulku vaḷanakar kaṭaimuṭiyē
is the fertile city of Kaṭimuṭi, where the water in the cool tanks and the flowers that grow in creepers are respectively guarded and fragrant (kaṭi protection, fragrance; In some remote villages tanks are the only source for supply of drinking water; so they are protected, so that people cannot bathe, wash clothes and vessels, throughout the year)
{1:111}__8+
{$} arakkaṉai nōtal ceytu
Having made the arakkaṉ to suffer pin
nōkku aḻiyaccātal ceytu
who almost met with death, as his eyes were destroyed.
avaṉ aṭi caraṇ eṉalum
as soon as he sid, your feet are my refuge
ātaravu aruḷ ceyta aṭikaḷ (avar) kātal cey vaḷanakar kaṭaimuṭiyē
the fertile city which is desired by the god who granted his grace out of love is Kaṭimuṭi.
{1:111}__9+
{$} aṭimuṭi kāṇkilar ōriruvar puṭaipulki, aruḷ eṉṟu pōṟṟu icaippa
the two persons who could not find the feet and head, and, approaching him, prised, Bestow your grace.
caṭaiyiṭaip puṉal vaitta caturaṉ iṭam kaṭaimuṭi
the place of the able person who placed the water on his matted hir is Kaṭimuṭi.
ataṉ ayal kāviri
Kāviri is near that place.
{1:111}__10+
{$} maṇṇutal paṟittalum māyam avai
Bathing in the water often and plucking the hirs on the head are deceptive acts.
eṇṇiya kāl avai iṉpam alla
When we think about them they are not things which give happiness.
oṇṇutal umaiyai ōr pākam vaitta kaṇṇutal vaḷanakar kaṭaimuṭiyē
the fertile city of the Lord who has a frontal eye and has Umi of bright forehead on one half, is Koṭimuṭi.

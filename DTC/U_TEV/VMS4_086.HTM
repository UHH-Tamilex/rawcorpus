tāṉ araṅku āka uṭaiyatu kāṭu akam
what Civaṉ has a dancing stage is the cremation ground.
tāṉ taṉ aṭaintār ūṉ akam nāṉum uṭai talaiyil pali koḷḷum
himself receives alms in the broken skull which has flesh sticking to it belonging to the devotees who have reached him.
tēṉ akam nāṟum tiruoṟṟiyūr uṟaivār avar tām; tāṉē akam vantu pōṉakam vēṇṭi uḻitarvarē
the Lord who dwells in tiruoṟṟiyūr where the honey spreads its fragrance all over the place; comes voluntarily to every house and wanders requiring food.

vēlaikkaṭal nañcam uṇṭu veḷḷēṟṟoṭum vīṟṟirunta mālaic caṭaiyarkku uṟaiviṭam āvatu
the dwelling place of Civaṉ who has a caṭi like the evening sky and who sits majestically on a white bull, having consumed the poison which rose in the ocean which has a shore.
ālaik karumpoṭu vārikuṉṟā cenneṟ kaḻaṉi aruku aṇainta cōlait tiruvoṟṟiyūrai eppōtum toḻumiṉkaḷē
(People of this world!) is tiruvoṟṟiyūr which has gardens which have adjacent to them fields in which superior variety of red paddy is produced and sugar-cane fit to be crushed in the press; worship that shrine with joined hands.

peṟṟu uṭaiyāṉ
Civaṉ who has a bull (peṟṟu - peṟṟam.)
perum pēccu uṭaiyāṉ
is the meaning of the great, sentence in Upanishads;
[[cf. perum poruṭ kiḷaviyāṉai (nāvukkaracar, potu, niṉinta tirunerici 3)]]
piriyātu eṉai āḷ viṟṟu uṭaiyāṉ oṟṟiyūr uṭaiyāṉ taṉ viricaṭaiyē
the spreading caṭi of the Lord who has oṟṟiyūr as his place, who does not leave me, who has me as his slave and who has the right to sell me as a slave.
puṟṟiṉil vāḻum aravukkum tiṅkaṭkum kaṅkai eṉṉum ciṟṟiṭaiyāṭkum ceṟitaru kaṇṇi kkum cērviṭamām
is the abode of the cobra that lives in the ant-hill, the crescent and the lady with a small waist, who is kanki and a chaplet of dense flowers (Kanki is feminie gender in sanskrit; so she personified as ciṟṟiṭaiyāḷ.)

oṟṟiyūraṉ cuṟṟik kiṭantu eṉ cintai pirivu aṟiyāṉ
the Lord in oṟṟiyūr will not leave my mind, lying there, surrounded it.
nī ulakam ellām 1. oṟṟit tiritantu eṉṉa ceyti
what would you do united with the world and wandering in all the places?
ittīviṉai

oṟṟiyūr uṟai paṇṭaṅkaṉē
Civaṉ who performed the dance of pānṭaraṅkam and who dwells in oṟṟiyūr!
tarukkiṉa vāḷ arakkaṉ muṭipattu iṟappātam taṉṉāl orukkiṉavāṟu
just as he broke the ten heads of the arakkaṉ who was proud and cruel, by the tip of one toe of one foot.
aṭiyēṉaip piṟappu aṟuttu āḷa vallāṉ
is capable of destroying the births of his slave and admit me as his protege.
nerukkiṉa vāṉavar tāṉavar kūṭik kaṭainta nañcaip parukkiṉavāṟu eṉ ceykēṉ
what cruel act it was to make Civaṉ to drink the poison which rose when the celestials and acurar joined together and were thick close to one another? what can I do for that!

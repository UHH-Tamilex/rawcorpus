{3:79}__1+
{$} ayalavarkku eṉṟum ariyāṉ
Civaṉ is difficult to be known to others who are not his devotees.
iyal icaip poruḷ āki eṉatu uḷ naṉṟum oḷiyāṉ
he is the big light within me being that division of Tamiḻ poetry or prose, conforming to the rules of Tamiḻ grammar and Tamiḻ poetry composed to suit the several melody-types and time measures.
oḷi ciṟanta poṉ muṭik kaṭavuḷ naṇṇum iṭam
the place which the god who has a caṭi coiled into a crown glittering like superior gold, remins.
oṉṟiya maṉattu aṭiyar kūṭi imaiyōr paravu nīṭu aravam ār kuṉṟukaḷ neruṅki viri taṇṭalai miṭaintu vaḷar kōkaraṇamē ām
is Kokaraṇam where the blossomed gardens are close to each other and hills are close to each other and the loud sound of the celestials who prise joining with devotees of concentrated minds, fills the whole place.
{3:79}__2+
{$} pētai maṭamaṅkai oru paṅku iṭam mikuttu
rejoicing in having been united with an artless, beautiful lady on the left half.
iṭapam ēṟi
riding on a bull
vātaipaṭa vaṇkaṭal eḻunta viṭam uṇṭa civaṉ vāḻum iṭam
the place where Civaṉ who consumed the poison that rose in the ocean having many kinds of wealth to make the celestials and acurar feel distressed;
[[PP: vāṉavarkaḷ tāṉavarkaḷ vataipaṭa vantatu orumākaṭal viṭam (campantar, Kaḷatti (2) 1)]]
mātaroṭum āṭavarkaḷ vantu aṭi iṟaiñci niṟai māmalarkaḷ tūya
gents and ladies come and prostrate at the feet of Civaṉ and scatter big flowers to hide the feet.
kōtai viri vaṇṭu icaikoḷ kītam mural kiṉṟa vaḷar kōkaraṇamē ām
is Kōkaraṇam of increasing fame where in the garlands the bees with lines hum like music compositions.
{3:79}__3+
{$} āla niḻal vāy
under the shade of the banyan tree.
muṟaittiṟam uṟap poruḷ terintu muṉivarkku aruḷi
granting the meaning of the vētam investigating in accordance with the rules of initiation to the sages.
maṟaittiram aṟattokuti kaṇṭu camayaṅkaḷai vakuttavaṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who founded (created) the religions codifying the sum total of righteous conduct in the different vētams.
tuṟaittuṟai mikuttu aruci tūmalar cumantu varai unti matakaik kuṟaittu
eroding the head-sluices the streams which are numerous in every ghat carrying pure flowers and pushing bamboos.
kari aṟaiyiṭap purintu
cusing the elephant to roar.
iṭaṟu cāral mali kōkaraṇamē ām
is Kōkaraṇam in which there are many slopes which are dashed by the streams.
{3:79}__4+
{$} ilaittalai mikutta paṭai eṇkaram viḷaṅka
the weapon of a trident which has blades like leaves and other weapons to be conspicuous in the eight hands;
[[paṭai ilaṅku karam eṭṭu uṭaiyāṉ (campantar valitayam 2);
cf. kalai talai, cūlam, maḻukkaṉal, kaṇṭai, kaṭṭaṅkam, koṭi, cilai ivai ēntiya eṇ tōḷ civaṟku (poṉvaṇṇattantati, 88)]]
eri vīci
swiming the fire.
muṭimēl alaittalai tokutta puṉal ceñcaṭaiyil vaitta aḻakaṉ taṉ iṭam
the place of the beautiful god who placed on his red cati water which has a collection of waves.
malaittalai vakutta muḻaitoṟum uḷaivāḷ arikaḷ kēḻal, kaḷiṟu kolaittilai maṭappiṭikaḷ kūṭi viḷaiyāṭi nikaḻ kōkaraṇamē ām
is Kōkaraṇam where the bright lions with manes living in every cave formed in the mountin; wild boar, male elephants, and young female elephants which can cuse death join together and play together forgetting their natural enmities
[[tōṟu muḻai can be split into tōṟum uḻai ; for the reading uḻai the meaning is spotted deer.]]
{3:79}__5+
{$} talait toṭai malaittu
wearing a garland of skulls.
itaḻi tuṉṉiya erukku alari vaṉṉi muṭiyiṉ caṭaittalai milaicciya tapōtaṉaṉ em āti payil kiṉṟa pati
the city where our god who is the cuse of all things, who has penance as his wealth and who wears on the caṭi on the head leaves of indian mesquit, oleander flowers, yarcum flowers which are dense, and koṉṟi.
paṭaittalaip piṭittu maṟavāḷaroṭu vēṭarkaḷ payiṉṟu kuḻumik kuṭaittu alai natippaṭiya niṉṟu paḻitīra nalku kōkaraṇamē ām
is Kōkaraṇam, where the hunters are friendly with warriors who hold the end of the weapons and join together, bathe in the river with waves, plunging into the which grants grace to remove the reproaches.
{3:79}__6+
{$} nīṟu tirumēṉi micai āṭi
bathing in sacred ash his holy body.
vār kaḻal cilampu niṟai oli ceyya
the kaḻal which is tied tightly with a leather strap, an anklet to make a sound that fills the whole place.
ēṟu (1) viḷaiyāṭa vicaikoṇṭu iṭupalikku varum īcaṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ who comes to receive the alms that are given, driving the bull very speedily as if it is a play.
āṟu camayaṅkaḷum virumpi aṭipēṇa araṉ ākamam mikak (2) kūṟum maṉam vēṟu irati vantu aṭiyār kampam varu kōkaraṇamē ām
is Kōkaraṇam where people belonging to the six civa religious systems cherish his feet with love and talk about the ākamam-s revealed by Araṉ, and where the devotees quake with joy as they derive a different sweetness
[[PP: ākam viṇṭu kampam vantu kuñci añcalikkaṇē āka eṉkai kaṇkaḷ tārai āṟu atu āka aiyaṉē (Tiruvācakam, tiruccatākam 72)]]
[[Variant reading: viḷaiyāṭalicai (2) kūṟum vaṉamēṟirati]]
{3:79}__7+
{$} kallavaṭam montai kuḻal tāḷam mali kokkaraiyar
Civaṉ dances to the measure of times of these instruments such as Kollavaṭam, monti, flute, cymbals, and many Kōkkari.
akku araimicai pallapaṭa nākam viri kōvaṇavar āḷum nakar
the city of Civaṉ who wears an expanded loin-cloth of a cobra with which many teeth and hoods on the waist in which he wears beads.
ayalē
by the side of the (holy water)
nalla maṭa mātar
young ladies who have good character and acts.
araṉ nāmamum naviṟṟiya tiruttam muḻuka
when they bathe in the holy water bearing the names of araṉ.
kolla 1. viṭanōy akaltarap pukal koṭut taruḷu kōkaraṇamē eṉpar
people say it is Kōkaraṇam which grants refuge by removing the disease due to poison that can kill;
[[tiruttam : holy water;
kayilai malaiyiṉ uyar kuṭumit tiruttam payilum cuṉai (Kōviyar, 62)]]
[[Variant reading: 1. viṭar nōy ]]
{3:79}__8+
{$} varaittalam nerukkiya muruṭṭu iruḷ niṟattavaṉ vāykaḷ alaṟa
all the ten mouths of Irāvaṇaṉ who had a black colour and and who attacked the mountin Kayili.
viraltalai ukirc ciṟitu vaitta perumāṉ iṉitu mēvum iṭam
the place of the god who fixed slightly the nil of the toe (and crushed him) desires and dwells with pleasure.
puraittalai keṭutta muṉivāṇar polivāki viṉaitīra
as the sages who destroyed all faults, gin brilliance as their acts perish.
ataṉ mēl
over and above that.
kuraittu alai kaḻaṟpaṇiya ōmam vilakum pukai cey kōkaraṇam ām
is Kōkaranam where the smoke that issues from the sacrifices performed by them, when they pay homage to his feet wearing Kaḻal which moves making a sound, rises in the sky.
{3:79}__9+
{$} villimaiyiṉāl viṟal arakkaṉ uyir ceṟṟavaṉum
Māl who destroyed the life of the victorious arakkaṉ by his skill in archery.
vēta mutalōṉ (um)
and Piramaṉ who gave out the vētams.
illai uḷatu eṉṟu ikali nēṭa eriyāki uyarkiṉṟa paraṉ ūr
the place of the supreme god, Civaṉ who grew tall as a column of fire for both of them being opposed to each other, to search the head and feet, thinking they existed and did not exist.
ellai il varaitta kaṭal vaṭṭamum iṟaiñci niṟai vācal uruva
the people inhabiting the world surrounded by the ocean which is limitless; worship with incense which fills the whole space and as the fragrance penetrates.
kollaiyil 1. iruṅkuṟavar tammayir pulartti vaḷar kōkaraṇamē
is Kōkaraṇam where the young Kuṟavar ladies living in the forest tract dry their locks of hir and which is prosperous.
[[Variant reading: 1. iruṅkuṟavar: iṉaṅkuṟavar ]]
{3:79}__10+
{$} nēcam il maṉac camaṇar tērarkaḷ niranta moḻi poykaḷ akal vittu
having separated the lies arranged in the proper order by the camaṇar who have no love and terarkaḷ (buddhists).
ācai koḷ maṉattai aṭiyār avar tamakku aruḷum aṅkaṇaṉ iṭam
the place of Civaṉ, the gracious-eyed who grants to his devotees a mind which desires him.
pācam (atu) aṟuttu 1. avaṉiyil peyarkaḷ pattu uṭaiya maṉṉaṉ avaṉaik kūca vakaikaṇṭu piṉ avaṟku aruḷkaḷ nalka vala kōkaraṇamē
is Kōkaraṇam which is capable of bestowing many kinds of grace to aruccuṉaṉ who had ten names to feel ashamed and after cutting all his attachments in this world (The ten names of aruccuṉaṉ)
[[paṟkuṇaṉ cuvētavākaṉaṉ, pārttaṉ, cavviya cāci, kirīṭi, takañcayaṉ, vicayaṉ, vīpaṟcu, kāṇṭīvaṉ, kiruṭṭiṉaṉ, aruccuṉaṉ tacanāmaṅkaḷ ākum (tivākaram 12)]]
[[Variant reading: 1. avaṉiyaṟ peyarkaḷ ]]
{3:79}__11+
{$} kōṭal aravu īṉum viricāral muṉ neruṅkivaḷar kōkaraṇamē īṭam iṉitāka uṟaivāṉ aṭikaḷ pēṇi
cherishing with love the feet of Civaṉ who dwells with pleasure in Kōkaraṇam as his chosen place where in the extensive slopes trees grow very dense and where the malabar glory lily blossoms flowers like the hood of the cobra.
aṇi kāḻi nakarāṉ
the native of the city of Kāḻi who adorns his head with the lord's feet.
nāṭiya tamiḻk kiḷavi iṉṉicai cey ñāṉacampantaṉ moḻikaḷ pāṭavalla pattar (avar) etticaiyum āḷvar paralōkam eḷitē
those pious people who can sing the words of ñāṉacampantaṉ who composed with music choice tamiḻ words; will in this birth itself rule over the country bounded by all the directions; after that reaching the superior world is easy for them.

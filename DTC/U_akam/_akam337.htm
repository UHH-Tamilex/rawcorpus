Table of contents (by lines):
1-4. Description of the beauty of talaivi and the inadvisabilitiy of staying the curam, having parted from her.
5. Talaivaṉ deciding that staying with talaivi was real wealth.
6-11. The warriors in the desert region killing a pārppāṉ who was on his errand as a messenger mistaking the roll of palm-leaf in his hand to be gold.
12-12. The warriors regretting their hasty action.
14-18. The desert where the solitary male fox having bitten the intestine of the pārppāṉ, keeps howling under the shade of kaḷḷi (kaḷḷi : spurge).
19. Talaivaṉ rebuking his mind.
20-21. Talaivaṉ thinking that talaivi would suffer from the chill north wind in the shivering night.
Colophon(s):
Talaivaṉ who returned once before, after acquiring wealth told his mind which again desired to part (from talaivi) for acquiring wealth.

TRANSLATION
  • (My mind)! Telling me, "As it is not pleasant for (4)
  • this talaivi" with big and cool eyes that seem to fight with each other (3),
  • and a body having the complexion of the wet leaves in the high branches of the yā tree (yāmaram) which were put forth in the winter (2)
  • growing in the adjacent hills (1),
  • to stay alone (3)
  • it is not proper for me to stay here parting from her (4),
  • and staying with her is real wealth (5)
  • because the talaivi would suffer from the north wind of excessive chillness (21)
  • as it rained with great bustle (20)
  • in the shivering night (19)" The cruel warriors (11)
  • who hold weapons in their hands (8),
  • on seeing the pārppāṉ with a rolled palmyra leaf who was on his errand of going as messenger (7)
  • many times (6)
  • passing through the rocks (7)
  • which are like the rows of herds of bullocks of salt-sellers (5),
  • and thinking, "It is possible that this man whose sides are lean as he has not eaten, has gold (10)
  • in his hands" (9)
  • killed him at once and cause him to fall (11)
  • without any benefit to themselves (10).
  • They thought of his poverty as he had worn only rags which snapping their hands (13).
  • You, once before, returned leaving me beside loneliness (19), in the difficult place of hot forest paths, where rain had failed (18),
  • where the solitary male fox living in the way of the maral (maral) with lines having bitten the hanging bowel of that pārppāṉ, from which blood was flowing to a great length (14),
  • stays howling loud under the shade of the kaḷḷi (kaḷḷi: spurge).

NOTES

The leaves of yā tree are compared to the complexion of talaivi; cf. akam 333-1-2.

Staying with talaivi is real wealth; cf. Kalittokai, 2-11-22. avaṇatāka poruḷ eṉṟum, vāṭaiyoṭu varuntuvaḷ eṉṟum kūṟik kaḻitantōyē eṉak kūṭṭuka palkāl can be added along with kuṟum poṟai - and be interpreted as the hillocks which have boulders resembling legs.

paṭaiyuṭaik kaiyar

- should be put in apposition with kaṭuṅkaṇ maḻavar-

pārppaṉar were sent as messengers with messages written on palm-leaves in ancient times; ""ōtalum tūtum uyarntōrmēṉa'' (tolkappiyam, akattiṇaiyal, 26);

nacciṉārkkiṉiyar has quoted a stanza from puṟam for pārppaṉar going as messenger, which is as follows; ""vayalaik koṭiyiṉ vāṭiya maruṅkul, uyavam ūrtip payalaip pārppāṉ, elli vantu nillātu pukkuc, colliya collō cilavē ataṟkē ēṇiyum cīppum māṟṟi, māṇviṉai yāṉaiyum maṇikaḷain taṉavē'' (305) kaṇanirai

the new commentators have taken it to mean donkeys, which were used by salt-sellers to carry burden; but in many places in akam itself and naṟṟiṇai the bullocks (pakaṭu, erutu) are mentioned as carrying bags of salt; ""koṭuṅkōl umaṇar pakaṭu teḻi teḷviḷi, neṭum peruṅkuṉṟattu imiḻ koḷa iyampum'', ""uppu oy umaṇar arun tuṟaipōkkum, oḻukai nōṉtāḷ, tiruntu pakaṭṭiyampum koṭumaṇi'', ""kaṉim cāl umaṇar, uyaṅku pakaṭu uyirppa acaii'', ""umaṇar, kaṇanirai maṇiyiṉārkkum'', ""umaṇar, koṭunukam piṇitta ceṅkayiṟṟomukaip, pakaṭu ayāk koḷḷum'' (akam 17-15-17, 30-5-6, 191-4-7, 295-5-10, 303-17-18, 329-5-6); nīrum pullum īyātu umaṇar, yārum il omaciṟai muṭattoṭu tuṟatta, vāḻā nōṉpakaṭu'' (puram, 307-7-9) Only in perumpāṇaṟṟuppaṭai donkey is mentioned as carrying bags of pepper; ""miriyaṟ; puḷirp poṟai tāṅkiya vaku āḻ nōṉpuṟattu, aṇarc cevik kaḻutaic cāttoṭu vaḻaṅkum, ulkuṭaip peruvaḻika kavalaikākkum, villuṭai, vaippiṉ'' ($$l$$l.78-82).

The warriors moved from that place snapping their hands thinking about their hasty action which was of no benefit to them

Talaivaṉ admonished his mind, "My mind ! As you once before returned like that how is it possible to trust your words again and go along with you!"

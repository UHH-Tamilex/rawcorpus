Table of contents (by lines):
1-4. Description of the beauty of talivi and the inadvisabilitiy of staying the curam, having parted from her.
5. Talivaṉ deciding that staying with talivi was real wealth.
6-11. The warriors in the desert region killing a pārppāṉ who was on his errand as a messenger mistaking the roll of palm-leaf in his hand to be gold.
12-12. The warriors regretting their hasty action.
14-18. The desert where the solitary male fox having bitten the intestine of the pārppāṉ, keeps howling under the shade of kaḷḷi (கள்ளி : spurge).
19. Talivaṉ rebuking his mind.
20-21. Talivaṉ thinking that talivi would suffer from the chill north wind in the shivering night.
Colophon(s):
Talivaṉ who returned once before, after acquiring wealth told his mind which agin desired to part (from talivi) for acquiring wealth.

TRANSLATION
  • (My mind)! Telling me, "As it is not pleasant for (4)
  • this talivi" with big and cool eyes that seem to fight with each other (3),
  • and a body having the complexion of the wet leaves in the high branches of the yā tree (யாமரம்) which were put forth in the winter (2)
  • growing in the adjacent hills (1),
  • to stay alone (3)
  • it is not proper for me to stay here parting from her (4),
  • and staying with her is real wealth (5)
  • becuse the talivi would suffer from the north wind of excessive chillness (21)
  • as it rined with great bustle (20)
  • in the shivering night (19)" The cruel warriors (11)
  • who hold weapons in their hands (8),
  • on seeing the pārppāṉ with a rolled palmyra leaf who was on his errand of going as messenger (7)
  • many times (6)
  • passing through the rocks (7)
  • which are like the rows of herds of bullocks of salt-sellers (5),
  • and thinking, "It is possible that this man whose sides are lean as he has not eaten, has gold (10)
  • in his hands" (9)
  • killed him at once and cuse him to fall (11)
  • without any benefit to themselves (10).
  • They thought of his poverty as he had worn only rags which snapping their hands (13).
  • You, once before, returned leaving me beside loneliness (19), in the difficult place of hot forest paths, where rin had failed (18),
  • where the solitary male fox living in the way of the maral (மரல்) with lines having bitten the hanging bowel of that pārppāṉ, from which blood was flowing to a great length (14),
  • stays howling loud under the shade of the kaḷḷi (கள்ளி: spurge).

NOTES

The leaves of yā tree are compared to the complexion of talivi; cf. akam 333-1-2.

Staying with talivi is real wealth; cf. Kalittoki, 2-11-22. அவணதாக பொருள் என்றும், வாடையொடு வருந்துவள் என்றும் கூறிக் கழிதந்தோயே எனக் கூட்டுக பல்கால் can be added along with குறும் பொறை - and be interpreted as the hillocks which have boulders resembling legs.

படையுடைக் கையர்

- should be put in apposition with கடுங்கண் மழவர்-

pārppaṉar were sent as messengers with messages written on palm-leaves in ancient times; ""ஓதலும் தூதும் உயர்ந்தோர்மேன'' (tolkappiyam, akattiṇiyal, 26);

nacciṉārkkiṉiyar has quoted a stanza from puṟam for pārppaṉar going as messenger, which is as follows; ""வயலைக் கொடியின் வாடிய மருங்குல், உயவம் ஊர்திப் பயலைப் பார்ப்பான், எல்லி வந்து நில்லாது புக்குச், சொல்லிய சொல்லோ சிலவே அதற்கே ஏணியும் சீப்பும் மாற்றி, மாண்வினை யானையும் மணிகளைந் தனவே'' (305) கணநிரை

the new commentators have taken it to mean donkeys, which were used by salt-sellers to carry burden; but in many places in akam itself and naṟṟiṇi the bullocks (பகடு, எருது) are mentioned as carrying bags of salt; ""கொடுங்கோல் உமணர் பகடு தெழி தெள்விளி, நெடும் பெருங்குன்றத்து இமிழ் கொள இயம்பும்'', ""உப்பு ஒய் உமணர் அருந் துறைபோக்கும், ஒழுகை நோன்தாள், திருந்து பகட்டியம்பும் கொடுமணி'', ""கனிம் சால் உமணர், உயங்கு பகடு உயிர்ப்ப அசைஇ'', ""உமணர், கணநிரை மணியினார்க்கும்'', ""உமணர், கொடுநுகம் பிணித்த செங்கயிற்றொமுகைப், பகடு அயாக் கொள்ளும்'' (akam 17-15-17, 30-5-6, 191-4-7, 295-5-10, 303-17-18, 329-5-6); நீரும் புல்லும் ஈயாது உமணர், யாரும் இல் ஒமசிறை முடத்தொடு துறத்த, வாழா நோன்பகடு'' (puram, 307-7-9) Only in perumpāṇaṟṟuppaṭi donkey is mentioned as carrying bags of pepper; ""மிரியற்; புளிர்ப் பொறை தாங்கிய வகு ஆழ் நோன்புறத்து, அணர்ச் செவிக் கழுதைச் சாத்தொடு வழங்கும், உல்குடைப் பெருவழிக கவலைகாக்கும், வில்லுடை, வைப்பின்'' ($$l$$l.78-82).

The warriors moved from that place snapping their hands thinking about their hasty action which was of no benefit to them

Talivaṉ admonished his mind, "My mind ! As you once before returned like that how is it possible to trust your words agin and go along with you!"

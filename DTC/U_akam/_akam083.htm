(1-10) veṅkatam where the followers of pulli catch the calves of elephants and tether them in the gates of liquor-shops.
(11-2) The qualities of talivi appear in the mind of talivaṉ though he is far way from her.

TRANSLATION
  • (My mind)! Even if we went beyond veṅkatam which is situated in the expansive good country (10)
  • of pulli (pulli) who is the chief of the young hunters who know no other trade except hunting, (9)
  • who tether in the doors of good liquor-houses (8)
  • in the old village which has bazaars where long flags move (7-8)
  • having tied securely the calves of elephants with the ropes of white barks which were peeled off in big sizes (6)
  • by splitting the fertile branches of the indian oak (marāmaram) of black trunks (5-6)
  • with great bustle, and who have separated the calves of elephants and feel jubilant on account of that to make the young female elephants having feet like mortar, to cry aloud out of sorrow in the forest (3)
  • having adorned on their heads having curls of hir like the hir-plume of horses, prominently with the fresh flowers of indian oak (marāmaram) which have petals arranged clock-wise and which spread their fragrance throughout the desert (1),
  • the qualities of our loving wife who has cool eyes of excessive beauty (14)
  • which are like the bright flowers (12)
  • of blue nelumbo (neytaṟ pū) which relaxes the tightness of its petals when blossoming (13),
  • appeared before us by being driven by love, (12)
  • not thinking that we are in a distant country (11)

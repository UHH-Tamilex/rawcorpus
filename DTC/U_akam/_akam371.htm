Table of contents (by lines):
1-2. The description of hunters.
3-5. The stag full of sorrow thinking of its deer killed by the hunters; the young ones not aware of the misfortune playing by its side.
6-9. The desolate path in the curam where the stag sleeps without grazing and drinking water.
10-14, Talivaṉ anxious about the condition of his lovign wifes eyes which would be shedding tears.
Colophon(s):
Talimakaṉ who was going (in the desert path) having parted from talivi to acquire wealth told his mind.

TRANSLATION
  • (My mind)! The path in the desolate curam which is severely hot as the moisture had dried (9)
  • where the bereaved stag of twisted horns (5)
  • and not included to graze the grass its grief being increased (6)
  • and also not drinking the small amount of water in the small pit in the saline land (7)
  • lies sleeping with agony like the people on whom the vēl has pierced (8),
  • as its young ones of small and large sizes play by its side (4)
  • (unaware of the misfortune that had befallen them), thinking of its loving deer which died by the arrow shot by hunters of angry looks having reddish arrows with blood stined ends (2)
  • and a strong speedful bow on which the beautiful string was fixed by passing the fingers over the beautiful rim (1),
  • is in such a condition; what greater suffering will the eyes of our loving wife whose tresse of hir have been adorned fully with flowers (14)
  • which are sought after by bees desirous of honey, thinking that they resemble the bright flowers in the beautiful mountin pools of clear water, undergo shedding tears, than ourselves who are crossing (that way in the desert) all alone ?

Talivaṉ spoke to his mind to explin the distress cused to him by saying what greater suffering the eyes of our loving wife will undergo who is staying having parted from us than ourselves who are undergoing suffering by travelling in the cruel curam having parted from talivi

TRANSLATION
  • As the large number of clouds which have taken the water amply in abundance from the eastern sea (1),
  • ascend clockwise and look like the elephants which have been arranged in a row (3)
  • in the army of kings who have drums and several weapons (2),
  • emit lightning like the stright flag held on a staff, to cleave the sky (4),
  • produce roaring noise of thunder (5).
  • Tommorrow (7)
  • it is definite that the stream which resembles the shining sword will spread in all places (7),
  • dash aginst the tender shafts of big bamboos, so as to bend them (8) ,
  • and reach the bathing ghat of vast space in our village (10),
  • pushing from its position the wooden horse (kutiraimaram) (9)
  • (If the floods arrive) shall we bathe at midnight (11)
  • in the water which came touching the beautiful and high mountins belonging to (15)
  • talivaṉ who gave us never decreasing distress (14)
  • to make our beautiful complexion comparable to sapphire, to vanish (13),
  • to make our cool eyes like ice to become red (11)
  • and to remove the distress we hate (12)?

NOTES

As tamiḻnāṭu receives its rins during the north-east monsoon it is mentioned as kuṇakaṭal mukantu

muracu is an important percussion instrument for kings; it is sid to be of three kinds; ""muraicu mūṉṟu āṉpavar'' (kalittokai, 132-4); ""cimiḻkural muracam mūṉṟuṭaṉāḷum.....taṇkōl vēntē'' (puṟanāṉūṟu. 58-12-13)

Elephants are compared to sable clouds in the following also :- ""oḷiṟuvāṭ poruppaṉ uṭal camattiṟutta, kaḷiṟu niraittavai pōṟkoṇmū'' (paripāṭal, 22-1-2); ""neyyaṇi kuñcaram pōla iruṅkoṇmū, vaikalum ērum valam'' (kār nāṟpatu. 12);

tōl will also mean shield; ""neyyaṇi nēṭuvēl eḵkilai imaikkum, maḻaimaruṉ paḵṟōl māvaṇ cōḻar'', ""māḷiyampiṉ maḻaittōṟpaḻaiyaṉ'' (akam. 123-9-10, 186-15.)

pari - a technical word used for a log of wood set up in a river to dam it, ""pari niṟuttu vār'' (tiruviḷaiyāṭaṟ purāṇam, maṇ cumanta paṭalam 5.); ""parittiraṉ niṟuttu meṉpār'' (tiruvātavūrar purāṇam, maṇkamantacarukkam, 21

If one bathes for a long time in the water the eyes will become red; ""nīr nīṭāṭiṟ kaṇṇum civakkum'' (Kuṟuntoki, 354-1);

This idea will be found in Kovi in the tuṟi, ""cuṉaiviyanturaittal''

The friend sid ""Though the talivaṉ has parted from us and has not given us his chest to be embraced by us, as a reseult of which we lost all our beauty, we will be satisfied with having embraced him if we bathe in the water that comes touching his mountin.'' By these words talivi's excessive longing was hinted. The effect of the friend saying these words is that talivaṉ should marry talivi soon. The shining sword is compared to the mountin stream; ""varaittāḻpu, vāḷ niṟaṅ koṇṭa aruvittē'' (kalit tokai, 42-10-11). 

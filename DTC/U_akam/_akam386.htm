TRANSLATION
  • Chief of the village where the otter of smelling flesh and living in the natural tank (1)
  • searches for its prey of vāḷi (vāḷai mīṉ: scabbard fish) in the early morning ! (2)
  • Eminent one !. (13)
  • (your harlot) of bright forehead came slowly hiding herself (8),
  • and speaking words expressing civility (9)
  • (accosted talivi) as "young lady with big black tresses of hir ! (10)
  • (and added)" I am in the same street like you I am your next-door neighbour (11).
  • I stand in the relationship of an younger sister to you (12)
  • and so saying (12),
  • rubbed gently (14)
  • the forehead and the tresses of hir (14)
  • to become cool, (13)
  • with her soft fingers of her hand, on which she wore a ring, (15)
  • during daytime and returned (15).
  • On seeing her (15),
  • I was ashamed just as Kaṇayaṉ of fair battle was ashamed, on seeing the shoulders of globularity and great strength which resemble in shape the muḻavu (muḻavu) lying having been separated from the body being cught by pāṇaṉ, belonging to āriyapporunaṉ who challenged pāṇaṉ in wrestling, having suffered from the enormous strength of the chest of pāṇaṉ which is very much skilled in wrestling.

NOTES

The incident referred to in line 3-8 is as follows : - pāṇaṉ who was well skilled in wrestling and his opponent āriyapporunaṉ wrestled; the shoulders of porunaṉ were cught in the hands of pāṇaṉ and separated from the body and he lay dead. On seeing him dead Kāṇayaṉ was ashamed. Kanayaṉ who is mentioned here must be a friend of porunaṉ or must have sworn that porunaṉ would win.

tiṟaṉ vēṟu kiṭakkai means lying dead life having expired

oḻinta kiṭakkai The body lying without the shoulders.

If tōṭkaiyakattu had been the reading we can interpret that pāṇaṉ died being cught in the hands of āriyapporunaṉ; then Kaṇayaṉ should be taken to be the friend of pāṇaṉ.

maṭavōy this is how the harlot addressed talivi.

For nuṅkai nacciṉārkkiṉiyar gives the meaning as friend.

maṇi stands for a ring set with precious stones.

toṭutal wearing; "naṟā vitaḻ kaṇṭaṉṉa cevviraṟ kēṟpac; cuṟā ēṟu eḻutiya mōtiram toṭṭāḷ'' (Kalittoki, 84-22-23); ""maṇi nāma mōtiram toṭṭu'' (cīvakacintamani. 1040)

By pakal vantu peyarnta is meant that the harlot came to be seen by many without any sense of shyness. By mentioning that she rubbed gently the forehead and tresses of talivi, behaved without showing any respect to talivi.

The friend took upon herself feeling shameful for the behaviour of the harlot when actually it was talivi who felt ashamed. Noble sould will feel ashamed of the bad behaviour of other people.

The inner meaning of the description of the otter if that talivaṉ cused the whole village to indulge in public gossip by his living with the concubines at night and coming to his own house in search of talivi. 

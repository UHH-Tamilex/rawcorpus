Table of contents (by lines):
1-2 The sun worshipped by the world.
3-5 The forest region changes into desert during scorching summer.
6-8 The highway robbers hiding themselves in the top-most branch of yā tree, being on the look-out for unwary new travellers.
9-10 The mother repenting her ignorance of the determination of her dughter's love towards her lover.
11-12 The mother compares her house to vañci, the capital of cēraṉ gladly unite her dughter and her lover in wedlock.
Colophon(s):
The mother who was the cuse of her dughter eloping with her lover without the mother's knowledge, spoke thus.

TRANSLATION
  • In the season when the land is completely without moisture and the heat is at its zenith to destroy all crops (4)
  • from the fertile earth (3),
  • when the sun which strikes fear (2)
  • and spreads out its bright rays (2)
  • and rises in the big ocean of shining waves to be worshipped by the world. (1)
  • in the big and dreadful forest which has many different branching paths (5)
  • and in the difficult desert where the hunters well trined in archery (7),
  • hiding themselves in the branches of the yā of tall stature (8),
  • are on the look-out for unwary new passers-by who go that way, (7)
  • the youthful and unperturbed talivaṉ led my dughter and went away with her (9).
  • Had I but known her firm love towards him, alas (10)!
  • I would have united then in wedlock very happily, without hatred on displeasure and got him (13)
  • the sweet and pleasant sleep on the expanse of the appearing breasts (15)
  • of my dughter who is beautiful by her faultless forehead (14),
  • in my house which has plenty of every kind to add to its greatness (12)
  • and which can be compared to the city of Vañci (12)
  • which is protected from enemies and ruled justly by Kōti who possesses a shining lance (11),
  • to make splendid. That could not come to pass (13)

NOTES

In line 3 kaṇ can be taken as an expletive particle also;
in puṟam, stanza, 109; ""mīṉkaṇaṟṟataṉ cuḷaiyē'' kaṇ is an expletive particle.

oṟṟik kōṭu koḷ should be taken as being on the look-out, having climbd up the yā tree and being seated there, and hiding themselves.

kāḷaikku-kāḷaiyiṉpāl - this is (urupumayakkam) the use of one case affix for that of another,

aṉṉō - a word of exclamation expressing regret.

ōmpik kākkum - rulling taking much care; ie. protecting his subjects as a poor farmer who has a single field would bestow all his care and attention on that (Kampa rāmāyaṇa, pālakāntam chapter araciyaṟ paṭalam 12)

Poets of the caṅkan speak of lance highly as it is the weapon of murukaṉ,

As the author of this poem prises the cēra king he must belong to Karuvūr. karuvūr is another name for vañci (vañci)

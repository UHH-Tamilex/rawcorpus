TRANSLATION
  • My mind (3)!
  • Teṉṉaṉ (pāṇṭiyaṉ) has undying good fame (10);
  • he is the chief of the robbers who live in ancient villages and lift enemies' cows, (6)
  • in addition to opening the places of defence of forests which have anthills constructed with mud (5);
  • he is the leader of the warriors who obey and carry out his commands (6);
  • he has a big hands resembling thunder, who with his might destroyed difficult forts of petty chiefs, along (7)
  • with winning many battles (8).
  • When the talivi who has a waist with beautiful lines and who is difficult to be reached by us (13)
  • like the nymphs who are concealed in the mountin cave by the side of the pool where water descends (11),
  • which was not dug by human beings (10);
  • you did not give up the desire even though I admonished you by strong words; you did not cease when I tried to restore you to in the proper position by kind words. (1)
  • Am I not in the position of a relation (3)
  • to you (2) ?
  • Respect my words taking them to be the words of friends united in mind (2).

NOTES

ஒறுத்தல் - here means punishing by words; admonishing by speaking harsh words.

For நிறுப்ப: we have to supply `kind words' which is the opposite of harsh words. It is the duty of relations to give advice in both the ways; so talivaṉ sid, "Am I not your relation?" The talimakaṉ who was misled by signs other than his own, thought that talivi was rare to get; so he advised his mind `Do not go there; stay here ! Though advised by him in this way his mind did not listen to his words and went after talivi; so he spoke like this;

நன்னாட்டுள்ளது - is an epithet qualifying கல்லளை

அருப்பம் is the word from which the end அம் - is removed.

அருப்பம் - forest used as defence.

மேவார் - is a common word denoting enemies who are petty

செரு - battle fought with kings.

Though there is a gloss for the word செல் - as மேகம், thunder is more appropriate as it is used as a simile for destroying enemies. thunder; ""செறுநர்த்தேய்த்த செல்உறழ் தடக்கை'' (tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭi, 5);

தொடா - not dug; the mountin pools are natural depressions in the mountins into which water flows; that is called poyki; poyki - a reservoir of water not dug by human effort 

Table of contents (by lines):
1-3 The sun rises in the ocean in order to cuse many activities and thereby increase wealth which is useful to the world.
4-6 The description of talivaṉ's region where the gentle breeze blows touching the leaves and flowers of east indian kino tree and makes water drops in the branches to drip; they are compared to the sun.
7-10 The danger in the way of the big forest where the big elephant roams about.
11-14 talivaṉ coming in that path full of dense darkness with a lance only as his companion and driving away the bees that hum on his garland.
15-17 Talivi being afrid of the guard kept by the forest-mother in the well-protected mansion of her father.
18-19 Talivi telling talivaṉ that she was complining to her friend about his cruelty and not being bold enough to express her longing which she had concealed in her mind.
20-21 Friend replying to talivi about talivaṉ.
22-23 The friend being the counselling companion and keeping awake along with talivi at night.
Colophon(s):
Talimakaḷ told talimakaṉ at the secretly appointed place at night.

TRANSLATION
  • Chief of the land (6)
  • where the gently-moving wind pervades the cool drops and makes them shed (6)
  • in the big branches which are thriving and having bright sprouts of the east indian kino tree (5),
  • which blossoms in the evening which is resembling sapphire, (4)
  • to create abundance (3)
  • just like the sun of many rays which is the cuse of the earth yielding several fruitful wealth (1)
  • in order to give good activities to this worldd, climbs above clock-wise and is shining (2)
  • and appears on the big ocean (3).
  • I did not give expression to my longing concealing it in my mind (18)
  • at midnight being afrid of my mother (17)
  • who stays guarding me without leaving me alone in the spacious well-protected mansion (16)
  • of my father who has swift-moving chariots and abundant toddy like rin (15),
  • lest she should have any inkling of our clandestine love (17).
  • I mentioned about your cruelty of not coming at night to my friend (19).
  • She, on understanding my idea at that time (19)
  • replied, "Do not lament; our lover who parted from us (20),
  • will come quickly (21)"
  • and she, who was my counselling companion at night (22)
  • and was keeping awake along with me (23)
  • rejoiced at your arrival (23)
  • Her rejoicing is greater than your coming (14)
  • here at dead of night (13).
  • you came, holding a white lance in order to dispel the thick darkness (11)
  • in the way of the big forest (10)
  • where the big male elephant (10)
  • which lives in the fertile mountin (9)
  • roams about, (10)
  • being proud of its must resembling heavy rin (9)
  • neither joining the group of its own herd of big female elephants, (8)
  • nor its opposition being emaciated, and without its anger being reduced even after killing its enemy (7),
  • driving away the bees that settle on your beautiful garland (12)
  • made of just blossomed buds (13),
  • and hanging low on your chest (12).

NOTES

Special notes :- If the word tiruviṟpal katir is split into tirul pal katir, it will mean rinbow with many colours.

vayaṅku toḻil activities which help living beings to lead a good life.

The sapphire is compared to the darkness in the evening and the east indian kino tree with its red foliage and golden flowers in the evening are compared to the blue sea and the sun rising above the sea.

Elephants gin the upper hand at times in a fight with the tiger; this can be known from the following quotations in akam itself; ""irumpuli tolaitta peruṅkai vēḻattup, pulavu nāṟu pukar nutal kaḻuva''
""koṭuvarip, puliyoṭu porutu ciṉam ciṟantu valiyōṭu, uravuk kaḷiṟu otuṅkiya maruṅkil'' (akam. 272-1-2, 291-5-6).
""muṟam cevi maṟaip pāypu muraṇ ceyta puli ceṟṟu.................kūrnuti maṭuttu ataṉ, niṟaṉ cāṭi muraṇ tīrnta nīḷ maruppu eḻil yāṉai'' (kalittokai, 52-1-4).

Though there in no object for koṉṟu we must supply the word "tiger" from the foregoing quotations.

""That my friend's joy was more pleasant than your coming at night'' hinted that talivi's joy was pleasant to her. Talivi sid, ""Though I knew your excessive love towards me, I mentioned about your cruelty to my friend''. The friend replied "Do not lament, he will definitely come" She was talivi's conselling companion throughout night without sleeping; so talivi sid, "The joy that my friend felt on your coming was more pleasant than your coming"

In line 15 the father is described as having as much toddy as rin itself and swift moving chariots. In akam, stanza 271, lines 12, aviyaṉ, a chieftin is described as having" kaḷimali kaḷḷiṉ naṟṟēr aviyaṉ

- In this line both toddy and chariots are mentioned, but the new commentators has split kaḷḷiṉaṟṟēr as kaḷḷil naṟṟēr and they have given kaḷḷil as the place belonging to aviyaṉ.
cf. ""cēṟu cey māriyiṉ aḷikkum niṉ, cāṟu paṭu tiru viṉ naṉai makiḻāṉē'' (patiṟṟuppattu 65-16-17).
""iṉkaḷi naṟaviṉ iyaltēr naṉṉaṉ'' (akam. 173-16). 

  • (spreading) your many fragrant tresses of hair into which small garlands (6),
  • made of cool and sweet Kuvaḷai (kuvaḷai) flowers which have petals which may be likened to the eye (5)

  • dancing (4)

  • If the mother questioned me about the change in colour (9)

NOTES

pīli should be added to tōkai: The neck of the peacock is compared to the precious stone, sapphire.

iṉ tīm kural - very sweet voice; the first two words are what is called in Tamiḻ grammar "oru poruṭpaṉ moḻi'

As many peacocks with sweet voices join together, and dance spreading their tails, you should also join with your playmates, bathe in the mountain stream spreading your tresses of hair and singing. In the simile the peacocks are described as having sweet voices; in the upamēyam also we have to supply the words singing with sweet voices :

nīṭu here means depth, as neṭuñcuṉai denotes its length; ll 12.14- cf. ""vilkeḻu tāṉaip pacum pūṇ pāṇṭiyaṉ, kaḷiṟu aṇi vel koṭi kaṭuppak kāṇvara, oḷiṟuvaṉa iḻitarum uyarntu tōṉṟu aruvi'' (akam, 162-21-23);

The spider's web on the ñemai tree is also compared to the flag raised aloft on the elephant; akam 111-4-6. 

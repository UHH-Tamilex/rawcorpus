TRANSLATION
  • (Friend)! You do not bathe (7)
  • joining with your (4)
  • playmates in the long mountin pool which has deep water (7)
  • (spreading) your many fragrant tresses of hir into which small garlands (6),
  • made of cool and sweet Kuvaḷi (குவளை) flowers which have petals which may be likened to the eye (5)
  • are inserted (6)
  • like the eminent befitting beauty (4)
  • of the flock of peacocks having til of attractive feathers (2)
  • and necks of bright colour like the sapphire, (1),
  • dancing (4)
  • to the slow measure of time (3).
  • If the mother questioned me about the change in colour (9)
  • that has come over you who are besides with solitude (8),
  • having a mind that has suffered (8),
  • who are this nature (9),
  • alas (10),
  • what is the reply that I should give (to her), about the cuse of the distemper cused by the chief of the mountin of high peaks (15),
  • where the long and white streams that descends down the big mountins (12)
  • that are united (11)
  • appear like the moving white flag (14)
  • rised aloft on the elephant wearing a frontlet (13) ?

NOTES

பீலி should be added to தோகை: The neck of the peacock is compared to the precious stone, sapphire.

இன் தீம் குரல் - very sweet voice; the first two words are what is called in Tamiḻ grammar "ஒரு பொருட்பன் மொழி'

As many peacocks with sweet voices join together, and dance spreading their tils, you should also join with your playmates, bathe in the mountin stream spreading your tresses of hir and singing. In the simile the peacocks are described as having sweet voices; in the upamēyam also we have to supply the words singing with sweet voices :

நீடு here means depth, as நெடுஞ்சுனை denotes its length; ll 12.14- cf. ""வில்கெழு தானைப் பசும் பூண் பாண்டியன், களிறு அணி வெல் கொடி கடுப்பக் காண்வர, ஒளிறுவன இழிதரும் உயர்ந்து தோன்று அருவி'' (akam, 162-21-23);

The spider's web on the ñemi tree is also compared to the flag rised aloft on the elephant; akam 111-4-6. 

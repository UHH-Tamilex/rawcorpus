TRANSLATION
  • My dughter (1)!
  • Please see my present state of having been very much attached to talimakaḷ (1)
  • The parrots (4)
  • with red neck-stripes (3)
  • do not drink the sweet milk (4),
  • though talivi's friends keep it on their forearms wearing tight-fitting bright bangles by turns (2)
  • and coaxed it in many ways (3).
  • The circle of playmates (5)
  • who are as tender as the peacock (4)
  • and wear red-coloured jewels, do not play (5).
  • The earthern pots too do not put forth many flowers (6).
  • The portrits too drawn on the strong and beautiful wall and adorned with garlands of pearl to add to them beauty (7)
  • do not receive anything as offering (8).
  • In addition to the agony that I undergo on seeing these things (9)
  • which cuse me to suffer (8)
  • I who was thoughtless (9),
  • did not understand what the cruel girl had in mind (10)
  • and sid, `Darling wearing bangles ! adorn today your luxuriant tresses of hir (11).
  • (when I sid thus) she disliked me and became disheartened (12).
  • Having decorated the good and opulent house fresh (15),
  • which is as great as the city of Uṟanti of good and never perishing fame (14),
  • of the liberal Cōḻar who have an army as extensive as the ocean, (12)
  • and even though her relations were prepared to celebrate her marriage (16)
  • befitting her wealthy status (12),
  • she did not agree to that (15)
  • I suffer whether (26)
  • my dughter of great artlessness and respectively who was in every way equal to the youth (21)
  • who had a determined mind, great physical strength, a long vēl of great handle, and a shield into which many balls were fixed, who led her in the different curam in a strange country (20),
  • in the long stretch of a forest where ōmi trees of forked trunk grow to a great height (17)
  • became united with the talivaṉ (26),
  • having removed the anklets by performing a ceremony for that (25)
  • in the strange and empty house (25)
  • which has a frontyard supported by a single pillar to which is tied a single cow (24),
  • which is the hut thatched with grass, of a poor woman (23)
  • in the small village (22)
  • without respectability and greatness (22)

NOTES

It is natural for ladies to keep the parrot on the forearm and fondle it ""centārp paiṅkiḷi muṉkai ēnti'' (akam, 34-14); Parrot was the pet bird of talivi; ""kiḷiyum pantum kaḻaṅkum veyyōḷ'' (akam 49-1); ""itu eṉ paiṅkiḷi eṭutta paiṅkiḷi' (ainkuṟunūṟu, 375-2) tāḻi an earthern pot in which flower plants and other plants are grown; tāḻi mukaṟ kalitta kōḻilaip parutti'' tāḻikkuvaḷai vāṭu malar cūṭṭi'' ""ōṅku nīlaittāḻi malkac cārttik kuṭai aṭai; nīriṉ maṭaiyiṉaḷ eṭutta, pantar vayalai'' (akam, 129-7, 165-11, 275-1-3) ""tāḻikkuvaḷaiyoṭu taṇ ceṅkaḻunīr'', ""tāḻik kuvaḷai taṇ ceṅkaḻunīr'' (cilappatikāram, 4-64, 5-192) In olden days it was the custom to draw the portrits of deities on the walls; ""cuvar micaic, cārttiyum vaittum toḻuvār ulakaḷanta mūrtti uruvē mutal'' (mutaṟṟiruvantāti, 14); ""cuvaril purāṇa niṉpōr eḻutic cuṟavanaṟ koṭikaḷum turaṅkaṅkaḷum, kavarippiṇākkaḷum karumpuvillum kāṭṭit tantēṉ kaṇṭāy kāmatēvā'' (nācciyār tirumoḻi, 4) The incidents mentioned in ll 2.8. agonized the foster-mother. The house will be newly decorated on the occasion of marriage; ""tāyē, puṉai māṇ iñci pūval ūṭṭi, maṉai maṇal aṭuttu mālai nāṟṟi, uvantu iṉitu ayarum amma (akam, 195-2-5); putuvatu puṉaintu may be interpreted in this way also; having adorned the talivi in a novel way. Possessing only one cow was considered to be a sign of poverty; ""ōrāṉ valcic cīril vāḻkkai, peru nalakkuṟumakaḷ vanteṉa, iṉi viḻavu āyiṟṟu eṉṉum iv ūrē'' (Kuṟuntoki, 295-4-6) cilampuṭaṉ kaḻīi see notes for akam, 315-8; ""aripeytu potinta teri cilampu kaḻīi'' (akam, 321-15) ""ciṟuvaṉ kaṇṇi cilampu kaḻīi, aṟiyāt tēettaḷ ākutal koṭitē'' (akam, 385-17-18); ""cilampu kaḻīiya celvam, piṟaruḻaik kaḻinta eṉ āyiḻai aṭiyē'' (naṟṟiṇi 279-10-11) 18. mākkāḻ neṭuvēl: ""maṭaiyamai tiṇcurai mākkāḻ neṭuvēl'' (akam, 119-13) iṉaivataṉ talaiyum mēyiṉaḷ kol eṉa nōval this is the syntactical link

maṇṇal: a verb in the optative mood. ciṟappu, cīr: ""cīrmai ciṟappoṭu nīṅkum'' (Kuṟaḷ, 195) taṟṟaka should be added along with tamar maṇaṉ ayara.

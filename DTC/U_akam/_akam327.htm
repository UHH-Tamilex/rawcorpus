TRANSLATION
  • Wavering mind (6)!
  • As you have understood that pleasure and pin, the act of uniting and separation (1)
  • are of varying natures (3)
  • just like the bright daytime and night (2)
  • and are dissymetrically opposed to each other (3-4),
  • is it proper for you (6)
  • to go into the paths of the unpleasant curam (5)
  • leaving this lady (4),
  • being goaded by the good ambition of acquiring wealth (5) ?
  • If you had decided definitely to go into the desert tract (7)
  • will you remember us (8)
  • at least for a little while (7)
  • after having gone beyond the forest in which bamboos grow densely (19).
  • That forest has ways where the robbers who killed travellers without pity (17)
  • after following them feel sorry only for the loss of their arrows, in the branching paths (16).
  • There the crows which have the colour of the dark cloud and eyes as blue as the sapphire (15)
  • perching on the trees (14)
  • eat with relish the fresh fat of the fallen corpses lying (16)
  • in the paths. Those trees whose banches are dried up and have anthills round their trunk (14)
  • grow in the high places of rocks where slough with beautiful lines cast off by snakes are found (13)
  • Those sloughs resemble the washing water rolling down a steep hill (12)
  • in the vast expansive area where saline soil stretches to a great distance (11).
  • In that curam maral (maral: bowstring hemp) crumples (10)
  • and the beasts desirous of water (10)
  • mistaking the waves of vapour produced (9)
  • by the hot rays of the sun, to be water (8)
  • wander very much running here and there (10).

NOTES

Day and night are compared to pleasure and pin, the act of uniting and separation - as they come one after the other in quick succession.

iṉpamum iṭumpaiyum māṟu etirntuḷa, pirivum puṇarvum māṟu etirntuḷa: This is how it should be taken. The act of uniting and the pleasure derived from it, separation and the distress resulting from it : this is also another way of interpreting the first line; Then we have to take that pleasure and pin are respectively like day and night.

Talivaṉ told his mind "You who are enjoying pleasure in the company of talivi, will certinly suffer from pin if you part from her"

tuṇivu - resolution; it may also mean clearness.

The skin cast off by the snake is compared to the mountin stream; this simile is found in Kuṟuntoki; ""pāmpiṉ, tūṅkutōl kaṭukkum tū veḷḷaruvi'' (235-1-2).

The variant reading in line 8 "niṉaitalum niṉaitiyō - is a better one as such usages are found in the following,
""iṉiyāṉ, uṇṇalum uṇṇēṉ vāḻalum vāḻēṉ'' (Kalittoki, 23-6-7);
""kaṟaimiṭaṟu aṇiyalum ayintaṉṟu'',
""avvurut taṉṉuḷ aṭakkik karakkiṉum karakkum'' (puṟam - 1-5, 7-8);
""iyaṅkalum iyaṅkum mayaṅkalum mayaṅkum'' (cilappatikāram, 22-154);
""cuḻalalum cuḻalum, ōṭalum ōṭum'' (maṇimēkali 3-110-111, 113). 

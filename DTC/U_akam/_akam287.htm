4-6 The deserted altar in the village temple where offerings are forgotten.

TRANSLATION
  • In the vast expanse where rin has completely failed (9),
  • is the temple on which the creeper calabash (curaikkoṭi) spreads, (5)
  • and which has a pillar of black bottom, and shaking position (3)
  • as a result of the male of the Indian elk rubbing its body aginst it (4);
  • the inhabitants of the small villages of beautiful dwelling places (5)
  • forgot to give offerings in the early morning (6)
  • and as a result of that the altar constructed of brick has become clean white (6);
  • it is surrounded by a banyan tree having a trunk of rough cracked surface like a wall of enclosure (7).
  • The solitary large aerial root of that tree dashed by the western wind (8)
  • the sound of which makes the twin pigeons, male and female, to flee from there (9).
  • The stag licks the back of the beautiful female deer of cylindrical legs, (10).
  • We are looking at it staying in the diminishing shade of lines cast by the tall branches of the yā tree of shaking nature (11)
  • In the evening which appears like petty chieftins and inflicts pin, (13)
  • we are alone and suffer in this never-ending long way (14);
  • Will (our talivi), having collected and closed up all her fingers as in prayer in the wrist on which are worn bangles (1),
  • be suffering (3)
  • with the daytime only as her companion with a mind performing penance (12)
  • (to see us back) ? She has to be pitied.

NOTES

tokuka viral kuvaii - This is what women do when they are very sad.

pakal tuṇaiyāka: means that there was no other companion.

talivan sid that the talivi was pitiable becuse he could not return quickly and remove her sorrow.

kantu:- a pillar set up in the bottom portion is black it is called māttāḷ The following quotations will explin it better; ""kalikeḻukaṭavuḷ kantam kaiviṭapa ""puṟṟuṭaic cuvara putalivar potiyaṟ, kaṭavuḷ pōki karuntāṭ kantattu'' (akam. 207-11-12); ""aruntiṟaṟ kaṭavuḷ tiruntupalikkantamum'', ""ilakoḷik kantamum'' (maṇi mēkalai, 6-60, 24-162).

am kuṭic cīṟūr This refers to its former flourishing state when it had beautiful houses. As the inhabitants had deserted that place the common place became dilapidated as there was none to give offerings in the early morning. So it had become the abode of the Indian elk and calabash also spread over it,

iṭṭikai brick; as the altar is constructed of bricks, it is called iṭṭikai

tūmaḻai- should be changed into maḻaitū; tū means failure.

The stag licked the back of the deer to lessen the heat. This sight brought back memories of talivi to talivaṉ.

Petty chieftins who suddenly appear on the scene after the death of a great king and before the ascendancy of his successive, and harass the people for a while, are compared to the evening; ""puṉ kaṇ mālaik kuṟum peṟin tōṭṭi'' (cilap.4-24); niṟaikaṭal maṉṭalam nēmi yuruṭṭiya, iṟai keḻu peruviṟal eñciya piṉṟaik, kaṭamkaṇ arinta kaiyarāki, iṭam toṟum palkiya maṉṉar pōla'' (peruṅkatai, 1. 33-45-48). 

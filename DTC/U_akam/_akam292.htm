TRANSLATION
  • Confidante (1)!
  • Seeing the distress which made my shining bangles to become loose, (7)
  • even our foster-mother who mistook it to be the effect of my being possessed by spirits, is wasting money, being whirled in mind (2).
  • I am ashamed (6)
  • of the performance of veṟi dance too by vēlaṉ who is in no way and that (5)
  • with the sole intention of sacrificing a young lamb (4)
  • which has just begun to eat small quantity of leafy food and has giving up drinking its mother's milk (3).
  • If the chief of the hilly region where the hunter of strong hands who watches the field sitting on a rised platform built on the rock, and drives away beasts and birds which cuse damage to the crop (10),
  • keenly observes (9)
  • the movement of the elephant (8)
  • which places its feet securely and firmly and goes to another field to graze at midnight, (8)
  • and discharges a small stone (11)
  • from a sling with great speed (11)
  • which goes swiftly like an arrow fixed with feathers (12),
  • scatters the blossomed bunches of east indian kino-tree, destroys the honey-comb (13)
  • and gets lodged inside the fruit of the jack-tree, does not marry me, please tell me what is to be done (1)

NOTES

Special notes :- vēṟu uṇaral

- means foster-mother mistaking the distress of talivi cused by talivaṉ to be cused by something else. poruḷ ukuttu ala marutal
- The mother being confused in mind even after wasting money on diviners who tell things by kaṭṭu, kaḻaṅku and on velaṉ, the disease of talivi is not cured. tuḷaku
- means the food of leaves of beasts; kuḷaku aruntum maṟi; tāy mulai peṟā amaṟi it means such a young lamb. atūum from the ummai we have to infer that talivi is ashamed of foster-mother's confusion also; great people will be ashamed of misdeeds committed by others. cellal(7) vēṟu uṇarntu(1) eṉak kūṭṭuk oṟṟi may be interpreted in this way also; knowing the movement by shadowing it; without being seen by it. uṭu feathers fixed to the arrow to give it more speed; "uṭu amaipa kaḻi-iṟaku amainta pakaḻi' cīvaka cintāmaṇi; 2191,nac.) ""uṭuvuṟum pakaḻi'' (kuṟiñcippāṭṭu. 170); uṭu. nāṇaik koḷḷum iṭam'' (kṣa.urai). It may be of interest to compare the following lines in Kalittoki by the same kapilar; ""piṭiyoṭu mēyum puṉcey yāṉai, aṭi otuṅku iyakkam kēṭṭa kāṉavaṉ, neṭuvarai āciṉip paṇavai ēṟik, kaṭuvicaik kavaṇaiyiṟ kalkai viṭutaliṉ, iṟu varai vēṅkai oḷvī citaṟi ākiṉi meṉpaḻam aḷintavai utirāt, tēṉ cey iṟāal tuḷaipaṭap pōki, naṟuvaṭimāviṉ paintuṇar uḻakkik, kulaiyuṭai vāḻaik koḻumaṭal kiḻiyāp, palaviṉ paḻattuḷ taṅkum, malaikeḻu veṟpaṉ'' (41-7-6) with the lines 8-14 in this poem.

Inner meaning:
The stone discharged by the hunter scatters the flowers of east indian kino-tree, destroys the honey-comb and gets lodged in the fruit of the jack tree. From this we have to infer the scandal about two lovers that arose on account of veṟi, spoiled talivaṉ's coming to visit talivi by secret appointment, destroyed our pleasure and stay always in our mind.

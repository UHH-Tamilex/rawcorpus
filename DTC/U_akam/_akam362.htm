TRANSLATION
  • Friend ! May you flourish (11)!
  • The rivers are swollen with floods (2)
  • as the cool water running from the rock-splits which have snakes, flows (2)
  • through the small tuṟi (tuṟai: ghat) (1)
  • having mixed with honey (2).
  • People do not travel that way (6)
  • as the strong tigress of sharp claws like the buds of murukku (murukku muḷḷu muruṅkai: red coral tree) is protecting the tiger of greenish eyes which received many wounds as it fought with the elephat of white tusk (3)
  • and is hiding itself in the mountin cave (4).
  • At this stage (6)
  • if the good natured talivaṉ who comes (vēl: lance) unmindful of the darkness that has enveloped our place (7),
  • returns disappointed without his desire being fulfilled (9)
  • I shall not survive today or even tonight (10)
  • The beautiful rays of the moon have spread on the mountin (15)
  • like the garland of pearls that lie on the extensive space of breasts adorned with yellow spots and like the shining white (14)
  • cool (13)
  • What is to be done by us ?

NOTES

pāmpuṭai viṭara paṉi nīr - explins the harmful nature of the water;

iṭṭut tuṟai, the fault of the tuṟi;

ṟāṟu niṟaintaṉa: the fault of the river.

tiṅkaḷaṅ katir nilam koṇṭaṉa - By this is meant that the rising of the moon is an obstacle to the iravukkuṟi (iravukkuṟi) As the talivaṉ who is good by all means comes to the secretly appointed place by night, and returns without union, I will not to live. Becuse of the bright moonlight iravukkuṟi (iravukkuṟi) is not possible. By saying `what can I do for this ' the friend hinted that talivaṉ should marry talivi early.

vāḻalaṉ This word is, in my opinion, a scribal error, becuse aṉ as a verb termination is a later usage for first person singular; it should be vāḻaleṉ. 

Lines: 1-6. Talivaṉ addressing his mind.

Lines: 7-9. The potiyil belonging to titiyaṉ, a chieftin.

Lines: 10-11. The group of tigers roar after eating the piece of flesh of the pig

Lines: 12-13. The ability of Kuṟavar to clim up steep hills to collect honey and they too are not able to climb up the high peaks in potiyil.

Lines: 14-15. Talivi compared to that potiyil.

Translation: My mind (6)! Will you faint (6) having stayed all alone like the snake beaten with a stick (5), shivering out of fear (4), like those who fall down from the top of a big mountin and put an end to their lives (4) as they are dashed and afflicted by Kāmam (longing) which is instiable (3), at dead of night (2), when the clouds having made the bright sunlight to decrease in severity in the day and spreading in the sky and emitting lighning to cleave them, poured excessive rins (2)? She who afflicted us is as difficult to attin (15) as the potiyil is difficult to enter (14). That potiyil has high peaks (13) which are not easy to climb even by the Kuṟavars (13) who try (13) to cut the honey-comb to collect the sweet honey which is at the topmost point of the rock. (12) It has adjacent hills where the tiger join together and roar after having eaten the piece of flesh (11) of the fearless pig having a horn resembling the crescent (10), in the thickest of dense tree where the water rolling down the mountin, descends down to the earth. (9) That potiyil belongs to titiyaṉ (a chieftin) who possesses swift-moving chariots and an army of shining lances (8), in the battle where swords get confused and elephants, roaring like thunder (7) get close together.

Syntactical link : - neñcē(6)! em aṇaṅkiyōḷ(15); potiyil pōlap(14) peṟalaruṅkuraiyaḷ(15); (ataṉai uṇarātu) pāṉāṭkaṅkul(2), ārākkāmam aṭū u niṉṟu alaippa(3) varai vīḻ nariṉ naṭuṅkik(4), kōl eṟi pāmpiṉ tēmpu ti kollō(6) titiyaṉatu(8) cāralaiyum(11) cimaiyaiyum uṭaiya(13) potiyil(14).

The talimakaṉ who returned being misled at night by the occurence of signs happening casually spoke to himself.

-paraṇar

Notes: : - The subject `cloud' has to be supplied for the ver "pā ay' "vacipu' should be changed into "vaciya'. People whose Kāmam out of control take the extreme step of putting an end to their lives by falling from the top of a hill, this implicit in the following verse in Kuṟuntoki; ""māveṉa maṭalum ūrpa; pūveṉak, kuviṇamukiḻ erukkaṅ kaṇṇiyum cūṭupa, maṟukiṉ ārkkavum paṭupa, piṟitum ākupa kāmam kāḻkkoḷiṉē'' (17); "kalakkam eṉpatu collattakātaṉa collutal; iṉṉum itaṉāṉē, talaimakaṟku āyiṉ ataṉiṉūṅku varuvatōr kalakkamum uḷatām eṉṟavāṟu; avaimaṭalūrtal; varaipāytalē eṉṟaṟ, ṟoṭak kattaṉ'' (tolkāppiyam, meyppāṭṭiyal, 22, pēraciriyar); ""virai ūr kuḻaliyar tanta kiḻikaip piṭittut, tarai ūr toṟum peṇṇai māmaṭal ūrvar; tavirntupiṉṉum, varaiyūrvar tañcaiyar kōṉ vāṇaṉ māraiṟayil vāṇutalē'' (tañcivāṇaṉ Kōvi, 102). iṟuvarai a big mountin; ""iṟu varai vīḻa'' (puṟapporuḷ veṇpāmāli, 146). pāmpu eṟikōliṉ

- should be taken as kōl eṟi pāmpiṉ. cf. ""kuṭumiyāka vāka piṟar kuṉṟu keḻunāṭē'' (puṟam, 58-32); In this line what the poet had in his mind is ""piṟarnāṭṭuk kuṉṟukaḷeṉṟatākac koḷka'' (the commentary for this line). The snake which is beaten with a stick will be prostrate; talivaṉ adresses his mind as being prostrate like that. The crescent is compared to the pig's horn for whiteness and curved nature. For the reading paṉaṟi kuṟaiyā
- the meaning is having killed the pig. kuḻumum
- is the appropriate word for the roaring of tigers. kaṅkulil tamiyai vaiki ēṉa iyaikka muyalunar
- is put in apposition with Kuṟavar. Kuṟavar are able to climb up steep and high peaks; even they could not climb up the peak of potiyil, denotes its height; titityaṉ who is mentioned in this poem is mentioned in akam 25, as "potiyiṟ celvaṉ polantērt titiyaṉ'' (l.20) We are not certin whether he was one of those seven people who fought at taliyālankaṉam with netuñceḻiyaṉ.

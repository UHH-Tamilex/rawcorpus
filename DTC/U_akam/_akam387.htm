Table of contents (by lines):
1-2. Talivaṉ parting from talivi to spoil her beauty.
3. Talivi requesting to tell about talivaṉ's return to those who can put up with separation and survive.
4-7. The waist of talivi wearing a beautiful cloth well-starched.
9-12. The description of the forest in which female of the partridge calls its male frightened by the calling sound of hunters.
13-15. Rows of memorial tablets erected in memory of warriors who lid down their lines in the act of rescuing cows from enemies.
15-19. The sound of lizard which is on the memorial stone hinders even Kings from proceeding further and makes them retrace their steps.
8,20. Wives who are looking at their spoiled beauty and putting up with their separation and surviving it.
Colophon(s):
The friend knowing the hint of talimakaḷ requested talimakaṉ to desist from parting.

TRANSLATION
  • Oh talivar who is parting away from me to make the cool, self-conceited eyes having lines shed tears, (2)
  • the flawless ornaments to slip off, and the big shoulderto become lean (1)!
  • In that curam (12)
  • where the female of the partridge having lines on its back, and has eyes resembling sapphire (10)
  • being frightened by the calling sound of the hunters (9)
  • of cruel actions (8)
  • who lie in wait in the intertwining low jungle (9)
  • calls its male which has a neck adorned with minute dots (11),
  • and red legs (12)
  • having claws as sharp as thorn (11)
  • If the old lizard which lay without winking desirous of prey (6)
  • joining the memorial tablets of warriors erected in a row, who lid down their lives and established their fame actuated by a high sense of honour (14),
  • having killed the enemies shattering their tough battle in which an army of archers came close (13),
  • hinders by its slight sound (7),
  • even the Kings who ride on elephants wearing big frontlets (18),
  • do not proceed further and return in the forest (9);
  • saying that our talivar went there, looking at the beautiful lines (8)
  • which are in the waist on which are worn strands of golden chins on which the beautiful cloth of floral border shines (7),
  • to which was added starch having knead it for a long while by the washerwoman who has fingers on which starch is sticking (6),
  • and sharp stined nils which removed the blots in the clothes (5)
  • joining with lads with bald heads from which hirs have disappeared being gnawed by fuller's earth; please tell your early return (3)
  • to those who have the strength to put up with the separation and can survive. (20)

NOTES

Notes:

vari in the blots on the cloth

koṉṟa kaṟai the stin cused by removing the blots.

pacai: ""pacai kol melviral peruntōṭ pulaitti'' (akam, 34-11) It is also called Kāti (kāṭi):
""kāṭi koṇṭa kaḻuvuṟu kaliṅkattut, tōṭu amai tūmaṭi viritta cēkkai'' neṭunalvāṭi (134-135)

pulaitti: In caṅkam literature only the female of the dhobi caste is mentioned;
""vaṟaṉ il pulaitti ellit tōytta, pukāp pukar koṇṭa puṉ pūṅkaliṅkamoṭu'' (naṟṟiṇi 90-3);
""navattakaip pulaitti pacai tōyt teṭuttu'' (Kuṟuntoki, 330-1);
""āṭai koṇṭu olikkum niṉ pulaitti kāṭṭu eṉṟāḷō'', (Kalittoki, 72-14),
""kaḷarppaṭu kūval tōṇṭināḷum, pulaitti kaḻī iya tūveḷ aṟuvai'' (puṟam, 311-1-2)

nōkki this is action of talimakaḷ

ital having a thorn on its claws is mentioned in akam;
""putal micaittaṉaviṉ ital muḷ cennaṉai'' (akam, 23-3).

maṟavar here denotes warriors who fought for rescuing cows from the hands of enemies, having adorned themselves with Karanti flowers.

The sound made by the lizard from an auspicious place is taken as a good omen;
""maṉaivayiṉ palliyum pāṅku ottu icaittaṉa'' (Kalittoki, 11-20-21);
""pāṅkarppalli paṭutoṟum paravi'', ""maṇi ōrttaṉṉa teṇkural, kaṇivāyp palliya kāṭu iṟantōrē''
""pakuvāyp palli paṭutoṟum paravi, nalla kūṟu eṉa naṭuṅki'',
""uḷḷutoṟum paṭūum palli, puḷḷut toḻutuṟaivi, cevimutalāṉē'' (akam 9-19, 151-14-15, 289-15-17. 351-16-17).

Even lower animals are to act according to the omen cused by the sound of lizard;
""mutaic cuvaṟ kalitta mūric centiṉai ōṅkuvaṇarp peruṅkural uṇīiya pāṅkarp pakuvāyp pallip pāṭu, ōrttuk kuṟukum puruvaip paṉṟi'', (akam, 88-1-4);
""muṉṉiyatu muṭittaṉamāyiṉ, "naṉṉutal', varuvam eṉṟa paruvaral tīrap, paṭum kol vāḻi neṭuñcuvarp palli''
""neṭuñcuvarp palliyum pāṅkil tēṟṟum'',
""uyar pukaḻ nal il oṇ cuvarp porunti,
""nayavarukurala palli, naḷḷeṉ yāmattu uḷḷu toṟum paṭumē'' (naṟṟiṇi 162-1-3, 246-2, 333-10-12)

teṟṟuvatu this may mean also the chirping of the lizard.

When the talimakaṉ told the friend, that he would return quickly, the friend replied to him,
"Please tell this to people who say, "our talivaṉ went into the forest" and looking at the lines getting spoiled, "and are able to bear the separation" and desisted him from parting. That talivi will not survive is implied.

The friend on behalf of talimakaḷ spoke as there was no difference between her and talimakaḷ
cf. ""cellāmai uṇṭēl eṉakkurai; maṟṟuniṉ, valvaruvu vāḻvārkku urai'' Kuṟaḷ 1151). 

Table of contents (by lines):
1-4. Talivaṉ returning home after acquiring wealth.
4-9. Description of the forest way through which talivaṉ returns.
10-14. The state of talivi in sorrowful mood.
15-16. Talivi taking the sound made by the lizard as a remedy for her distress.
17, 5. Talivaṉ wishing the lizard to bring to talivi's knowledge his home coming.
Colophon(s):
The talimakaṉ who was returning having finished acquiring wealth, spoke to his mind.

TRANSLATION
  • (My mind!) Though I am coming with a mind to return (9)
  • in the paths of the forest which is without splendour, which has iruppi (mahuna trees) trees of flowers having holes inside them (8)
  • which grow in the grove of beautiful leaves resembling tongues of flame (7)
  • in the low branches which are dashed by wind and which have dropped the leaves that have become loose (6),
  • as the sun's heat scorches them (5),
  • will the lizard which makes a sound every times she thinks of us, (6)
  • bring to the knowledge of talivi who is always praying for good omens, into her ears, its sound (17),
  • thinking it to be the remedy for the removal of the distress of her suffering shoulders (15),
  • who is sitting on the bed and lying on it (14),
  • fixing on her cheek the forearm wearing perfect ornaments (13);
  • when the little drops of tears which step aside and roll down from the cool eyes which are always weeping (11),
  • to strike at the golden ear-rings (12)
  • and to make the wall on which the signs of small circles (10)
  • created (by her) (9)
  • and to count the days of separation, (10)
  • become wet (11),
  • our return, being satisfied with the idea of having finished the business on which we went (4)
  • with a desiring mind, having acquired wealth, (3)
  • and having gone, being permitted to go by my dear relations who followed me, to give me a send-off (2)
  • bearing in mind the intense desire of staying in a foreign country having gone there (1)?

NOTES

kēḷir - in line 2 refers to the friend of talivi and others.

kuṟai - indispensable business that must be finished; ""payakkuṟai illait tām vāḻum nāḷē'' (puṟam, 188-7).

niṟaivu here means a mind full of satisfaction.

aṟivuṟūm kol - cusal form of verb.

maṟutaral uḷḷamoṭu kuṟukat tōṟṟiya - to inform our returning near the place with a completely satisfied mind; in that case tōṟṟiya should be taken as an adverbial participle.

iruntu aṇaimītu poruntuḻik kiṭakkai - in this line the order of words should be changed in the following manner; aṇaimītu iruntu, kiṭakkai poruntuḻi.

The sound made by the lizard was considered as a good and sometimes as a bad omen, ""muṉṉiyatu muṭittaṉa māyiṉ naṉṉutal! varuvam eṉṉum paruvaral tīrap, paṭum kol vāḻi, neṭuñ, tavarp palli, naḷḷi'', naḷḷeṉ yāmattu uḷḷu toṟum paṭumē'' - (naṟṟiṇi, 169-1-3, 246-2, 339-9-12); ""maṉaivayiṉ, palliyum pāṅku ottu icaittaṉa'' (Kalittoki, 11-19-20); ""pāṅkarp palli paṭutoṟum paravik, kaṉṟu puku mālai niṉṟōḷ eyti'', ""pakuvāyp palli paṭutoṟum paravi, nalla kūṟeṉa naṭuṅkip, pulleṉ mālaiyoṭu poruṅ kol tāṉē'' (akam, 9-19-20, 289-14-17); ""pūṅkaṇ iṭam āṭum kaṉavum, tiruntiṉa; ōṅkiya kuṉṟam iṟantārai yām niṉaippa vīṅkiya meṉ tōḷ kaviṉip piṇitīrap pāṅkattup, palli paṭum'' aintiṇi eḻupatu, 41); ""veñcuram ceṉrur varakkaṇṭu, vāy māṇṭa palli paṭum'' (Kainnili, 18).

Even inferior beasts and kings are prohibited from carrying out their intention if the lizard's sound is not auspicious; ""eym muḷḷaṉṉaṉa parū u mayir eruttiṉ, ceymm mēval ciṟukaṇ paṉṟi, ōṅku malaiviyaṉ puṉam papī iyar vīṅkupoṟi, nūḻai nuḻaiyum poḻutil tāḻātu, pāṅkarppakkattup palli paṭṭeṉa, mella mellap piṟakkē peyarntu taṉ; kal aḷaip paḷḷi vatiyum nāṭaṉ'' (naṟṟiṇi, 98-1-7; ""irai nacai ik kiṭanta mutuvāyp palli, ciṟiya teṟṟuvatāyiṉ periya, ōṭai yāṉai uyarntōrāyiṉum; niṉṟāṅkup peyarum kāṉam'', (akam, 387-16-20). 

Table of contents (by lines):
1-5. Talivaṉ following talivi and her friend in search of redress of his suffering, emaciated like an elephant which lost its female, and therefore refused eating its food.
6-9. Friend telling talivi that talivaṉ approached her to support him in his request for a favour.
10. Friend pleading with talivi that talivaṉ should be turned away and asking her to consent to his wish.
11-18. The chin of actions in the mountin due to the stone thrown from a sling by the Kuṟavaṉ.
19-28. Talivaṉ will not part from them; if he does, friend assures talivi to give back her beauty.

TRANSLATION
  • Friend (11)!
  • May you flourish (11)
  • The chief of the hilly tract of big stones where all the creatures in the mountin are frightened (18)
  • in the night when the whole forest ceased its activities, when the group of clouds rined with great noise (13)
  • when the Kuṟavaṉ who is seated high above in the platform constructed in the mountin (14)
  • threw a stone from the sling, with a great speed in order to make the herd of elephants grazing the tiṉi to flee (13),
  • to make the ferocious tiger, and elephant to roar (16)
  • aloud (15),
  • to cuse the beautiful peacock living in the extensive forest to dance (17)
  • (mistaking it to be the sound of thunder), knew fully well the emaciated condition of his beautifu body (4)
  • which can be compared to the distress (3)
  • of the elephant of white tusks (2)
  • and big long hanging trunk (1)
  • which refused to take its food of leaves as it lost its loving female in the forest, did not dislike to wait on us in reverent submission and as he troubles me often it will be very unpleasant (10)
  • if he goes away to make us suffer (10)
  • saying, "you become a support on my behalf without any evil, to get at the talivi of lovable tenderness, and exquisite beauty like the doll made wholly of gold, is rare to part from us; moreover, if he perhaps happens to part from us due to some cuse quite foreign to his nature, then I shall be the person who shall agin unite with you your beauty by joining him with you (28)
  • like naṉṉaṉ who lived in the forest (27)
  • by the side of the mountin where the bamboos grow high to be desired by sight-seers (26),
  • who established his old fame (25)
  • (having fought with that King) so that his prominence (25)
  • due to the strength of the vēl be held in his hand (24),
  • might not be lost (25),
  • as the rampart of the permanent fortress which had him as his support (23),
  • could not withstand the onslught (24)
  • of the enemy King having a victorious vēl (21)
  • who stayed outside the fortress agin with an army of killing enemies in the battle (22),
  • who returned as his effort to capture it became ineffective (21)
  • Agree to his request (11).

NOTES

The male elephant placed its trunk on its tusks on account of the sorrow of having lost its female; this is natural for elephants.

மறுத்த உயக்கம் the refusal of the elephant to have its food is the effect of its distress

பண்பு in line 4 means beauty; it may mean also well proportioned body.

தாது means gold.

தீது means the evil of separation.

The word கூறி should be supplied after புகுகென to complete the sense.

The new commentators have interpreted "என்னும் தண்டுமாயின்' as `if talivaṉ leaves us even for a while; but it is better to say `as he troubles me with his request

this can be understood from the following lines from Kalittoki;
""கயமலர் உண் கண்ணாய்! காணாய் ஒருவன், வயமான் அடித் தேர்வான் போலத் தொடை மாண்ட, கண்ணியன் வில்லன் வருமென்னை நோக்குபு; முன்னத்திற் காட்டுதல், அல்லது தான் உற்ற நோய் உரைக் கல்லான், பெயரும் மற்பல் நாளும்'' (37-1-5).

For ஒல் the same people have given the meaning as `Listen to what I say with a mind to receive it'; it is better to say `Consent to his request'

சேணோன் one who is seated in a high place; he will be seated on a platform in a shed which cannot be reached by the trunk of the elephant even it it stretches it to its full length
""களிறு அணந்து எய்தாக் கல்முகை இதணத்து'' (akam, 308-9)

வெடி the sound produced by the stone thrown from the sling.

The whole mountin is frightened by the sound of the stone thrown from the sling to drive away elephant, by the roar of the tiger and the elephant. The peacock begins to dance thinking these sounds to be the sound of thunder.

The friend requested talivi to consent to make up talivaṉ's wants by saying "If talivaṉ leaves us asking me to be messenger to you on his behalf to support him, then his leaving us will be very unpleasant; but he will not leave us; more over if by chance he leaves us I shall unite him with you and restore to you your beauty"

வினை தவப் பெயர்ந்து having fled after being unable to fight

விழைதக ஓங்கிய கழை துஞ்சு மருங்கின் கான் அமர் நன்னன்
- naṉṉaṉ should have lived in a forest to enjoy the scarry in the forest or he should have been in hiding to prepare himself for a tough fight

பெற்ற and அமர் both qualify naṉṉaṉ.

An enemy King who besieged the fort belonging to naṉṉaṉ fought and fled having been defeated. He invaded agin and attacked the fort with a bigger army, the soldiers within the fort were not equal match to the army and unable to stem the attack, so naṉṉaṉ should have come out of the forest and infused courage in his soldiers and drove the King saved those within the fort and established his former fame. So the friend compared herself to naṉṉaṉ.

In akam, 345 an incident is mentioned about ēḻiṟkuṉṟu; The chieftin of the hill was blessed with a horse by Kāṉamar celvi (கானமர்செல்வி) ; In many places ēḻiṟkuṉṟam is sid to belong to naṉṉaṉ.
""பாரத்துத் தலைவன் ஆர நன்னன், ஏழில் நெடுவரைப் பாழிச் சிலம் பில்'',
""நன்னன் நன்னாட்டு, எழிற்குன்றத்துக் கவா அன்''
(akam, 152-12-13, 349-8-9);

Perhaps the incident mentioned in stanza 345-3-7, has some connection with the fight mentioned in this stanza ll 21-27. 

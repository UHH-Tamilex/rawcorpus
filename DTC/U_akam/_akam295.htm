TRANSLATION
  • Lady having fragrant tresses (21),
  • and cool, red-edged eyes with beauty shining forth (20),
  • which can be compared to the wet petals of the large flowered jasmine of winter (19)!
  • The groups of sellers of salt produced from the sea-water; (9)
  • crosses with difficulty the forest with stony paths, (8)
  • in the extensive place where the very hot bamboos split (3)
  • as the severe rays of the sun scorch them (2)
  • and as a result of that the peaks of hills break into parts (2);
  • the mountin pools get dried up; the earth is without water (1);
  • in that place the elephant resembling a hillock (6)
  • having big feet and striking fear as it had won a tiger in a fight, (5)
  • places its big trunk on its tusk which is white like the crescent moon (4),
  • having suffered and decreased in must, (5)
  • stays in a grove of many trees with its female; (7)
  • stays for a while for their tired bullocks to rest (10);
  • dig the hard ground (10)
  • and makes wells with wide mouths which were hollowed in the extensive space (11);
  • water flows from the wells to remove the weariness of travellers in that way; (12)
  • our talivaṟ crossed the forest difficult to travel (14)
  • situated in the hill of Pulli who wears tinkling anklets as a mark of valour (13),
  • where the vaṭukar living close together have strong bows to which arrows are fixed (15),
  • being joyful after drinking toddy and shout at the height of their hughtiness (16);
  • though he has gone beyond the region where a different language is spoken (17)
  • he will return and restore (18) the blemishless and glorious beauty (18)
  • of your big and soft shoulders on which the goddess of beauty resides permanently (22).

NOTES

As the earth is without its sub-soil water the mountin pools also get dried up:

akaiya - to split into many parts;
""kāyntu celaṟ kaḷai kalpakat teṟutaliṉ'' (akam.55-1);
""eri acaintaṉṉa-erikappu viṭṭāṟ pōli (akam. kaṭavuḷ vāḻttu, 10, paḻaiyavurai);

akaiya - to burn;
""akai eri yāṉātu'' (kalittokai. 139-26).

The heat was so great that not only did the bamboos get withered but also got split into many parts. It is natural for the male elephant to place its trunk on its tusk;
cf. ""valiyuṭai maruppiṉallāl vāraṉam taṭakkai vaiyātu'' (cīvaka cintāmaṇi; 2904).

In a fight with a tiger sometimes the elephant gets the upper hand;
""puliyoṭu porutu ciṉam ciṟantu valiyoṭu uravukkaḷiṟu otuṅkiya maruṅkiṉa'' (akam. 291-6-7);
""irum puli tolaitta peruṅkai vēḻttup pulavunāṟu pukar nutal kaḻuv); (akam. 272-1-2).

The salt sellers dug well in the hard ground to find water to quench their thirst;

pulli a chieftin who lived in vēṅkatam;
""kaṭumāṉ puliliya kāṭiṟan tōrē'' (naṟṟiṇai. 14-11);
""kaḻalpuṉai tiruntaṭik kaḷavar kōmāṉ, maḻapulam vaṇakkiya māvaṇ pulli, viḻavuṭai vaḻuccīr vēṅkaṭam peṟiṉum'
""kallā iḷaiyar perumakaṉ pulli, viyaṉtalai nal nāṭṭu vēṅkaṭam kaḻiyiṉum'',
""mā al yāṉai maṟap pōrp pulli, kāmpuṭai neṭu varai vēṅkaṭattumpar'',
""pulli nal nāṭṭu umpar celaruñcuram'',
""poyyā nallicai māvaṇ pulli....... peruvarai attam'',
""nirai pala taḻī iya neṭu moḻip pulli, tēṉ tūṅku uyar varai nal nāṭṭu umpar, vēṅkaṭam'' (akam. 61-11-13, 83-9-10, 209-8-9, 311-11-12, 359-12-16, 293-18-20);
""pulliya, vēṅkaṭa viṟal varaip paṭṭa ōṅkal vāṉattu'' (puṟam. 385-101-12).

In line 13 kuṉṟu refers to (vēṅkaṭam) veṅkaṭam, the northern boundary of tamiḻakam.
In line 2, for kuṉṟu akaiya. the right meaning is the tops of hills break into two or more parts due to excessive heat.

puṭaiyal - garland;
""irumpaṉam puṭaiyal " (puṟam 99-5)
("" aṭaṅkiya puṭaiyal polaṅkaḻal nōṉ tāḷ'', "
"mā irum paṉam puṭaiyal īkai vāṉkaḻal'',
""irum paṉam puṭaiyaloṭu vāṉ kaḻal civappa'',
""puṭaiyal am kaḻaṟkāl'' (patiṟṟuppattu, 31-31-37-8, 42-1, 57-2, 80-7).

Beyond veṅkatam other languages were spoken; so that part of the country is called "moḻipeyar tē em!

The petals of large flowered jasmine is compared to eyes;
""malipeṟ kalitta mārippittikattuk, koyalaru nilaiya peyalēr maṇamukaic cevverin uṟaḻum koḻuṅ kaṭai maḻaikkaṇ'' (akam. 42-1-3);
""mārippittikattu nīrvār koḻumukaic cev verin uṟaḻum koḻuṅkaṭai maḻaikkaṇ' (kuṟuntokai, 222-5-6).

Ancient tamiḻar considered big shoulders as beautiful for women;
""akalalkul tōḷkaṇ eṉa mūvaḻip peruki'' (kalittokai, 108-2).

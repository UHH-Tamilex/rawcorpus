(1-6) The description of desert where the cricket makes a harsh sound in the vaṟṟal (vaṟṟalmaram) tree, and the blood-sucker with a golden head hops on the white ñemi (ñemaimaram) as it could not bear the excessive heat
(6-10) talivi went into the desert where the wounded elephant roars like thunder which is produced in the top of peaks
(11-14) The mast wood tree (puṉṉai maram) belonging to titiyaṉ (titiyaṉ) which was cut down by aṉṉi (aṉṉi) in the battle of kurukki (kuṟukkai) compared to the fostermother's hands which were cursed by her
(14-17) Description of the house of talivi's father
(18-22) The respect of talivi towards her foster-mother and the mother cursing her hands which beat talimakaḷ
Colophon(s):
The foster-mother who cused her dughter to go to desert spoke to herself.

TRANSLATION
  • The blood-sucker which has a head like gold and which lives in the vaṟṟal (vaṟṟalmaram) tree (3)
  • which has a hollow trunk and which is completely withered from roots upwards (1),
  • where the cricket makes a loud noise like the sound of the bells tied in a chariot, (2)
  • hops slowly (5)
  • in the places which have lost their beauty due to sun's heat (4)
  • as it suffers (4)
  • in the extensive forest of white ñemi (ñemaimaram), tree (5),
  • People say my dughter who deserves to be pitied (6)
  • crossed the difficult desert region (10)
  • in whose paths people do not travel, with him (talivaṉ) (6)
  • In that desert region (10)
  • in whose paths people do not travel, with him (talivaṉ) (6)
  • In that desert region the elephants which were badly wounded in their fight with tiger having stripes like a sickle (7)
  • have their dots on their faces destroyed (8)
  • and roar like thunder which is produced (by clouds) on the tops of peaks of hills, (9)
  • while blood flows down their faces (8).
  • In her father's extensive mansion which has splenty of food (17),
  • where the drum muḻavu is always played without rest, where he leads a life enjoying his wealth, (16)
  • the mirthful peacock (14)
  • makes a sound (15)
  • My dughter feels tired if she walks for a little while by placing a few continuous steps in that mansion (18).
  • My hands which cught hold of her short and luxuriant tresses having five divisions, with the garland that was adorned on it (18-9),
  • inflicted pin on my mother (dughter) who has eyes as if fighting with each other, without compassion. Even when (19-20)
  • I beat her with a stick on her back till it broke into several pieces, she stood without moving (20)
  • and did not think, `This small back does not belong to me as of right' (21).
  • Let those hands which inflicted pin (22)
  • undergo suffering like the mast wood tree (puṉṉaimaram) which has good and soft bunches of flowers, (13)
  • which belonged to titiyaṉ, () (11)
  • and which was cut down including its old and bulky trunk (12)
  • in the battle field of kuṟukki (kuṟukkaip paṟantalai) by aṉṉi (aṉṉi) (11)
  • of great fame (10).

Table of contents (by lines):
1-3. The description of the dull evening.
4-6. The elephants injures its nail by kicking the hero-stones, thinking them to be actual human being.
7-8. The hot curam where highway robbers, find no means to remove their poverty as none travels that way, in which they lie in ambush with an effort to discharge arrows.
9-11. Talaivaṉ rebuking his mind for thinking of talaivi in the curam.
12-15. The tresses of hair of talaivi which are as fragrant as the flowers of venkai, which was donated to Kaḻuvuḷ by a pūtam and growing in Kāmūr.
Colophon(s):

TRANSLATION
  • My mind (11)!
  • May you flourish (11)!
  • you brought me in the severly hot and cruel way of the curam, (9)
  • which is the place where the merciless highway robbers (7)
  • do not find anybody who can remove their poverty as nobody travels, that way (8)
  • when they are hiding to discharge arrows with effort (7),
  • in the spot where the sharp nail of the wild elephant (5)
  • which kicked the hero-stone thinking it to be a human being (4)
  • is injured (5),
  • and breaks like the cover of the pulpy kernel of tender fruit of a big palmyra tree (6)
  • in the very angry evening which inflicts suffering (3)
  • with a dull appearance having despatched the sun (2)
  • to make the thick darkness spread in the widening sky (1),
  • and made not only yourslef unable to bear and made me also suffer; and having gone back from your prupose (10),
  • began to think about (11)
  • the beautiful artlessness of our talaivi who has a few tresses of hair (15)
  • on which the cool and fresh flowers of the vēṅkai (vēṅkai: east indian Kino tree) having a trunk of rough cracked surface which was given by a pūtam; (pūtam: demon) in the city of Kāmūr belonging to Kaḻuvuḷ of great munificence, having a victorious vēl.

NOTES

āl iruḷ - increasing darkness. It can be split into s vāṉam iruḷ pōkiya increased

mālaiyal veñcuram tanta

- is the order of words. It can be interpreted in this way also;

mālaiyil tuyar ceytu āṟṟāyāki,

katuvāy scar; ""katuvāy pōkiya tuti vāy eḵkamoṭu'' ""kāttuyar tol kuṭic katuvāyāka'' (peruṅkatai, 1-35-166 foot notes) ""arakkaṉ katuvāyttalaikaḷ pattu alaṟa yiṭakkaṇṭāṉ'', ""katuvāyttalaiyiṟ palanī koḷḷak kaṇṭāl aṭiyār kavalārō'' (tēvāram) ""kaṭal vāyc ceṉṟu mēkam kaviḻntu iṟaṅkikkatuvāyppaṭa nīr mukantu ēṟi'' (periyāḻvār, 3-5-4). The cause for the elephants nail becoming injured is its kicking the hero-stone. From this we can infer that the hero-stones were of human size.

itakkai - this is a rare word.

varunar iṉmaiyiṉ - this means that there are no people who travel that way on account of the cruel nature of the forest. Talaivaṉ rebuked his mind in the following manner: "You yourself who brought me without thinking of the cruel nature of the forest, go back on your resolve thinking about the beauty of talaivi. How is your idea ?

""kaḻuvuḷ the name of a petty chieftain, he is mentioned to be strong as to require the combined efforts of fourteen petty chieftains to overthrow him; akam, 135-11-13). He is mentioned in two places in patiṟṟuppattu; ""āṉpayaṉ vāḻnar kaḻuvuḷ talaimaṅka'' ""poru muraṇ eytiya kaḻuvuḷ puṟam peṟṟu'' (71-17, 88-7). From the old commentary on pattiṟṟuppattu we learn that he was chief of the shepherds, giving trouble to the cera Kings. 

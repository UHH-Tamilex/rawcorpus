Lines: 1-2 Friend advising talivi

Lines: 3-5 To perpetuate the fame of Karantayār who rescued cows from vetciyār and lost their lives in that act.

Lines: 5-7 engraving the images of warriors who died in rescuing cows, in the natural stones which appear like set-up hero stones.

Lines: 9-13 The desert where the warriors worship the hero-stones by pouring water, by garlanding and by smearing turmeric.

Lines: 14 The flag end of the riny season in the mouth of ti (தை) (January 13th to Feb.13th

Lines: 15-20 Young girls bring and place the doll for their sport in the ghat where all drink water and perform the dance of Kuravi.

Lines: 21-24 The red indian water lily flowers in the paddy fields of ciṟukutī belonging to vāṇaṉ, compared to talivi's eyes

Lines: 25 The return of talivaṉ to wipe out the tears of talivi.

Translation: In order to perpetuate the good fame of the undunted and corageous Karuntayār (5) who lid down their lives in preventing the warrior who had victorious lances fitted into a tight hollow screw (4), not leaving the fighting place though the herds of cows with bulls were rescued (3) their comrades but the many idols hewn in the big space of the tall naturals stones (8) which appear like hero-stones set up for that purpose (7), in the small hillocks which have the appearance of female elephants lying on the ground (6), and smeared the turmeric on the wet surface, (9) and adorned the idols with the garlands of arrow head plant flowers of red colour (11) which were knitted (11) in a fibre of the bark of common mountin ebony which was got by cutting it with arrows, (10) the warriors who wearing anklets garland the stones for the bees to settle on them (13) and to hum (12), return to their places in the difficult desert (13); our talivar who has crossed that desert (13) has returned hurriedly to wipe out the tears rolling down your eyes (25) which resemble the bright red indian water lily flowers (24) which have unfolded their petals in the cool fields yielding fresh income (23) where the paddy crop is bent with the weight of the ears of corn in ciṟukuti (சிறுகுடி) belonging to vāṇaṉ, a place surrounded by sea-shore grove (21) There the young girls of beautiful foreheads, (20) and waist with rised sides, on which is worn an ornament of gold coins arranged in a row (15) on which swings from side to side the garment made of tender leaves and bright-coloured big bunches of flowers, (16) place the doll for vaṇṭal game at the ghat where all drink water (19) to the accompaniment of the clear music produced by long bugle (18) whose sound can be heard at a distance, and which has a large and round mouth (17), and perform the dance of Kuravi (குரவை) clashing each other's hands at the fag end of the cool rins in the month of ti (தை) (14). Let the bracelets rise up on the shoulders ! Let your distress be over (1) Adorn your black curly tresses with garlands (2)

The friend asseverated with talimakaḷ when she became during talivaṉ's separation

maturi marutaṉiḷanākaṉār

Notes: : From the first three lines we have to infer that the talivi had become lean during talivaṉ's absence and hence the shoulder ornaments had become loose; she was always full of sorrow; she did not adorn herself with flowers and garlands. Here மறவர் indicates warriors who lift cattle, தறுகணாளர் those who went to rescue them. The Karanti warriors having rescued the cows from vetci warriors, prevented the latter from lifting them agin and in that act lid down their lives. In order to perpetuate their memory and fame warriors belonging to their gorup chiselled their form in the stones and worshipped the hero-stones. Even before the Pallavas the people who spoke tamiḻ knew to use natural stones to chisel out idols. Young warriors used to bathe the hero-stones, smear turmeric over them, adorn them with garlands as they were considered demi-gods. Unmarried girls used to place the doll in the ghat and play and observe fast; It is found in Kalittoki, 59. Setting up hero-stones in memory of warriors who rescued cows and claves is mentioned in akam, stanza 131, ll.6-11; As the friend was very sure of talivaṉ's return, she sid in the past tense instead of using the future tense.

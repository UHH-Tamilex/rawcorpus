Table of contents (by lines):
1-5. Description of the peacock on whose tail the pollen of the vēṅkai tree sheds.
6-10. The peacocks run frightened by the bleating of the ram of the mountian sheep, and perch on the bamboos.
11-15. The three things that afflicts talaivi.
16-17. Friend wondering how talaivi manages to live.
18-19. Talaivi reply in her friend.
20-24. Talaivi manages to exist as the peak of the mountain of talaivaṉ is visible to her.
Colophon(s):
When the friend began to speak standing by the side of the fence at the secretly appointed place by night, talaimakaḷ spoke to her.

TRANSLATION
  • Friend (17)'.
  • You say "The solitude caused by the parting of chief of the big mountainous tract (11)
  • where the peacock of beautiful tail which has feathers made more beautiful (5),
  • by the shedding of fragrant pollen which is gold in colour, (4)
  • of the vēṅkai (vēṅkai maram: east indian Kino tree) of beautiful leaves (3)
  • which were shaken by the winds to absorb fragrance (2)
  • like the tresses of hair of the girls who were wedded in the tall house where great quantity of wealth lies buried (1),
  • perches on the waving bamboo of big joints, to make itself shine (10),
  • rising high in the sky, (9),
  • making a sweet sound like the vayir (vayir : bugle) blown in the dance arena (8),
  • as it runs being frightened by the bleating of the young ram which has full grown horns (7)
  • belonging to the species of the mountain sheep which are of different groups (6)
  • the angry looks of the hostile mother, (12)
  • and the evening of intense distress when the sun disappears in the western sea (15)
  • when its bright orbit becomes dim (14),
  • even when all the three combines together and inflict pain on you (16)
  • how are you able to live ?" Though our talaivar who made an oath not to leaves us, parted from us (18)
  • does not think of us, I still exist (19),
  • as the peak (belonging to talaivaṉ) which seems to reach the sky (24)
  • is visible, having worn the stream of sweet sound in the big and high mountain where deities of great powers, reside, and where the female monkey which has not learnt anything except jumping from branch to branch, which has in its hands the fruit of the jack tree of crowed leaves, having siezed it (20),
  • growing in his country, (19)
  • and loiters with its male :

NOTES

The first line is found without any change as line 478 in malaipaṭukaṭām, "vatuvaikku ellāmaṇamum uḷavātal paṟṟi vatuvai nāṟum eṉṟār'' (commentary nacciṉārkkiṉiyar on ll 30-31, malaipaṭukatām)

vaṅkūḻ wind, ""virai celaliyaṟkai vaṅkūḻāṭṭa'' (akam, 255-4)

As the pollen sheds on the feathers they look more attractive

varuṭai mountain sheeps, as there are severl kinds among them, the author says "vēṟu vēṟu iṉatta varuṭai';

in the commentary on cūttiram 32 of marapiyal, pēraciriyar says, "kiḷai eṉpaṉa eṇ kāl varuṭaiyum kuraṅkum pōlvaṉa; eṇ kāla āyiṉum mā veṉappaṭutaliṉ varuṭai kiḷai āyiṟṟu'

In the commentary on ll 502-503 of malaipuṭukatām nacciṉārkkiṉiyar writes, "mutu kiṭattē koṇṭa, nilattaik kaik koḷḷum celaviṉaiyuṭaiya vaḷainta kālaiyuṭaiya varaiyiṭattē vāḻum eṇkāl varuṭai'

viṟantu being frigtened; "avaṟṟuḷ, viṟappē verū up poruṭkum ākum'' (tolkappiyam II - uriiyal 49).

The subjects for naliya are pulampum, nōkkamum; mālaiyum

For the variant reading "uñaṟṟi' the meaning is `having endured them' `Though our talaivar who parted from us has not returned thinking of us I am able to bear my distress as the peak of his mountain is visible this was the reply given by talaivi to her friend cf. ""avar nāṭṭukkuṉṟam nōkkiṉeṉ tōḻi, paṇṭaiyaṟṟō kaṇṭiciṉ nutalē'' (Kuṟuntokai 249-3-5); ""tōḻi eṉ, neñ ciṟ pirinta tū u milarētam, kuṉṟa nōkkam kaṭintatum ilarē'' (tolkappiyam, Kaḷaviyal, 20, quoted by nacciṉārkkiṉiyar.

In line 19, the new commentators have adopted the reading uḷaḷē and added a note that the verbal suffix aṉ is used for 1st person singular; I think it to be scribal error; so I have adopted uḷeṉ as the correct reading. 

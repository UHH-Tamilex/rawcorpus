Table of contents (by lines):
(1-4) The description of warriors who drink intemperately the undistilled toddy secreted by the web of palms and got carried by the women who sell liquor,
(5-9) The dreaded path in which the elephant bends the creeper of atiral (அதிரல்) spreading on common cung (கோங்கு) which grows on the mound of gravel beneath which those who lost their lives by being killed by arrows, are buried
(9-10) The talivaṉ telling convincingly talivi that he would go in the path alone
(11-4). Talivi comparing herself to a portrit pinted in a common place (மன்றம்) which wears away by rin and is powdered by sun's heat and thereby loses its beautiful workmanship.
Colophon(s):

TRANSLATION
  • Companion (10)!
  • The dreadful paths (in the desert) (9)
  • have mounds of gravel under which those who lost their lives (5)
  • by the arrows discharged by red-eyed warriors who rise a tumultous war-cry, measure the undistilled toddy of clear colour which has been filtered by the fibrous cloth-like web of palms (3),
  • which was poured by the globular end of wide open pot (2)
  • and which was brought by the women who sell toddy, carried in their waist (1)
  • The elephants that live in the forest drags the creeper of wild jasmine (அதிரற் கொடி) with its buds which had blossomed at night spreading on the common cung (கோங்குமரம்) tree which grows on the mound of gravel, and eats it as a mouthful of food at early dawn when the thick darkness disappears.
  • Our talivar used to tell convincingly, in order that he may go alone in the desert, to those people who are capable of staying behind in their houses alone becoming lean and weak like the portrit that is drawn on the place of village common place which has lost its beauty as the battle fronts had evacuated people from their places, which wears away as it is washed by rin, becomes powdered by sun's head and loses its artistic beauty
  • (I am not capable of doing that; so I will die)

Table of contents (by lines):
1-5. Talivi finding fault with talivaṉ for prolonging his stay without understanding the difficulty of concealing the change coming over her body.
6-7. Talivaṉ took away talivi's beauty with him.
8-11. The male elephant which lost its female roars in contrast to the thunder, like the sound of the instruments of Kōtiyar.
12. The munificence of pulli.
13-16. The description of the curam in whihc talivaṉ had gone.

TRANSLATION
  • Friend (5)!
  • Why do you feel prostrate (5)
  • thinking that our taliv[an] prolonged his stay and did not return (4),
  • without understanding our nature in staying here concealing (3)
  • dily (2),
  • the eyes coated with collyrium from which tears flow (1),
  • and the shoulders on which sallowness has spread (1),
  • from being well known to, others (2),
  • as they are emaciated (2) ?
  • He (5)
  • has gone in the desert situated in the big mountin (16)
  • having taken with him our glorious beauty (7)
  • which can be compared to veḷiyam, (veḷiyam) a city belonging to vāṉavarampaṉ (vāṉavarampaṉ:) ceraṉ (6).
  • There the lustrous smoke which rises due to the forest being burnt which surrounds the table land which has cultivated ground (14)
  • which has green stalk of ever fresh income of varaku (varaku) common millet) of forked ears of grin (13),
  • which gets mingled with the tiny particles of streams (15)
  • In that curam where the male elephant of long legs which lost its female elephant loved by it (10),
  • roars in contrast to the thunder in the side of the mountin where deities reside desiring that place (11),
  • like the sound of the drum of Kōtiyar who rested in the branching paths of the difficult curam (desert region) (8)
  • which is in front of them (7),
  • and which rose on the big mountin (9)
  • It belongs to Pulli (pulli) of excessived munificence, of good fame, who never fails supplicants

NOTES

nāḷum can be added along with cāay

also avar is put in apposition with iyaṅkiyōr,

kōṭiyar iyam peruṅkal mīmicai eḻuntāṅku - is the order in which words are to be arranged

muṉāatu may means also the old curam, it can be also added with māṇalam; then it will mean our former glorious beauty.

iyam kal micai eḻutal - here means the echo produced in the mountin

pulli should be taken with peruvarai.

mūḻkiya is used in the sense of surrounded by.

The statement that talivaṉ took away with him talivi's glorious beauty suggests that it will return when he returns.

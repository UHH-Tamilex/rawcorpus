Table of contents (by lines):
Lines: 1-2. Talaivaṉ's mind bent upon acquiring wealth crossing many mountains.
Lines: 3. The hospitality of cowherds in Vēṅkatam
Lines: 4-6. The fallings of the common millet trodden by cattle of cloven hoofs.
Lines: 7. Heaping it in a clean place in the rock.
Lines: 8. The different stage of process in cooking varaku and placing the cooking pots on an improvised oven by piling stones one above the other.
Lines: 15-16. The food prepared out of varaku resembling in colour the pollen of koṉṟai (koṉṟai: indian laburnum).
Lines: 17-20. The Vēṅkatam of Pulli where the cowherds share with the travellers the cooked rice of varaku along with good cow's milk.
Lines: 20-26. Friend assuring talaivi that talaivar will not delay, forgetting the sweet sleep he had on her tresses of hair.
Colophon(s):
The friend asseverated with talaimakaḷ who became changed during separation.

TRANSLATION
  • Friend ! May you flourish ! (21)
  • The mothers of long ears and big shoulders having lines (7)
  • heap in a clean place in the rock of broad space (7)
  • the fallings from the many branches which were trodden upon by the cloven hoofs of cattle (6)
  • in the unsifted grain on the threshing floor of the forked ears of corn of common millet which has long blades of twin leaves collected with the stalks (5),
  • which grew abundantly in the plot of ground newly cultivated for dry crops (4),
  • the white rice which was sifted by the winnowing fan after the husk was pealed off in the winding screw (10)
  • to resemble in colour the grains of white fruits growing in the kīrai called paṇṇai (paṇṇaik kīrai) (9),
  • and directed the pestle of beautiful ferules to polish it by turns (11)
  • slightly by putting into the mortar the rice that filled the hollow interior (12)
  • to be pounded
  • They drew the water from the big pools at that place along with the rice (13)
  • and placed on the oven improvised by piling stones one upon another in pots made of clay (14).
  • In that Vēṅkatam which is beyond many good mountainous contries where honey-combs are hanging (19)
  • and which belongs to Pulli (pulli) of great fame, possessing many herds of cows (18)
  • the cowherds who cooked the food having single grains of boiled rice (6),
  • resembling in colour the pollen which was full in the dense branches of kaṭukkai (koṉṟai: indian laburnum tree) (15)
  • share (17)
  • with the new travellers in order to appease their burning hunger (3),
  • along with the milk of the good and plump cows (17)
  • Though our (talaivar) has gone beyond that Vēṅkaṭam (20)
  • with a determined mind reaching a different country (2)
  • to acquire wealth after crossing many mountains of rocks and high peak (1)
  • he will not delay (27)
  • there (20),
  • forgetting the sweet sleep born of union with you with pleasure (26)
  • in the cool and fragrant tresses of hair which was smeared with an aromatic unguent (23),
  • to resemble the thriving tail (22)
  • of the collected feathers (21)
  • of the beautiful peacock (22)
  • and on which were inserted choice petals of jasmine flowers that blossom in summer along with faultless flowers of Kuvaḷai (kuvaḷai: blue nelumbo flowers)

DIFFICULT WORDS
tōḻi! vāḻi
Friend ! May you flourish (our talaivar)
kōṭu uyar piṟaṅkal kuṉṟupala nīnti
- having crossed with difficulty many mountains having rocks and high peaks.
vēṟupulam paṭarnta viṉai taral uḷḷattu
- with a mind to acquire wealth by going to another country.
āṟu cel vampalar
- the new travellers
kāy paci tīriya
- to remove the burning hunger.
itai cuval kalitta
- which prospered in the plot of ground newly cultivated for dry crops.
īrilai neṭuntōṭṭuk kavaik katir varakiṉ
- of the varaku (common millet) which has forked ears of corn and long blades of two leaves
kāl toku poṅkaḻi
the grain collected together unsifted on the threshing floor with the stalks.
kavaṭṭu aṭip poruta pal ciṉai utirvai
- the fallings from many branches, which were trodden upon by the cloven hoofs of cattle
akaṉ kaṇ pāṟaic cevvayiṉ teṟīi
having heaped it in a clean place in the broad rocks
vari aṇi paṇaittōḷ vār cevi taṉṉaiyar
- the mothers who have longs ears and big shoulers with lines on them.
paṇṇai veṇ paḻattu arici ēyppa
- to resemble the rice of the white fruit of paṇṇai (a kind of greens)
cuḻal maram colitta kaḷaku alai veṇ kāḻ
- the white rice which was sifted by the winnowing fan after being crushed by the grinding screw.
toṭi māṇ ulakkai ūḻiṉ pōkki
- having directed the pestle of beautiful rings by turns.
ural mukam kāṭṭiya
- having polished after pouring it into the mortar
curai niṟai koḷḷai
- that rice which filled the hollow interior of the mortar.
āṅkaṇ iruñcuṉai nīroṭu mukantu
- having drawn it along with the water in the mountain pool at that place.
kaḷipaṭu kuḻicikkal aṭuppu ēṟṟi
having placed it on the oven constructed by piling stones one upon other in the pot made of clay.
iṇartatai kaṭukkai īṇṭiya tātiṉ
- like the pollen of the Katukkai (koṉṟai: indian laburnum tree) which has close bunches of flowers.
kuṭavar puḻukkiya poṅku aviḻppuṉkam
the food consisting of shining single grains of boiled rice cooked by cowherds.
matarvai nallāṉ pāloṭu pakukkum
- in the place where they share it with the milk of good and plump cows.
nirai pala kuḻīiya
possessing herds of cows.
neṭumoḻip pulli
- Pulli who swore oaths
tēṉ tūṅku uyarvarai nal nāṭṭu umpar
situated beyond the fertile countries having mountains where honey-combs are hanging
vēṅkaṭam iṟantaṉarāyiṉum
though (he) has gone beyond Vēṅkatam
āṇṭu avar nīṭalar
he will not delay there.
tōṭu koḷ, urukeḻu, maññai olicīr ēyppa
- to resemble the luxuriant collection of feathers of the beautiful peacock.
takaram maṇṇiya taṇ naṟu mucci
on the cool and fragrant tuft of hair on the head on which takaram (takaram: aromatic unguent) has been smeared
pukar il kuvaḷaip pōtoṭu teri itaḻ vēṉil atiral vēynta
- adorned with the faultless Kuvaḷai flowers and choice petals of wild jasmine which blossoms in summer
niṉ ēmuṟu puṇarcci iṉtuyil maṟantu
- forgetting the sweet sleep of union with you with pleasure.

NOTES
paṭarnta viṉaitaral uḷḷam mind that makes effort for acquisition of wealth having thought of it uḷḷattu-uḷḷattāl; uḷḷattāl vēṅkaṭam iṟantaṉarāyiṉum is the order of words. uḷḷattu
- may be added along with vampalar but it is not so good.
itaiccuval
- new high level land in the forest which was prepared and made suitable for cultivation of dry crops. The ear of corn of common millet (varaku varaku) are reaped with the stalks and they are heaped; therefore it is called "kavaikkatir varakiṉ kāl toku poṅkaḻi'; kāl stalk; poṅkaḻi unsifted grain on the threshing floor.
kavaṭṭu aṭi cloven hoofs of cows and buffaloes.
utirvai grains that have fallen.
teṟīi-teṟuttu;
""viruntiṉ maṉṉar aruṅkalam teṟuppa'' (akam, 54-1); "teṟuppa kuvippa' (the old gloss)
paṇṇai the place where girls play,
""eṇṇiya paṇṇai eṉṟu ivaṟ ṟoṭu piṟavum', ""paṇṇait tōṉṟiya eṇṇāṉku poruḷum'' tolkappiyam, Kaṟpiyal, 10; meyppāṭṭiyal, 1)
The fruit of paṇṇaik kīrai will be a white in colour like the spinner's spindle. The white rice of varaku which is got by sifting, resembles the rice in paṇṇai
cuḻal maram
- winding screw made of wood
colittal
- pealing off the husk.
ulakkai ūḻiṉ pōkki ural mukam kāṭṭiya
- this means that of the mortar or the interior of the pot.
kuṭavar- shepherds;
""kuṭavar pālar iṭaiyar eṉpa'' (cūṭamaṇi nikaṇtu, 2-69)
The verbal participle tīriya in line 3 modifies the adjectival participle pakukkum
vampalar paci tīriya(3) taṉṉaiyar(8) puṉkam(16) pāloṭu pakukkum(7) is the order of words
pāloṭu pakukkum, nirai pala kuḻī iya naṉṉāṭu eṉka;
pulli should go with naṉṉāṭu neṭumoḻi swearing an oath or boast as of a warrior
pulliyum vēṅkaṭamum see notes on akam 61-12-13.
The country under the rule of Pulli had a rich crop of varaku (varaku) is mentioned in these lines; ""poyyā nallicai māvaṇ pulli, kavaik katir varakiṉ yāṇarp paintāḷ, mutaic cuval mūḻkiya kāṉ cuṭu kurūup pukai'' (akam, 359-12-14)

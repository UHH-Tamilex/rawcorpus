TRANSLATION
  • The forest where the bamboos are turned into charcoal (4)
  • where the sun which moves with severe heat scorching everything, splits the rocks (1)
  • where the tender pith of īntū (īntu) withers and decays in the entensive area of excessive heat (2),
  • where nobody knows what kind of danger may befall one in any one particular place (4)
  • and where the hot pebbles of chisel-like tops run into the feet and cause pain (3);
  • there my daughter went with a youth of the desert tract (kāḷai) who can be compared to a strong male elephant. I do not feel sorry for that (5-6).
  • Having stayed here by being separated from her (6),
  • I breathe hot like the hole in the centre of a smith's forge for the nozzle of the bellow, and am languished in mind (7),
  • and do not get sleep to rare[MS UNREADABLE??] in my dreams as my burning heart distressed by grief, resembles a thing being burnt in fire (8-10).
  • When cēralātaṉ who fought against Karikāl vaḷavaṉ having shining weapons at the battle field of veṇṇi, he became ashamed of his wound; He immolated himself sitting face north wards with his sword by his side at that battle field where he was defeated (9-12)
  • Like those noble souls who heard about the sad as well as pleasant news and who put an end to their own lives along with him to enter the world of which is difficult of attainment, I have disliked my life which does not like to leave my body, being desirous of the pleasures of this world.

SYNTACTICAL LINK

yāṉ(6) eṉmakaḷ(5) kaḻintataṟku aḻintaṉṟō ileṉ(6); neñcamo evcuvaip(9) pōtal cellā eṉ uyiroṭu pulantēṉ(17).

The words may be joined syntactically in this way also: kaḻintataṟku(6) uyiroṭu pulantu(17) aḻintaṉṟō ileṉ(6). Then pulantu is an adverbial participle.

NOTES

In line 13. iṉṉā iṉṉurai: is paradoxical. It is sad news because a good king was immolating himself. It is pleasant news because he was putting an end to his life to wipe out the disgrace and humiliation caused by a wound

Note : In line 2, the modern commentators have split the words teṉutaliṉintu kurukurukum as tēṟutaliṉ nīntu kuruku urukum and the meaning is given as follows the birds that cross the desert suffer.
It is not good; īntu kuruku ukum: means even the tender pith of date palm withers and dies on account of the excessive heat though it is native to the soil and draught resisting) I have adopted the reading kurukukum It agrees with the alliteration eṉṟūḻ pulantu: I person singular verb in the past tense) 

TRANSLATION
  • (My mind!) pacumpūtpāṇṭiyaṉ has wealth prised by many (5),
  • strong shoulders of great victories which rose into fame by destroying the valour of the enemies who gave battle to him (4),
  • along with an impartial rule not swerving from righteousness (3),
  • among kings who have an army of great valour (2);
  • he rules over a country which is prised to be superior to others in the world which has very high mountins (1).
  • Let the intimacy that I have contracted (15)
  • with the talivi of māmi complexion who has tresses of hir of similitude and shining appearance (8),
  • a fragrant forehead which spreads smell like the bright Kāntal (kāntaḷmalar) which branches in the slopes of the potiyil (potiyil) which is high and in which deities reside (6),
  • belonging to that pāṇṭiyaṉ); though it is not possible to embrace her, when her shining group of bangles of fine workmanship in circle our well-formed back (9),
  • like the Kolli mountin of widening and dark rests (14)
  • which belongs to Poṟayaṉ who has a victorious vēl and strength that enemies are not come near him (13),
  • who devastated the countries of enemies to lay waste (12),
  • to always a counselling companion to the mind desirous of obtining her (9),
  • not perish (15).
  • Let the messengers (16)
  • sent to her (15)
  • be ceaselessly going (16)
  • many times more than the number of waves of the roaring big sea (21)
  • in the big ghat where they reach the shore and recede (20)
  • in the maritime town full of merchandise, which belongs to cōlaṉ who won battles in which enemies were combined aginst him (19)
  • and who is the chief of the young warriors who lift cows recently calved and who holds a brigh sword with strong hilt which was unsheathed (16)
  • in the battle to sent the enemies to heaven (17)

NOTES

vaippiṉ - should not be taken as an epithet qualifying nāṭu; the pāṇṭiya nātu has all the five regions
cf. ""aimpāl tiṇaiyum kaviṉi'' (maturikkāñci, 327).

mīkkūṟum: this adjectival participles should be added to nāṭu;
cf. ""kāṭcik keḷiyaṉ cuṭuñcollaṉ allaṉēl mīk kūṟum maṉṟiṉaṉ nilam'' (Kuṟaḷ, 386);
That country will be prised as being to superior to heaven;

""māntarañ cēral ōmpiya nāṭē, puttēṉ ulakattu aṟṟeṉak kēṭṭu vantu, iṉitu kaṇṭikiṉ'' (puṟam, 22-34-36) pacumpūtpāṇṭiyaṉ is the proper name of a pāṇtiyaṉ; he is mentioned Kuṟuntoki and in akanāṉūṟu;
""kūkaik kōḻi vākaip paṟantalaip, pacum pūṇ pāṇṭiyaṉ, viṉai val atikaṉ'' (Kuṟuntoki, 393-3-4);
""vilkeḻu tāṉaip pacum pūṇpāṇṭiyaṉ'',
""pacumpūṇpāṇṭiyaṉ pākupeṟu ciṟappiṉ kūṭal'',
""nāṭupala tanta pacum pūṇ pāṇṭiyaṉ, poṉ mali neṭu nakarc kūṭal'',
(akam, 162-21, 231-12-13, 253-5-6).

Some take pacum pūṇ - as an epithet qualifying pāṇṭiyaṉ; that it is not correct may be known from the above quotations.

poruppu: Though this is a common name, sometimes it denote potiyil, the abode of akattiyar;
""kāmar kaṭuntērp poruppaṉ'',
""vakai amartaṇṭārāṉ kōṭuyar poruppiṉ mēl';
ciṉai alar vēmpiṉ poruppaṉ poruta muṉai, araṇ'' (Kalittoki, 35-24, 57-14, 92-27-28)

Lines 9-10
cf. ""vaṉaintuvaral iḷamulai ñemuṅkap pal ūḻ, viḷaṅkutoṭi muṉkai vaḷaintu puṟam cuṟṟa'' (akam, 58-7-8)

poṟaiyaṉ kolli
- It originally belonged to ceraṉ. Ōri, a petty chieftin who is prised as one of the seven munificent persons of the ceṅkam age usurped it and ruled it for some times, Kāri, another of the above-mentioned seven, killed Ōri and restored it to ceraṉ; this can be understood from the following quotations from akam and puṟam;
""ōri, palpaḻappalaviṉ payam keḻukolli'' muṉḷūr maṉṉaṉ kaḻal toṭik kāri, cellā nallicai niṟutta valvil, ōriccoṉṉu cēralaṟku ītta, cel vērppalaviṉ payaṉ keḻu kolli'' (akam, 208-21-22, 209-12-15);
""valvil ōric kollik kuṭavaraip pāvaiyiṉ maṭavantaṉaḷē'' (Kuṟuntoki, 100-5-6);
""ōri kollō....ōṅpeṅ kollip porunaṉ'',
""kolliāṇṭa valvil ōriyum'' (puṟam, 152-12-31, 158-5).

The fight between kāri and ōri is mentioned in ciṟupāṇāṟṟuppaṭi as follows;
""kārikkutiraik kāriyoṭu malainta, ōrikkutirai ōriyum'' ($$l$$l.110-111).

It was a belief that soldiers who fought with valour and lid down their lives will ascend into a heaven called vīrasuvarkkam (vīracuvarkkam); That is hinted by
"poṟā ar viṇ peṟak kaḻittavāḷ;
""nōṟṟōr maṉṟa niṉ pakaivar niṉṉoṭu, māṟṟār eṉṉum peyar peṟṟu, āṟṟārāyiṉum āṇṭu vāḻvōrē''
(puṟam. 26-16-18)

The author of this poem who is supposed to be the father of nakkīrar has prised all the three Kings of tamiḻakam; his son has prised the three Kings and their capitals in the poem beginning ""kēḷkēṭu ūṉṟavum'' (akam, 93)

By saying "muyaṅkal iyaiyātāyiṉum' we have to infer that talivaṉ could not meet talivi as he was misled at night by signs happening casually.

By the phrase "neñcattu uyavuttuṇaiyāka' talivaṉ told his mind that he could desire pleasure by at least thinking about the intimacy, and by tūtu aṟāaliyar

- if messengers are sent several times it could be possible to embrace sometimes hence. 

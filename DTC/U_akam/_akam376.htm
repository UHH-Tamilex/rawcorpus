TRANSLATION
  • Chief (1) !
  • Do not go leaving me (1)
  • we won't commit the act of sulking (12)
  • when the harlot whom you loved went away seizing you, just like the Kaviri which carried away and hid in the sea (11),
  • enamoured of the beauty of atti (atti) who bathed desiring the freshes when he celebrated the festival of freshes (6),
  • when the beautiful anklets (7)
  • of sweet sound (6)
  • and bright spots rolled over his red feet (7),
  • and when the big cymbals made of gold made a sound along with the bells (9)
  • tied round the beautiful waist (8)
  • pleasing to look at, on which he had fastened the black girdle (8),
  • to be witnessses by Karikāl in the company of his relations of great bustle (5),
  • in the tuṟai (tuṟai) of Kaḻār which has fields having thriving ears of paddy (4),
  • and gardens of marutam (marutamaram: myrobalam tree) on which the floods dashed (3),
  • like the elephant not trained by the mahout, which learnt to bathe in the freshes (2)
  • Though we were so, see the forehead (13)
  • in us (2)
  • which became sallowish in colour (13)
  • You go, after returning the beauty of mine who can be compared to marantai (18)
  • belonging to Kuttuvaṉ who has horses of beautiful bending manes (12),
  • where the curved prawn which has soft parts on its head leaps (16)
  • so as to make the thriving and bending ears of paddy concealed within fertile sheaths to come out (15),
  • having caused the bright creeper of veḷḷai (vaḷḷai) which has beautiful tubes and which grows in the marshy and miry places (14)
  • to intertwine (13)
  • I owe many duties to you (1).

NOTES

Elephants are compared to atti who bathed in the freshes; ""pūḻiyar, kayanāṭu yāṉaiyiṉ mukaṉ amarntu āṅku, ēntu eḻil ākattup pūntār kuḻaiya, nerunal āṭiṉai puṉalē'', ""nerunal, tār pūṇ kaḷiṟṟiṉ talaip puṇai taḻīi... kōṭutōy malir niṟai āṭiyōrē'' (akam 6-8-11, 166-11-15)

The new commentators have taken yāṉai as the cause for the rising of level of water and its dashing against the marutam trees, though they have given as a secondary meaning comparison of elephant to atti.

The bells making a sound is common to bothe elephant and atti.

kaṇpatam koṇṭu tavirnta atti, puṉal nayantu āṭum atti eṉak kūṭṭi uraittalumām; then the meaning will be the following; As atti who was bathing in the freshes stayed there without returning with his relation, Kaviri carried him away enamoured of his beauty. There is no point of comparison to the simile, ""kāviri koṇṭu oḷittāṅku'';

we have to supply, ""cērip parattai niṉṉaik kavarntu ceṉṟāḷāka

maṉ this word can be treated as an expletive or as a clitic that suggests an implied meaning.

arantai the proper form of the word; the reason for determining that is it rhymes with kuraṅkuḷai in the penultimate line; this place has been mentioned in a previous stanza in akam; ""naṉṉakar marantai muṟṟattu oṉṉar'' (127-6); in patiṟṟupattu also this form of the word is clear, as it is placed to rhyme with iraṅku; ""iraṅku nīrp parappiṉ marantaiyōr poruna'' (90-28); marantai aṉṉanalam: ""tuṟai keḻu marantai aṉṉa ivaḷ nalam'' (naṟṟiṇai, 35-7) ""kuṭṭuvaṉ marantai'' (Kuruntokai 34-6);

Talaivi demanding the return of her beauty is based upon the line ""māṇ nalam tā eṉa vakuttaṟkaṇṇum'' (tolkāppiyam, Kaṟpiyal, 9-13)

This idea is contained in the following verses also; ""kaṭal keḻu marantai aṉṉa em, vēṭṭaṉaiyllaiyāl nalam tantu ceṉmē'' (naṟṟiṇai, 395-9-10) ""toṇṭi aṉṉa eṉ nalam tantu, koṇṭaṉai ceṉmō makiḻna niṉ cūḷē'', ""taṇṇan tuṟaivaṟ ṟoṭuttu nam nalam, koḷ vām eṉṟi'' (Kuṟuntokai, 238-4-5, 349-3-4); ""teṇṇīr iruṅkaḻi vēṇṭum irai māntip tuṟaivaṟkuraiyāy maṭa moḻi, vaṇṇantā eṉakam tokuttu'', ""nuṇ nāṇ valaiyiṟ paratavar pōttanta, pal mīṉ uṇaṅkal kavarum tuṟaivaṉaik kaṇṇiṉāṟ kāṇa amaiyum kol? eṉ tōḻi, vaṇṇam tā eṉkam toṭuttu'' (aintiṇai eḻupatu, 64, 66) 

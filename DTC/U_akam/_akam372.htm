TRANSLATION
  • My mind ! May you flourish (14)!
  • The talivi is as difficult to get as the wealth of rare ornaments (4),
  • which was hidden in the extensive city belonging to the ancient petty chieftins (4)
  • in Pāḻi which place strikes terror (in the minds of enemies) (3),
  • in the expansive mountin from the top of which one can have a view of the whole country, where big honey-combs are hanging (2)
  • being protected by the gods who have indestructible by others (1)
  • Though I sid, "We suffered vinly (6)
  • thinking about the pleasure we can derive from her (5)
  • you were not prepared to give up that idea (6)
  • you became very much tired on account of going and coming like the rope of the swing on which many sit and play (7)
  • which is tied to the big branch (7),
  • and which goes up and down (8)
  • you are resolute and weak like the leather-straps of the big tuṭi (tuṭi: a small drum) which are tightened and loosened which is beaten to produce a big sound in the fight during cattle-lifting (11)
  • by the warriors, holding bent bows with which they harass the countries of enemies (10);
  • who belong to Añci (añci) having a sharp vel and ruling the mountin of longy ways, called kutiri (kutiraimalai) (9),
  • like the cobra which spat out its jewel (on its head in the light of which it can search its prey) (13)
  • The lady who afflicted us in different to get (16)
  • as she is under strict guard (15)
  • like the fortification (16)
  • which is the cuse for reducing the hostilities (15)
  • of enemy kings (14)

NOTES

For the variant reading in .1. aruntiṟal the meaning is rare strength.

The honey combs are hanging always as they are protected by gods and are not stolen

nāṭu kāṇ naṉantalai! this phrase occurs in malipatukatam,
.270; ""nāṭukāṇ naṉantalai meṉ mela akaṉ miṉ''; the commentary on that line by nacciṉarkkiṉiyar is as follows; ""nāṭukaḷai yellām kāṇalāyirukkiṟa akaṉṟa iṭattaiyuṭaiya malaiyai mella mellap pōvīr; naṉantalai:

ākupeyar! This phrase occurs with a slight variation in patiṟṟuppattu, 85-7; ""kōṭu pala virinta nāṭukāṇ neṭuvarai''; the old commentary on this line; "nāṭukāṇ neṭuvarai' eṉṟu peyarāyiṟṟu In big cities they was a tall tower from the top of which a complete view of the city could be had; it is called "nakar kāṇ ēṇi'; ""cuvar cār vākatta tuṉṉupu niraitta, nakar kāṇ ēṇi viraivaṉar ēṟiṉar'' (peruṅkati, 27-46-47; Vacavatatti, the queen of utayanaṉ was shown her husbands city when she climbed up on the top of a mountin;
""kōṭuvāy cilampil koḻun cikaik kuṉṟiṉ, pāṭu amai paṭukāl paiya ēṟi, naṅkāy kāṇuṉ perumāṉ naṉṉakar untat ticaiya teṉṟu oṉṟap piṟavum; ukappak kūṟi mikappala varuṭṭi'' (peruṅkati, 2-10-70-74).
""cēṇ amarar kōṉ, vēṇu viṉai ēṇi nakar kāṇi tivi kāṇa naṭuvēṇu puramē'' (tēvāram, 2- patikam 325-2).

The incident mentioned in the first five lines has been mentioned in a previous stanza by the same author; ""naṉṉaṉ utiyaṉ aruṅkaṭip pāḻit'', toṉ mutir vēḷir ōmpiṉar vaitta, poṉṉiṉum arumai naṟku aṟintum aṉṉōṉ, tuṉṉalam mātō eṉiṉum aḵtu ollāy'' (akam, 258-1-4) This stanza is identical with 372 in its content and the central idea of the poem. The rope with which the swing is tied is compared to the mind of talivaṉ.

neṭu neṟi is an epithet to distinguish it from the animal "kutirai'; its chieftin was añci or eḻiṉi; ""ūrātu ēntiya kutiraik kūrvēl, kūviḷaṅ kaṇṇik koṭum pūṇ eḻuṉiyum'' (puṟam, 158-8-9); it was sometimes ruled by piṭṭan Koṟṟan; ""ūrāk kutiraik kūrvēl ... vaṭi navil ampiṉ villōr perum'' (Do. 168-14-16) This Koṟṟaṉ was commander of Cēraṉ's army; He is mentioned as such in two places in akam; ""vāṉavaṉ maṟavaṉ vaṇaṅkuvil taṭakkai; āṉā naṟaviṉ vaṇ makiḻp piṭṭaṉ'',
""vacaiyil vem pōr vāṉvaṉ maṟavaṉ, .... poyyā vāymaip puṉaikaḻaṟ piṭṭaṉ, maikavaḻ uyar cimaik kutiraik kavā aṉ'' (akam, 77-15-16, 143-10-13)

It is a popular fallacy that cobras have a jewel on their hood which they will spit at night to help them to catch their prey with the help of its light;
aravumiḻ maṇiyiṟ kuṟukār, niraitār mārpiṉ niṉ kēḷvaṉaip piṟarē (puram 294-8-9)
""nāma nallarāk katirpaṭa umiḻnta, mēymaṇi viḷakkiṟ pulāva īrkkum'',
""uraṉ aḻi pāmpiṉ tirumaṇi viḷkkiṉ'' (akam, 72-14-15, 92-11-12);

Kampar also has used this simile; iḻanta maṇi puṟṟaravetirnta teṉalāṉāḷ'' (5) (Sita was over whelmed with joy when she saw the ring of Rāman, as the cobra which got back the jewel it lost).

pāḻi is the name of a mountin belonging to naṉṉaṉ; ""naṉṉaṉ pāḻi aṉṉa kaṭiyuṭai viyal nakar'', ""kaṟaiyaṭi yāṉai naṉṉaṉ pāḻi'' ""naṉṉaṉ, eḻil neṭuvaraip pāḻic cilam pil'', ""naṉṉaṉ utiyaṉ aruṅkaṭippāḻi'', ""polam pūṇ naṉṉaṉ puṉṉāṭu kaṭinteṉa, yāḻicai maṟukiṟ pāḻi āṅkaṇ, añcal eṉṟa āy eyiṉaṉ'' (akam, 15-10-11, 142-9, 152-12-13, 258-1, 396-2-4) pāḻi malai iṭattākiya nakarilē vēl mutu makkaḷ karantu vaitta veṟukkai eṉavum āñciyiṉ āṭavar, māṟṟār āviṉaik kavarntu varum pūcalil eṟiyum tuṭi eṉavum iyaikka. 

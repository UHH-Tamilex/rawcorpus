TRANSLATION
  • Lady, with cool and shining tresses of hir (20),
  • who can be compared to the paṟampu mountin of naṉṉaṉ (19),
  • which has a ghat (tuṟai) in the front portion of which āmpal flowers grow abundantly where the mountin streams are swollen with water (8)
  • The chief of the village (5)
  • where the vessels into which toddy are poured are washed in the upper portion of the tuṟi (tuṟai: ghat) (1),
  • the tortoise with a splitted mouth (2)
  • leaps like the drum of clear eye fastened tightly (by straps) (4),
  • to drink the turbid water mixed with toddy (2)
  • in the lower portion of the tuṟi (tuṟai) (1),
  • the water fowl acting as a drummer, saw me quickly in the middle of the long street (5)
  • and cught hold of my forearm wearing gold bangles (6)
  • I shouted `mother' without leaving the desire which he had since a long time, he released my hands. I, having guarded my tongue strongly like the whetting stone (10)
  • made by the low whetstone-maker who mixes glue with stones (9)
  • in the paṟampu mountin of naṉṉaṉ (8).
  • I did not tell mother about it (11).
  • The descendant of Coḻar who have furious male elephants (12)
  • and good chariots (11),
  • who is the chief of Vallam(vallam) which has paddy of long ears (13),
  • and though he has good qualities his enemies (14)
  • try to capture him (15);
  • Leaving it aside (15),
  • one who has luxuriant tresses of hir (16)!
  • even if he afflicted us (17)
  • without any grace (16),
  • it will not be a defect (17)
  • what is your idea ? (please tell me) (20)

NOTES

koḷīiya

- the vessels that were used for pouring toddy; this is a verbal noun. $$l$$l 1-2,
cf. ""cempūttūya cetukkuṭai muṉṟil, kaḷḷaṭu makaḷir vaḷḷam nuṭakkiya, vārntuku ciṉṉīr'' (perumpāṇāṟṟuppaṭi, 338-340).

The tortoise drinking the water where the cups are washed is mentioned in akam;
""vaḷ ukir yāmai...akaṉtuṟaip, pakuvāy niṟaiya nuṅkiṟ kaḷḷiṉ, ukuvār aruntum'' (256-2-5).
The tortoise opens its mouth to drink the water mixed with toddy.
The tortoise is compared to the Kiṇi, a kind of drum;
The point of comparison is with regard to shape;
""kayattuvāḻ kāḻ kōttaṉṉa nuṇkōl, takaitta teṇkaṇ mākkiṇai valaiñar arikkural'' (""yāmai), miḷira'' (puṟam, 70-2-3, 249-4);
āmai akaṭu pōl aṅkaṇ taṭāri'', ""yāmai aḷḷakaṭṭaṉṉa arikkiṇai'' (puṟapporuḷ veṇpāmāli, 186, 206).

There is a variant reading in line 8 as "cālāmai', for that also the meaning is `without guarding what is to happen'.

Line 9. This simile is found in a previous stanza in akam itself;
""ciṟukārōṭaṉ, payiṉoṭu cōttiya kaṟpōṟ piriyalam'' (1-5-6)

The aṉpil plates give the name of cenkaṇāṉ's son, nallatikkōn (K. A. N. Sastri, The Coḻas p. 53; 2nd edition 1955 (Revised) Kulottunka III - V.R. Ramachandra Dikshitar p.10. 1941. If the person mentioned here is the same one mentioned in those plates this verse should have been composed after his time. It is worth noting that he is mentioned as a descendant of the Coḻa line. The enemies āriyar, besieging vallam and fleeing has been mentioned in akam verse, 336-20-22). There is a variant reading for line 14, it is better to adopt the reading nallaṭi in preference to nallaḷi; Then we get the proper name lso fo the chief of vallam.

Though talivaṉ who deserted us inflicted make it as an evil act. The question "what is your idea" means that talivi should consent to fulfil talivaṉ's desire. The variant reading in .14. ollār agrees in rhyme with nallaṭi; it is a better reading.

TRANSLATION
  • Lady, with cool and shining tresses of hir (20),
  • who can be compared to the paṟampu mountin of naṉṉaṉ (19),
  • which has a ghat (துறை) in the front portion of which āmpal flowers grow abundantly where the mountin streams are swollen with water (8)
  • The chief of the village (5)
  • where the vessels into which toddy are poured are washed in the upper portion of the tuṟi (துறை: ghat) (1),
  • the tortoise with a splitted mouth (2)
  • leaps like the drum of clear eye fastened tightly (by straps) (4),
  • to drink the turbid water mixed with toddy (2)
  • in the lower portion of the tuṟi (துறை) (1),
  • the water fowl acting as a drummer, saw me quickly in the middle of the long street (5)
  • and cught hold of my forearm wearing gold bangles (6)
  • I shouted `mother' without leaving the desire which he had since a long time, he released my hands. I, having guarded my tongue strongly like the whetting stone (10)
  • made by the low whetstone-maker who mixes glue with stones (9)
  • in the paṟampu mountin of naṉṉaṉ (8).
  • I did not tell mother about it (11).
  • The descendant of Coḻar who have furious male elephants (12)
  • and good chariots (11),
  • who is the chief of Vallam(வல்லம்) which has paddy of long ears (13),
  • and though he has good qualities his enemies (14)
  • try to capture him (15);
  • Leaving it aside (15),
  • one who has luxuriant tresses of hir (16)!
  • even if he afflicted us (17)
  • without any grace (16),
  • it will not be a defect (17)
  • what is your idea ? (please tell me) (20)

NOTES

கொளீஇய

- the vessels that were used for pouring toddy; this is a verbal noun. $$l$$l 1-2,
cf. ""செம்பூத்தூய செதுக்குடை முன்றில், கள்ளடு மகளிர் வள்ளம் நுடக்கிய, வார்ந்துகு சின்னீர்'' (perumpāṇāṟṟuppaṭi, 338-340).

The tortoise drinking the water where the cups are washed is mentioned in akam;
""வள் உகிர் யாமை...அகன்துறைப், பகுவாய் நிறைய நுங்கிற் கள்ளின், உகுவார் அருந்தும்'' (256-2-5).
The tortoise opens its mouth to drink the water mixed with toddy.
The tortoise is compared to the Kiṇi, a kind of drum;
The point of comparison is with regard to shape;
""கயத்துவாழ் காழ் கோத்தன்ன நுண்கோல், தகைத்த தெண்கண் மாக்கிணை வலைஞர் அரிக்குரல்'' (""யாமை), மிளிர'' (puṟam, 70-2-3, 249-4);
ஆமை அகடு போல் அங்கண் தடாரி'', ""யாமை அள்ளகட்டன்ன அரிக்கிணை'' (puṟapporuḷ veṇpāmāli, 186, 206).

There is a variant reading in line 8 as "சாலாமை', for that also the meaning is `without guarding what is to happen'.

Line 9. This simile is found in a previous stanza in akam itself;
""சிறுகாரோடன், பயினொடு சோத்திய கற்போற் பிரியலம்'' (1-5-6)

The aṉpil plates give the name of cenkaṇāṉ's son, nallatikkōn (K. A. N. Sastri, The Coḻas p. 53; 2nd edition 1955 (Revised) Kulottunka III - V.R. Ramachandra Dikshitar p.10. 1941. If the person mentioned here is the same one mentioned in those plates this verse should have been composed after his time. It is worth noting that he is mentioned as a descendant of the Coḻa line. The enemies āriyar, besieging vallam and fleeing has been mentioned in akam verse, 336-20-22). There is a variant reading for line 14, it is better to adopt the reading நல்லடி in preference to நல்லளி; Then we get the proper name lso fo the chief of vallam.

Though talivaṉ who deserted us inflicted make it as an evil act. The question "what is your idea" means that talivi should consent to fulfil talivaṉ's desire. The variant reading in .14. ஒல்லார் agrees in rhyme with நல்லடி; it is a better reading.

Table of contents (by lines):
1-5. The fight between other and vāli (scabbord fish), and the water in the pond becoming turbid.
6-10. The village women folk performing Kuravi (kuravai) dance under the Kāñci tree, and singing about the consorting of their husbands with harlots.
11-12. The harlot loved by talivaṉ accusing other harlots of abusing her beauty.
13-14. The fact that mahout lives long is due to the elephant sparing his life, out of grace.
15-17. The harlots of others going about is due to the fact of this harlot absenting herself from the festival where the tunaṅki (tuṇaṅkai) dance is performed.
18-21. The harlot loved by talivaṉ swearing that her bangles would break if she failed to cuse to whirl round her those people.

NOTES

Cempu is what is called in Tamiḻ nīrccēmpu which grows near the banks of streams and tanks. It is different from the other one which yields bullous roots and is used as food article.

paṟaḻ is a word denoting young one. The otter is partial towards vāḷi fish (scabbard fish); ""vāḷai mēyntavaḷ ḷeyiṟṟu nīr nāy'' (akam, 6-18); ""ariṟ pavarp pirampiṉ varippuṟa nīr nāy vāḷai nāḷirai peṟūum'' (Kuṟuntoki 381-1-2); ""oṇ ceṅkuralit taṇkayal kalaṅki, vāḷainīr nāy nāḷirai peṟūum'' (puṟam 283-1-2). ""pū nāṟu ilañcip porukayal ōṭṭi, nīr nāy kauviya neṭum puṟa vāḷai'' (cilappatikāram, 10-78-79).

parattaimai is an abstract now derived from parattai

pākaṉ This word will denote charioteer as well as the man who looks after the elephant; here it is used in the sense of mahout; elephant, though it moves for many years with the mahout it is capable to killing him for even a slight mistake; cf. ""yāṉai aṟintaṟintum pākaṉaiyē kollum'' (nālatiyar, 213); its ability to kill the mahout is expressed by the two epithets: "kāyciṉam and kol'. As the reason for the mahout living long is the elephant sparing him, the harlots and others moving about as if they possess eminence is due to this paratti not going to the festival.

māṟu : a clitic used in the sense of ētu (ētu); puṟam 4, 20, 26, 92-93, 271-380.

According to a stanza in paṇtikkōvi, ""veñcuṭar nōkkum neṭuñciyil ūraṉai veṇmuṟuval, ceñcuṭar vāṇ mukattāṉ muṉṉai eṉpāl tiriyalaṉēl, añcuṭar vēl arikēcari kōṉ aḷanāṭṭuṭaintār, tam kaṭar vāṭpaṭaipōla uṭaikāṉ caṅkaṅkaṉē'' (verse no. 316l M. V. Duriswamy ayyar, 1st edition, 1957)

the harlot prides upon the fact that she can make the chief of the village to whirl round her. The simile is taken from akam and there is a slight change about the person she will make to whirl round her.

That neruñci (cow's thorn) will always turn towards the direction of the sun is found in the following quotations; ""oṅkumalai nāṭaṉ, ñāyiṟu aṉaiyaṉ eṉtōḻi!, neruñci yaṉaiya eṉperum paṇait tōḷē'' (Kuṟuntoki, 315-2-4); ""pāḻūr neruñcip pacalai vāṉpū, ērtarukaṭariṉ etir koṇṭāṅku'' (puṟam, 155-4-5); ""aruntalarkakāyiṉum timantu mukamiṟaiñcātu, ceṅkatir, virumpum paiṅkoṭi neruñcip poṉpuṉai, putumalar (peruṅkati, 2-4-13-15); ""nīḷ cuṭar neṟiyai nōkkum nirai ikaḻ ceruñcippūp pōl'' (Civakacintāmani, 461); ēḻuḷaippuraviyoṭu eḻukatir nōkkiya, ciṟṟilai neruñcip poṟpū eṉṉa'' (Kallāṭam, 65-14-15).

The black cloud is compared to the shield in the quotations from akam; ""maḻai maruḷ pal tōl māvaṇ cōḻar'', ""māriyampiṉ maḻait tōṟ paḻaiyaṉ'', ""koḷḷai taṉaiya āki'' (123-10, 186-15, 278-1-3).

vallam is now a village some eight miles of south of Tanjore; a descendant of the coḻar dynasty by name nallaṭi is mentioned as the chief of that race of people or to any tribe :

puṟanāṉuṟu has a poem whose author is one āriyappaṭi katanta neṭuñceḻiyaṉ; he is spoken of as living at the time of Kōvalaṉ. patiṟṟuppattu has one line ""āriyar tuvaṉṟiya pēricai muḷḷūr'' (170-6)

one kind of dancers is called āriyar; cf. ""āriyar, kayiṟu āṭu paṟaiyiṟ kāl porak kalaṅki'' (Kuṟuntoki, 7-3-4)

In akam itself āriyar is used in the sense of people residing round about the Himālayas; ""āriyar alaṟat tākkip pēricait, toṉṟu mutir vaṭavarai vaṇaṅkuviṟ poṟittu'' (396-16-17);

In the second patikam of patiṟṟuppattu we find a line "pēricaimarapiṉ āriyar vaṇakki'' (7)

A kind of dance is called āriyakkūttu as aginst tamiḻkkūttu;

we find the names of the authors of poems 70,164, 256, 354 in akam and the author of poem 334 in puṟam as maturait tamiḻk kūttaṉ nātaṉ tēvaṉār, maturait tamiḻak kūttaṉ(ṉār) kaṭuvaṉ maṉṉaṉār and maturait tamiḻk kūttaṉār respectively.

There is a proverb in Tamiḻ āriyak kūttāṭiṉālum kāriyattē kaṇ'' which means though one performs āriyakkūttu his mind will be fixed on saving his life.

There is one cūtram quoted by aṭyārkkunallār; ""āriyam tamiḻeṉum cīrnaṭam iraṇṭiṉum'' (cilappatikāram, 3-13; p.89. Dr. V.Swaminatha Ayyar 6th edition, 1955).

Though the harlot sid, "Let my bangles be broken" the idea is she will break her bangles if she fails in her effort. 

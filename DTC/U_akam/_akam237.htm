(237) Pāli pācaval

- Rice flakes prepared from fresh harvested paddy. pakukkum apportion. puṉal poru putavu
- water sluices. tiraḷvī
- globular flowers. pōḻa
- to pierce through as if to cleave it.

(

Lines: 1-5) Description of the spring (

Lines: 5-6) talivi spending sleepless nights (

Lines: 7-8) Companion advising talivi not to give room to suffering

Lines: 8-14 The fertility of uṟanti.

Lines: 15-17 talivaṉ will not forget the water that secretes in the teeth of talivi.

Translation: Tālivi wearing beautiful ornaments ! (8) Let you desist from your suffering (8) to make the beautiful complexion of your good body, to disappear (7) becuse of the thoughts that nights pass away without sleep (5) and with sorrow (6) even druring the spring when the Indian cuckoo (Kuyil) begins to sing sweetly (5). (In that spring) the globular and striped flowers of trumpet flower tree of tawny-coloured trunk (1) makes the fine sand beautiful along with flowers of the thin hog-creeper (2); the southern gentle wind blows as if to split the fragrant branches of bottle flower tree (kurāmaram) on which bees settle humming and whose mature buds resemble the teeth of serpents; (3-4) Even if our lover is able to get uṟanti which has many water-sluices (14) aginst which waterr dashes, and where people share the rice flakes of green paddy mixed with milk (14) along with syrup of sugar obtined by crushing the sugar-cane which grows in the fields where the big ears of paddy whirl in the breeze (11-2) together with the earthern vessel resembling skull (into which is poured the food prepared out of soft millet in addition to pieces of meat contining fat (10) which were roasted in the well- lit fire (9), he will not stay behind on account of acquiring wealth (15) without enjoying the sweet water that flows from the sharp beautiful and white teeth belonging to you who has eyes whose edges are reddish (15) and set in to create wonder and coated with collyrium (16).

Syntactical link :- āyiḻai(8)! vēṉilum(5) kaṅkul iṉṉā kaḻiyu meṉṟu niṉ(6) mēṉi nalam pulampa(7) iṉaital āṉṟikiṉ(8); (nam talaivar) uṟantai eytiṉum(14) niṉ(16) vāl eyiṟu ūṟiya nīriṉai(17) viṉai poruḷākat tavirvilar(15).

The companion consoled the talimakal who became changed during the separation of talimakaṉ

tayaṅkaṇṇaṉār

Notes: :- puṉkāl may be added to vī and translated as small stem. ari can be translated as lines. As it is sid that kuyil begins to sing it denots spring. Spring is the season when lovers should not part from each other. eyiṟūṟiya nīr is what is called in Kāmesāstra as adharapānam. maṇṭai in an earthern vessel resembling skull in shape; so it derives its name by resemblance.

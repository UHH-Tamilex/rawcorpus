Table of contents (by lines):
1-3. Talivi wanted once to accompany talivaṉ, after the forest became cool after rins.
4-5. ēḻil hill which was sung by a poet who got a horses through the grace of `kāṉ amar celvi' (turkkai; Durga).
8-10. Talivaṉ promising to talke talivi saying. `Let some days pass after the arrival of the season but going away without taking her with him.
11-18. Talivi wishing the forest to put forth servants to adorn their beads and the nuṇa tree to beautify the paths in the red sand.
19-21. Talivaṉ might stay in the shade of the ways of the desert where the iṟṟi tree with a single aerial root sweeps the rock.

TRANSLATION
  • Friend (8)!.
  • We requested talivaṉ several times (2),
  • desirous of going along with him (3)
  • telling him, `Please take us also along with you' (2)
  • as the season has reached the proper cool state (2)
  • and as the heat has subsided due to clouds pouring rin (1)
  • (that being so) Though our talivar uttered falsehood to us sometime before and went away saying, `Let the few days of the season pass (10)
  • when we rejoice wearing on our beautiful and rised waists (9),
  • the garlands made of red flowers of the vēṅki (vēṅkaimaram) tree of black trunk (8)
  • which grows in the hill, called ēḻil (eḻil) on which groups of clouds tranverse (7)
  • and whose greatness was sung by a poet (6)
  • of old fame who composed works with very subtle meanings (6),
  • who received through the grace of kāṉ amar celvi (kāṉ amar celli; turkkai: Durga) of increasing fame, a horse (5)
  • of white legs (4)
  • with many saddles (5);
  • let the forest put on a beautiful appearance (18)
  • so that our talivar and his servant having perfect vēls (vēlkaṉ) who accompany our him (11),
  • may adorn their heads for the bees to drink the honey as the branches of tree sprouted to put forth big tender leaves (13),
  • and the white flowers of nuṇa (nuṇā - Indian mulberry tree) of black trunk (15)
  • which blossomed on account of the particles of water (falling on them) from the small white mountin streams in the hills; after the rins poured and stopped (14),
  • to make the small path in the red sand beatiful and fragrant (17),
  • so that our talivar might stay under the diminishing shade during sunlight (21),
  • in the ways of the desert (20)
  • where the single solitary and long aerial root of iṟṟi (iṟṟi: commonly known as icci) of tall nature (19)
  • which sweeps the surface of the rocks (20),
  • which gets weak (19)
  • by the moving wind (18).

NOTES

paṭutaliṉ uṭaṉ koṇṭu celka eṉa: some words must be supplied after "paṭutaliṉ

kāṉ amar celvi aruḷaliṉ puravi eytiya pulavaṉ pāṭiya ēḻiṟ kuṉṟam'
by this we learn the poet who sang the greatness of ēḻiḷ hill got through the grace of durga (turkkai) a horse, we also learn from this that the worship of durga is very old custom

ēḻiṟkuṉṟam: This belongs to naṉṉaṉ; and is situated on the west coast; cf. ""poṉpaṭu koṇkāṉa naṉṉaṉ ṉal kāṭṭu, eḻiṟkuṉṟam peṟiṉum'' (naṟṟiṇi, 391-6-7); ""em il ayalatu ēḻil umpar, mayil aṭi ilaiya mākkural nocci'' (Kuṟuntoki, 138-2-3; ""pārattut talaivaṉ āra naṉṉaṉ, ēḻil nēṭu varaip pāḻic cilampil'', ""naṉṉaṉ naṉṉāṭṭu, ēḻiṟ kuṉṟattuk kavā aṉ'' (akam, 152-12-13, 349-8-9).

tammōṭu is used in thr sense of simultaneously.

Talivi told her friend beautiful so that our talivar can rest in the shade of the paths and proceed further.

iṟṟi: this belong to the family of banyan tree which has aerial roots; ""vēṉil iṟṟit'' tōyā neṭu vīḻ, vaḻi nār ūcaliṉ kōṭai tūkku toṟum tuñcupiṭi varuṭum attam'' (naṟṟiṇi, 162-9-11); ""pulvīḻ iṟṟik kal ivar veṇvēr, varai iḻi aruviyiṉ tōṉṟum nāṭaṉ'' (Kuṟuntoki, 106-1-2) ""kuṟuṅkāl iṟṟip puṉtalai neṭuvīḻ, irumpiṇart tuṟukal tīṇṭi vaḷiporap, peruṅkai yāṉai nivappiṉ tōṉṟum'' (akam, 57-6-8). 

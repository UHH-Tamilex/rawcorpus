TRANSLATION
  • Oh chief (1)
  • I hemmed in you Do not go (1)
  • naṉṉaṉ wearing gold ornaments (2)
  • and possessing chariots adorned with flags (1)
  • went to war against the people of puṉṉaṭu (2)
  • The hero, āy ayiṉaṉ (4), who was standing in the city of paḻi which has streets where music from yaḻ is played (3),
  • assured, "Do not fear" (4)
  • and true to his word (6),
  • laid down his life in the war with miñili (5)
  • who was well trained to, emerge victorious in battles; but (on the contrary) you without giving me your chest (10),
  • to make the desiring mind to prosper ever increasingly (9),
  • become a stranger to me (11),
  • having gone back on you word (8)
  • which you made clear in the presence of the indestructible god (7),
  • holding my forearms of soft joints (8).
  • I will not leave you hereafter (11).
  • I am afraid of you being carried off by your wife (15)
  • just like the Kaviri of deep water which carried away and hid (14)
  • having desired the beauty of atti (atti) of ferocious strength (13),
  • to make his wife, manti (11),
  • to stay disliking many things and shedding tears Please return my beauty (29)
  • which can be compared by veñci (19)
  • which belongs to the cēraṉ who made captives of Kings of cruel anger (18),
  • inscribing the bending bow in the hoary and old north mountain (17)
  • of great fame, (16)
  • attacking with anger (15)
  • the āriyar to cry aloud (16).

NOTES

(Kātaṟprattai : woman belonging to the courtesan class warmly attached to a hero and not residing in his quarters).

toṭutteṉ I surrounded you, caught hold of

puṉṉāṭu a part of the country near the Arabian sea. A variant reading "puḷḷu nāṭu' is also found; this is substantiated by the following quotations;
""kaṟaiyaṭi yāṉai naṉṉaṉ pāḻi, ūṭṭaru marapiṉ añcu varupē eyk, kūṭṭu etir koṇṭa vāymoḻi miñili puḷḷiṟku ēmam ākiya, perum peyar veḷḷattāṉai atikaṟ koṉṟu'' (akam, 142-9-13);
""kaṭumparik kutirai ā ay eyiṉaṉ, neṭuntēr miñiliyoṭu porutu kaḷam paṭṭeṉak, kāṇiya cellāk kūkai'' (akam, 148-7-9);
""aṭupōr miñili ceruviṟku uṭaii, muruku uṟaḻ muṉpoṭu porutu kaḷam civappa, vaṇmai eyiṉaṉ vīḻnteṉa ñāyiṟṟu, oṇ katir uruppam putaiya ōrāṅku, vampap puḷḷiṉ kampalaip peruntōṭu, vicumpiṭai tūra āṭi'' (akam, 181-5-10);
""veḷiyaṉ vēṇmāṉ ā ay eyiṉaṉ, aḷi iyal vāḻkkaip pāḻip paṟantalai, iḻai aṇi yāṉai iyal tēr miñiliyoṭu, naṇ pakal uṟṟa ceruviṟ puṇ kūrntu, oḷvāḷ mayaṅku ñāyiṟṟu, oṇkatir terumaic ciṟakariṟkōli, niḻal ceytu uḻaṟal kāṇēṉ yāṉ eṉap paṭukaḷam cellāṉ ciṉai ciṟantu, uruviṉai naṉṉaṉ aruḷāṉ karappa'' (akam, 208-3-14)
In the first quotation atikaṉ is perhaps another name for āay eyiṉaṉ like veḷiyaṉ vēṇmāṉ in verse 208;

perhaps the country known as puḷḷu "nāṭu' might have corrupted into puṇṇāṭu and then changed into puṉṉāṭu
There is some connection between birds and āay eyiṉaṉ

- in the foregoing quotations; there is variant reading "puḷḷunāṭu'

paḻi is spoken of as having streets where music of yāḻ could be heard, from this we can know that pāṇar and others came to pāḻi without any break.

āey eyiṉaṉ was the enemy of naṉṉaṉ and miñili was his friend. āay eyiṉaṉ fought with miñili at paḻi and laid down his life.

tēṟṟi muṉkaip paṟṟiya col - should be changed into muṉkaip paṟṟit tēṟṟiya col
In the edition of poruḷatikaram by C.W. Tāmōtāram pillai, this verse quoted by the commentators has the reading "tēṟṟiya'; this is added as a foot note to the commentary by the new commentators so tēṟṟi is a better reading than "tōṟṟi' which is found in the printed edition of akanānūṟu,

āay eyiṉaṉ sacrificed his life to keep his word he gave to strangers; on the contrary you thought me to be a loving person enjoyed my beauty and having transgressed the oath you swore in the presence of god; you have deserted me and become a complete stranger; so saying the Kātaṟparattai impressed upon talaivaṉ his cruelty; she added that she would leave such an unfaithful person.

toṭutteṉ can be split into toṭuttu eṉ the meaning is what is the use of speaking many words one after another ?

manti-ātimanti; atti-āṭṭaṉ atti; these are parts of the full name.

The cēraṉ who attacked āriyar to make them cry aloud and inscribed the bow on the north mountain is imaya varampaṉ netuñcēralātaṉ; imayam viṟpoṟittu.......pēricai marapiṉ āriyar vaṇakki'' (ll 4-7). Cf. ""kuraṅkuḷaip polinta kuṭṭuvaṉ, marantai aṉṉa eṉ nalam tantu ceṉmē'' (akam, 376-17-18); these lines quoted are also addressed to talaimakaṉ by this Kātaṟparattai. 

(1-5). Description of the maritime tract of talaivaṉ where girls of the fishermen caste sell fish caught by their brothers in their boats.
(6-7). talaivaṉ became unfaithful to his word as not to remove the distress of talaivi and the scandal of the village.
(8-10). The description of the big ghat on the sea-shore.
(11-12). talaivaṉ coming in his chariot so as to destroy the doll made on the sand during talaivi's play.
(13-14). talaivaṉ swearing before the god of the sea on the first day of meeting. Friend questioning whether it was also a false one.

TRANSLATION
  • Chief of the maritime tract having small hamlets surrounded by sea-shore groves (5)
  • where the petted sisters who wear on their waist a garment of leaves (3)
  • sell in the streets where festivals are celebrated (4)
  • the ferocious and strong fish caught by (their brothers) in their boats (2)
  • dragging the fishing nets with great force in the expanse of the rising waves in the ocean (1)!
  • Even if you become unfaithful to your word by not bestowing your grace on my friend (7),
  • who has eyes ((that are)) as beautiful as flowers and ((that are)) taking in collyrium (6),
  • to remove her distress (6)
  • and the scandal of the village folk (7),
  • Is the oath that you swore (14)
  • on that day when you had first union with her (13),
  • coming in your tall chariot having a part resembling lotus bud, cutting the loose fine sand (11)
  • so as to destroy the doll made of sand during play (12)
  • in the big ghat which was made beautiful by the pollen of the puṉṉai tree (puṉṉai; mast wood tree) of curved stature (10),
  • where the swan of short legs (18)
  • adjusts its feather with its beak and dries them (9)
  • (eating the fish) after stirring the long back-water (for fish), (8)
  • sitting on the mound of sand on which the creeper of aṭumpu (aṭumpu) is resting (9),
  • also false (14)
  • for which the sea bore a witness (14) ?

NOTES

timilōṉ - here means the brothers who own fishing boats, as in the 3rd line their sisters are mentioned.

kaṭuṅkaṇ vayamīṉ

- may mean shark. After stirring the back-water for fish the feathers become wet; so the swan adjusts its feathers and makes them dry.

Talaivaṉ coming to meet talaivi when she was playing with her mates is mentioned in akam, 110-7-9.

tōṉputitu uṇṭarāṉṟai - a polished of saying `having had union!;

putitu uṇṇal - enjoying the fresh thing;
""uḻāatu vittiya parūukkuraṟ ciṟutiṉai, muntu viṉaiyāṇar nāṭpu titu uṇmār'' (puṟam, 168-6-7);
""uyyāmal malaintu umar'' āruyirai, meyyāka irāmaṉ vikantiṭavē, kaiyāramukantu koṭantakaṉār, aiyāputitu uṇṭatu aṟintilaiyō'' (Kamparāmāyaṇam, catāyu uyir nītta patalam, 96).

cūḷ: the oath that talaivaṉ swore; He banished talaivi's doubts by saying, `I won't part from you; if I do that I shall be committing sinful acts". This is mentioned in akam, 5-16-17 as ""aṟattāṟu aṉṟeṉa moḻinta toṉṟu paṭukiḷavi''.

kaṭal here means the deity that resides in the ocean;
""kariyamalar neṭuṅkaṭ kārikai muṉ kaṭal teyvam kāṭṭik kāṭṭi, ariya cūḷ poyttatār aṟaṉilar eṉṟu ēḻaiyam yāṅku aṟikō aiya'' (cilappatikāram, 6-5, ll.1-2) also substantiates this.

The oath may be this also; `Talaivaṉ said at the time when he had the union with talaivi (iyaṟkaip puṇarcci) "I shall marry you and lead a house-holder's life"

As it is a grave error to go back on one's oath the friend urged talaivaṉ to marry talaivi as early as possible.

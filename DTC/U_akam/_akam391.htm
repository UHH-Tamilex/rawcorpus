Table of contents (by lines):
1-6. Talivaṉ sleeping on the bed of tresses of hir of talivi.
7-8. Talivi reminded of her former beauty whenever she looks at the tuft of hir adorned by talivaṉ.
9-10. Talivi complining that her eyes do not get sleep.
11-14. The description of the forest in which talivaṉ had gone.
Colophon(s):
Talivi replied to her friend who asseverated that whenever she saw the tuft adorned by talivaṉ she was reminded of her former beauty and her eyes lost sleep; how she could bear the separation, as talivar had gone into the curam

TRANSLATION
  • Friend (8)!
  • our talivar who slept on the cool and fragrant tresses of hir (6)
  • after throwing out (5)
  • the fragrant flowers that are suitable for adorning the head which were left over by flowers sellers as the broad flower-baskets which were full and could not hold more flowers (4)
  • of the slender wild jasmine which has soft buds of lines (2)
  • which resemble the sharp teeth of the wild-cat on the look-out for its prey (1)
  • has gone beyond the forest which has hot places (14)
  • where the elephant (11)
  • having must (10)
  • which was unable to remove the white tusks which is fixed firmly in the earth, by shaking them; (11)
  • and whose big emaciated trunk which was thrust into it mouth to occupy it in full (12)
  • appears like the snake which enters into a mountin cave (13)
  • Whenever I see my tuft of hir full of glory (7)
  • which was adorned by him (6)
  • I am reminded of my former beauty (8)
  • that I had before (7)
  • Though only some days have gone since he left us including today, (9)
  • my eyes do not sleep (10)
  • well (9).

NOTES

vaṭṭi a nasket made of palmyra stem fibre; It is used to carry venison, fish and seeds; ""kāṉ uṟai vāḻkkaik kata nāy vēṭṭuvaṉ, māṉtacai corinta vaṭṭiyuḷ'' (puṟam, 33-1-2); ""vittoṭu ceṉṟa vaṭṭipaṟpala, mīṉoṭu peyarum yāṇar ūra'' (naṟṟiṇi 210-3-4);

muḷ eyiṟṟup pāṇ makaḷ iṉ koṭiṟu corinta, akṉa peru vaṭṭi niṟaiya maṉaiḷ, arikāṟ perum payaṟu niṟaikkum ūra'' ""valaival pāṇmakaṉ vāl eyiṟṟu maṭa makaḷ, varāal corinta vaṭṭiyuḷ, maṉaiyōḷ, yāṇṭukaḻi veṇṇel niṟaikkum ūra'' (ainkuṟunūṟu, 47-1-3, 48-1-3); ""mutup puṉam koṉṟa ārkali uḻavar, vitaik kuṟu vaṭṭi pōtoṭu potuḷa'' (Kuṟuntoki, 155-1-2); tēṉ ār kiḻaṅkiṉar ūṉ ār vaṭṭiyar'' malipaṭukaṭam, 152); ivaḷ tāṉ tiruttāc cumaṭṭiṉaḷ, ēṉait tōḷ vīci, varikkaḻavaṭṭi taḻīi, curikkuḻai''; (Kalittoki 109-13-15)

talaivēy should be added mullai also, then the meaning will be that the talivaṉ removed the wild jasmine flowers together with mulli flowers

puṉainta here means the flowers that were adorned fresh by talivaṉ after union with talivi.

kaṭāa is the epithet that qualifies yāṉai in line 11.

maruppu acaittal cellātu The elephant which turned down emaciated by the excessive heat of the curam, could not shake its tusks

kuṉṟu here stands for cave in the mountin;

snake is compared to the trunk of the elephant in a previous poem; ""kaḷiṟu taṉ, varinutal vaitta vali tēmpu taṭakkai, kuṉṟu puku pāmpiṉ tōṉṟum, col per tē etta curaṉ'' (349-11-14); see notes for those lines.

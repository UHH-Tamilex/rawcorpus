Pāyiram (preface)

The following verses, the first in akaval metre and some sentences in prose and three verses in veṇpā metre about the numerical arrangements of this anthology according to tiṇai are found in printed editions of akanāṉūṟu


.2. paḻutu il koḷkai vaḻutiyar avaikkaṇ in the academy supported and patronized by the vaḻutīyar (vaḻutiyar: pāṇṭiyar) of blemishless prinicples.


.1. niṉṟa nīti veṉṟa nēmi

- who possessed stable justice and an authority with which they won enemies.


.3-4. aṟivīṟṟirunta ceṟivuṭai maṉattu vāṉ tōy nalli caic cāṉṟōr kuḻīi the poets who had a rare combination of good qualities and good fame that can reach the sky and a mind of sefl-restraint where knowledge sat majestically, having assembled together.


.5. aruntamiḻ mūṉṟum terinta kālai

- when they conducted research into the rare tamouḻ of three kinds.


.6. ayanta koḷkait tīntamiḻp pāṭṭuḷ

- among the verses of sweet tamiḻ which they chose with some principles.


.7. neṭiyavāki aṭinimirntu oḻukiya those verses which were long and had flowed having many lines.


.8-9. iṉpap pakuti iṉporuṭ pāṭal nāṉūṟu eṭuttu having chosen four hundred verses bearing upon that part of love and having fine meaning.
.9. nūl navil pulavar poets who had studied works many times.


.10-12. kaḷitta mummatak kaḷiṟṟiyāṉai nirai, maṇiyoṭu miṭainta aṇikiḷar pavaḷam, mēviya nittilakkōvai eṉṟāṅku The row of male elephants who became proud due to three musts, the beautiful coral which is intermixed with rubby, string of pearls having such a beautiful nature.


.13. attaku paṇpiṉ muttiṟamāka Havign the three parts which have those qualities


.14. muṉṉiṉar toṭutta naṉṉeṭun tokaikku for that work known as good natuntokai, which they anthologized bestowing some thought.


.15. karutteṉap paṇpiṉōr uraittavai nāṭi when the commentary whichwere written by good people as the central theme of the poems was scrutinized.


.16. avvakaikku avaitām cevviya aṉṟi

- as they were not suited to those poems.


.17. arivaiyākiya poruṇmai nōkki looking at the rare meanings.


.18. kōṭṭam iṉṟip pāṭṭoṭu porunta

- without crookedness of mind and to be in unison with the poems.


.19. takavoṭu ciṟanta akaval naṭaiyāl

- in the style of akaval which is eminant by ites suitability.


.20. karuttu iṉitu iyaṟṟiyōṉē

- composed the central themes without any great effort.


.21. parittēr vaḷavar kākkum vaḷa nāṭṭuḷḷum

- in the fertile country protected by vaḻavar having chariots drawn by horses.


.22. nāṭu eṉac ciṟanta pīṭukeḻu ciṟappiṉ

- having superiority and fit to be called a country.


.23. keṭal aruñ celvattu iṭaiyaḷa nāṭṭu

- in the itaiyaḷanattu where wealth seems never to decreases.


.24. tītu il koḷkai mūtūruḷḷum

- among the ancient villages which flawless principles.


.25. ūr eṉac ciṟanta cīr keḻu maṇakkuṭi

- maṇakkuti which is in every way fit to be called a village.


.26. cemmai cāṉṟa tēvaṉ

- tevaṉ who has abundance of straight forwardness.


.27. toṉmai cāṉṟa naṉmaiyōṉē who has godd qualities which are very old.

ittokaip pāṭṭiṟku aṭiyaḷavu ciṟumai patiṉ mūṉṟu: The limit of minimum lines for this anthology is thirteen. perumai muppattoṉṟu maximum thirty-one. One who ordered this to be collected was uḵḵirapperuvaḻuti. One who actually collected these is uruttiracaṉmaṉ, son of maturai uppūreḵuti Kiḻāṉ, one hundred and twenty verses.

All the beginning from and ending in vaṇṭupaṭat tatainta kaṇṇi and ending in ""neṭuvēḷ mārpiṉ'' are called ""kaḷiṟṟiyāṉai nirai'' (row of male elephants) This is a derivative name. The reason is meaning.

All the hundred and eighty verses beginning from ""nānakaiyuṭaiyam neñcē'' to the end of nāḷ valai mukanta kōḷ valaip paratavar

- are called maṇi miṭaipavaḷam (coral intermixed with rubies); this is also a derivative name, what is the reason the poems and their meanings are at disagreement.

All the hundred stanzas beginning from ""vaṟaṉuṟu ceyti'' to the end of ""nakai naṉṟamma tāṉē'' are called nittilakkōvai, as the poems and their meanings are in agreement

akanāṉūṟṟiṉ tokuppu muṟai:

(The method of which akanāṉūṟu was collected)

1. viyamellām veṇṭē riyakkam; kayamalarnta tāmaraiyā ṟākat takai peṟīk, kāmar, naṟu mullai nāṉkāka nāṭṭi veṟimāṇṭa, eṭṭum iraṇṭum kuṟiñciyāk, kuṭṭattu ivar tirai pattā iyaṟpaṭa yāttāṉ tokaiyi ṉeṭiyataṉait tōlāc ceviyāṉ vakaiyi ṉeṭiyataṉai vaippu

(All the old numbers are pālai; the lotus blossomed in the tank (this stands for marutam) are nos. six the number four is fragrant mullai. The nos. two and eight are Kuṟiñci famous for teh dance by a priest possessed by murukaṉ. the sea from which waves dash on the shore is no ten. one who has knowledge aquired by listening to words of wisdom collected the long one among the anthologies in this order.

2. oṉṟu mūṉṟaintē ḻoṉ pāṉ pālai; ōtātu niṉṟavaṟṟiṉ nāṉku neṟi mullai; - aṉṟiyē āṟām marutam; aṇi neytal aiyiraṇṭu kūṟātavai kuṟiñcik kūṟṟu.

Verses of no. one, three, five, seven and nine belong to pālai, among those that remain without mention for no. four is mullai; Besides that no. six is marutam the beautiful neytal is no. ten. The which is not explicitly mentioned ie. no. two and eight they belong to the group of Kuṟiñci.

3. pālaiviyamellām; pattām paṉineytal; nālu naḷi mullai nāṭuṅkāl; - mēlaiyōr tēṟum iraṇṭeṭ ṭivai kuṟiñci; centamiḻiṉ āṟu marutam; akam.

In the akam of chaste Tamiḻ all old nos. are pālai; the cool neytal is no. ten when it is thought about, no. four is cool mullai; nos two and eight which are clearly understood by the ancients are Kuṟiñci.

TRANSLATION
  • Chief of great liberality (12)!
  • In the small and good village situated in the forest tract (16)
  • where the short hare (14)
  • hides itself in the great bush in the common place, (15)
  • afrid of the whistling sound (14)
  • made by shepherds living in the forest (13)
  • by folding the lips (14)
  • to collect the different kinds of sheep in one place (13)
  • the Kaḷā (kaḷāppaḻam) fruits have become ripe and turned sour; and the viḷavu (viḷavu: wood apple tree) have matured and become ripe (1)
  • when your servants eat first, the desirable food for agreeable sour taste to which is added winged white ants (5)
  • which rose from the anthill of wet place as it rined heavily during the rinly season (4)
  • cooked along with the well-pounded rice of varaku (varaku: common millet) which was grown in the new dry land (3)
  • in the pot of water set over the fire for cooking into which maturing curd having pink colour prepared from the milk of the sheep of small heads and pouring butter got from red cows to melt on the hot rice (6)
  • you please come and return (12)
  • on one day (11)
  • in order that your (10)
  • wife of black tresses of hir dressed beautifully (7)
  • may entertin guests, and you too (7)
  • may partake of the food into which milk was added (11)
  • afterwards (7)
  • in the small house which was coated with red soil resembling new earthern pots (9)
  • and has a pantal of bending posts inside a fence fastened with the thorns (8)

NOTES

kaḷā a forest tree that bears fruits of sour taste and black in colour; ""kaḷavup puḷi'' (puṟam, 238-8);
""kaḷaṅkaṉi aṉṉa karuṅkōṭṭuc cīṟiyāḻ'' (puṟam, 127-1, 145-5);
""puḷiccuvai vēṭṭa ceṅkaṇāṭavar, tīmpuḷikkaḷāvoṭu tuṭari muṉaiyiṉ'' (puṟam, 177-8-9)
kaḷaṅkaṉiyaik kāreṉac ceytārumil'' (nālatiyar, 103)

From the first line it is learnt that the village had everything to entertin guests.

In the 6th line after uruka the word peytu should be supplied to complete the sense.

The fruits of the banyan tree are compared to new earthernware.

cevvāyc ciṟṟil, puṉai iruṅkatuppiṉ niṉ maṉaiyōḷ ayarap pāluṭai aṭicil: all these words hinted that talivaṉ should get married and come as a guest to talivi to partake of special dishes.

The whistling sound made by the shepherd is found in the following quotations also;
""taṇṭu kāla /ūṉṟiya taṉi nilai iṭaiyaṉ, maṭi viṭu viḷai'' (akam, 274-8-9);
""paṟip puṟattu iṭṭa pāl noṭai iṭaiyaṉ, nuṇpal tivalai orutiṟam naṉaippat, taṇṭu kāl vaitta oṭuṅku nilai maṭi viḷi'' (naṟṟiṇi, 142-4-6); 

TRANSLATION
  • The King rised his flag having become victorious (3)
  • when the muracam (war drum) which makes a sound like thunder was roaring in the battle field (2)
  • in the war-camp where very strong elephants of both sides opposed each other (1)
  • The cowherds played upon their flutes placing them upon their lips (5)
  • when the many herds of milch cows were jumping in every jungle (4)
  • with their calves (3).
  • As you desire to enter your house with the joy of having won a victory (11)
  • when minute particles of dust adorn (10)
  • the chest (9)
  • which has been coated with cool and fragrant sandal paste, (10)
  • and on which the marks of severe wounds cused by enemies' weapons (9)
  • were prised by poets (8-9),
  • when the charioteer holds tight the bridle to make the horses of swift git to go on the right path, when your young servantss walk in advance (6)
  • very fast (5),
  • where will the sallowness (12)
  • which spread on the beautiful (12)
  • nīlam (nīlam: blue nelumbo) flowers (13) stay ? (12).

SYNTACTICAL LINK

DIFFICULT WORDS
mata vali yāṉai maṟaliya pācaṟai
- the war-camp in which the elephants of great strength which opposed the enemies.
iṭi umiḻ muracam poru kaḷattu iyampa
- the victorious muracam (war-drum) to sound like the thunder in the battle-field.
vēntaṉum veṉṟu koṭi eṭuttaṉaṉ
- the King also rised his flag having won a victory.
kaṉṟoṭu kaṟavaip palliṉam puṟavutoṟu ukaḷa
- the many herds of milch-cows to return along with their calves, jumping in every jungle.
kōvalar kuḻalvāyvaittaṉar
- the cowherds placed their flutes on their lips and played on them.
iḷaiyar valviraintu ēkuvaṉar pariya
- your servants to go in advance very hurriedly
viriyuḷaikkaṭu naṭaip puravi vaḻi vāy ōṭa
- in order to make the horses of expanding manes and swift git to go in the path.
valavaṉ vaḷpu valiyuṟuppa
- the charioteer to hold the briddle tight.
pulavar kuṟikoṇṭa polan tār akalattu
- in the chest wearing beautiful garlands, which had received wounds by weapons as marks of valour and which were prised by poets.
taṇ kamaḻ cāntam nuṇ tukaḷ aṇiya
- to be adorned with minute dust together with the cool and fragrant sandal paste.
veṉṟi koḷ uvakaiyoṭu nī pukutal vēṇṭiṉ
- as you have desired to enter your house with the joy born of victory.
yāṇṭu uṟaivatu kol
- where will it stay ?
māṇṭa pōtu uṟaḻ koṇṭa uṇkaṇ tītilāṭṭi tirunutaṟ pacappu
- the sallowness that had spread on the beautiful forehead of talivi who is without any evil and has eyes coated with collyrium, resembling the glorious nīlam (nīlam: blue nelumbo) flowers.

NOTES
mata- strength;
matavali, two words having the same meaning indicate excessive strength; it is called in grammar oruporuṭ paṉmoḻi!
muracam iyampa, veṉṟu pācaṟaik kaṇ koṭu eṭuttaṉaṉ is the syntactical order of words:
vēntaṉum eṭuttaṉaṉ
this means that the talivaṉ parted from talivi to help his King who was in distress. The fact that cowherds were playing upon their flutes shows that they were returning home in the evening.
puravi ōṭumāṟu valavaṉ vaḷpu valiyuṟuppavum, iḷaiyavar ēkuvaṉar pariyavum, cāntam nuṇtukaḷ aṇiyavum pukutal vēṇṭiṉ is the syntactical order of words; the conjuction um is to be added to each of the adverbial participles
pulavar pukaḻ kuṟi
- this means the wounds received on the chest, which are prised by poets.
cāntam nuṇtukaḷ aṇiya: this denotes what he will do after reaching his house
vēṇṭiṉ
- means as you wished it
pulliṉam
- is the variant reading means the cows mixed with goats and sheep.

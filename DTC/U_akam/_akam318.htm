TRANSLATION
  • Chief of the mountinous country of good yield, extensive space and ancient eminence (7)
  • where the sound of the piracam (piracam: bees) combining with the sound of the waterfall descending from the top of the mountin (5),
  • hums resembling the sound `imm' like the music produced by the string of yāḻ (yāḻ) in accompaniment with muḻavu (6)!
  • Elephants also roam about (at night) in the paths where other beasts move about (1)
  • The thunder also roars very much high above in the sky (2)
  • Those paths are dreadful as they are infested with (poisonous) snakes and tigers (3)
  • you come all alone in the narrow path at night (4)
  • If you wish for marriage you shall get it also; that is one thing (8)
  • (There is another request). Please do not come at night from today onwards; if at all you come (9)
  • you must blow (13)
  • your horn a little while, (13)
  • which you use to call your dogs (15)
  • and hunters (14)
  • who get separated from you in the forest of densely grown bamboos (14)
  • (where you go hunting), after you have reached safely your small village situated among hillocks, (12)
  • when you come to see us and return to your place, (11)
  • so that we who are here may be rid of the distress cused by the perplexed state of mind (about your safety) (10).

NOTES

The first-three lines enumerated the dangers in the path at night when talimakaṉ comes. The word iravu should be added to vaḻaṅkum, uraṉum, añcutakavuṭaiya.

The sound of the waterfall is compared to the music played on muḻavu and the humming of the bees to the music of the strings of yāḻ.

There are two requests of the friend; one is that he should stop coming at night; if he comes he must sound his horn after reaching his village to hint his safe arrival there.

The word oṉṟu should be added along with vāral(9) and with ūtal vēṇṭum(13).

The path in which talimakaṉ comes at night is beset with dangers, and if he comes with proposals of marriage, the relations of talivi would welcome him;

so the friend requested him not to come at night from that day onwards. If he persisted in coming, she informed him about the mental agony they would be undergoing, by asking him to sound his horn when he reached his village safe, to relieve them of the mental agony.

The annotators have the 11th lines as eṟkaṇṭu peyarum kālai. but nacciṉārkkiniyar has quoted this line in the commentary on the 15th cūtram in tokimarapu, eluttatikāram, tolkāppiyam, (TE157n) with the reading "ekkaṇṭu peyarum kālai! He says that "eṅkaṇṭu' becomes, according to canti, "ekkaṇṭu'! This is the correct reading. 

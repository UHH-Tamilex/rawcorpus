Table of contents (by lines):
1-2. Mother regretting that talaivi's playmates and herself had not the fortune to witness talaivi's wedding.
3-5. The mansion of talaivi compared to uṟantai belonging to coḻar.
6-7. Adorning talaivi for the wedding.
8-15. Talaivi playing in the swing of the aerial roots of the banyan tree in the curam, which was shaken by talaivaṉ
16-18. Mother's distress about talaivi removing her anklets in an unknown country and getting herself married.
Colophon(s):
The foster mother who caused her daughter (to go in the) curam spoke.

TRANSLATION
  • My daughter who has the tenderness of a peacock (1)
  • did not get married (7),
  • having anointed her tresses of hair with the aromatic unguent (6) (takaram a kind of scented hair oil)
  • and to unite with her many other special things (7)
  • in the auspicious and grand celebration of marriage (6)
  • which would take place conducted by great people in the long and wealthy mansion (5)
  • which can be compared to Uṟantai surrounded by several kinds of groves fed by the Kaviri (4)
  • and which belongs to Coḻar of swift-moving chariots, and elephants of strong trunks (3),
  • to be witnessed by mothers like me (2)
  • and her circle of friends who are equal to her (1).
  • The fearless young girl (17)
  • having red lips and sharp teeth went without minding the suffering on the way (16)
  • In the mountain slope having rocks on which nelli (nelli: emblic myrobalan tree) (8)
  • and bamboos (7)
  • grow to a great height (8);
  • the companion loved by her (15)
  • shook (15)
  • the swing of disturbed aerial roots of the banyan tree in the path (9)
  • many times so that they rubbed perfectly against the thighs having beautiful yellow spots (10),
  • catching holding of the broad waist (14)
  • wearing a waist girdle of many beautiful strands which are of many kinds and tight fitting like a garland (12),
  • and holding her forearm wearing bangles (11)
  • when the tresses of hair (13)
  • became dishevelled and expanded like the tail of a dancing peacock (13)
  • and she sung in it (15)
  • (That the girls) should get herself married (18)
  • after removing her anklets (17)
  • in an unknown country (18)
  • is very much distressing (18).

NOTES

ōr in line 1 et 2 is a clitic; ""aṉṉōrallāvēṟum uḷa avai, niṉṉōr aṉṉōr antaṇar aru maṟai'' (paripaṭal, 4-64-65)

Uṟantai has been compared to talaivi's mansion; ""kaṭal am tāṉaik kaivaṇ cōḻar, keṭal aru nal icai uṟantai aṉṉa, nitiyuṭai naṉṉakarp putuvatu, tamar maṇam ayaravum ollāḷ'' (akam, 369-13-16) Talaivaṉ improvised a swing with the aerial root of the banyan tree in the curam and made her enjoy playing in that, is suggested by the lines 9-15.

ūkka the shake the swing.

ūṅki - played in the swing; ""peruṅkayiṟu nālum irum paṉam piṇaiyal, pūṅkaṇ āyam ūkka ūṅkāṉ'' (naṟṟiṇai, 90-6-7); ""ūcal ūrntu āṭa oru ñāṉṟu vantāṉai, aiya ciṟitu eṉṉai ūkki eṉak kūṟa'', ""niṉ ūcal, kaṭai iyāṉ ikuppa, nīṭu ūṅkāy taṭameṉṟōḷ, nīttāṉ tiṟankaḷ pakarntu'' (Kalittokai, 37-14-15, 131-12-13)

cilampu kaḻīi this is a ceremony performed previous to marriage

paṭappai may mean side; ""peṇṇaiyam paṭappai nāṭu kiḻavōyē'' (puṟam, 126-23)

In the 7th line it may be read as "yām pala puṇarppa the meaning is we unite with her many special things.

For the reading āmpāl puṇarppa - the meaning is the fate which is the cause for prosperity to unite her with her husband.

The word alantalai which occurs in line 9 is found as an epithet qualifying animals trees on their parts; ""alantalai ñemaiyattu irunta kuṭiñai'' (naṟṟiṇai 394-2)

alantalai uṉṉattu am kavaṭu porunti'', ""alantalai vēlattu ulavai am ciṉai'' (patiṟṟuppattu, 23-1, 39-12) ""alantalai ñemaiyattu valanta cilampi'', "alantalai ñemai yattu atar aṭaintirunti'', ""alantalai mūtēṟu āṇkural viḷippa'' (akam, 111-5- 171-7, 367-3); ""alantalai iratti alaṅku paṭu nīḻal'' (puṟam, 325-11).

alantalai: This word cannot be split into alantu alai as the new commentators on akam have done as it is a single word. 

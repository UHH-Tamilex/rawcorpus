TRANSLATION
  • Friend (3)!
  • Our lover who went beyond the curaṉ which is situated in a country where a different languaged is spoken (14),
  • where the curved and emanciated trunk (12)
  • of the male elephant (11)
  • which is placed on its spotted forehead (12),
  • appears like the snake which crawls on the rock (13),
  • after eating the mouthful of flowers resembling fire (11),
  • of the vēṅkai (vēṅkai: east indian kino tree) of black trunk which is tall and perfect (10),
  • and which are of bright colour growing in the middle portion of the slopes of the ēḻil hill (9)
  • which is situated in the good country of naṉṉaṉ (8)
  • who, without thinking whether they deserve it, or not, gives liberally (8)
  • big ornaments of high value, after destroying the difficult fortresses of enemies (7)
  • devastating their battle-fronts with a mind of very great strength, when minstrels who have impoverished relations, approach him (5),
  • and who speaks faultless true words (4)
  • which can be compared to the pointer in the balance (3)
  • which correctly measure things (4);
  • what was the idea he had in his mind to make the former beauty which we had by his marriage when he caught hold of my forearm (2)
  • which wears tight-fitting beautiful beangles which were made by filing (conches) (1)?

NOTES

piṇitta caught hold cf. The bridegroom catches hold of the right hand of the bride during wedding. That is called `pāṇigrahaṇam in sanskrit;
cf. ""nēriṟai muṉkai paṟṟi numartara, nāṭu aṟinaṉmaṇam ayarkam'' (Kuṟiñcuppāṭṭu, 231-232).

muṉkaivaraintu tām piṇitta
- the order of words should be changed into muṉkaiyaip piṇittu rainta- to be correct to the sense.

tolkaviṉ

- the beauty that talaivi had on the occasion of her marriage with talaivaṉ.

ceñamṉ

- here it is aḷapeṭai: the meaning is pointer in the balance; it is called camaṉvāy in tamiḻ
""terikōl ñamaṉṉ pōla orutiṟam paṟṟal iliyarō'' (puṟam 6-9).
""tulaittālam aṉṉatiṉi nilai'' (Kampārāyaṇam 2 - mantaraippaṭalam, 19).

cemmoḻi - is an epithet qualifying naṉṉaṉ.

curakkum: adjectival participle qualifying naṉaṉ; The ēḻil hill belonging to naṉṉaṉ is mentioned in akam in other stanzas also
""naṉṉaṉ ēḻi l neṭuvaraip pāḻic cilampil'' (152-12-13);
see also the notes for akam 345-7l ll.10-12.

The elephant eats the flowers of vēṅkai for want of better food and its hunger has not decreased and therefore is diminishing in strenghth.

The elephants trunk is compared to the snake;
""yāṉaitaṉ, vāy niṟai koṇṭa valitēmpu taṭakkai, kuṉṟu pukupāmpiṉ tōṉṟum'' (akam, 391-11-13);
""nirai katir nīḷ eḵkam nīṭṭi vayavar, varai purai yāṉaikkai nūṟavaraimēl urum eṟi, pāmpiṉ puraḷum'' (Kaḷavaḻi nāṟpatu, 13);
""pāntaḷ ""aṉṉa parēreṟuḻttaṭak kaiyiṉ'' (peruṅkatai, 1-58-8);
""antaṉ aṉṉakai yāṉai'' (cīvaka cintāmaṇi, 2521). 

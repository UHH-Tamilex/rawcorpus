Translation: One who crossed the mountin with imposing appearance hidden y bamboos (19) where the herd of female deer which are in the company of stags, flee (18) crying aloud with grief in the mountin paths which have many different branching ways (170, having mistaken the sound of the western mind which carrys dust with it and sweeps over the place from which issues a bad smell of flesh (14) which issues a bad smell of flesh (14) which is cused by the maḻavar who wear garlands knitted with the feathers in the peacock's til (12) and having stayed in the long shade of the forest of many flowers (11), who slughtered a young cow and ate its flesh, (13), to be the sound of the deer that has been cught by a tiger with stripes resetmbling the sickle (16), which started desirous of its prey in the mountin where bamboos grow to a very great height (15), does not bestow his grace upon me though my beautiful and youthful charm (10) which is comparable to vēmpi belonging to mucuṇṭi gets spoiled (9). He adorns himself with bending jewels (8) and possesses an army of soldiers holding lances (9). If bards who come as supplicants and prise him holding a drum which produces a sweet sound if beaten forcefully with a stick and which is tied tightly with strong leather straps (3-4) (5) gives them dily (4) with pleasure (8) eminent bulls (7) of moving and beautiful hump adorning their horns with gold and their neck with lace garlands (5) smearing their bodies with sandal paste, (6) and placing them in front with heaps of large quantities of rice (7) in addition to tall chariots and male elephants (8). Friend ! Let you live long. Please listen to what I say (1) what is the reason for the calumny of the inhabitants of this village (2), going on for many days (1) ?

The talimakaḷ spoke to her friend who could not bear the change that came over talimakaḷ during the parting of talimakaṉ.

nakkīraṉār

Notes: அரிகோல்: a stick that produces sound intermittently, பொன் in line 5 refers to an ornament fixed to the sharp end of the horn, it is called `koppi'. The inner meaning of this stanza is this; the western wind sweeps over the place where the bad smell of flesh is offensive and it takes with it dust, is that the women folk who indulge in calumny take only bad things about other people. The herd of deer is afrid of the noise produced by the western wind mistaking it to be the crying sound of the deer cught by the tiger for its prey means, th emother thinks mistakenly the change that came over talimakal is due to some deity, instead of understanding it to be cused by talimakaṉ

TRANSLATION
  • Friend (8) !
  • You desired and accepted the words of the chief of the hilly tract superior to other mountains (13),
  • having groves that never know drying up (12)
  • where the female monkey of black fingers which plunged into the mountain stream, (9)
  • gets down in the tuṟai (tuṟai) of the big village situated in the adjacent hill (11)
  • using the fruit of the jack tree which bears fruits abundantly, as a float (10)
  • If others see other's suffering, they will sympathize and try to remove it (1),
  • (not only that); they will think lightly about their own suffering without thinking it to be big (2),
  • you must tell talaivaṉ that our relations have slowly (6)
  • desired to perform the vaṟi dance (7)
  • to make the big arena for that to shine (6)
  • to be in unison with the songs praising netuvēḷ (neṭuvēḷ: tall murukaṉ) whose abode is the forest (5)
  • the sweet instruments of a particular musical notes tuned of many different sounds played to the measure of time (4),
  • having worn garlands (chaplets) and having tied flags to the Katampu (kaṭampu) tree (3),
  • "when you see him so as to be understood by him.

DIFFICULT WORDS
piṟar uṟu viḻumam
- the suffering that other undergo.
piṟarum nōpa
- though they are strangers good people will feel it as their own and try to remove it;
tam uṟu viḻumam tamakkō tañcam
- the suffering that they themselves undergo they will not think it to be big and take it lightly. (This is the nature of great souls)
kaṭampu koṭi yāttu
- having tied the flag in the Kaṭampu (kaṭampu) tree.
kaṇṇicūṭṭi having adorned with garlands.
vēṟu pal kurala oru tūkku iṉ iyam
- to the accompaniment of sweet instruments tuned to a particular musical note of many different sounds played to measure of time.
kāṭu keḻu neṭuvēḷ pāṭu koḷaikku ēṟpa
- to be suited to the songs sung in praise of the tall Vēḷ having the forest as his abode
aṇaṅku ayar viyaṉ kaḷam poliya
- to make the arena in which veṟi dance is performed, shine
paiyaittūṅkutal purintaṉar
- desired to perform the dance
āṅku avaṟku aṟiyak kūṟal vēṇṭum
- you must tell talaivaṉ when you see him to make him understand that.
aruvi pāynta karuviral manti
the female monkey of black fingers which plunged into the mountain stream.
ceḻuṅkōḷ palaviṉ paḻam
- the fruit of the jack tree which bears fruits abundantly.
puṇaiyāka
- using it as a float.
cāral pērūr muṉtuṟai iḻitarum
- gets down from it in the tuṟai (tuṟai: ghat) of the village situated in the adjacent hill
vaṟaṉ uṟal aṟiyāc cōlai viṟal malai nāṭaṉ col nayantōy
you who desired and consented to the words of the chief of the victorious hilly tract which has groves that never know drought.

NOTES

As it is the nature of great souls to sympathize with other's suffering and not mind their own suffering, the friend said, that if talaivaṉ knew our suffering he would try to remove it as quickly as possible.

kāṭu Here it is used to denote mountain tract, called Kuruñci.

Here tōḻi means talaivi, as both of them used to address each other as friend ! 

TRANSLATION
  • The young girls wearing garlands having bends (1)
  • return home (2)
  • after playing the game of building toy-houses on sand (1)
  • If you stay (6)
  • at night (6)
  • with distemper (6)
  • in the good house of never decreasing wealth which is thatched with dried grass (5)
  • after playing with us in the spotless shade of puṉṉi (puṉṉaimaram: mast wood tree) of broad leaves (3)
  • during day (4)
  • you say your father will place you under strict guard ! as aginst it (7)
  • unable to bear my saying `go to your house', (8)
  • you say `I will not live if I go' (8).
  • If that is so (9),
  • when you alone stay in the sea-shore grove (13)
  • like the goddess of the sea standing on the shore in the evening (11)
  • the rare and fresh garlands she wears (11)
  • which are made of cool neytal flowers (10) (neytalmalar: white indian water-lily)
  • to sway (on the chest) along with chaplets of ñāḻal for the bees to swarm on them (10),
  • I am unable (16)
  • to return (15)
  • discarding my glorious beauty which can be compared all alone to the dancing damsel who perform `veṟi' (14)
  • and goes away all alone (after finishing her work) (15)
  • Let this village indulge in scandalous talk!

NOTES

kōtaiyar - should be put in apposition with iḷaiyōr: here refers to playmates other than the confidente.

The statement that the day has become dark hinted that they should also return home. As a reply to the words of the friend that talivi who played with her playmates during day should return home at night, talivi sid that if she stayed in the house with distemper, she would be placed under strict guard by her father.

"As the friend is reporting the words of talivi, she used the words, "vaikutiyāyiṉ! nuntai! paṭuvalum

In this word um is an expletive.

The words celval eṉṟalum āṟṟāy explins that talivi will not live if she returned home

veṟi koḷ pāvai the priestess who performs a dance being possessed by murukaṉ.

āṭumakaḷ in line 15 is used in the sense of avaḷ (a demonstrative pronoun)

teyya (16) is also an expletive.

I think that the reading found in line 11. "taṇṇarum paintār' should be taṇṇaṟum paintār; it may be a scribal error.

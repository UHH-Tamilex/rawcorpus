TRANSLATION
  • (Friend!) As we heard (15)
  • the noise made by the big tiger after killing its prey of a male elephant and the harsh sound of the drum in the shape of a sun glass and having a strong face (15),
  • produced by the forest dwellers who have bows as their weapons, in their habitations (14),
  • after consuming toddy (and bearing in mind that talivi could get frightened by them) we wished to have a look at the back of talivi made exceedingly beautiful (7).
  • She, having tucked beautifully in her few tresses which can be worn in five different styles (3)
  • the bright flowers of trumpet tree growing in the forest which has black inner petals (1),
  • mixing them with the wild jasmine flowers which blossom in summer (2)
  • and having inserted soft bunches of common cadamba from which honey drips (4),
  • and swinging the arms so as to make the bright bangles worn on them to make a jingling sound (5)
  • and walking a few soft steps slowly (6)
  • to make the anklets shine (5).
  • "we shall have a look at your back fully adorned (7);
  • so walk before us for a short while " we sid (8);
  • She did not proceed further on account of bashfulness (8).
  • She quickly stepped aside due to her simplicity, with the looks of a deer (9);
  • and bowed her head standing there (10);
  • on seeing that we did not precede her (11);
  • and at that place we stayed in the desert difficult to travel (12).

NOTES

The idea that talivaṉ desired to have a look at the beauty of talivi's back is found in the following verses :- ""kāṇpāṇ avāviṉāl kātalaṉ kātalipiṉ naṭavā niṟpa nāṇpāla ḷātalāl naṉṉutal kēḷvaṉpiṉ naṭavā niṟpa āṇpāṉmai muṉṟā ayilvē lavaṉ taṉakkum arcolāṭkum pāṇpāla vaṇṭiṉamum pāṭa aruñcuramum patipōṉṟaṉṟē" (tol : akattiṇaiyiyal, 40, nacciṉārk kiṉiyar mēṟkōḷ; yāḻppāṇam ci. kaṇēcaiyar patippu. pa.100) aṉpiṇaittu añcolli piṉ cellum āṭavaṉ; nīṭavaṉṟṉ piṉ paṇait tōḷi varum; ipperuñ curam celvataṉṟu; poṉpaṇaittaṉṉa iṟai uṟai tillaip polimalarmēl naṉpaṇait taṇnaṟavuṇai aḷi pōṉṟa ṟoḷir nāṭakamē'' (tirucciṟṟampalak kōvaiyāri 219)

kāṇkam : can be taken as a finite verb : it need not be changed into an adverbial participle

ēkeṉa can also be split into ēku + eṉa

Talivaṉ who heard the noise of the tiger which killed an elephat as its prey and the sound produced by the forest-dwellers by their drum thought that talivi would be frightened by these and so asked talivi to proceed in front of him, without disclosing to her his intention and used his desire to see her beautiful back and slow git as a pretext She thought that talivaṉ was prising her and due to her bashfulness, bowed her head and stood there. As talivaṉ could not do anything else, he stayed with talivi in the desert, for a short while. Talivaṉ narrated this incident to talivi's friend after his return from elopement.

vaḷaiteḷirppa vīcic cilampu naka iyali: by these words talivaṉ prised the slow git of talivi.

By the words kūntal aḻuttila, marāam aṭaicci, aṇimāṇ ciṟu puṟam talivaṉ expressed his desire to enjoy the beauty of that talivaṉ asked talivi to proceed a little before him, so as to have a look at her back.

talivi would certinly be afrid of the noises whether she precedes talivaṉ or not. Without bringing the idea of talivaṉ stayed in the desert for the day. 

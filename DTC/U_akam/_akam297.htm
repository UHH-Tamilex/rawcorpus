5-8 The stones of memorial tables are used for sharpening arrows by inhabitants of the desert tract and the sides have become thin and they strike fear.
9-11 The forest tract in which tooth brush trees grow. The way-farers do not read the writing on the tablets, joining the letters.
12-16 The small village of the inhabitants of the desert tract where the kitten of the wild cat surround their mother following her. The warriors beat the drum for lifting bulls belonging to enemies.
17-19 Talivi sid, `I would certinly come in the forest striking fear where the kite spreads its wings and flies in the hope of getting rich food'.
18-19, 1-4 talivi's look and her distressed state.

TRANSLATION
  • My mind (4)!
  • In the forest tract having tooth brush trees under whose foot deity resides, (11),
  • where the new travellers being very weary (10)
  • do not read joining the letters (8)
  • which are prominent and spread far and wide the deeds and fame of fallen warriors (8)
  • engraved (8)
  • on the memorial tablets which incrase fear (7),
  • as the sides of those tablets have become thin (7)
  • becuse the ferocious warriors (6)
  • who have no great beauty, and have shabby looking big beards (5)
  • sharpened their arrows on those tablets (6);
  • but leave them and go beyond (10);
  • in the forest striking fear (18)
  • where the male kite (hoping to get rich food) spreads its wings and flies (17)
  • on hearing the beating by the inhabitants of the desert tract in the small villages (16)
  • where in the front yard from which issues the smell of flesh, which has a fence of sawing thorns (12)
  • the kitten having attractive fur like the wool plant, (14)
  • of the wild cat which is like a pinting and has a long body (13)
  • follow their mother and surround her just as the stars surround the moon, the talivi sid she would also come (19)
  • with us (18).
  • Her rejoicing and ignorant looks have become confused with excessive distress with never ending distemper (2)
  • both in the midnight and the very dim evening (1)
  • As her distress exceeds the limit on account of thinking about us, (3),
  • what harm will befall us ? (4)

NOTES

In line 3.kalaṅki should be changed into kalaṅka.

In line 3 namvayiṉ-nammai; this is what is called vēṟṟumai mayakkam

""nōkku(19) kkalaṅka(2) iṭumpai kaimmika(3) namvayiṉ eṉṉai ākumō''
These were the thoughts of talimakaṉ about talivi

eṉṉai ākumō(4) may also mean what distress will befall talivi.

māyttal - sharpening, whetting; ""māytta pōla maḻukunuṉai tōṟṟi'' (akam. 5-12); koṭuvil eyiṉar pakaḻi māykkum'' (kuṟuṅtokai, 12-5)

As the warriors of the desert tract sharpen their arrows on the emmorial tablets the engraved letters have become worn out, so the way farers could not read them joining the letters, and on account of their weariness they pass it and go beyond.

peyar, payam: name and victorious deeds; ""nallamark kaṭanta nāṇuṭai maṟavar, peyarum pīṭum eḻuti atar toṟum, pīli cūṭṭiya piṟaṅku nilai naṭukal'' (akam 67-8-10).

puṟaviṉ(11) muṉṟilil(12) verukiṉ(13) piṉḷai(14) tāyvaḻippaṭū um(15) ciṟu kuṭi(16) eṉṟu iyaikka.

The white wild cat is compared to the white moon and the kitten that surround it are compared to stars.

The kite, as soon as it hears the sound of the drum, hopes to get good food; so it flies from where it was perched.

When talivi sid that she would accompany talivaṉ in the fearful forest, her looks were beautiful on account of the joy; those looks stood impressed permanently in talivaṉ's mind so he prised them as "makiḻ paṭa nōkku' 

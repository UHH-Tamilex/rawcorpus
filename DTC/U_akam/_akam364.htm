TRANSLATION
  • This part of the year has commenced the winter (9)
  • as the thundering clouds have poured abundantly (2)
  • descending in columns, having spread themselves to cover all the directions (1)
  • The frogs with lines sound (3)
  • in every shallow depression (2)
  • like the sound of the drum played in the dance-arene (3)
  • The Koṉṟai trees (koṉṟai: indian laburnum) having long bunches of flowers (5)
  • which are like shining ornaments made of superior gold (4)
  • that are hanged (4),
  • appear beautiful (5)
  • In the entire forest (5)
  • the bush has on it the toṉṟi flowers (tōṉṟi: white species of gloriosa superba which are like flames of lamps (6)
  • mullai (mullai: arabian jasmine) and illam (illam: clearing nut tree) have put forth flowers (7).
  • The beautiful pools of broad place in the mountain get filled up with water for the beasts to drink (8),
  • our lover (9)
  • being in the expansive big war-camp of the King of cruel anger (10),
  • does not even think of us, desirous of victory (11)
  • When the evening hastens to reach us (13)
  • to make us suffer (12)
  • as if it has the idea of killing us (13)
  • it is very difficult to cross over that (14).
  • What shall we do (12)?
  • Friend (12)!

NOTES

The sound of the drum is compared to the sound of the frogs in winter;
""neṭu nīr avala pakuvāyt tērai, ciṟupalliyattiṉ neṭu neṟik kaṟaṅka'',
""tēraiyiṉ oliyiṉ māṉaccīramaittuc cil ari kaṟaṅkum ciṟupalliyattoṭu'' (akam. 154-1-2. 301-19-20)

kāṭuṭaṉ - this word can be added along with kaviṉ peṟa, talaikkoḷḷa malara

The flowers of Koṉṟai are compared to gold jewels on account of their yellow colour
""iḻaiyaṇi makaḷiriṉ viḻaitakaṭ pūtta, nīṭu curi iṇara cuṭar vīk koṉṟai, kāṭukaviṉ pūtta'' (naṟṟinai. 302-1-3);
""poṉ cey puṉai iḻai kaṭṭiya makaḷir, katuppiṉ tōṉṟum pūtup pūṅkoṉṟaik kāṉam'' (Kuṟuntokai, 21-2-4)

Here tōṉṟi refers to its flowers;
""vāṭaiyoṭu nivanta āyitaḻt tōṉṉi cuṭar koḷ akaliṉ, curuṅku piṇi aviḻa'' (akam, 235-7-8);
""erivaṉap puṟṟaṉa tōṉṟi''
""nalamiku kārttikai nāṭṭavar iṭṭa, talai nāḷ viḷakkiṉ takai uṭaiyavākip pulamelām, pūttaṉa tōṉṟi'' (Kārnāṟpatu 9, 26)
""kuruti malart tōṉṟi kūr mukai īṉa'' (Kainnilai, 26);

koḷāa this word will mean koṇṭu; we have to change it into koḷa

kālai here means the part of the year; time;
""kaḻakattuk kālai pukiṉ'' (Kuṟaḷ 937)

For people who are separated from their lovers the evening will appear as if it is coming to kill them;

""kātalar ilvaḻi mālai kolaik kaḷattu, ētivār pōla varum'' (Kuṟaḷ, 1224)
ṉṟai, mullai, illam;
""mullai vainnuṉai tōṉṟa illamoṭu paiṅkāṟ koṉṟai meṉ piṇi aviḻa'' (akam, 4-1-2) 

TRANSLATION
  • The friendship made with us by the chief of the maritime tract (4-5)
  • where, as the fishermen of bending boats had an abundant catch of fish (1)
  • and prised the catch of the nets which had small meshes (3)
  • in the village situated in the foreshore having low inhabitants and issuing foul smell of fish (2),
  • divide the loach (ayilai) of fat eyes (4),
  • has been known already to many there by the spreading of scandal in a small circle by women who undulge in public scandal (5-7).
  • It has stopped (7).
  • Now, after the proposals of marriage have been announced (7-8),
  • this village of great uproar is silent (17)
  • like the banyan tree of many aerial roots, which (16)
  • was made silent by rāmaṉ of victorious battles and under which he held a confidential meeting (15)
  • at the foreshore at the ancient Kōṭi (kōṭi-tiruvaṇaikkarai) which belongs to Kavuriyar (kavuriyar-pāṇṭiyar) of victorious vēl (13)
  • where the big roaring sea produced a thunderous noise, (14)
  • where the girls who wish to adorn themselves during festivals combine with the garment of leaves to add its beauty (12)
  • blue nelumbo (neytal) flowers of fertile and thick stem and green leaves (11)
  • growing in the blackwater of fields in the big ghat (tuṟai) which are situated in the beautiful foreshore groves (10),
  • where the flowers of mast-wood tree (puṉṉaimaram) drop with the fresh and gold-coloured flowers of fetid cassia (ñāḻal-pulinakakkoṉṟai). make that place look beautiful like a pinting (8-9).

NOTES

When Ramaṉ went to battle with Rāvaṇaṉ, he held a confidential meeting with his army under a banyan tree at Kōtī which is now known as tiruvaṇikkari (tiruvaṇaikkarai), situated in Pāṇṭiyaṉ's country. The birds living in that banyan tree made such a loud noise that one could not hear what the other sid. Rāmaṉ commanded them to be silent. The noise stopped completely. That banyan tree is compared to the erstwhile noisy village. This is what is found in the old gloss.

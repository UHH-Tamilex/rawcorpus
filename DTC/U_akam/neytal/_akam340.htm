(1-5) Friend requesting talivaṉ to forget the desire of returning home in the evening.

(19-24) The life of the paratavar fishermen in talivi's village.

TRANSLATION
  • Chief of the maritime tract where there are flowers (13)!
  • The waves in the back-water have risen (9)
  • with a sound resembling `ol' (8).
  • In the ways (9)
  • the strong shark roams along with snakes having sharp teeth (10)
  • to make the good beauty and youthful charms (8)
  • of this lady (7)
  • who can be compared to pavattiri (pavattiri a city) of groves of many flowers (7)
  • which belonged to Tirayaṉ wearing gold jewels and having a good, never dying fame (6)
  • (a fame that does not go to other people), to perish (8)
  • what is the harm if you stay (13)
  • today here (13),
  • to be rid of the anxiety that she fells on your account (12)
  • thinking about the following things (11):
  • the time has definitely become confused; in spite of it our talivar has left us (11);
  • in the village which is pleasant to dwell and has an expanse of clear sea (24),
  • when the paratavar (paratavar fishermen) of cruel action, who went on their business (19)
  • in strong fishing-boats (18)
  • in the day and as the sharp instrument like chisel moved swiftly and struck (20)
  • killing the sharks (21)
  • which dashed together and tore the long nets of curved knots (21),
  • stopped their work (22),
  • (and left them on the) tāḻi (tāḻai) fragrant screw-pine tree) growing in the frontyard (23)
  • to move whenever the gentle sea-breeze blows (22)
  • (If you stay) your horses will feed on the gruel (15)
  • which was prepared by mixing curd along with the flour of white paddy got in exchange for fresh (14) fish,
  • (with the help of fingers), we will smear you with the sweet-smelling sandal wood paste on which bees hum (18)
  • which was prepared adding other perfumes to it (17)
  • of the sandal-wood got from the Potiyil hill which lies to the west (17),
  • rubbing it on the white round stone brought by people from the north (16).
  • You too (4)
  • come in the day to remove from us the distress, for many days (1);
  • spend the day in the pleasant shade of the thick grove of puṉṉi (puṉṉai: mast wood tree) (2)
  • when the evening arrives you look at it with delusion (3)
  • and when your charioteer drives the beautiful chariot (4)
  • choosing the horses to be harnessed (3)
  • you drop the desire of returning to your village (5);
  • that is my wish (5).

NOTES

evvam - here denotes the distress of talivaṉ

tiraiyaṉ: a Chieftin of the Tiriyar dynasty who ruled a part of toṇṭimaṇṭalam, toṇṭimāṉ iḷantiriyaṉ is the hero of the poem, perumpāṇāṟṟuppaṭi; perhaps he belonged to his dynasty.

The rising of the waves in the backwater and the way being infested with snakes and sharks are cuses for talivis anxiety; so her beauty perished.

noṭutta - is a rare word meaning selling,
""kaṭuṅkaṇ vēḻattuk kōṭu noṭuttu uṇṇum'' (Kuṟuntoki, 100-4);
""tuṭikkaṇ koḻuṅkuṟai noṭuttuuṇṭu āṭi'' (akam, 196-3);
""mīṉ noṭuttu neṟ kuvaii'', ""neṭuṅkaḻaittūṇṭil viṭumiṉ nokuttu (puṟam, 343-1, 399-15)

A verbal noun formed from this root "noṭai' occurs in the following numbers; perumpāṉ. 141; maturikkañci, 622, 633; paṭṭiṉappāli, 180; naṟṟiṇi; 142-4, 254-6; patiṟṟuppattu; 30-12; akam 60-4, 61-10, 83-8, 296-9; puṟam, 352-8; cilappatikāram 6-139

A form noṭaimai having the same meaning as noṭai (price) occurs in patiṟṟupattu, 68-14.

"kaṉnoṭai āṭṭiyar'' is found in cilappatikaram, 5-24; maṇimēkali, 28-32. (This phrase means women who sell toddy)

That the system of barter was in vogue in those days can be understood from the quotations;
""uppu noṭai nelliṉ mūral veṇcōṟu'',
""nelliṉ nērē veṇkal uppu eṉa'',
""nellum uppum nērē'' (akam, 60-4, 140-7, 390-8);
""tam nāṭṭu viḷainta veṇṇel tantu, piṟanāṭṭu uppiṉ koḷḷai ..... umaṇar (naṟṟiṇi, 183-1-5);
""mīṉ noṭuttu neṟkuvaii'' (puṟam, 343-1).

The stone on which the sandal-wood is rubbed to get the paste was got from North India; the 16th line is found in neṭunalvāṭi, 51, without any change;
""vaṭavaraippiṟanta vāṉkēḻ vaṭṭattut, teṉmalaip piṟanta cantaṉam maṟuka'' (cilappatikāram, 4-37-38).

kūruḷi is the correct reading; the new commentators have adopted that reading in akam;
""kuṟiyiṟaik kurampaik kolai vemparatavar, eṟiyuḷiporuta ēmuṟuṟu perumīṉ (210-1-2); there is no variant reading for this line; this uḷi is mentioned in Naṟṟini and Kuṟuntoki respectively in the following quotations,
""nōṉpuric, kayiṟukaṭai yātta kaṭunaṭai eṟi uḷit, tiṇtimir paratavar'' (338-2-4);
""kol viṉaip polinta kūr vāy eṟi uḷi mukampaṭa maṭutta muḷi vetir nōṉkāḻ, tāṅkku nīrccurattu eṟintu vāṅku vicaik koṭuntimiṟ paratavar, kōṭṭu mīṉ eṟiya'' (304-1-4)

The fishing net being torn by the ferocious shark is found in the following quotations from naṟṟiṇi;
""ceṅkōl, koṭumuṭi avvalai pariyap pōkiya, kōṭcuṟākakuṟitta muṉpoṭu'', vaṉkaip pōkik, kaṭu muraṇ eṟicuṟā vaḻaṅkum'' (315-9-11, 303-9-11).

The verse no.254 in naṟṟiṇi completely agrees with the idea contined in this poem.

kuṭapul vuṟuppu the perfumes prepared in Yavaṉam which lies to the west of Tamiḻnātu;
cf; ""kuṭaticai maruṅkiṉ veḷḷayir'' (Cilappatikāram, 4-35).

TRANSLATION
  • Chief of the big maritime tract (9)!
  • The waves in the sea are surging (2)
  • to make the Kāvi (kāvi: blue nelumbo) flowers and neytal flowers (neykaṟpū - white indian water-lily) of small bunches to make their petals close, in the backwater (1).
  • As the crab which lives in the wet holes in the black mud(4),
  • and has on its sides big forked horns (3)
  • comes out of the hole (4)
  • the tuṟai (tuṟai: ghat) is without noise as no one passes that way (5).
  • The paratavar (paratavar: fishermen) who have big boats (11),
  • having avoided the sharks that attack people (10)
  • dive into the bright extensive area of the sea and bring to the surface pearls formed in valampuri conch (11),
  • get down from their boats, (13)
  • when the conches that can produce sound are blown, (12)
  • to be received warmly by the people of Koṟkai (koṟkai: a seaport town belonging to Pāṇṭiyar where there are pearl fisheries) of great bustle (13),
  • with a great noise resembling `Kal', (12)
  • at the long coast-line having mound of sand (14)
  • yonder you can see our small but good village; please have a look at it (15).
  • Without ordering (7)
  • (your charioteer) to harness the mules of well-shaped legs which are willing to be harnessed in the curved yoke (6)
  • ready to start, in the tall chariot having refined bells (7),
  • please stay to-night (in this village) to fulfil the wish of talaivi who has very beautiful and cool eyes (8).

NOTES

Talaivaṉ coming to talaivi's village riding on a mule is mentioned in akam in a previous stanza;
""kaḻaccuṟā eṟinta puṇtāṉ attiri neṭunīr iruṅkaḻip parimelin tacaii'' (120-10-11);
""vāṉa vaṇkaiyaṉ attiri ēṟa'' (cilappatikāram, 6-119).

The first five lines indicate that the day has come to an end.

pākaṉai - this is the object for ēvātu; it should be supplied this can be taken as the conch itself as it is highly valued.

Here the chariot drawn by mules is mentioned.

The friend refused to talimakaṉ who came at the secretly appointed place by day, that, and consented to his coming to the secretly appointed place by night.

TRANSLATION
  • Chief of the strong maritime tract (15)!
  • After spending the whole of the day-time in the cool grove of many flowers (1),
  • if you return your place (9)
  • in the evening which comes (9)
  • when the sun-god of mono-wheeled vehicle (2)
  • vanishes in the big mountin in the west (3),
  • when the group of bees after eating the fine pollen (5)
  • of the neytal (neytaṟpū- white indian water-lily) flowers of globular stem which are found in the cool mud of the bending backwater (4),
  • abandon them (5),
  • when it has the blue colour of the sea and the red colour of the evening sky (8)
  • resembling the red and blue colours of the two great gods (Civaṉ and Tirumāl), of great strength and inspiring awe (6),
  • are united in their appearance (7),
  • this lady (9)
  • is very much grieved (10).
  • Therefore (10),
  • guarding the proud horses of glorious action and running to the measure of time (11),
  • aginst making sound in the night when the whole village is asleep (12).
  • and leaving the tall chariot at a great distance (13),
  • and crossing over with difficulty the mound of accumulated sand like the elephant which descends the hill (14)
  • slowly (13),
  • please come at night (15),
  • at the secretly appointed place (19)
  • in the fragrant grove where there are puṉṉi trees (19)
  • from which well-blossomed fragrant flowers (18)
  • spread like gold (17)
  • in the palmyra tree (16)
  • growing in the frontyard (17),
  • where the bird aṉṟil (17)
  • and nāri (nārai pelican ibis) which feeds on the group of fish (16),
  • dwell (17).

NOTES

kaḻippi: this is the action of talimakaṉ, this should be joined to peyariṉ in line 9.

The sun is supposed to come in mono-wheeled chariot and is supposed to set in the west mountin.

The group of bees leave the water-flowers as they fold their petals at sunset and go elsewhere;
cf. ""niṟaintōrt tērum neñcamoṭu kuṟaintōr, payaṉ iṉmaiyiṉ paṟṟu viṭṭu orū um nayaṉ il mākkaḷ pōla vaṇṭiṉam, cuṉaippūnīttuc ciṉaippūt toṭara'' (akam, 71-1-4)

Lines 6-8. contin a beautiful simile. Civaṉ and Tirumal are considered as two great gods In puṟam Balarāmaṉ of white colour and having banner of palmyra tree, and Tirumal of blue colour wielding a disc are mentioned as two great gods and they are compared to one coḻaṉ and pāṭṭiyaṉ.

The horses are trined to walk to measure of time; so they are called naṭṭiyakkutiri;
""pantupuṭaip paṉṉa pāṇippallaṭic cilparikkutirai'' (akam, 105-9-10)

tēr akala nīkki;
""tērcēṇnīkkit tamiyaṉ vantu'' (akam, 380-1)

paṉaimicai aṉṟil cēkkum palmyra is the tree frequented by aṉṟil;
""ēṅku vayir icaiya koṭuvāy aṉṟil, ōṅkirum peṇṉai akamaṭal akala'' (Kuṟiñcippaṭṭu 219-20);
""pariyaraip peṇṇai aṉṟiṟ kuralē'' (naṟṟiṇi 218-11); akam, 50-11, 120-15, 260-6, 305-13;

puṉṉai naṟum poḻiṟ kuṟi: akam 30-13-14, 240-14-15, 290-9-10)
The friend advised talivaṉ to come by night without any obstacles to happen; so she sid;
""kutirai teviṭṭal ōmpi, tēr akala nīkki, paiyeṉa maṇal nīnti.

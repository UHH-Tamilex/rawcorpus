1-6) Foster-mother complins having seen talivi climbing up and descending down the loft in the house at midnight on a riny day.
(7) companion requesting mother not to tease talivi
(7-9) Gods roam about the place taking the forms they like (10-11)
Dreams too mystify sleeping people like true incidents during waking state.
(11-12) talivi afrid of remining along without light.
(12-14) talivi reaching a place of safety when she hears the cry of rock-horned owls which live in the common Kaḍamba (மராமரம்) tree
(15-16) Will not talivi be afrid of doing that when the father stays at home, though dogs do not follow her.?

TRANSLATION
  • Foster-mother! May you live long! (7)
  • Please do not afflict talivi (7)
  • by telling me, ``I saw talivi who has curly tresses which are spread out as the briding has become loose (4),
  • climbed up the loft and climbed down (6)
  • walking wearily like a peacock that descends down a hill, (5)
  • when her heavy ear-rings shone with brilliance like the stright lightning (3),
  • at midnight having dense darkness (2),
  • when the gathered clouds which thundered tumultuously poured torrential (1)
  • rins and became silent after that (2)''
  • Please hear with attention what I have to say (7)
  • In our garden which is adjacent to the hill where gods reside (7-8),
  • one deity adorns its head with bright flowers and hunts this place occasionally, assuming whatever forms it likes to take (9)
  • Not only that. Dreams also mystify people during their sleep just like the real happenings during waking state (10-11)
  • She will tremble even if she has to remin alone in the darkness without any light (11-12)
  • She will reach a place of protection with a trembling heart if she happens to hear the fearful cry of the rock-horned owl that lives in the common cadamba (மராமரம்) tree in the common place where villagers meet (13-14)
  • In adition to that our father too who has wrath like Lord Murukaṉ (முருகன்) and great strength stays in the house though she is not followed by dogs which can be compared to flock of tigers (14-17)
  • Will not this talivi be afrid of going to the garden and returning from there ? (18)

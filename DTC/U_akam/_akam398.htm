Table of contents (by lines):
(1-6) The pitiable condition of talivi
(7-12) Talivi afrid of admonishing and being sulky with, the river that flows from talivaṉ's country.
(13-15) Talivi thinking the river to be a messenger of talivaṉ.
(15-16) Talivaṉ strong to forsake people whom he loved.
(17-21) Talivi requesting the river to stay for that day in her place and then proceed further.
(22-25) Description of the talivaṉ's mountinous country.
Colophon(s):
Talimakaḷ spoke sulkily to the river which came from the mountin belonging to talimakaṉ, employing words expressive of acute distress as her longing increased.

NOTES

The flowers of Koṉṟi compared to sallowness:
""ceṉṟa nāṭṭa koṉṟaiyam pacuvī, nam pōṟ pacakkum kālai'' (Kuṟuntoki, 183-1-2)

nummōṉ one who is your chief.

kuṉṟu keḻu nāṭaṉ - in line 10 is used in the sense of a demonstrative pronoun as talivaṉ has been mentioned by the name of nummōṉ - As talivi sid your chief and chief of the mountinous country her admonishing and being sulky are with regard to the river.

maṟaintu kaḻital - covering itself with flowers so that water cannot be seen. Being afrid of being sulky with you, you covered yourself with flowers and passed away shrinking yourself; that being your case the stranger of a lover having brought and directed you, is strong enough to forsake me" These are the ideas of talivi

iṉṟu ivaṇ cēntaṉai celiṉ citaivatu uṇṭō - talivi spoke these words, "As there is no harm that may befall you and as you are coming from the mountinous country of talivaṉ. I can stay with you for a day and thereby asssuage my distress" to give vent to her excessive distress. The emaciated elephant will breathe like the musical instrument, `tūmpu';
""ōykaḷiṟu eṭutta nōyuṭai neṭuṅkai, toku coṟ kōṭiyar tūmpiṉ uyirkkum'' (akam, 111-8-9); ""ceṟi naṭaip piṭiyoṭu kaḷiṟu puṇarnteṉṉak, kuṟu neṭuntū m poṭu muḻavup puṇarnticaippa'' (akam, 301-16-17)

kaḻai here stands for `tūmpu' (tūmpu) a musical instrument made of bamboo.

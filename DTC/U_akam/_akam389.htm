TRANSLATION
  • Friend ! May you flourish ! you talk without knowing the facts (1)
  • Having protected me (8)
  • in the following ways by smearing red pigment in the red, beautiful and small feet (7)
  • which have small ankles (7),
  • drawing figures on the big shoulders (6),
  • sticking inner petals (5),
  • nipping fresh flowers of many petals to be in contrast in colour (4)
  • in the good, young and beautiful breasts, (5),
  • fixing the circular mark on the beautiful forehead which has a sweet smell (3),
  • having dried (2)
  • the curly locks of hair (1)
  • on which a paste has been smeared, by passing the fingers through them (2)
  • and adorning them with flowers (2),
  • and speaking praisingly about you (8)
  • (our talaivar) used to be in the house formerly (10)
  • without leaving, even in the daytime, the bed on which many flowers are spread (9),
  • has now gone crossing the paths in the mountains which has tall trees (24)
  • where in the place adjacent to the cruel forest of intense heat (17),
  • which is situated beyond the fertile country of vaṉavarampaṉ (16)
  • of strong vēl (15),
  • the big tiger of bright colour (20)
  • which killed the big male elephant (20)
  • which stays in a different place leaving its own place, desirous of killing (19)
  • to make the new travellers to cry aloud running in different directions (18)
  • by jumping and bending its legs (21),
  • in the ground made red by blood (21),
  • to make it fell on the right side (22),
  • roars its roar to be echoed by the mountain (22)
  • like the thunder produced in the raining cloud (23);
  • to establish good fame in their places (15),
  • and to destroy the affliction born out of the mean and ridiculing words (14)
  • of enemies, (13)
  • thinking of acquiring rare wealth (13),
  • which is the cause for rejoicing of protectors (11)
  • after having given new things (12)
  • returning quickly (10)
  • with a mind not knowing grief (12)
  • and filling the stretched-out hands of supplicants (11).

NOTES

aṇintu the clitic um should be added to this word also, as that word is found in ll 3, 5 and 7.

If eṟpuṟantanta is the reading the meaning will be : you who protected me having adorned me in all these ways.

putuva wealth that was newly acquired.

purappōr(11) tuṉaitantu(10) uvakkum(12) is the syntactical order.

tantu uvakkum: rejoicing seeing the gleaming face of the recipients to whom money was given.

Enemies will speak ridiculing words about people who have no money; great souls will not brook such words; cf. ""kaṟuttōr, eḷḷal neñcattu ēec col nāṇi'' (akam, 111-1-2)

vāṉavarampaṉ : this is the family name of cēra Kings; the meaning is one who royal authority extended upto the celestial world; ""vāṉa varampaṉai nīyōperuma'' (puṟam, 2-12); vāṉavarampaṉeṉap pēriṉitu viḷakki'' (patikam of patiṟṟuppattu, 6th decad)

There was a curam beyond the frontiers of cērantu; this is found in an old vempa that mentions the boundaries of Konkunāṭu; ""vaṭakkup perum pālai vaikāvūr teṟkuk, kuṭakkup poruppu veḷḷik kuṉṟu-kiṭakkum, kaḷit taṇṭalai mēvum kāviri cūḻ nāṭṭuk, kuḻittaṇṭalaiyaḷavum koṅku'' This is mentioned as eighteen in number in the three ulās by oṭṭakūttar; ""pāṇpakaloṉṟiṉ īroṉpatu curamum, koṇṭu malai nāṭu koṇṭōṉum'' (vikkirama cōḻaṉula, 17); ""ēṟippaka loṉṟil eccuramum pōy utakai, nūṟṟittaṉ tūtaṉai nōkkiṉāṉ'' (Kulottunkaṉula, 24); ""matakayattāl īroṉpatu curamu maṭṭittu utakaiyait tītta uravōṉ'' (rācarācacoḻaṉula, 21)

Talaivi replied to the friend who asked her to put up with the separation by telling her, that, when the talaivar who did not leave her even during daytime by protecting her in so many ways and speaking praisingly about the friend, had gone into the cruel forest-paths beset with dangers, how could it be possible for talaivi to do so ? 

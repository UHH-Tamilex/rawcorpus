TRANSLATION
  • The season has come to stay as the first half of the dewy season (5),
  • when the swift-moving water rushes like snakes (3)
  • in those places where the wheels of strong spokes (2)
  • of the tall fast-moving chariot to which are harnessed speeding horses (1),
  • have made deep depressions (2);
  • the unripe fruits of green gram plant having been closed first have now become ripe like the fingers that are leaving their connection (4)
  • My mind (8)
  • being pushed in front of manliness (7)
  • which does not know weariness, and started for manly effort (6)
  • and hindered at the back by longing, (7)
  • is being divided in itself (8)!
  • We are (at present) like the ant which is cught in the middle of a fire burning at both ends at the joints of a hollow stick, which suffers (on account of wavering) as it cannot go either way (to escape). (Will our talivi) who has a friendship like that which exists between soul and the body (12)
  • and (11)
  • a love that is like the life (12)
  • existing with pleasure in the body (13)
  • a separation which gives distress like death (14),
  • and very difficult to get at (14),
  • be staying with sorrow ? She is to be pitied (11).

NOTES

This idea that rin water flows swiftly in the depression made by the wheels, like the snake, is found in another poem in akam;
""nēmi, taṇṇila maruṅkiṟ pōḻnta vaḻiyuḷ, nirai cel pāmpiṉ viraipunīr mukuka. (324-11-13)

ōṭa, uḻappa: these adverbial participles take the finite verb niṉṟaṉṟu.

As manliness pulls the mind in front, we have to infer that longing hinders it behind.

The mind is sid to be divided as it has not decided either in favour of going or staying.

neñcam: nominative of address

akaiya - to burn;
""akai eri... āṉātu uḷḷam cuṭum'' (Kalittoka, 139-26-27)

uṟavi-eṟumpu (ants)
""irutalaik koḷḷi eṟumpu pōla'' is a proverb;
""periya naṭṭārkkum pakaivarkkum ceṉṟu, tiriviṉṟit tīrntār pōṟ colli avaruḷ, oruvarōṭu oṉṟi oruppaṭātārē irutalaik koḷḷi eṉpār'' (Paḻamoḻi, 10);
""nāṇ=orupāl vāṅka nalaṉ orupāl uḷ nekiḻppa, kāmaṟātōḷ kiḷḷikku eṉ kaṇ kavaṟṟa-yāmattu, irutalaik koḷḷiyiṉ uḷliṉumpu pōla, tilitarum pērum eṉ neñcu'' (muttoḷḷāyiram, 92);
""irutalaikkoḷḷiyiṉ uḷ eṟumpu ottu niṉaippirinta, viritalai, yēṉai'' (tiruvācakam, 9).

The parting from talivi produces such suffering as pinful as death;
Cf. ""kātaliṉ iṉṉātatu illai'' (Kuṟaḷ, 230)

If we expand the simile avvuyir vāḻtal aṉṉa kātal, it will mean that life exists in the body enjoying pleasure; just like that the love also gives pleasure.
""vāḻtal uyirkku aṉṉaḷ āyiḻai; cātal, ataṟku aṉṉaḷ nīṅkum iṭattu'' (Kuṟaḷ, 124)

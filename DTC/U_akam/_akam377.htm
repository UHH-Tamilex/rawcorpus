Table of contents (by lines):
1-5. The habits of hunters.
6-7. The common place of the village having become completely desolate; as the residents have left it.
7-10. The public hall which was used as a place for playing dice by elders lost its beauty by the termites gnawing it.
11-13. Talivaṉ admonishing his mind which is reluctant to proceed further.
14-15. People who want to do the good act of giving supplicants what they ask to establish their fame, will not mind going in the curam.
Colophon(s):
Talimakaṉ spoke to his mind which was determined to carry out the plan of acquiring wealth.

TRANSLATION
  • (My mind)! Thinking of the great beauty of the young lady of soft tenderness (12)
  • and sweet smile (11)
  • staying in a side which is not pleasant (1),
  • of the public hall of increasing fear, and spoiled great beauty (10)
  • which is without splendour as it has been gnawed by the bright termites of lines (9)
  • to destroy the beauty of the ground (8)
  • on which the grey-hired elders (7)
  • play (the game of dice) having bent their shaking heads (7)
  • having thought about the movements of coins in their divided mind, in the assembly of great desolation from which the rich residents have left (6)
  • and the hunters of big bows and food which they did not sow which they got from the holes of many small ants in the ground, where they went in a line (2)
  • and collected the small grins of cluster grass (2)
  • which shed in the droughty soil as the heat of the summer was acute (1),
  • people who wish to give (15)
  • the supplicant who are prompted by the desire fo receiving gifts, and go to donors (14),
  • will definitely go further even if they become new travellers (13)
  • in order to establish their fame (15).

NOTES

The grins collected and preserved by ants is the food for hunters; ""nōṉ kāḻ irumpu talaiyātta tiruntu kaṇai viḻukkōl, uḷivāyc curaiyiṉ miḷira miṇṭi, irunilak karamppa paṭunīṟāṭi, nuṇpul aṭakkiya veṇpal eyiṟṟiyar'' (perumpāṇaṟṟuppati, 90-94); ""mika viḷaintu utirnta nellai eṟumpu iḻuttuc cēra iṭṭu vaitta iṭam aṟintu eṭuttal annilap paṇpu'' (Do. commentary of nacciṉārkkiṉiyar) The huntes do not sow and reap but eat the grins gathered by ants with great effort in their holes, taking them out by digging the ants holes.

maṉattu iruttum vallu the game of dice about which they think in thier minds. vallu the coins of dice; here it stands for the place where it is played; grey-hired elders play the game of dice in the public hall of the village assembly; ""kalikeḻu kantam kaṭavuḷ kaiviṭap, palikaṇ māṟiya pāḻpaṭu potiyil, narai mūtāḷar nāyiṭak kuḻinta, valliṉ nallakam niṟaiyap palpoṟik, kāṉavāraṇam īṉum, kāṭāki viḷiyum nāṭuṭaiyōrē'' (puṟam, 52-12-17).

By saying "āṟṟuvōrkaḻipa' talivaṉ hinted to his mind, `you think of the beauty of talivi in the middle of the curam and wish to return. 

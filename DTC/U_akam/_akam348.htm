Table of contents (by lines):
1-7. The toddy called tōppi and the ingredients with which it is prepared.
7-9. The Kuṟavar woemn first offer it to the deities residing in the mountin and cuse their men to drink it.
10-11. The elephants graze in the millet farm cusing damage to the crops when the Kuṟavar forgot to guard it as a result of drunkeness.
12-13. The young and the old wander in search of the elephants, having selected their bows.
14-1. Talivaṉ fearing the harm that may befall her, by trusting in the words of tālivaṉ which are not firm.

TRANSLATION
  • My mind which believed the good words of talivaṉ which were not firm (14)
  • what harm, will befall us ! (1)
  • He is the ruler of the country where the young and the old wander having selected their bows (13)
  • and having assembled with their relations (12)
  • without enduring the idea that the elephants grazed the crops in the tīṉi (tiṉai) farm (11),
  • when the Kuṟuvar men forgot to guard the big millet farm in the adjacent mountin (10),
  • after having consumed the clarified toddy which was poured into the hollow of the moving bamboo between two long joints (6)
  • on which bees swarm and which was prepared mixed with honey (5),
  • and adding pulps of equal sweetness of the jack fruit which is full of resin (4),
  • along with the sweet-smelling fruits of sweet taste like honey which ripened (3)
  • in summer in the mango tree of globular trunk (2),
  • growing in the frontyard (1)
  • and goes by the name of tōppi which is as wrathful as the anger of the great roughness, which was to cused to be drunk by their women folk of wearing garments of leaves, who served them that liquor after offering it to the high mountin of tall peaks which is the abode of gods.

NOTES

tēṉ tēr cuvaiya - the sweetness which reminds one of honey.

payirppu - the stickly resin in the pulp of the jack fruit; ""palkōṭ palaviṉ payirppuṟu tīṅkaṉi''
- (Kalittoki, 50-12);

iṟāl - honey-comb; here it means the honey in it. The toddy is poured into hollow of the bamboo and left to ferment: that is why it is mentioned as "āṭu amaippaḻuni'
""ampaṇai viḷainta tēkkaṉ tēṟal, kuṉṟakac ciṟu kuṭikkiḷaiyuṭaṉ makiḻntu'' (murukarruppaṭi, 194-196);
""perumalai, vāṅku amaippaḻuniya naṟavuṇṭu, vēṅkai muṉṟiṟ kuravaiyum kaṇṭē'' (naṟṟiṇi, 276-8-10);
""kuṟiyiṟaik kurampaik kuṟavar mākkaṉ, vāṅmaip paḻuniya tēṟal makiḻntu vēṅkai nīḻaṟ, kuravai ayarum'' (puṟam, 129-1-3)

pāppuk kaṭuppaṉṉa tēṟal: The higly fermented toddy has such intoxication that it is compared to the snake bite and scopion sting;
""pāmpuvekuṇṭaṉṉa tēṟal nal'' (ciṟupāṇāṟṟuppaṭi, 237;
""tēṭkaṭuppu aṉṉa nāṭpaṭu tēṟal'' (puṟam, 292-16).

ariyal: should be put in apposition with tōppi, tōppi:
""illaṭu kaṉṉiṉ tōppi paruki'' (perumpāṇāṟṟuppati, 142);
in the commentary on this line nacciṉāṟkkiṉiyar says that tōppi is a variety of toddy prepared from paddy; here the ingredients are different;
""tōppik kaḷḷoṭu tūrū uppali koṭukkum'', tukaḷaṟa viḷainta tōppi paruki'' (akam, 35-9, 265-16)
""muḻaṅku munnīr muṉtuṟaik kalam, puṇar kammiyar, tuḻantu aṭu kaḷḷiṉ tōppi paruki'' (maṇimēkali, 7-70-71);

Even the Kuṟavar women offered the toddy first to the gods and then served it to their men-folk.

kaṭavuḷ ōṅkuvaraikku ōkki: the order of the word may be changed like this: ōṅkuvaraik kaṭavuṭku ōkki:
""manti......peruvaraimēl, tēṉ tēvarkku ōkkum malai nāṭa'' (tiṇimāli nūṟṟimpatu, 10) line 12: this line is found respectively in perumpānāṟṟuppati and akam with a slight variation;
""iḷaiyarum mutiyarum kiḷaiyuṭaṉ tuvaṉṟi'' (267, 30-4). The Kuṟavar forgot their duty of guarding the millet farm due to drunkenness. 

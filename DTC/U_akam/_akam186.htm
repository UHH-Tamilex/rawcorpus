(186) marutam

Lines: (1-3) The tank abundant with fish and the fishermen do not depend on rin for their livelihood (3-6) The broad leaf of the lotus is cused to move like the ears of an elephant, by the wind (6-7) The idle gossip of the village which rose on account of talivaṉ is great (7-9) Talivaṉ's intimacy is only for sporting in water with the help of a float, made of European bamboo reed (9-13) talivaṉ still remining in the company of some other lady (13-4) His wife is angry with the harlot (14-7) harlot not intending to break her bangles taking liberty with talivaṉ (18-20) Talivi's husband and not the harlot is the enemy permanently remining with her.

Translation: The fishermen who earn their livelihood by dragging the fishing-hook with a strong string (2) knowing the fish swallowing the hook covered with a bit, (2) do not depend on rinfall nor do they know any poverty (1) In the tank of fertile lotuses (3) where fish are in abundance (3), the broade leaf of the lotus which has a long stem (5) and which rises above the surface of the water (4) whenever the wind blows (5) The big and placid tank is disturbed (5) and the leaf is cused to move (6) like the ears of a big elephant (6) The scandal for which the chief of agricultural tract was the cuse (6) and which rose in connection with me (7) is great (7). His friendship (7) was only to that extent of sporting in water (9) with the help of a float made of European bamboo reed which had grown fertile (8) He, on his part, is still in some lady's house (13) having embraced the shoulders which have the cool fragrance of sandal (12) when ladies wearing bright bracelets play upon the ancient instrument of yaḻ (யாழ்) (10) and rap with a stick the much cool muḻavu (முழவு) (11). (When such is the state of affairs) people say (14) that teh wife of talivaṉ (14) hates me (14) I am not breaking with liberty the tight-fitting bangles of mine (17). I can be compared to pōr (போர்

- a village in coḻa nāṭu) which is in the country waterd by Kāviri (16) and which belongs to paḻayaṉ (பழையன்) (15) who has a victorious lance (14), arrows showering like rin, and a shield resembling the cloud (15). We are in the relationship of enemy to her (18). Her husband himself (20) who deserts ladies with whom he had union, for their beautiful foreheads to turn sallow (19) and who permanently lives with her, is sufficient to be her enemy (20)

Syntactical link :- ஊரனொடு(6) (எனக்கு) எழுந்த கௌவையோச் பெரிது(7); நட்பே(7) வேழத்துப் புணை துணையாகப்(8) புனல் ஆடு தேண்மை அனைத்தே(9); அவனே இன்னும் பிறள் வயினான்(13); மனையோள்(13) எம்மொடு புலக்கும் என்ப(14); செறிவளை உடைத்தலோ இலென்(17); யாம் தன் பகையேம் அல்லேம்(18); கொழு நன் தன் உடன் உறை பகை சாலும்(20).

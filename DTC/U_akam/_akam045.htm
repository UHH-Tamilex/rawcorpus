TRANSLATION
  • Our beloved one went crossing the forest (6)
  • where the tiger roams about in the desert where there is no movement of (5)
  • people, in the wide and big hill where summer stays with its severity in the difficult path which has no water (3-4)
  • having killed a tall male elephant (4).
  • The beautiful and dry bunches of mature seeds of sirissa tree (uḻiñcil) (1)
  • make a sound intermittently like the drum (karaṭikai) (Karatikai) which is played on the dance stage (2).
  • Let the māmai (māmai change its colour like the beautiful flowers of sponge gourd (pīram) by the spreading of beautiful and minute sallowness (pacalai) (6-7).
  • The noise of scandal born out of it is like the tumultuous uproar by the dancers in singing the fame on the battle field of Kurukkai (kuṟukkai) when Aṉṉi (a chieftain) felled down and cut into pieces the big trunk of the old mast wood tree (puṉṉai) belonging to Titiyaṉ (8-12)
  • Shall I, who is without sleep because of the fearful sorrow like the people inside a single battered defence of a fortress which became possessed with fear in the valourous battle front of Vāṉavarampaṉ who has a good lance by which he made the sea recede, and a garland made of gold, wander like Ātimanti and search for my husband whom I could not find?

NOTES

Ātimanti is alluded to in stanzas 76, and 366 of this work and 31 of Kuṟuntokai. Veḷḷivīti also lost her husband. It is alluded to in stanza 147, akam, and stanza 27, Kuṟuntokai.

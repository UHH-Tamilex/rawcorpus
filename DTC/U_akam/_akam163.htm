(163)

Lines: (1-2) The end of the autumn season (3-4) The state of talivi eagerly expecting the return of talivaṉ (6-9) talivi addresses the northern wind which is without any mercy upon people who are alone (10-13) talivaṉ has gone, so that he might be reminded of talivi.

Translation: I am besides myself with distress that may cuse death (6) and I am eagerly looking in the direction (5) from which talivaṉ may return (5), and suffer on account of that (5). At the end of the decreasing rinfall at the end of autumn after the clouds had a heavy downpour (2), gathering themselves in the sky with thunders, (1) to make different kinds of flowers to fade (1), I am alone (3) I am thinking of talivar who parted from me to increase the hatred of my mind (3) and the few bangles that remined have also slipped off from my hands, when I thought of him (4) The chill northern wind which makes even the hill to shiver with cold (9) at midnight in the season when dew falls during the early part of night and when lotus flowers become scorched (charred) (8) by the drops of dew which fall to cover the ground and which resembles the sound of the elephant's sigh after drinking water (7)! You seem as if you have come to inflict pin on me only (10)! You go to that country to which the merciless talivar has gone (12), to make his stony heart to melt, and allow pity to enter into the place where water (11), with the same nature without (10-13) becoming weary and tired (12) If so the talivar whose longing in acquiring wealth is very great, may perhaps be reminded of me, and return (14)

Syntactical link :- tāḻ peyaṟ kaṭai nāḷ(2) ceṉṟōr uḷḷi(4) evvamoṭu(6) (iruttaliṉ) aḷiyaḷ eṉṉātu(6) kuḷir keraṉvāṭai! eṉakkē vantaṉai pōṟi(10); koṭiyōr ceṉṟa tē ettuc(12) ceṉmati(13)! viṉaivituppuṟunar uḷḷalum uṇṭu(14).

The talimakaḷ replied to the companion who consoled her during separation, as her unbearability exceeding its limits.

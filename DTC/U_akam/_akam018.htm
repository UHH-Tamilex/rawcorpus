AKAM 018

(18) Kuṟiñcī

  • Lines 1-8 Describing the forest and the talivaṉ coming by night
  • (9-10). The talivi's distress
  • (11-2). The dangers in the way by which talivaṉ comes
  • (13-18). Requesting him to come by day.

Translation

  • Chief of the high mountinous tract (8)!
  • In the forest rivers which flows swiftly the colour of the water is concealed by the beautiful flowers which drop into it profusely, and they become old (1-2).
  • (In that river) there are whirlpools which dash upon the rocks and recede and which abound in crocodiles (3).
  • Having swum the frightful floods which dash aginst the musty male elephants which are not moving with their females and calves, and drag them by its force; (4-5)
  • Having crossed and reached the bank without trembling, like the fearless pig and crossing and difficult and fearful ghat (துறை), are there people who would come to such a place as this at dead of night? (6-8).
  • My friend will not survive even on the next day if perchance some mishap befalls you on a particular day (9-10).
  • Even people who travel many times in the paths in which there are absolutely no obstacles quickly lose their way (11-12).
  • Therefore we, being perturbed in mind, undergo suffering (12-13).
  • Even if you come by day in the beautiful bush of malabar glory lily plants which is inside a dense grove of trees from which fruits are hanging in the long mountin of high peaks adjoining our garden, where the bees have constructed round bee-hive (13-16)
  • you can embrace the soft and big shoulders which were brought up protected by the mother and which resemble the middle portions between two joints of a bending bamboo growing in the broad mountin (16-18).

Syntactical link: வெற்ப(8)! யாற்று(2) நீத்தம்(5) நடுங்காது துறந்து(6) யாமத்து(7) ஈங்கும் வருபவோ (8)? தோழி வாழ்குவள் அல்லள்(10); வழிகளும் வழங்கு ஒர்(11) இழுக்குவர்(12); அதனால்(12) வருத்தம் உறுதும்(13); தோள்(18) பெறும்பில்(15) பகல்வரினும் புணர்குவை(16).

The companion asked talivaṉ who was coming by night to come by day.

(225) pāli

Lines: 1-4 talivaṉ embracing talivi My mind (14) ! Having embraced each other by reciprocating (3) the qualities of love, being blind to each other's frilties and faults, softness, good conduct (1), sweet words that are capable of cusing the bones to lose their stiffness and other desirable qualities being equal on both sides (2) we will stay today in our house (4) (

Lines: 5-14) Description of the desert. Tomorrow (4) we will stay alone at the small hamlet inhabited by good-natured people, where rin has forgetten its duty of pouring even drops of water (13); there the closed bunches of mahua tree covered by tender leaves (11) which have blossomed, appear like the arrows collected and placed in a quiven, whose heads are open to view (10), fall on the red ground (12) in the difficult desert where teak trees grow thickly and is full of forests having boulders (9). The flowers drop like pearls in which small holes have been made (12); there the western wind that enters into, and passes out of, the sound (5-6) akin to that of the music produced by cowherds holding a long stick, in their flutes when they follow the herd of cows (7) which go to drink water (7) (

Lines: 15-18) The state of talivaṉ We will be thinking of the looks showing a confused mind and sulks (17) of our loving wife (16) who wears on her wrist tight fitting banges (16) and who has jet-black tresses flowing on the back , like the sable cloud (15) and decorated with luxuriant flowers (14)

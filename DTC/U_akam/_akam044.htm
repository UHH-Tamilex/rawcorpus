TRANSLATION
  • The king having gone to the battle-field has brought the war to a victorious end (1).
  • The enemies have pid the tributes which were decided to be pid by them and became his kith and kin (1-2).
  • The two armies which had the greatest enmity towards each other beat the war drum, signifying their oneness. These warriors bearing the names of Naṉṉaṉ, Ēṟṟi, Atti who wore good jewels, Kaṅkaṉ, of such great valour as his enemies were afrid of coming close to him, and Puṉṟuṟi who had golden ornaments and a bow that can pierce through many aims had confederated among themselves and were ready to fight (7-9)
  • Paḻayaṉ fought with them in the enemies'tents whose greatness could not be measured, for the kites to hover over the battle-field, and fell in the battle (10-11)
  • Perumpūṭceṉṉi on seeing that, could not brook it and captured Kaḻumalam in addition to Kaṇiyaṉ who had strong chariots (12-3).
  • Kutavāyil which belongs to that Ceṉṉi who has a chaplet tied round his head (14),
  • never ceasing income like Aḻumpil, a place in Pāṇtiya country (15),
  • old paddy in abundance, dense and spreading population (16),
  • deep tanks in which elephants immerse themselves for bathing. (17)
  • and a green forest for defence (17)
  • our talivi is like that cool Kuṭavāyil (18)
  • In order to enjoy sweet sleep lying on the breasts of our talivi who endured the separation till our return without dying (19)
  • charioteer! drive your chariot which is in front of the army, not to lag behind avoiding and giving way to chariots that have returned, and to leave the desert behind us.

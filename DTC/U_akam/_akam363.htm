Table of contents (by lines):
1-4. Friend advising talivi not to get depressed in the evening.
5-9. The forest where the fruits of nelli (nelli) spread like gold coins as the violent winds shed them.
10-15. The description of the curam where talivaṉ had gone.
16-17. Talivar returning having desired talivi.
18-19. The looks of talivi's eyes will become very beautiful
Colophon(s):
The friend spoke to the talimakaḷ who came changed during separation

TRANSLATION
  • Friend (17)!
  • In the forest (9)
  • where the stinless green fruits of nelli of tiny leaves (6)
  • and stout trunk (5)
  • spread beautifully like the gold coins (8)
  • in the stony paths as they are made to shed by the violent wind (7).
  • Having reached (that path) (9)
  • the inhabitant of the desert region of cruel deed and delighting in their principle of killing people (10),
  • were on the look out on that way, (9)
  • the male of the Kite having sharp talons and a curved beak (14)
  • which reached the travellers who died having received severe wounds by the vēl being thrown (12)
  • on their eminent chest in that way (11),
  • calls by its clear voice its kind to eat their flesh (14).
  • Our talivar who went beyond that hot curam inflicting suffering (15),
  • crowning himself with success in the business of acquiring wealth which he had in mind (15)
  • has returned (16)
  • here having desired us (16)
  • to make (16)
  • your looks resembling deer (19)
  • in your collyrium coated eyes (17)
  • which are like the twin black and beautiful flowers fixed opposite to each other (18),
  • to regin their superb beauty (19);
  • (Therefore) lady who wears beautiful ornaments (4)!
  • After driving the horses moving in a row to go fast (1),
  • and having gone in the broad and expansive sky for the day to pass (2),
  • when the sun of mellow rays sets in the big (western) mountin (3),
  • do not be of this nature having reached the bed (5)
  • and having become emaciated like the flowers that close when the day comes to an end (4).

DIFFICULT WORDS
nirai celal ivuḷi viraivuṭaṉ kaṭaii
- having driven fast the horses which go in a row.
akal iru vicum pil pakal celac ceṉṟu
- having gone, for the day to pass off in the spacious big sky.
maḻuku cuṭar maṇṭilam mā mālai maṟaiya
- the sun of mellow light to vanish in the big mountin
poḻutu kaḻi malariṉ cāay
- fading like the flower that folds its petals when the day is over.
puṉai iḻai
- lady wearing beautiful ornaments!
aṇai aṇaintu
- having reached the bed.
aṉaiyai ākal
- do not be of this nature.
kaṇai aru
- the stout trunk.
pul ilai nellippukar il pacuṅkāy
- the green and stinless fruits of nelli (nelli: emblic myrobalma) of tiny leaves.
kal aṟai maruṅkil kaṭuvaḷi utirppa
- as the violent wind makes them shed; in the paths having stones.
polaṉ cey kāciṉ poṟpat tāam
- spread lying beautifully like gold coins
attam naṇṇi
- having reached the forest
atar pārttu irunta
- keeping watch on people who go that way
kolai veṅ koḷkaik koṭun toḻil maṟavar
- the inhabitants of the desert tract who have murder as their principle and other cruel deeds
āṟu cel mākkaḷ aru niṟattu eṟinta eḵkuṟu viḻuppuṇ kūrntōr eytiya
- that reached the people who received many severe words by the vēl being thrown on the eminent chest of travellers and are dead
vaḷai vāy vaḷ ukirp paruntiṉ cēval
- the male of the kite of curved beak and sharp talons
keḻu muṭai
in order to eat the flesh that is avilable there
kiḷai taru teḷ viḷip payirum
- calls by its clear voice which invites its kind
iṉṉā veñ curam iṟantōr
- our talivar who went beyond the cruel curam which inflicts suffering
muṉṉiya ceyviṉai valattar
- having been crowned with success in the business of acquiring wealth which he had in mind.
mai eḻil tuṇai ēr etir malar uṇkaṇ piṇai ēr nōkkam
- your looks resembling the looks of the female deer, of the collyrium coated eyes which are like two black and beautiful flowers fixed opposite to each other.
peruṅ kaviṉ koḷa
- to become very beautiful.
ivaṇ nayantu eyta vantaṉar
- he has arrived here, having desired us.

NOTES

It is mythology that there are seven horses drawing the sun's chariot, the author of this poem describes them as speeding in a row

maṇṭilam - is the subject for kaṭaii, ceṉṟu, maṟaiya

ākal: is a verb in the negative mood

viḻup puṇ - wounds that are received on the face and chest

eyta vantaṉar - may also mean has arrived to be near us.

AKAM 26

(26) marutam

  • Lines 1-4. Description of the village belonging to the chief
  • (5-10). His flattery in former times
  • (11-15). The talivi's wish at present
  • (16-26) talivi accepting and embracing him and narrating it to her companion.

Translation

  • The talivaṉ formerly sid, "Do not prevent the breasts which have black teats, and which have the beauty of the ornamental bond made of iron fixed to the tusks of elephants which break open, by battering the big doors of fortified walls, from embracing my chest so as to completely hide into it." (6-9)
  • There were times when he flattered these even when I sid feeling pin, "you please stop this', not agreeing to it (9-11).
  • But now when we very much wish that our breasts which are adorned with spreading yellow spots and which are beautiful and soft and which droop downwards with the weight of milk which prevented my son from wishing any other thing should be swollen when the milk flows in the colourful chest of the talivaṉ adorned with fragrant sandal-paste and be in tight embrace, he is afrid of the milk spilling on his chest (12-16).
  • At that juncture, on noticing the loosening of his hands which formerly would not leave from the sung embrace I saw my son who was slowly walking in the arms of the foster-mother and sid to him, "you are united to your beautiful harlots; we would be united to this beloved son" and slowly moved near my son (16-21)
  • On seeing that (21)
  • the talivaṉ sid, "I too have attachment towards him" and, bowing low, (22)
  • he embraced my back (23).
  • My mind became tender (25-26)
  • like the red soil in the field which was ploughed several times and which received copious downpour of rin (23-25)
  • and deserted me at night (5)
  • "Friend,! Is it possible for me who has a mind which went after him forsaking me (26)
  • to be sullenly to the chief of the village which has beautiful fields of great fertility (4-5)
  • where the girls who indulge in games collect for adorning during festival the black flowers with white stalks which resemble the white bones of fish and which drop from the circular bunches of thorny plant which has curved thorns (1-3)?

The talimakaṉ requested the companion for mediation. Not getting mediation from her, he entered into the house having as mediation his own unbearableness and has union with the talivi. The talimakaḷ narrated this to her companion who entered into the house at the time of his exit.

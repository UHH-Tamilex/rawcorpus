TRANSLATION
  • Chief ! talivi had a greater eagerness to elope with you (3)
  • who gently stroked her good long tresses and big shoulders (1),
  • than myself who was afrid of staying here without you (2).
  • To go along with you in the elopement which brings misfortune to me (17),
  • let the ways (8)
  • where the ladies living in small hamlets bring small quantities of food articles (5),
  • after driving away the old deer of several forked horns, that came to eat them, grind them in a wooden mill (6)
  • and the sound produced by that is similar to that (6)
  • of the mournful soft sound made by the rock horned-owl living in a distant place (6)
  • and every (7),
  • not be sid to be difficult to travel (9)
  • but become pleasant, the heat having subsided to a great extent (16).
  • Let the charred trees (9)
  • put forth tender leaves from the trunk, to make them beautiful (10)
  • as the cool clouds rined spreading drops of water, (12)
  • in order to remove the heat, (11)
  • the sky becoming dark (11).
  • The grass puts forth fresh blades in the good and long forest (13)
  • where the colourful cochineals (15)
  • which resemble the fine fibres of dyed cotton spread everywhere (14)
  • and beautiful the cool earth (15).

NOTES

añciya - means being afrid of separation and therefore having agreed to elopment.

It will be very difficult for talivi to be separated from you who lavished such love on her as to gently stroke her hirs and shoulders. Being afrid of separation from you I consented to elopement. Such being my position, the talivi was more eager than myself for elopment.

As the sound of the rock horned-owl comes from a distant place it is called meṉkural

pul may also mean trees such as date, and palmyra whose outer part is hard.

After rinfall the red coloured cochineal will spread themselves and make the earth beautiful.

In line 6 all have the reading tirivayiṉ and the commentators It makes no sense. tirivaiyiṉ - should be the correct reading.

In chariots is compared to the sound produced by the tirimaram into which millet and other grins are put and ground; ""tirimarak kuralicai karuppa''

The sound of the distant rock horned-owl is compared here to the sound produced when food grins are put into the wooden grinding machine and rotated.

Though talivi goes in the company of talivaṉ she leaves her bosom friend, her circle of playmates and places she was familiar with for a very long time; it is a literary convention to consider that aspect also as pāli.

As elopement bring misfortune to the friend, the mother and other peoplem it is called "iṉṉā nīppu' by the friend of talivi. 

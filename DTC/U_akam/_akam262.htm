TRANSLATION
  • (The Kōcar) had the interwining creepers trampled down by the hoofs of many bullocks (2)
  • in the old forest which was overgrown with wild vegetation (1)
  • and had it ploughed, by yoking several bullocks, many times (2)
  • the field of red soil (2);
  • They had manured profusely and had removed the weeds and feneed it and irrigated it (3);
  • on account of these there was bumper crop. In that field of green gram which had densely grown leaves, the cows of the father of aṉṉi miñili entered and destroyed the crop by grazing (4)
  • Though the father of miñili being truthful admitted the fact of his cow having grazed in the field the old Kocar of that village (6)
  • pulled out his eyes without any grace (5)
  • On account of the cruel punishment meted out to her father (6),
  • she did not eat from any vessel nor did she wear any white cloth and she did not change the guise of an ascetic she took upon herself due to anger (8).
  • She informed Kuṟumpiyaṉ (9)
  • titiyaṉ (10)
  • who was always victorious and had a valourous army (9)
  • and good horses well trained in warfare (10)
  • and had their precious lives taken out (with his help) (11)
  • She saw that and her anger cooled down and she rejoiced (12)
  • The talaivi who speaks beautiful and sweet words (18)
  • had all the fragrance of the mountain on which clouds seem to traverse (17),
  • belonging to pēkaṉ, who had toddy on which bees settled, and joy born out of munificence (16).
  • In the adjacent hills (14),
  • which have flowers abundantly (13)
  • the streams fall roaring down and make the mountain pools look beautiful with water (14).
  • The fine spray rising from the pools make the bushes wet (15).
  • As she came and embraced us (18)
  • it makes our body swell (12)
  • with limitless joy (13)
  • like aṉṉi miñili (12).

NOTES

The incident about aṉṉi miñili has been mentioned by paraṇar himself, in akam, verse 196 ll.8-12. This verse is the second time the incident is mentioned by paraṇaar, with a little more detail.

By the term green forest is meant the forest in which grass and other vegetation have grown wild after rains.

Trampling down the intertwining creepers means that the creepers and others were mixed with the soil.

pala here means many ploughs.

Some have read yākuviṉai as varaku viṉai but it does not make any sense.

vāy moḻit tantai means that the father admitted the offence and did not try to hide it.

The order of words in kaṇkaḷaintu aruḷātu should be changed into aruḷātu kaṇkaḷaintu.

For a trifle offence the Kōcar inflicted very cruel punishment of removing the eyes of the father of aṉṉi miñili. It enraged her to such an extent that her anger cooled down only when the Kocar were killed by Kuṟumpiyan titiyaṉ in battle.

Even now in villages, grazing cattle in the fields where green gram crop is cultivated during the month of māci (Feb. 13th to March, 13) is the custom. Lean catle will become stout. There is a proverb in Tamil that the purohits during mahālaya paksha, the temple priests during mārkaḻi, and the he-buffaloes during māci will become plumpy. 

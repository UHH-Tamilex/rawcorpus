Table of contents (by lines):
1-9. Talivi and her friend were driving away the birds that came to eat tiṉi (தினை), being seated on a rised platform, and were listening to the melodious humming of the bees.
10-13. Talivaṉ questioning about an elephat which he shot with an arrow.
14. Talivaṉ followed by a hunting dog.
15-17. Talivi desirous of talivaṉ's beauty and his chest on which sandal paste was smeared.
18-21. Mother inviting vēḻaṉ to perform veṟi to reduce the distemper of talivi.
21-26. Talivi wishing to question vēlaṉ about talivaṉ, as he says he knows the remedy to reducethe distemper of talivi.

TRANSLATION
  • Friend ! May you flourish ! Hear what I say (1)
  • When we were hearing attentively the sweet humming sound (9)
  • of the tumpi which fed upon the fragrant pollen which is golden in colour (8),
  • seated in the high shed put up with a platform from which one can watch the field, and which was constructed high in the forked branch of the vēṅki tree (வேங்கை மரம்: east indian Kino tree) (7)
  • of sweet smelling flowers and bunches growing in the high and big mountin (6),
  • driving away the Kuruvi (குருவி a small bird) in the slopes of the big mountin (5)
  • where bamboos grow thickly (4),
  • as they (the Kuruvi) seize the small tiṉi (millet) which have ears of corn having fine fibres (4)
  • and which was sown and cultivated after ploughing the farm cutting down all the good and odourous sandal-wood trees (3),
  • with the taṭṭi of harsh sound, made of bamboo by cutting it between joints (2)
  • which grows in our mountin (1)
  • a youth (15)
  • (came there followed at his side by an angry dog looking sternly (14),
  • and questioned addressing us (13)
  • "young ladies with tresses of hir having oiliness and black colour ! (10)
  • Did a male elephant of rised tusk (12)
  • go by the side of your millet farm (13),
  • with many wounds cused by the speedful arrows piercing through its body (11),
  • and with pin? (12)?
  • After which questioning the youth with strong bow went away (15).
  • Not realising the suffering we undergo here (17)
  • having much desired his beauty and chest smeared with sandal-paste (16),
  • if (21)
  • the vēlaṉ endowed with great (19)
  • knowledge who is invited by our mother (19)
  • who has misunderstood in her good mind our affliction and decided definitely to perform veṟi dance, (18)
  • says (21),
  • `This distress is due to talivi being afflicted by our god (20),
  • "will your god of murukaṉ, go hunting shadowing the footprints of the wild animals, with arrows rolled over with flesh, which appears if they are dyed with red colour; and can pierce through the body of elephants of burning anger and mental delusion cused by must ?".

NOTES

தட்டையின்- must be addes to the adverbial participle ஓப்பி

உழுதல் - includes sowing, weeding, protecting and other agricultural operations

தட்டை an a instrument made of a bamboo of two joints which is cut into several parts and beaten aginst something to make a sound; cf. Kuṟiñcippāṭṭu, line 43, nacciṉārḵḵiṉiyars commentary.

நயந்து is the cuse of undergoing distress.

இந்நோய் must be added to as இந்நோய் வந்தன்று; and இந்நோய் தணி மருந்து

மெய் உளம் means the centre of the body; ""குன்றின் நெஞ்சு பக எறிந்த அஞ்சுடர் நெடுவேல்'' Kuṟuñtoki invocatory verse, 4.)

Ūṭṭiyār is the author of verses 68 and 388 in akam; perhaps the real name of the author was lost when this anthology was collected; so the poet who collected this gave him a name from a phrase used by this poet in verses 68 and 388, namely "ஊட்டியன்ன'. In Kuṟuntoki also the names of some poets are derived from the happy phrases they have used; cf. அணிலாடு முன்றிலார்(41); ஓரேருழவனார்(131); ஓரிற்பிச்சையார்(277); கங்குல் வெள்ளத்தார்(287); கல் பொருசிறு நுரையார்(290); கவை மகனார்(324) காலெறி கடிகையார்(267); குப்பைக் கோழியார்(305); கூவல் மைந்தனார்(224); செம்புலப் பெயனீரார்(47); நெடுவெண்ணிலவினார்(47); பதடிவை கலார்(323); மீனெறி தூண்டிலார்(54); வில்லக விரலினார்(370);

The idea of talimakaḷ telling her friend, "Our mother invited vēlaṉ not knowing the real cuse of our distemper, and if he says that the distemper was cused by murukaṉ and he knew the remedy for reducing it", amounts to this talivi would be placed under guard and restrint in the house and therefore she wanted to inform talivaṉ that she could not meet talivaṉ during clandestine love. Its effect would be for talivaṉ to understand this and get himself married soon.

Talivaṉ used to be accompanied by hunting dogs; ""பரியல் நாயொடு பல்மலைப் படரும், வேட்டுவற் பெறலொடு'', ""இயல் முருகு ஒப்பினை வயநாய் பிற்படப், பகல் வரிற் கவ்வை அஞ்சுதும்'', ""வேட்டொடு, வேய்பயில் அழுவத்துப் பிரிந்தபின், நாய் பயிர் குறி நிலை கொண்ட கோடே'' (akam, 28-7-8, 118-5-6, 318-13-15); ""முளைவாள் எயிற்ற எள் உகிர் ஞமலி, திளையாக் கண்ண வளைபுகு நெரிதர'' (Kuṟiñcippāṭṭu, 131-132) 

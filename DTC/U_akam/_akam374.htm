TRANSLATION
  • Charioteer who is clever in driving the chariot (16)!
  • In the dawn flowers spread their fragrance after the clouds poured good rin (10)
  • many times combining with many drops, and sweet sound (9)
  • which is like the music produced by the strings of the flawless yāḷ which has been stroked over, and rubbed, by the fingers (8)
  • by pāṇar (pāṇar : wandering minstrels) (9),
  • without thunder and loud sound, having practised pouring as if they were falling down (6)
  • descending nearer and nearer as if they had come down (5)
  • and emitting many streaks of lightning at the same time as if they were cleaving the sky (4),
  • by the many clouds pregnant with water, suffered from its weight (3),
  • rising clockwise to cover the wide world (2),
  • having become dark, spreading themselves in all the direction, having drawn the water from the big ocean (1)
  • (At that time) in the paths where saline soil appear and there is a mound of dense sand (11)
  • the cochineals of small stomacha run short spaces (12)
  • and vanish hiding themselves under the old faded beautiful flowers of Kāyā (kāyā- iron wood tree), resembling the coral mixed with sapphire (13)
  • At that time when the pleasant winter appears beautiful, let the chirot go fast (16)
  • so that our talivi - (talaivi) wearing flawless ornaments (18)
  • and having a slender waist and big shoulders (17)
  • can receive and entertin guests (18)

NOTES

paḻaṅkaṇ - suffering; that is due to the difficulty of carrying the weight of water.

pōḻnta pōla, tāḻnta pōla, cērnta pōla these are figures of speech called in tamiḻ taṟkuṟippēṟṟam; a figure of speech in which the qualities and functions of an object are ascribed to another object entirely different in nature. The mild sound made by the clouds is compared to the music produced by pāṇar on their yāḻ;
""pāṇar paṇṇiya paṭumalai eḻāliṉ, vāṉattu eḻum cuvar nallicai vīḻap, peyta pulattu'' (Kuṟuntoki, 323-2-4);
""palavayiṉ nilai iya kuṉṟiṟ kōṭu tōṟu, ēyiṉai urai iyarō peruṅkali eḻili, paṭumalai niṉṟa nalyāḻ vaṭi narampu, eḻīiyaṉṉa uṟaiyiṉai'' (naṟṟiṇi, 139-2-5)
The old faded flowers of Kāyā are compared to sapphire and the cochineals to coral;
""arakkattaṉṉa cennilap peruvaḻik, kāyā ñ cemmal tā ayp palavuṭaṉ, īyal mūtāy varippap pavaḷa moṭu, maṇi miṭaintaṉṉa kuṉṟam'',
maṇi miṭai pavaḷam pōla aṇimikak, kāyāñ cemmal tā ayappala palavuṭaṉ, īyal mūtāy īrmpuṟam varippa'' (akam, 14-1-4, 304-13-15)

The new commentators have understood "viruntu etir koḷḷa' as talivi receiving talivaṉ as a guest but it would be better to say that talivi joining with talivaṉ would receive guest and entertin them, which duty she could not perform in his absence. 

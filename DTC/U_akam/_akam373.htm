Table of contents (by lines):
1-6. Talivaṉ staying in the public hall of village assembly of devastated houses; where (sponge gourd) pīr grows and spreads.
7-9. Talivaṉ sitting tying his hands over the kness, when his mind emphasizes to return home, thinking of talivi.
10-15. The state of talivi weeping in her house lying on the bed and sparking the drops of tears with her nils.
16-19. Talivaṉ pitying talivi's sleepless condition like a King besieged within a single fortification.
Colophon(s):
Talimakaṉ who was going, having parted (from talivi) spoke to his mind in the middle of the curam.

TRANSLATION
  • (My mind)! In the long and dull evening loneliness reaches me (6),
  • after becoming prostrate with suffering cused by crossing with great effort the difficult curam (5)
  • and staying in the public hall which has old pillars on which the weak couples have fallen down (4),
  • as the elephant of stout legs rubs its big back on it (3)
  • which is part of the common place; where the mari (marai: indian elk gather together in the desolate house (2)
  • where pīr (pīr: sponge gourd) grows and spreads as the place has been captured by the war-fronts and became confused (1)
  • when the strong mind emphasizes returning home (7),
  • our talivi who wears red ornaments and has many black tresses of hir (10)
  • does not understand our state of mind which thinks of her only (9)
  • sitting all alone in the posture of the hands being fastened to the knees (8)
  • lies in the bed at midnight of dense darkness (11),
  • breathing softly with a burning mind disliking us (12)
  • Her distemper being on the increase, the cool eyes which are like beautiful petals are filled with tears and she sparks the drops of tears which wet her big shoulders with the nils of her soft fingers. Did she dispense with sleep like the King besieged within a single ramport which has a battered bastion, which was surrounded by many Kings of conquering wars possessing victorious vēls and superior elephants ? She is to be pitied.

NOTES

The idea contined in ll.1-4 is found in a previous stanza in akam itself;
""koṭuvil āṭavar paṭupakai verīi; ūr eḻuntu ulaṟiya pīrkeḻu mutu pāḻ, muruṅkai mēynta peruṅkai yāṉai, veriṅ ōṅku ciṟupuṟam uriña olki, iṭṭikai neṭuñcuvar viṭṭam vīḻnteṉa'' (167-9-13)

pāḻpaṭṭa maṉṟam, maraicēr maṉṟam Talivaṉ was sitting in the public hall of the common place.

perumpuṉ mālai - this phrase may mean the evening that inflicts great suffering.

pulampu here means the loneliness having been separated from talimakaḷ

tāḷ kai pūṭṭiya taṉi nilai irukkai: people are engaged in deep thought who are fasten their knees with their hands.

maṭantai can be put in apposition with aḷiyaḷ,

uṇarāḷ pulakkum neñcu - is the syntactical order of with aḷiyaḷ,

uṇarāḷ pulakkum neñcu - is the syntactical order of word.

ōr eyil maṉṉaṉ - this refers to a petty chief who rules over a small tract of land; this simile is found in naṟṟiṇi;
"añcal eṉṟa iṟai kai viṭṭeṉap, paiṅkaṇ yāṉai vēntu puṟattu iṟuttaliṉ, kaḷaiyunarkkāṇātu kalaṅkiya uṭai matil, ōr eyil maṉṉaṉ pōla, aḻivu vantaṉṟāl oḻital kēṭṭē'' (43-8-12)

From the epithet pāṇṭiyaṉ ēṉāti we learn that this poet was the commander of the forces of pāṇtiyaṉ; ēṉāti is a title conferred on commanders; a ring was also presented along with this ;
""pōrkkaṭal āṟṟum puravit tērp pal paṭaikkuk, kārk kaṭal peṟṟa karaiyaṉṟō-pōrkkellām, tāṉ āti ākiya tār vēntaṉ, mōtiram cēr, ēṉātip paṭṭattu ivaṉ'', (an old verse quoted by nacciṉārkkiṉiyar in his commentary on tolkāppiyam 3 - puṟattiṇi iyal, 8)
ēnāti nāta nāyaṉar, (ēṉāti nāta nāyaṉār) one of the 63 sivite sints, perhaps got his family name from this tittle. 

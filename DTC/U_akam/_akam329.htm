Table of contents (by lines):
1-3. Talivi doubting the strength of mind of talivaṉ, and her distress.
4-7. The bullocks harnessed to the yoke of the carts of salt-sellers taking rest in the desert tract.
7-9. The desert path in which the blowing wind whirls the dust, where talivaṉ will rest for while.
10-12. The tiger's cub roars having bitten the trunk of the elephant which put it into the dried mountin pool to drink water to quench its thirst.
13-14. Talivi doubting whether talivaṉ would go beyond many mountins covered with snow.
Colophon(s):

TRANSLATION
  • If our lover has the strength of mind to live separtely (3)
  • having decided to part from us though we are suffering from acute distress (2),
  • the forehead and the beautiful eyes to grow sallow (1),
  • after halting for a little while in the desert paths where the blowing wind (8)
  • collects and whirls the dust that rises in the cruel places, where the bullocks (7)
  • drag the carts, and are harnessed by stright ropes to the bending yokes (6)
  • by the salt-sellers who divide the weight equally on both sides, and match the bullocks for their bells to produce sound (5)
  • in the small village which has beautiful dwelling places of huts of conical tops (4),
  • will he go, crossing the many mountins whose peaks are covered with snow, which has dried-up mountin springs where the tigress enters and gives birth to cubs (13)
  • in the thicket in which the cloud seems to be slumbering (12)?
  • There the cub of short lines which bends its stright back (10)
  • to pounce (upon its prey) roars having bitten the trunk (12)
  • of the tall and defectless elephant which placed it (inside the spring) to quench its thirst. (11)

NOTES

pūṅkaṇ: it may mean also eyes resembling lotus.

"pakal iyaintu paṇitta pakaṭu', eṉavum, "maṇi iyampappakaṭu ayāk koḷḷum' eṉavum kūṭṭuka

pakal- the central point in the yoke.

pakal iyaital choosing the bullocks which will be matching each other on both sides of the yoke, as to divide the weight equally between them.

If we take pakal to mean daytime the meaning will be that the bullocks take rest in daytime.

For ayāk koḷḷum - the new commentators have given the meaning as dragging with great strin. It is better to take it to mean the bullocks are resting.

eṟi vaḷi cuḻaṟṟum - means the whirlwind.

ciṟitu acaintu - halting there till the whirlwind is over

verin oṭikkum - stretching its back to its full length. 

TRANSLATION
  • (Chief!) Having enjoyed aquatic sports and bathed in the floods of vaiyai today too, (5)
  • and having slept in the fragrant grove yesterday also (4)
  • with a self-conceiting harlot of black complexion having cool eyes in the white portion of which there are lines, (3)
  • who has tresses of hair on which were adorned sweet-smelling pollen of fully blossomed buds (2)
  • of river portia tree of short trunk and with bunches of flowers which are like a garland (1),
  • and having returned from there and standing before us, with a chest still wet, (6)
  • can you conceal that incident (7)
  • from us (6) ?
  • The ruler of Koṟkai of great fame (10)
  • where the fishermen who catch different kinds of fish (8)
  • also draw from the expanse of the sea oysters containing pearls and pay them as price for the pride they derive from taking the toddy strained by the web of the cocoanut tree (9);
  • pacumpūtceḻiyaṉ has a victorious lance (10),
  • rough and large elephants, tall chariots, (11)
  • When he gesticulated in Kūṭal of high places resembling hills, (10)
  • the clamour was full; like that the incident is on the lips of many people and has become the subject of scandal (14)

NOTES

special notes :- The tree kāñci blossoms flowers in bunches; this is found in Kuṟuntokai also; ""payaṟu pōliṇara.......kāñciyūraṉ'' (2-4) (2-4)

Women adorn their tresses with the pollen also; this is found in tirukkōvaiyār and peruṅkatai; ""aḷinīṭakaittiṉa ataṭṭiya tātum'' (122); ""alamaru tirumukat talikata tappiya, cem poṟcuṇṇam'' (1.33-119-120). "kāviriyum taṇporuṉaiyum āṉporuṉaiyum kaiyaiyumu/ pōlum yāṟṟilum, irukāmattiṇai yēripōlum kuḷaṅkaḷilum tirumarutantuṟaik kāvē pōlum kākkaḷilum viḷaiyāṭi, uṟai patiyaik kaṭantu pōy nukarcci eytutalum, talaivaṟkum kāmakkiḻattiyarkkum talaiviyarkkum uriya' (tolkāppiyam, poruḷiyal 50, nacciṉārkkiṉiyar's commentary) may be compared with the lines 3-5).

The poet being a native of maturai has mentioned vaiyai.

pularāmārpiṉai: may mean this also; talaivaṉ standing before talaivi with the marks made by the harlot which are still visible.

ippi stands for the pearl inside it.

pacumpūṭpānṭiyaṉ won a victory over Koṅkar; his warriors performed a dance out of joy in his capital, kūṭal; ""alarē, vāṭāp pūviṟ koṅkar ōṭṭi, nāku pala tanta pacum pūṇ pāṇṭiyaṉ, poṉmali neṭu nakark kūṭal āṭiya, iṉṉicai ārppiṉum peritē'' (akam. 253-3-7).

The new commentators have given the name of paṇṭiyaṉ as neṭuñceḻiyaṉ and has given the above lines as a parallel quotation; it is wrong, pacumpūṇ paṇtiyaṉ performed the dance with his warriors to express his joy born of victory

Oysters are found in such abundance in Koṟkai that fishermen not knowing their value given them a price for toddy.

In line 14 ākiṉṟatu should be split into ākiṉṟu atu;

In naṟṟiṇai in two places the word "ākiṉṟatu' is found in print 227-9, 354-11, but "ākiṉṟatu' is a later usage; in those two places also the form of the word should be only ākiṉṟu 

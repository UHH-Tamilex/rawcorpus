mukavuri.

icciṟukati āṅkilēya pāṣiyil "pārṉel" (PARNELL) eṉṟa kaviñarāliyaṟṟappaṭṭa "tuṟavi"
eṉṟu aruttaṅkoḷḷum "hermiṭ" (HERMIT) eṉṉum kavitiyiṉ moḻipeyarppu.
"ellām īcaṉ ceyal" "avaṉaṉṟiyōraṇuvu maciyātu" eṉṟu nammavarkkuḷ vaḻaṅku mūturikaḷuk
kiṇaṅkiyatāy, avvavar viṉik kēṟpap payaṉūṭṭuvittu ekkālattum karuṇikkaṭalāyilaṅkum
kaṭavuḷiṭattu kuṟṟaṅkūṟal koṭiya pāvam eṉṟu oḻukivarum naṅkoḷkik kottatāy, kaviti
curukkameṉiṉum pēriṉpappēṟṟiṉ aṟivip pukaṭṭuvatāy, mēṟkūṟiya kaviñarāl coṟcuvi
poruṭcuviyiraṇṭum kalantamittiyaṟṟap paṭṭati tamiḻ kaṟkum māṇākkariṉupayōkattukkāka eṉ
ciṟṟaṟivilutittavaṇṇam moḻipeyarttacciṭat tuṇintēṉ. evvitat tavaṟukaḷiruppiṉum
kaṟṟaṟintōr karuṇikūrnteṉakkuṇartta vēṇṭukiṉṟēṉ.
āṅkilēya pāṣiyil tērcciyuḷḷōr ottuppārttuk koḷvataṟkāka mūlakirantamum ittuṭaṉ
cērttu acciṭappaṭṭirukkiṟatu.
cēlam.
si.irāmacantira ayyar.
1904-varuṣam pipravari mātam

maṉaṅkuḻampiya mātavattōṉ.

nāṭṭukku vekutūrattiluḷḷatōr kāṭṭil maṉitar kaṇṇukkup pulappaṭāmal yāvarālum naṉku
matikkat takkavarāy oru tuṟavi vālipam mutal vārttikamīṟāka vaḷarntuvantār. pācipaṭarnta
pārē pāyalākavum nilavaṟiyē nilayamākavum kaṉivarkkaṅkaḷē uṇavākavum teḷḷiya kiṇaṟṟut
taṇṇīrē pāṉamākavum koṇṭu maṉitar kūṭṭuṟavi nīttuk kaṭavuḷē katiyeṉak
kālaṅkaḻittuvantār. avar ceyyum toḻil ellām kaṭavuḷippirārttippatutāṉ. avar
nukarumiṉpamuṟṟum kaṭavuḷi vaḻuttalētāṉ.
ittakiya paricuttamum niccalamāṉa maṉavamitiyumuḷḷa ittuṟavikku oru cantēkama
uṇṭāyiṟṟu. inta cantēkam uṇṭākiṟavariyil ivaruṭiya vāḻkki mōṭca cāmpirācciyameṉṟē
collalām. ulakil "maṟam" mēliṭṭu "aṟam" kuṉṟi nallōr tīyōri vaṇaṅkum
paṭiyiruttalāl, kaṭavuḷ taṉ āṇiyic carivara celutti varukiṉṟaṉarō ilarō eṉṟa caṅki ivar
maṉatil kuṭikoṇṭatu. ivarukkut tāṉ koṇṭirunta eṇṇaṅkaḷil niccayaputti kuṉṟi yatāl
maṉōnimmati cuttamā yillāmal pōyviṭṭatu. evvāṟeṉil, amitiyuḷḷa nīrpparappil atic
cuṟṟiyuḷḷa karikaḷ maraṅkaḷ mutaliyaṉa talikīḻākat toṅkikkāṭṭiyum ākāyattil tōṉṟum
varṇaṅkaḷ yāvum piratipalittut tōṉṟiyum niṟkum camayattil ataṉmēl kalleṟiyappaṭiṉ
amitiyuṟṟirunta vannīrpparappu pakukkappaṭṭu kalakkamuṟṟut tirikaḷ eṅkum mēṉmēlum
ativēkamāy viruttaṅkaḷākaccuḻaṉṟum atil kāṇappaṭum cūriyapimpam palavāṟākak
kūṟupaṭṭum, karikaḷ, maraṅkaḷ, ākāyat tōṟṟaṅkaḷaṉittum oṉṟāyk kuḻampuvatu pōlāyiṟṟu.
iccantēkam teḷitaṟporuṭṭu ulakiṉarippaṟṟit tāṉ puttakaṅkaḷil paṭittatum
kuṭiyāṉavarkaḷāl ceviyuṟṟiruntatum uṇmitāṉōveṉṟu tāṉē nēril kaṇṇāl pārttaṟintu
koḷḷat taṉ nilavaṟiyiviṭṭup puṟappaṭṭār. (ituvariyil ivar ulakiṉarippaṟṟik
kēṭṭiruntatellām taṉṉiṭattil irākkālattil paṉiyāl varunti taṭumāṟṟamkoṇṭu
vaḻitappivarum kuṭiyāṉavarkaḷālēyām.) taṇṭattittarittār. oṭṭi taṉ taliccīrāvil
muṉṉāka māṭṭiṉār. cūriya utayamāṉavuṭaṉ etiyum cāntamāy maṉatiṟ koḷḷavum ovvoru
campavattiyum jākkiratiyuṭaṉ kavaṉikkavum tīrmāṉittup pirayāṇam pōṉār.
puṟkaḷ ceṟintu pātiyillāta taṭattil kālippoḻutik kaḻittār. tolitūram
tuṇiyārumiṉṟi ivvaṉattiṟ ceṉṟār. eṉṟālum poḻutu ēṟi teṉpāloḷirum[1] veyyōṉ
veppamuṟittapiṉ oru vālipaṉ kuṟukkuvaḻiyāy ativēkamāy vantāṉ. avaṉ nallāṭiyaṇintu
cuntaravaṭivamāy taliyil aḻakiya curuṭṭi mayirkaḷ melliya kāṟṟāl aciyap
peṟṟiruntāṉ. camīpattil vantu "eṉpitāvē vantaṉam" eṉṟāṉ. ataṟku mutiyōrum "eṉ
kuḻantāy vāḻi" eṉṟāci kūṟiṉār. vārttimēl vārtti vaḷarntu oruvari yoruvar
viṉavutalum viṭiyaḷittalumāy palavāṟākap pēcikkoṇṭu naṭiyiṉ varuttantōṉṟātu
ceṉṟaṉar. muṭivil iruvarukkuḷḷum anyōnya pirītiyuṇṭāki oruvariviṭṭu maṟṟoruvar
piriya maṉamiṉṟi vayatil tāratammiyam iruntālum oru mutirnta vaṉavirukṣamāṉatu
paṭarkoṭiyāl kaṭṭuṇṭatupōl mutiyavarum, iḷaṅkoṭiyāṉatu virukṣattit
taḻuviyiruppatupōl vālipaṉummaṉamorumittu naṭantārkaḷ.
[1] āṅkilēya nāṭu uttara akṣāmcam pāki 50kku mēṟpaṭṭiruttalāl annāṭṭiluḷḷōrkku
evviṭattum yāṇṭum kiḻakkilutittu mēṟkil maṟiyum cūriyaṉ vāṉ mukaṭuṟātu ataṟkut
teṉpuṟamāyt tōṟṟumātalāl "teṉpāloḷirum veyyōṉ" eṉamutaṉūlāciriyar kūṟiṉar.
ippaṭiyirukkap pakalavaṉ kuṭaticiyil mūḻkiṉāṉ. mālippoḻutu maṅkiṉa veṇṇiṟap pōrvi
pōrttu mēleḻumpivara ulakiṉar uṟaṅkakkaṭṭaḷi yiṭappaṭṭatu pōṉṟiruntatu. appoḻutu
pātikkaṇittatāka periyatōr araṇmaṉiyik kaṇṭārkaḷ. atinōkki nilāveḷiccattil
pātiyiṉiru cārpukaḷilum kīḻē pulmuḷittu mēlē pacumiyākat taḻittōṅkum
variciyāyuḷḷa maraṅkaḷiṉaṭuvē pōṉārkaḷ. anta araṇmaṉiyil vatiyum perumāṉ eṉṟum
taṉṉillatti vaḻitappivarum vitēciyiṉ viṭutiyākkivantāṉ, eṉṟālum avaṉuṭiya
karuṇiyāṉatu pukaḻi virumpiyatāl āṭamparattuṭaṉ vicēṣacelavu ceytupeṟṟa cukamāy
muṭintatu. ivviruvarum aṅkuṟṟavuṭaṉ uṭiyaṇinta vēliyāṭkaḷālupacarikkappaṭṭu aṉṉōriṉ
talivaṉāl āṭamparamākavirunta talivāyiliṭattē nalvaravu koṇṭāṭap peṟṟārkaḷ. tīṉimējiyō
rāja pōjaṉavakikaḷ aṭukkaṭukkāy vikkappaṭṭu cumitāṅka muṭiyāmaliruntatu.
oṉṟuṅkuṟiviṉṟi, cātāraṇamāy viruntiṉar makiḻac ceyyum ēṟpāṭukaḷukku mēlākavē
yiruntatu. uṇṭikaḻittup paḷḷiyaṟi ceṉṟu nāḷmuḻutum naṭanta varuttamtīra paṭṭuvēynta
pañcaṇi mettiyil paṭuttayarntuṟaṅkiṉārkaḷ.
piṟaku poḻutu viṭintatu. aruṇōtayamāṉavuṭaṉ akaṉṟa kālvāykaḷiṉ vaḻiyāy iḷaṅkāṟṟu
vīciviḷiyāṭi maṉōkkiyamāṉa nantavaṉaṅkaḷiṉmēl paṭarntu arukē niṉṟa
virukṣaṅkaḷiyaciyacceytu tūkkattit tolikkakkaṭṭaḷiyiṭuvatu pōliruntatu.
ikkaṭṭaḷikkukkīḻppaṭivatupōl viruntiṉariruvarum tuyil nītteḻuntaṉar. uṭaṉē
tivviyamāṉa pōjaṉamaṇṭapamāṉatu viruntuṇavukaḷālalaṅkarikkappaṭṭatu.
oru taṅkakkiṇṇattil viliyuyarnta māturiyamāṉa tirāṭca racam paḷapaḷaveṉṟu
pirakācittukkoṇṭiruntatu. ati yaṉpuṭaṉ ammaṉik kiḻavaṉ avarkaḷi yaruntavēṇṭiṉāṉ.
pōjaṉa muṭittuc cantōṣattuṭaṉ vantaṉamaḷittu avviṭatti viṭṭu iruvarum puṟappaṭṭārkaḷ.
āṉālanta vīṭṭiṉ ejamāṉaṉukkumāttiram tukkās patamāṉa ceykiyoṉṟu naṭantatu.
ateṉṉaveṉil, avaṉuṭiya taṅkakkiṇṇam kāṇāmaṟ pōyviṭṭatu. eppaṭiyeṉil vālipap
paruvamuḷḷa viruntiṉaṉ ati rakaciyamāy maṟittu tiruṭikkoṇṭu pōyviṭṭāṉ.
maṟupaṭi vaḻinaṭantu vekutūram ceṉṟapiṟakuk kapaṭamuḷḷa vālipat tōḻaṉ tāṉ tiruṭik
koṇṭuvanta takattakāyamāṉa poṟkiṇṇattit tuṟavikkuk kāṭṭiṉāṉ. atikkaṇṭuṭaṉ evvaṇṇam
oru pirayāṇi vaḻiyil paḷapaḷappuṭaṉ cūriya kiraṇattiṉ veppattil kuḷirkāyntu koṇṭu
paṭuttirukkum oru carppattik kaṇṭāl naṭukkamuṟṟu oṉṟun tōṉṟātu kiṭṭiṉa
āpattiṉiṉṟu taṉṉi vilakkikkoḷḷa caṟṟuniṉṟu piṟaku payantavaṉāyc cōrvuṟṟu mella naṭanta
pōvaṉō avvaṇṇamāyp periyavarum vāy pēcāmalcaṟṟu niṉṟu piṟaku maṉanaṭukkattuṭaṉ naṭantu
ceṉṟār. avvālipaṉiviṭṭu vilakippōkavēṇṭumeṉṟu avar maṉatil iccikoḷḷiṉum avaṉuṭaṉ
colla añciṉār. taṉakkuḷ muṟumuṟuttukkoṇṭu vāṉatti nōkki, utāramāṉa ceykikaḷ
iḻivāṉa kimmāṟaṭiyalāyiṟṟē eṉṉa kaṣṭameṉṟēṅkiṉār.
ivvaṇṇamiruvarum cellukiyil katirōṉ taṉ ṉoḷiyi maṟittāṉ. māṟupaṭṭa ākāyaṅkaḷil
nīlamēkaṅkaḷ tōṉṟiṉa. vāṉattil iṭimuḻakkam uṇṭākavē maḻivarum pōliruntatu. vilaṅkukaḷ
vēkamāy uṟiviṭatti nōkkic ceṉṟaṉa. ikkuṟikaḷikkaṇṭa pirayāṇikaḷiruvarum camīpattiluḷḷa
oru vīṭṭil pukaliṭam tēṭicceṉṟaṉar. anta vīṭō palamuḷḷatāyum peritāyum cuṟṟilum
tiruttappaṭātatāyum cikaraṅkaḷvittu oru mēṭṭup pūmiyil kaṭṭappaṭṭiruntatu.
ataṟkuriyavaṉōpayaṅkāḷi; kaṭiṉacittamuḷḷavaṉ; irakkamaṟṟavaṉ. evvitatāṉum paṇam cērppati
leṇṇamuṭiyavaṉ. ākiyāl avviṭam pāḻnilamāyiruntatu.
kaṉatta katavukaḷāl aṭipaṭṭirunta intak kirupaṇaṉuṭiya vīṭṭinōkki avviruvarum vantu
koṇṭirukkiyil tiṭīreṉṟu peruṅkāṟṟu aṭittatu. paḷīreṉṟu vāṉam miṉṉikkoṇṭu
maḻipeyya vārampittatu. payaṅkaramāy iṭikaḷ iṭittaṉa. pirayāṇikaḷiruvarum vīṭṭukkuvantu
katavi vekunēram iṭittārkaḷ. urakkak kūppiṭṭārkaḷ. eṉṉaceytum kēḷvimuṟiyilli.
kāṟṟaṭippatālum maḻi poḻivatālum metta varuntiṉārkaḷ. vekunēramkaḻittu vīṭṭu
ejamāṉaṉukku maṉatiḷakiṟṟu. avaṉ vīṭu viruntiṉarikkaṇṭatu avaṉ āyuḷil itutāṉ
mutaltaṭavi. camucayattuṭaṉ atijākkiratiyāy kiṟicceṉṟu katavu cattikka melleṉak
katavittiṟantu kuḷirālum maḻiyālum naṭuṅki niṟkumiruvariyum
arimaṉatuṭaṉuḷḷēyaḻittāṉ. erintukoṇṭirunta orēoru viṟaku kucciyāl veṟṟuccuvarkaḷ
veḷiyāyiṉa. uḷḷē pōṉatum ivarkaḷukku carīrattil iyaṟkiyāyuḷḷa uṣṇam paraviniṉṟatu.
vīṭṭu ejamāṉaṉ vitiyillāmalkoṭutta mikavummaṭṭa tiṉucu roṭṭiyum puḷitta
tirāṭcarasamuntāṉ ivarkaḷukku uṇavāka utaviṉa. kāṟṟum maḻiyum koñcam taṇiyavē "nīṅkaḷ
pōyvaralām" eṉṟu vīṭṭukkāraṉ eccarittāṉ.
tuṟaviyāṉavar oṉṟum pēcāmal muṉamāyiruntu "ivvaḷavu paṇakkāraṉ tarittiraṉip pōlum
nākarīkam koñcamēṉumiṉṟi vāḻkiṉṟaṉaṉē. anēkamāyiram ēḻijaṉaṅkḷ piḻikkak kūṭiya
iccelvatti ivaṉ yātukāraṇam paṟṟi pūṭṭivittup pāḻākkukiṉṟāṉ." eṉṟu taṉakkuḷḷē
ālōcittaṉar. ippaṭiyirukkiyil putitāṉatō rāccariyattiṉ kuṟikaḷ ivar mukattil
viḷaṅkiṉa. atāvatu avvutāra cīlaṉuṭiya viliyuyarnta kiṇṇatti ivvālipaṉ
taṉṉuṭikkuḷḷiruntu veḷiyileṭuttu veṭuveṭuppuḷḷa anta lōpi tīrātu pārāṭṭiṉa
aṉpukkukkimmāṟāka avaṉukku koṭuttuviṭṭāṉ.
itaṟkuḷ mēkaṅkaḷ kalintu cūriyaṉ veḷippaṭavē ākāyam nīlavarṇam porunti
nirmalamāyviṭṭatu. vācaṉiyuḷḷa ilikaḷ putiya pacumi pūṇṭu aciyuntōṟum paḷapaḷaveṉṟu
tulaṅkip pārkka ullācakaramāyiruntaṉa. kālaniliyum ivarkaḷip pukaliṭattiruntu
veḷiyēvarumpaṭi viḷippatu pōṉṟiruntatu. ivarkaḷveḷippaṭṭatum anta kirakastaṉ
cantōṣattuṭaṉ jākkiratiyāyk kataviccāttit tāḷiṭṭukkoṇṭāṉ.
iruvarum avviṭamviṭṭuppōykkoṇṭirukkiyil tuṟavi taṉṉuṭiya tōḻaṉ ceytakāriyaṅkaḷiṉ
kāraṇam niccayamāytteriyāmal maṉaṅkuḻampip piracava vētaṉiyippōṉṟa varuttamaṭintār. avaṉ
aṅkuceytatu pāvacceyki. iṅkiyaṟṟiyatō pittiyakkāraṉuṭiya ceyki. muntiyati
veṟuttukkoṇṭu pintiyatik kuṟittu paritapittut taṉkaṇṇukkup pulappaṭum nāṉāvitat
tōṟṟaṅkaḷāl matimayaṅki oṉṟuntōṉṟātu ceṉṟaṉar.
maṟupaṭikaṅkuṟpoḻutu vāṉattik kavarntatu. pirayāṇikaḷukku uṟiviṭam vēṇṭumē eṉ
ceyvār? maṟupaṭi tēṭicceṉṟu camīpattiloru viṭutiyikkaṇṭārkaḷ. atu cuṟṟilum pūmi
naṉṟāyt tiruttappaṭṭu, māḷikiyāṉatu kēvalam tarittiramiṉṟiyum vicēṣa
āṭamparamiṉṟiyum paricuttamāyk kāṇappaṭṭu pukaḻivirumpātu aṉpuṭaṉ aṟampūṇṭoḻuki
tiruptiyuṭaṉirukkum taṉ ejamāṉaṉuṭiya maṉōtarmam iṉṉateṉak kāṭṭuvatu pōliruntatu.
ivvillattinōkki vaḻi naṭappōriruvarum varuntivantu māḷikiyi vāḻtti ejamāṉaṉikkaṇṭu
vaṇakkattuṭaṉ ucitamāṉa upacāravārttikaḷ colla ati avaṉ vinayattuṭaṉ kēṭṭup piṉvarumāṟu
maṟumoḻi kūṟaluṟṟāṉ.
" 'yāṉ' 'eṉatu' eṉṟa cerukkaṟṟu
maṉatilaḻukkāṟiṉṟi nammaṉōrkku ellāmtaravalla kaṭavuḷukku eṉṉiṭattiliruppati arppaṇam
ceyvēṉ. nīvir akkaṭavuḷiṭattiruntuvarukiṉṟi rātalāṉavarukkāka yāṉ niṣkapaṭamāyum
cātuvāyum āṉatupaṟṟi atikaporuṭcelavuṭaṉ kūṭiya kaḷiyāṭṭukkum mēmpaṭṭatāyum
vaḻaṅkuvati nīvir aṅkīkarikkavēṇṭum." avaṉ ivvaṇṇamurittut tīṉimējiyipparappi uṇṭi
kaḻintapiṟaku paṭukkappōkumvariyil aṟaneṟikaḷippaṟṟip pēcivantāṉ. pēriyalpuḷḷa

PARNELL'S HERMIT.

Far in a wild, unknown to public view,
From youth to age a reverend hermit grew;
The moss his bed, the cave his humble cell,
His food the fruits, his drink the crystal well;
Remote from man, with God he passed his days,
Prayer all his business, all his pleasure prise.
A life so sacred, such serene repose,
Seemed heaven itself, till one suggestion rose-
That vice should triumph, virtue vice obey;
This sprung some doubt of Providence's sway.
His hopes no more a certin prospect boast,
And all the tenour of his soul is lost.
So, when a smooth expanse receives imprest
Calm Nature's image on its watery breast,
Down bend the banks, the trees depending grow,
And skies beneath with answering colours glow:
But, if a stone the gentle sea divide,
Swift ruffling circles curl on every side,
And glimmering fragments of a broken sun,
Banks, trees, and skies, in thick disorder run.
To clear this doubt, to know the world by sight,
To find if books or swains report it right,
(For yet by swains alone the world he knew,
Whose feet came wandering o'ver the nightly dew,)
He quits his cell; the pilgrim staff he bore,
And fixed the scallop in his hat before;
Then, with the rising sun, a journey went,
Sedate to think, and watching each event.
The morn was wasted in the pathless grass,
And long and lonesome was the wild to pass;
But when the southern sun had warmed the day,
A youth came posting o'er a crossing way:
His riment decent, his complexion fair,
And soft in graceful ringlets waved his hir;
Then, near approaching, "Father, hil!" he cried,
And, "Hail my son!" the reverend sire replied.
Words followed words, from question answer flowed,
And talk of various kind deceived the road;
Till each with other peased, and loth to part,
While in their age they differ, join in heart.
Thus stands an aged elm in ivy bound,
Thus youthful ivy clasps an elm around.
Now sunk the sun; the closing hour of day
Came onward, mantled o'er with sober gray;
Nature is silence bade the world repose:
When near the road a stately palace rose.
There, by the moom, through ranks of trees they pass,
Whose verdure crowned the sloping sides of grass.
It chanced the noble master of the dome
Still made his house the wandering stranger's home;
Yet still the kindness, from a thirst of prise,
Proved the vin flourish of expensive ease.
The pir arrive; the liveried servants wait;
Their lord receives them at the pompous gate.
The table groans with costly piles of food,
And all is more than hospitably good.
Then, led to rest, the day's long toil they drown,
Deep sunk in sleep, and silk, and heaps of down.
At length 'tis morn; and, at the dawn of day,
Along the wide canals the zephyrs play;
Fresh o'er the gay parterres the breezes creep,
And shake the neighbouring wood to banish sleep.
Up rise the guests, obedient to the call;
An early banquet decked the splendid hall;
Rich luscious wine a golden goblet graced,
Which the kind master forced the guests to taste,
Then, pleased and thankful, from the porch they go,
And, but the landlord, none had cuse of woe:
His cup was vanished; for in secret guise
The younger guest purloined the glittering prize.
As one who spies a serpent in his way,
Glistening and basking in the summer ray,
Disordered stops to shun the danger near,
Then walks with faintness on, and looks with fear,
So seemed the sire, when far upon the road
The shining spoil his wily partner showed.
He stopped with silence, walked with trembling heart
And much he wished, but durst not ask to part:
Murmuring he lifts his eyes, and thinks it hard,
That generous actions meet a base reward.
While thus they pass, the sun his glory shrouds,
The changing skies hang out their sable clouds;
A sound in air presaged approaching rin,
And beasts to covert scud across the plin.
Warned by the signs, the wandering pir retreat,
To seek for shelter at a neighbouring seat.
'Twas built with turrets on a rising ground,
And strong, and large, and unimproved around,
Its owner's temper, timorous and severe,
Unkind and griping, cused a desert there.
As near the miser's heavy doors they drew,
Fierce rising gusts with sudden fury blew;
The nimble lightning mixed with showers began,
And o'er their heads loud rolling thunder ran.
Here long they knock, but knock or call in vin,
Driven by the wind, and battered by the rin.
At length some pity warmed the master's breast;
('Twas then his threshold first received a guest;)
Slow creaking turns the door with jealous care,
And half he welcomes in the shivering pir:
One frugal faggot lilghts the naked walls,
And nature's fervour through their limbs recalls:
Bread of the coarsest sort with eager wine,
(Each hardly granted,) served them both to dine;
And, when the tempest first appeared to cease,
A ready warning bid them part in peace.
With still remark the pondering hermit viewed,
In one so rich, a life so poor and rude;
"And why should such," (within himself he cried,)
"Lock the lost wealth a thousand want beside?"
But what new marks of wonder soon took place,
In every setting feature of his face,
When from his vest the young companion bore
That cup the generous landlord owned before,
And pid profusely with the precious bowl
The stinted kindness of this churlish soul!
But now the clouds in airy tumult fly;
The sun emerging opes an azure sky;
A fresher green the smelling leaves display,
And, glittering as they tremble, cheer the day:
The weather courts them from the poor retreat
And the glad master bolts the wary gate.
While hence they walk, the pilgrim's bosom wrought
With all the travil of uncertin thought;
His partner's acts without their cuse appear;
'Twas there a vice, and seemed a madness here:
Detesting that, and pitying this, he goes,
Lost and confounded with the various shows.
Now night's dim aines* shade involve the sky;
Agin the wanderers want a place to lie:
Agin they search, and find a lodging nigh.
The soil improved around, the mansion neat,
And neither poorly low, nor idly great,
It seemed to speak its master's turn of mind,
Content, and not for prise but virtue kind.
Hither the walkers turn with weary feet,
Then bless the mansion, and the master greet:
Their greeting fair, bestowed with modest guise,
The courteous master hears, and thus replies:
"Without a vin, without a grudging heart,
To Him who gives us all, I yield a part;
From Him you come, for Him accept it here
A frank and sober, more than costly cheer."
He spoke, and bade the welcome table spread,
Then talked of virtue till the time of bed;
When the grave household round his hall repir,
Warmed by a bell, and close the hours with prayer.

At length the world, renewed by calm repose,
Was strong for toil; the dappled morn arose.
Before the pilgrims part, the younger crept
Near the closed cradle where an infant slept,
And writhed his neck: the landlord's little pride,
O strange return! grew black, and gasped, and died.
Horror of horrors! what! his only son!
How looked our hermit when the fact was done?
Not hell, though hell's black jaws in sunder part,
And breathe blue fire, could more assult his heart.
Confused, and struck with silence at the deed,
He flies; but, trembling, fails to fly with speed.
His steps the youth pursues; the country lay
Perplexed with roads; a servant showed the way;
A river crossed the path; the passage o'er
Was nice to find; the servant trod before:
Long arms of oaks an open bridge supplied,
And deep the waves beneath them bending glide.
The youth, who seemed to watch a time to sin,
Approached the careless guide, and thrust him in:
Plunging he falls, and rising lifts his head,
Then flashing turns, and sinks among the dead.
Wild sparkling rage inflames the pilgrim's eyes;
He bursts the bonds of fear, and madly cries,
"Detested wretch!" - But scarce his speech began
When the strange partner seemed no longer man.
His youthful face grew more serenely sweet;
His robe turned white, and flowed upon his feet;
Fair rounds of radiant points invest his hir;
Celestial odours breathe through purpled air;
And wings whose colours glittered on the day,
Wide at his back their gradual plumes display.
The form ethereal bursts upon sight,
And moves in all the majesty of light.
Though loud at first the pilgrim's passion grew,
Sudden he gazed, and wist not what to do;
Surprise in secret chins his words suspends,
And in a calm his settling temper ends.
But silence here the beauteous angel broke;
(The voice of music ravished as he spoke.)
'Thy prayer, thy prise, the life to vice unknown,
In sweet memorial rise before the throne:
These charms success in our bright region find,
And force an angel down to calm thy mind;
For this commissioned, I forsook the sky-
Nay, cease to kneel-thy fellow servant I.
Then know the truth of government Devine,
And let these scruples be no longer thine.
The maker justly clims that world He made:
In this the right of Providence is lid.
Its sacred majesty through all depends
On using second means to work His ends.
'Tis thus, withdrawn in state from human eye,
The power exerts his attributes on high;
Your actions uses nor controls your will;
And bids the doubting sons of men be still,
What strange events can strike with more surprise,
Than those which lately met thy wondering eyes?
Yet, tught by these, confess the Almighty just,
And, where you can't unriddle, learn to trust.
"The great vin man, who fared on costly food
Whose lilfe was too luxurious to be good;
Who made his ivory stands with goblets shine,
And forced his guests to morning drughts of wine,
Has, with the cup, the graceless custom lost,
And still he welcomes, but with less of cost.
"The mean suspicious wretch whose bolted door
Ne'er moved in pity to the wandering poor,
With him I left the cup to teach his mind
That Heaven can bless if mortals will be kind.
Conscious of wanting worth, he views the bowl,
And feels compassion touch his grateful soul.
Thus artists melt the sullen ore of lead,
With heaping coals of fire upon its head:
In the kind warmth the metal learns to glow,
And loose from dross, the silver runs below.
"Long had our pious friend in virtue trod;
But now the child half-weaned his heart from God.
Child of his age, for him he lived in pin,
And measured back his steps to earth agin.
To what excesses had his dotage run!
But God, to save the father, took the son.
To all but these in fits, he seemed to go;
And 'twas my ministry to deal the blow.
The poor fond parent, humbled in the dust,
Now owns in tears the punishment was just.
But how had all his fortunes felt a rack,
Had that false servant sped in safety back.
This night his treasured heaps he meant to steal,
And what a fund of charity would fail!
Thus Heaven instructs thy mind: this trial o'er
Depart in peace, resigned, and sin no more."
On sounding pinions here the youth withdrew;
The sage stood wondering as the seraph flew.
Thus looked Elisha when, to mount on high,
His master took the chariot of the sky:
The fiery pomp ascending left the view;
The prophet gazed, and wished to follow too.
The bending hermit here a prayer began:
"Lord! as in heaven, on earth thy will be done."
Then, gladly turning, sought his ancient place,
And passed a life of piety and peace.
